## 2. Pain clusters

### C1. Mid-term (temporary) lease evidence and lifecycle compliance
- **Pain IDs:** HOU-01, HOU-02, HOU-04, HOU-05, HOU-12, HOU-13, HOU-15, HOU-16, HOU-17, HOU-19, HOU-23, HOU-24.
- **Problem statement:**
  - **Who and how often.** Every landlord, manager or coliving operator letting a furnished flat or room for 32 days to 12 months must now do the following for every contract.
  - **Cause.** Write the specific temporary-displacement cause into the lease and keep proof of it. The burden of proof is on the landlord. Without it the lease counts as habitual housing from signature, with a 5-year minimum (7 for company landlords) (HOU-01; [BOE, RDL 29/2026](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2026-20823)).
  - **Duration.** Count total duration and chained contracts. More than 12 months, or more than two chained contracts, turns the first one into a habitual lease. Tenants can leave after one month on 10 days' notice (HOU-05).
  - **Deposits.** Lodge the fianza with the regional body: within 2 months in Catalonia, 30 working days in Madrid, one month in Canarias. An unlodged deposit can cost up to €90,000 in Madrid (HOU-12; [El Español, 18 Mar 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/sociedad/20260318/entrado-vigor-penalizara-caseros-no-depositen-fianza-comunidad-autonoma/1003744163850_0.html)).
  - **Move-out and repairs.** Sign an end-of-lease condition record, or the flat is presumed returned in good condition (HOU-15). Answer repair requests within 5 working days, or the tenant may repair and deduct (HOU-16).
  - **Rooms and charges.** Keep the sum of room rents at or below the whole-flat rent (HOU-04), and keep bundled utilities and "mandatory extras" transparent (HOU-24).
  - **Tax.** Classify each stay for VAT: furnished stays of 30 nights or fewer pay 10% from 1 Dec 2026, but only if the decree is convalidated (HOU-17).
  - **Tenant fit.** Triage tenants who do not fit the cause, such as relocating employees on indefinite contracts and "try-out" movers (HOU-23).
  - **Courts.** Judges already re-classify on objective indicators. Albacete (11 Jul 2025) rejected an eviction; A Coruña (26 Feb 2026) granted a 7-year term (HOU-13; [idealista, 6 Jul 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/07/06/901838-la-clave-no-es-la-duracion-cuando-un-alquiler-de-temporada-pasa-a-ser-de-vivienda)).
  - **Scale.** Seasonal lets are 34% of rental listings nationally: Barcelona 57%, Málaga 37%, Madrid 35%, Valencia 35% ([Pressdigital/idealista, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.pressdigital.es/articulo/economia/2026-10-20/6058369-oferta-alquiler-permanente-cae-10-espana-mientras-temporal-crece-7-segun-idealista)). Consumo estimates 1.15M people live in seasonal or room rentals, with an undisclosed method ([idealista, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/finanzas/economia/2026/09/07/912998-consumo-estima-que-mas-de-un-millon-de-personas-vive-en-alquiler-de-temporada-o-de)). My calculation from HOS-13: temporary rentals were 14.7% of 400,362 registry applications, about 58,900 by 8 Jan 2026. That is a floor proxy for units, not a count of landlords.
  - **Conversion pressure.** Barcelona's 10,101 tourist-flat (HUT) licences end in November 2028. Madrid's seasonal offer is up 52% year on year; Barcelona's is down 37% since the Catalan law (HOU-19).
- **Driver and status as of Oct 2026:**
  - RDL 29/2026 has been in force since 8 Oct 2026. The Diputación Permanente vote is expected around 12 Nov 2026 (single source: [gestionatualojamiento](https://gestionatualojamiento.com/convalidacion-del-decreto-de-vivienda/)). If the decree is not convalidated, the national seasonal rules, the VAT change and the IBI surcharges lapse, and the status of contracts signed between 8 Oct and the vote is unclear (HOU-02).
  - Rules changed three times in 2026: RDL 26/2026 on 1 Oct, reverted on 2 Oct, RDL 29/2026 on 8 Oct.
  - The notes date the dissolution of the Cortes to 5 Oct (dissolution decree in the BOE on 6 Oct), with elections on 29 Nov and the new Cortes sitting on 23 Dec 2026.
  - Catalonia's Llei 11/2025 has been in force since January 2026. The Constitutional Court admitted the PP's appeal on 15 Jun 2026 but did not suspend the law.
  - Re-classification under art. 6.4 Código Civil applies whatever happens to the decree.
- **Used today and price:**
  - Law-firm templates, agencies, gestorías and spreadsheets.
  - Homming has a free tier (1 or 5 properties, depending on the source); it was not verified to track causes or chaining.
  - Spotahome offers e-signed contracts.
  - Signaturit charges €35 per user per month for 5 envelopes (voice table).
  - INCASÒL deposits are filed by hand with a digital certificate.
  - No tool validates and archives cause evidence (HOU-01), and no rules engine versions contracts by regime (HOU-02).
- **Conflicts in the notes:**
  - No official guidance says whether a temporary lease outside Catalonia carries one month's fianza or two (HOU-12).
  - Canarias fines for unlodged deposits are given as 35–100% (Lázaro Amable) and as 5–75% (El Español).

### C2. Rent-setting caps and annual updates (long-term and temporary)
- **Pain IDs:** HOU-03, HOU-22, CON-17, CON-18.
- **Problem statement:**
  - **Where caps apply.** Declared tensioned zones: Catalonia's 271 municipalities plus 53 more declared on 19 Oct 2026 and pending in the BOE; 17–19 in País Vasco; 21 in Navarra; A Coruña; 5 Asturian municipalities from 30 Jul 2026 (CON-17; [OCU, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.ocu.org/fincas-y-casas/gestion/alquiler/analisis/2026/10/nuevas-zonas-tensionadas-cataluna)).
  - **First rent.** Every new lease, including seasonal and room lets in Catalonia, must be priced from the last rent of the past five years or the SERPAVI index, which has no values under 30 m² or over 150 m². Ads must show the previous rent and the index (HOU-03).
  - **Annual updates, nationally.** Updates may not exceed the IRAV and are capped at 2% until 31 Dec 2027 unless agreed. If the rent already exceeds the index, no increase is allowed (CON-17, CON-18; [idealista, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/13/918359-que-cambia-para-los-propietarios-con-los-decretos-prorrogas-tope-al-alquiler-del-2)).
  - **Successive temporary contracts.** Rent on successive temporary contracts for the same dwelling may not rise faster than the IRAV. The notes read this as nationwide, which is a literal reading (HOU-22).
  - **Frequency and cost.** It comes up at every new lease and every anniversary. Catalan fines run from €3,000 to €90,000; actual fines include €30,000 ×2, €3,000 ×2 and €1,750 paid for omitting the index from an ad (HOU-03; [Consum, 3 Feb 2026](https://consum.gencat.cat/ca/detalls/noticia/primers-sancionadors-contencio-preu-lloguers)).
  - **Target cities.** Only Barcelona is tensioned. Madrid, Valencia, Málaga, Sevilla, Alicante and Palma are not (HOU-03, CON-17).
- **Driver and status:**
  - Ley 12/2023 and the regional declarations are in force, and so is Llei 11/2025.
  - The 2% cap and arts. 17.8–17.9 come from RDL 29/2026: in force, pending convalidation.
  - RDL 28/2026 (automatic 5/7-year renewals, due 15 Nov 2026) is expected to fail; build nothing on it (CON-18).
- **Used today:**
  - The free public SERPAVI lookup, hand calculations by agencies and gestorías, and lawyers.
  - Full rental management costs 6–10% of rent (CON-12).
  - Secondary blogs (Arrenta, Inmostats, Vivara) publish lists of tensioned zones because the ministry page blocks automated access (CON-17).
  - No cap calculator for seasonal or room lets was found (HOU-03).
- **Conflict in the notes:** the País Vasco municipality count varies between sources (17–19).

### C3. Tenant screening, guarantees and trust under the fee and guarantee caps
- **Pain IDs:** CON-11, CON-13, HOU-06, HOU-07, HOU-09 (HOU-23 as a triage input).
- **Problem statement:**
  - **Lead volume.** An average rental listing drew 135 contacts in 10 days in 2025, and 462 in Barcelona province ([Forbes/Observatorio del Alquiler, 28 Jan 2026](https://forbes.es/economia/864744/la-oferta-de-alquiler-pierde-33-400-viviendas-al-cierre-de-2025-y-se-ralentiza-el-ritmo-de-encarecimiento/)) (CON-13).
  - **Screening the tenant cannot be charged for.** Agencies and landlords must triage, check solvency and document their selection, but cannot charge the tenant. Management and formalisation costs may not be passed on "ni directa ni indirectamente" (art. 20.2 LAU). Consumo proposed a €3,600,902 fine on Alquiler Seguro over disguised fees, including an "estudio de solvencia" ([Noticias de Navarra, 27 Oct 2025](https://www.noticiasdenavarra.com/economia/2025/10/27/consumo-propone-multar-alquiler-seguro-10269149.amp.html)) (CON-11).
  - **Guarantees.** Since 8 Oct 2026 the additional guarantee is capped at one month for temporary leases and two for habitual ones, and landlords cannot require tenant-paid rent-default insurance (HOU-06).
  - **Foreign tenants.** The core mid-term demand is foreign tenants with no Spanish payslips or credit files. Landlords used to ask them for bank guarantees (avales) of about six months' rent (HOU-07). About 60% of Barcelona's coliving tenants are foreign.
  - **Scams.** Newcomers are targeted by fake listings. OCU's fake ad passed 8 portals unnoticed, and one Asturias-based network had more than 80 victims and took over €80,000 ([OCU, 16 Sep 2025](https://www.ocu.org/organizacion/prensa/notas-de-prensa/2025/portalesvivivenda160925)) (HOU-09).
  - **Cost.** Landlords now pay rent-default insurance themselves: €306–658 a year for a €950/month lease ([OCU, 28 Sep 2026](https://www.ocu.org/fincas-y-casas/gestion/seguros-y-prestamos/analisis/2026/07/comparativa-seguros-impago-rentas)). An aval blocks, for example, €6,000 at 0.5–2% per quarter.
- **Driver and status:** the Ley 12/2023 fee rule is in force and enforced. The RDL 29/2026 limits on guarantees and insurance are pending convalidation; if the decree lapses, avales come back and urgency falls.
- **Used today and price:**
  - Didit KYC: $0.33 per applicant, with 500 free checks a month.
  - GarantíaYa: no prices published.
  - Spotahome Plus: 10% of contract value (UK/Ireland page).
  - Insurers: Mutua Alquiler Confort €306/yr, Arag €581, Mutua de Propietarios 2.98% of annual rent (minimum €129.78).
  - HousingAnywhere holds the first month in escrow.
  - Lead tools and CRMs: prices unverified.

### C4. Arrears, evictions and the MASC pre-filing step
- **Pain IDs:** CON-20, CON-23 (HOU-14 is filtered as a duplicate).
- **Problem statement:**
  - **Debt.** Defaulting tenants owed €8,489 on average in 2025, about seven months' rent: €11,620 in Catalonia, €14,037 in Barcelona province ([idealista, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/03/05/887280-los-inquilinos-morosos-ya-deben-siete-meses-de-renta-una-media-de-8-490-euros-en)).
  - **Evictions.** 18,317 rental-related (LAU) evictions were carried out in 2025 ([Forbes/CGPJ, 20 Mar 2026](https://forbes.es/economia/893652/los-desahucios-caen-un-11-en-2025-hasta-los-25-540-segun-cgpj/)) (CON-20).
  - **MASC.** Since 3 Apr 2025, a documented negotiation attempt is a precondition for filing an eviction for non-payment ([BOE, LO 1/2025 art. 5.2](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2025-76)). One landlords' lawyer says "los procesos se han complicado todavía más".
  - **Suspensions.** The vulnerable-household suspension lapsed at the end of 2025. Its extensions were repealed on 27 Jan and 26 Feb 2026, and RDL 29/2026 re-created a suspension regime until 31 Dec 2030, pending convalidation (CON-20).
  - **Communities.** Communities chase unpaid fees and one-off levies with the special payment claim under LPH art. 21 (monitorio). A textual reading suggests they also need a MASC attempt first; this has not been checked against case law (CON-23). There are about 1 million communities.
- **Driver and status:** LO 1/2025 and the LPH are in force. The RDL 29/2026 suspension rules are lapse-prone.
- **Used today and price:**
  - Rent-guarantee firms such as Alquiler Seguro, lawyers, arrears registers.
  - Rent-default insurance with legal defence: €306–658 a year (OCU, via HOU-14).
  - Legálitas subscriptions: €16.92–45.50/month, though one plan covers family life only, not business (voice table).
  - Community-management software: prices unverified.
- **Conflicts in the notes:**
  - Debt growth is reported as +16.5% year on year, but the 2024 average implies about +6.7%.
  - The CGPJ eviction parts add up to 24,540 against a stated total of 25,540; use 25,540.

### C5. Short-term-rental eligibility and enforcement through regions and platforms
- **Pain IDs:** HOS-19, HOS-20, HOS-21, HOS-22, HOS-24 (HOU-19 for context; HOS-18 is filtered as a duplicate).
- **Problem statement:** since the national registry was annulled in May 2026, eligibility and enforcement are run city by city and through the platforms.
  - **Madrid.** Plan Reside bans dispersed tourist flats (VUT) in the historic centre. Fines escalate to €30,001, €60,001 and €100,001, and can reach €190,000 per home ([Forbes, 11 Nov 2025](https://forbes.es/economia/825920/los-pisos-turisticos-en-la-ciudad-de-madrid-caen-un-157-desde-la-aprobacion-del-plan-reside/)) (HOS-19).
  - **Málaga.** No new VUT in 43 barrios, plus a 3-year suspension of new licences approved in July 2026 (HOS-20, HOU-19).
  - **Valencia and Alicante.** Moratoria (HOS-21).
  - **Baleares and Canarias.**
    - Palma has announced a ban, and Airbnb has removed more than 2,300 Mallorca listings without the island registration number.
    - The Balearic Ley 4/2026 adds daily coercive fines of €500–5,000 (single source).
    - Canarias' Ley 6/2025 fines advertising without a number at €30,001–300,000 (HOS-22; HOU notes).
    - The Consell de Mallorca opened 146 sanction files in January–July 2026, 40% more than in all of 2025.
  - **Platforms and consumer law.** Consumo fined Airbnb €64,055,311 over 65,122 listings with missing or mismatching registration numbers, and the TSJ de Madrid denied interim suspension in March 2026 ([Consumo, Mar 2026](https://www.dsca.gob.es/es/comunicacion/notas-prensa/justicia-obliga-airbnb-pagar-64-millones-euros-previstos-multa-que-le)). Consumo also opened proceedings against large managers, with fines of up to €100,000 or 4–6× the illicit profit (HOS-24).
  - **Scale.** 341,001 listed tourist homes (INE, May 2026: [idealista, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/06/24/903471-estos-son-los-municipios-con-mas-viviendas-turisticas-en-espana)). A manager present in Madrid, Málaga, Valencia and Palma faces four different rule sets.
  - **Cost.** A delisted unit loses all platform revenue; the fines are listed above.
- **Driver and status:**
  - Regional tourism laws and municipal plans are in force. Plan Reside is appealed at the TSJ de Madrid, with no ruling found. Final approval of Málaga's measures and of Palma's ban is unverified.
  - EU Regulation 2024/1028 has applied since 20 May 2026.
  - The notes say Spain had no penalty regime for it in June 2026 (Chekin), but RDL 29/2026 adds platform sanctions in a new Title V of the LAU, pending convalidation. This is a timeline update rather than a contradiction.
- **Used today:**
  - Lawyers and in-house compliance staff at large managers.
  - PMS and channel tools that store and display the registration number (Smoobu, Chekin, net2rent): storage, not auditing.
  - No cross-platform listing-audit tool was found.

### C6. Per-stay lodging duties: traveller reporting and tourist taxes
- **Pain IDs:** HOS-12, HOS-15.
- **Problem statement:**
  - **Traveller reports.** Every lodging business must file a report within 24 hours of each booking, cancellation and check-in. Each report carries about 42–43 data fields, including payment data; records are kept for 3 years, and guests over 14 must sign. Fines are €100–600 per faulty report and up to €30,000 for serious breaches ([BOE, RD 933/2021](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2021/10/26/933)) (HOS-12). Catalonia (Mossos d'Esquadra) and the Basque Country (Ertzaintza) run their own systems.
  - **Catalan tourist tax.** Ley 2/2026 raised the maximum Barcelona surcharge from €4 to €8 from 1 Apr 2026 ([Forbes, 25 Feb 2026](https://forbes.es/economia/879865/el-parlament-aprueba-la-ley-que-incrementara-la-tasa-turistica-a-partir-del-1-de-abril/)). Rates elsewhere in Catalonia rise 50% from April 2026 and 100% from April 2027.
  - **Basque tourist tax.** Gipuzkoa's Norma Foral 3/2026 takes effect on 1 Jan 2027 ([BOPV](https://www.euskadi.eus/bopv2/datos/2026/06/2602836a.pdf)):
    - per-night bands by category (VUT €4.50–6.50);
    - a maximum of 6 nights per stay;
    - municipal surcharges of up to 50%;
    - a quarterly self-assessment (autoliquidación) filed by the establishment.
    - Bizkaia and Álava are aligned to the same date.
  - **Galicia and Baleares.** Galician cities charge €1–2.50, and the Baleares have their own tax (HOS-15).
  - **Who and scale.** Hotels, hostels, campsites, VUT owners and managers. Catalonia has 51,300 listed tourist homes and the Baleares 21,304 (INE). Booking alone has about 200,000 partners in Spain.
  - **Frequency.** Every booking, plus quarterly returns.
- **Driver and status:**
  - RD 933/2021 is in force and unamended. EU infringement INFR(2026)4005 was opened in June 2026; its outcome is pending and could reshape the reporting regime.
  - Ley 2/2026 is in force. The Basque norms apply from 1 Jan 2027; the Bizkaia and Álava norm numbers are unverified.
- **Used today and price:**
  - Chekin: €3.95, €5.95 or €7.95 per property per month. Minimum unit counts are stated inconsistently (3, 10 or 20). Tourist-tax handling is included in all plans ([Chekin pricing](https://chekin.com/en/pricing/)).
  - Smoobu: €29–55 for the first unit plus €9.60 per additional unit; police reporting is not mentioned.
  - net2rent, gestorías, and the manual SES web form.
  - Documented complaints: outages, the 24-hour clock, lost credentials, re-enrolment and email-only support.
- **Conflicts in the notes:**
  - One vendor FAQ says card number and expiry are not mandatory, but the BOE annex lists the card expiry date.
  - Barcelona's exact tax amounts differ between Forbes and DonPorqué.

### C7. Consumer-complaint deadlines in Andalusia (Hoj@)
- **Pain IDs:** HOS-17.
- **Problem statement:**
  - **Duties.** Every business selling in Andalusia must:
    - register on the Hoj@ platform and designate authorised people;
    - display a QR poster and keep paper complaint forms;
    - answer electronic complaints within 10 working days. A complaint counts as received when opened, or after 10 calendar days.
  - **Sanction.** Failing to reply is an infraction under art. 71.8.3ª of Ley 13/2003; fine amounts are not stated.
  - **Deadlines.** Registration is due by 20 Nov 2026 for large firms and by 20 May 2027 for everyone else ([Chekin, 9 Jun 2026](https://chekin.com/blog/boja-andalucia-nuevo-sistema-hoj-de-quejas-y-reclamaciones/)).
  - **Scale.** Every consumer-facing Andalusian business; the count is unknown. Andalucía alone has 90,649 listed tourist homes (INE, May 2026).
- **Driver and status:** Order of 24 Apr 2026 (BOJA nº82, 30 Apr 2026), implementing Decreto 82/2022, in force since 20 May 2026. The only source is a vendor blog and the BOJA text was not checked, so this cluster is carried **provisionally**: it would become N1 if the BOJA text does not confirm it.
- **Used today:** nothing found.

### C8. Food-safety, allergen and food-waste evidence
- **Pain IDs:** HOS-08, HOS-09.
- **Problem statement:**
  - **Who.** Every bar, restaurant, café, hotel kitchen and caterer: more than 300,000 hospitality companies ([Hostelería de España](https://www.hosteleriadeespana.es/)).
  - **Food-safety records (APPCC).** Kitchens must keep records of temperatures, cleaning, pest control, maintenance and traceability; frozen-food temperatures must be kept for at least a year (HOS-09).
  - **Allergens.** Written or electronic allergen information is required for every dish and must be updated with every menu change ([BOE, RD 126/2015](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2015/02/27/126)).
  - **Sanctions.** Food-safety fines reach €600,000, plus temporary closure of up to five years ([BOE, Ley 17/2011](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2011/07/05/17)).
  - **Food waste.** Since about 3 Apr 2026, Ley 1/2025 requires every venue to let customers take leftovers free. Establishments that are not micro firms and exceed 1,300 m², or several premises under one tax ID that together do, also need a prevention plan and donation agreements; a missing plan costs €2,001–60,000 ([BOE, Ley 1/2025](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2025/04/01/1)) (HOS-08).
  - **Frequency.** Daily logs, and an update at every menu change.
  - **Gap.** Inspection frequency and fine counts were not found.
- **Driver and status:** Reglamento (CE) 852/2004, RD 1021/2022, RD 126/2015, Ley 17/2011 and Ley 1/2025 are all in force.
- **Conflict in the notes:** press stories about fines for not offering free containers ("tuppers") look overstated, because art. 20 of Ley 1/2025 lists no such infraction (HOS-08).
- **Used today:**
  - APPCC consultancies (prices unverified), paper logbooks, digital APPCC apps and sensors (prices unverified).
  - Allergen builders inside POS and menu tools.
  - Hostelería de España's hosteleriacircular.es service for food waste.

### C9. Construction subcontracting compliance chain (CAE, REA and the Libro, safety records)
- **Pain IDs:** CON-02, CON-03, CON-04.
- **Problem statement:**
  - **Per-platform fees and uploads.** Each client chooses its own CAE (coordinación de actividades empresariales) platform. Subcontractors and autónomos must register and pay on each one, then upload the same prevention paperwork (training, medical fitness, insurance, Social Security filings) to each. Clients can make site access or payment conditional on the documents being validated (CON-02).
  - **Fee signals from unverified reviews:** Nalanda fees rising from "300 y pico" to "más de 1400 euros"; "2000 euros anuales" for 15 workers; €50 for phone support; a TrustScore of 1.3/5 from 135 reviews, 97% of them one-star ([Trustpilot](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/www.nalandaglobal.com)).
  - **Published prices:** Dokify charges contractors from €102.90/yr plus €15.44 per employee or machine ([DocuPRL comparison](https://docuprl.es/blog/mejores-plataformas-cae-2026.html), vendor-authored).
  - **Main contractors' duties (Ley 32/2006).** Before each subcontract they must:
    - check the sub's REA registration, which lasts 3 years;
    - check its permanent-staff quota (10%, 20% or 30% depending on age);
    - respect chain limits (a third-level subcontractor may not subcontract);
    - keep the on-site Libro de Subcontratación.

    If any of this goes wrong, they become jointly liable for the sub's wage and Social Security debts ([BOE, Ley 32/2006](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2006-18205)) (CON-03).
  - **Safety exposure.** Construction deaths rose 21.5% (+29) in 2025 while national work deaths fell 9.6% ([INSST, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.insst.es/noticias-insst/cae-la-siniestralidad-laboral-y-se-reducen-un-9-6-por-ciento-los-accidentes-mortales)) (CON-04).
  - **Scale.** 1.53M construction workers; no count of firms.
- **Driver and status:** RD 171/2004, Ley 31/1995, Ley 32/2006, RD 1109/2007 and RD 1627/1997 are in force. No law requires any particular platform; the choice is the principals' market practice.
- **Conflict in the notes:** El Español's "29 deaths" misreads INSST's "+29".
- **Used today and price:**
  - Nalanda: price on request.
  - Dokify: €490/yr for the principal.
  - DocAcces: €32–99/month.
  - UCAE: from €80/month.
  - PlayCAE: €199/month, with no cost to subcontractors.
  - Ctaima, e-coordina, Obralia and Metacontratas: unverified.
  - Prevention consultancies, and office staff uploading by hand.

### C10. Owners' communities administration
- **Pain IDs:** CON-22, CON-24. CON-23 sits in C4; CON-26 is filtered as a duplicate and CON-25 under N3.
- **Problem statement:**
  - **Market.** About 1 million owners' communities are run by about 16,000 administradores de fincas, most managing 50–60 communities each. The profession is fragmented and ageing. Consolidation is under way: inmho manages 15,000 communities (under 3% of the market) and bought 50 firms in a year ([idealista, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/empresas/2026/07/30/906830-50-empresas-compradas-al-ano-y-menos-del-3-de-cuota-asi-es-inmho-en-el-sector)) (CON-24).
  - **Obligations per building:** CAE with suppliers, data protection, accessibility works, energy-renovation grants, certifications and maintenance deadlines. They are tracked across "correos electrónicos, documentación física, múltiples plataformas independientes", according to the compliance-services firm Conversia (CON-24).
  - **Tourist-flat votes.** Since 3 Apr 2025, opening a tourist flat needs express prior approval from 3/5 of owners holding 3/5 of the quotas. The same majority can set special fees of up to +20% ([BOE, LPH](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1960-10906)). Barcelona pays up to €2,500 per community that votes a ban, from a €256,000 budget ([idealista, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/08/18/910490-barcelona-quintuplica-las-ayudas-a-las-comunidades-de-propietarios-para-prohibir)) (CON-22).
  - **Cost.** Not quantified.
- **Driver and status:** the LPH as amended by LO 1/2025, plus PRL/CAE, data-protection, accessibility and grant rules, all in force. Consolidation is a market force.
- **Used today:** Gesfincas, Fincas Pro and Comunidad Feliz (prices and complaints unverified); compliance consultancies such as Conversia; consolidators.

### C11. Anti-money-laundering (AML) duties of real-estate agencies
- **Pain IDs:** CON-14.
- **Problem statement:**
  - **Current duties.** Agencies that broker sales are obliged entities under Ley 10/2010 ([SEPBLAC list](https://www.sepblac.es/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/sujetos_obligados_ley_10_2010.pdf)). They must identify clients and beneficial owners, assess risk and report suspicious operations.
  - **Awareness.** Many small agencies are "el perímetro que no se sabe sujeto obligado". SEPBLAC has flagged deficiencies such as missing whistleblowing channels (snippet-level).
  - **What changes in July 2027.** The EU AML Regulation 2024/1624 applies from July 2027 ([finReg360, 23 Dec 2025](https://finreg360.com/el-negocio-inmobiliario-ante-las-novedades-en-la-prevencion-del-blanqueo-de-capitales/)). It brings:
    - stricter due diligence on all parties and beneficial-owner identification;
    - an evidence-based risk self-assessment;
    - sanctions and adverse-media screening;
    - a separate compliance officer.
  - **Scale.** Agencies take part in about 70% of purchase and rental transactions (FADEI, via [idealista, 29 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/node/904122)). No count of agencies was found.
  - **Cost.** No sanction or compliance-cost data.
- **Driver and status:** Ley 10/2010 is in force; the AMLR applies from July 2027. The notes call this "a future pain, not yet an acute one".
- **Used today:** AML training courses and consultants (no prices). KYC vendors such as Didit offer screening components ($0.07 per user per year for ongoing AML checks).

## 4. Candidate SaaS ideas

Ten ideas are listed here. The eleventh, G3-I11, is the reshaped user idea U1 (ColivEs) and is written out in section 5. G3-I01 and G3-I11 overlap on purpose: G3-I01 is a light, cheap monitor for any landlord, while G3-I11 is the full per-contract file for mid-term operators. A later merge may combine them. The ideas are not ranked. All prices are hypotheses to test.

### G3-I01: Rent-cap, update and deadline autopilot for landlords and rental agencies
- **Type:** C (primary); secondary A, as a cheaper substitute for agency or gestoría hand calculations.
- **Buyer:**
  - Individual landlords with 1–10 homes, and small agencies or rental managers paying per lease. Agencies could also resell it to the landlords they manage.
  - Count: unknown — validate. The notes have no count of individual landlords. Proxies:
    - 683,920 long-term homes on the market at end-2025 (Observatorio del Alquiler, CON-12);
    - 510,091 permanent idealista listings in 2025 (CON-12);
    - tensioned zones: 271 + 53 Catalan municipalities, 17–19 Basque, 21 Navarrese, A Coruña and 5 Asturian (CON-17).
- **Pains addressed:** CON-17, CON-18 (only the RDL 29/2026 parts), HOU-03, HOU-22, HOU-12, HOU-02.
- **Problem:** Every lease carries recurring legal calculations that depend on region, zone and date:
  - the first-rent cap in tensioned zones, from the previous rent or the SERPAVI index, with ad disclosure;
  - the annual update (IRAV; 2% cap until 31 Dec 2027; no increase if rent is above the index);
  - regional deposit-lodging deadlines, with fines of up to €90,000 in Madrid.

  The rules changed three times in two weeks of October 2026 (CON-18, HOU-02), and landlords still work them out by hand with SERPAVI or a gestoría (CON-17).
- **Product:**
  - **Set up once per lease:** address, signing date, rent, lease type (habitual, temporary or room) and landlord type (individual or large holder).
  - **What it computes and sends:**
    - the legal maximum first rent and the ad-disclosure text where a cap applies;
    - the legal annual update on each anniversary, with a draft notice;
    - the deposit-lodging deadline and later the refund for the lease's region;
    - the extension windows created by RDL 29/2026 (extraordinary extension for leases ending before 31 Dec 2028);
    - "rule changed" alerts that name the leases affected.
  - **MVP:** national rules plus Catalonia, a zone lookup from the published municipality lists, an IRAV table, email or WhatsApp reminders and PDF notices.
  - **Deliberately not:** legal advice, rent collection, dynamic pricing, filing deposits on the landlord's behalf (possibly later), or any feature based on RDL 28/2026.
- **Why now:**
  - Catalonia declared 53 more tensioned municipalities on 19 Oct 2026, pending BOE publication. Asturian zones started on 30 Jul 2026 and Basque additions on 28 Apr 2026 (CON-17).
  - RDL 29/2026's 2% cap has applied since 8 Oct; the convalidation vote is around 12 Nov 2026.
  - The notes say the volatility "makes software more valuable, not less" (CON-17).
- **Alternatives today:**
  - The free SERPAVI index, which has gaps under 30 m² and over 150 m².
  - Hand calculation by agencies and gestorías; lawyers.
  - Full rental management at 6–10% of rent (CON-12).
  - Homming's free tier (1–5 properties); cap features unverified.
  - Blogs listing tensioned zones (Arrenta, Inmostats, Vivara).
  - None of these gives per-lease alerts or versions the rules.
- **Price hypothesis:** €4–5/month for up to 3 leases (individual landlords); €1–1.50 per lease per month white-label for agencies; annual billing.
- **Channels:**
  - Agencies and rental managers as resellers; landlord and owner associations (CON-18); gestorías.
  - Search terms: "límite alquiler zona tensionada", "actualización renta IRAV 2%", "plazo fianza INCASÒL".
  - Content partnerships with landlord-facing portal news (idealista).
- **Build notes:**
  - Needs official zone lists (the ministry page blocked automated access, CON-17), SERPAVI and Catalan index values, the IRAV series (the October 2026 value is unverified) and deposit rules for 12 regions.
  - Regular legal review is a running cost.
  - Data sensitivity: low to medium (addresses, rents). Complexity: low to medium.
- **Moat in an AI-flooded market:** Thin. Speed, rules upkeep and agency distribution are the only edges. Portals, Homming or PMS vendors could copy it quickly, and the core is easy to clone.
- **Key risks and kill criteria:**
  - If RDL 29/2026 is not convalidated around 12 Nov, the 2% cap and the art. 17.8 ceiling lapse. Ley 12/2023 caps and Catalonia's rules remain.
  - Small landlords' willingness to pay is unknown, and wrong calculations carry liability.
  - Kill if portals give equivalent calculators away free, if fewer than about 1 in 10 surveyed landlords would pay €5/month, or if official zone and index data cannot be obtained reliably.
- **Evidence strength:** Medium. The rules are well documented (BOE, OCU), but the time cost per landlord and the number of landlords are not quantified.
- **Validation questions:**
  1. Do Homming, Rentger, Vivara, Arrenta or Inmostats already sell per-lease cap and update calculators with alerts? At what price?
  2. Was RDL 29/2026 convalidated around 12 Nov 2026, and which update rules survive if not?
  3. What IRAV value is in force in Oct–Nov 2026, and where is it published?
  4. Is there a machine-readable official list of tensioned municipalities and SERPAVI values? Have the 53 new Catalan municipalities been published in the BOE?
  5. How many individual landlords exist in Spain (for example AEAT rental-income declarants), and how many are in tensioned zones?
  6. Do idealista or Fotocasa already offer free cap or update calculators?
  7. Can an automated cap calculation be sold without counting as legal advice, and what professional-liability cover is needed?

### G3-I02: Landlord-paid tenant screening and trust pack for foreign and mid-term tenants
- **Type:** B.
- **Buyer:**
  - Rental agencies, property managers, mid-term and coliving operators. The landlord or agency pays, per screening or per seat.
  - Count: unknown — validate. Agencies take part in about 70% of transactions (FADEI, CON-11), and Inmovilla alone claims more than 3,000 agency clients (CON-16).
- **Pains addressed:** CON-13, CON-11, HOU-06, HOU-07, HOU-09; HOU-23 as a triage input.
- **Problem:** Each listing draws 135 contacts in 10 days, and 462 in Barcelona (CON-13). Agencies must screen without charging tenants: Consumo proposed a €3.6M fine on Alquiler Seguro for an "estudio de solvencia" among other disguised fees (CON-11). Foreign tenants lack Spanish payslips and credit files, and the landlord's usual fallbacks (6-month avales, tenant-paid insurance) are now capped or banned (HOU-06, HOU-07). Meanwhile newcomers are targeted by fake listings (HOU-09).
- **Product:**
  - **Tenant side:** an applicant link that bundles:
    - commodity ID and liveness KYC;
    - cross-border income evidence (foreign payslips, employer or assignment letters, bank statements);
    - an affordability check against the 35–40% rent-to-income ratio insurers use (HOU-07);
    - a standard criteria scorecard;
    - an audit log of how every applicant was ranked, as evidence against discrimination claims (HOU-08).
  - **Landlord side:** an ownership check (nota simple, Catastro) that produces a "verified landlord" link tenants can check, to counter scams (HOU-09).
  - **MVP:** a KYC API (e.g., Didit) plus document OCR, a manual review queue, the scorecard and a PDF audit report, in Spanish and English.
  - **Deliberately not:** charging tenants, underwriting guarantees or insurance itself (a partner insurer could come later), using nationality or other protected traits, or taking fully automated decisions.
- **Why now:** RDL 29/2026 removed avales above one or two months and tenant-paid insurance from 8 Oct 2026, pending convalidation. The Ley 12/2023 fee ban is being enforced. Mid-term demand is mostly foreign: about 60% of Barcelona coliving tenants (HOU-07).
- **Alternatives today:**
  - Didit KYC, $0.33 per applicant (500 free a month): identity only, not solvency.
  - GarantíaYa (no prices).
  - Spotahome Plus "verified solvent tenants", inside a 10%-of-contract-value plan (UK/Ireland page).
  - Landlord-paid insurance (€306–658 a year).
  - Bank avales (0.5–2% per quarter).
  - HousingAnywhere first-month escrow.
  - Veriff, Onfido and Sumsub (unverified).
  - Weaknesses: identity checks do not prove a foreigner's solvency, and the marketplaces bundle screening into high commissions.
- **Price hypothesis:** €9–15 per screened applicant, landlord-paid; or an agency seat at €39–79/month with bundled screenings. Later, a revenue share from a guarantee partner.
- **Channels:**
  - Agencies and franchise networks; coliving operators (Be Casa, Cotown, Smart Rental, Node Living); mid-term managers.
  - Relocation agencies and corporate HR (expat track).
  - Agent associations (API, COAPI, AICAT, AMADEI, FADEI).
  - Search: "verificar solvencia inquilino extranjero".
- **Build notes:**
  - Financial and identity data is high-sensitivity under GDPR. The AEPD fined a hotel €2,000 for scanning guests' IDs (HOS-12), which shows how strictly data minimisation is policed.
  - Anti-discrimination design is essential.
  - Any embedded guarantee falls under insurance-distribution rules, so it needs a licensed partner.
  - Complexity: medium.
- **Moat in an AI-flooded market:** Moderate only if it secures data partnerships (foreign income verification) and a guarantee underwriter. The KYC layer alone has no moat.
- **Key risks and kill criteria:**
  - Agencies may refuse to pay for something they used to bill tenants for.
  - If RDL 29/2026 lapses, avales return and urgency drops.
  - Screening can draw discrimination complaints, and the marketplaces already screen.
  - Kill if agencies will not pay at least €9 per screening, or if no insurer will back a landlord-paid guarantee for foreign tenants.
- **Evidence strength:** Medium. The law and lead volumes are strong; the foreign-tenant screening gap rests on insurer ratios and a 2024 article on aval practice.
- **Validation questions:**
  1. What do GarantíaYa, Spotahome (Spanish rates) and HousingAnywhere (landlord commission) charge? Are there Spanish tenant-scoring startups?
  2. Does the rewritten art. 20.2 LAU also ban tenant-side screening or platform fees? Has Consumo acted against one?
  3. Will agencies in Barcelona, Madrid and Málaga pay per screening? Interview at least 10.
  4. Which insurers or guarantee firms would back a landlord-paid guarantee for foreign tenants, and at what premium compared with 3–5% of annual rent?
  5. What legal basis and retention periods apply to income verification under GDPR? Is there AEPD guidance on tenant screening?
  6. What does a nota simple cost per check through Registradores, and can it be automated?
  7. What are the official 2025–26 figures for nomad and relocation arrivals by city? (The 27,875 figure is single-source.)

### G3-I03: Rent-arrears and MASC case file for landlords, agencies and administradores
- **Type:** B (primary); secondary C, as an arrears-monitoring subscription.
- **Buyer:**
  - Small landlords, agencies managing rentals, and administradores de fincas (for community arrears).
  - Counts: 18,317 LAU evictions in 2025 (CGPJ, CON-20); about 16,000 administradores and 1 million communities (CON-24). Landlord count unknown — validate.
- **Pains addressed:** CON-20, CON-23 (HOU-14 is its duplicate).
- **Problem:** Defaulting tenants owe €8,489 on average, about seven months' rent (CON-20). Since April 2025 a documented negotiation attempt (MASC) is required before an eviction claim, and the vulnerability suspension regime has been switched off and on again until 2030 (CON-20). Communities chasing unpaid fees and derramas probably face the same pre-filing step (CON-23).
- **Product:**
  - **Early warning:** an alert when rent is missed (bank feed, or a manual "rent received" tick).
  - **Guided escalation:** reminder → formal demand (requerimiento) → a MASC offer with the content LO 1/2025 requires, sent through a certified channel (burofax or a certified-email partner) → a timestamped evidence log → a court-ready file (contract, debt statement, proof of the MASC attempt) handed to a lawyer.
  - **Community version:** debt certificate signed by the secretary with the president's approval, the 20% surcharge where statutes allow it (upheld by a Cantabria court), and a monitorio pack.
  - **MVP:** templates, a certified-email partner, a timeline and the evidence export.
  - **Deliberately not:** filing in court, acting as a lawyer, giving legal advice, or aggressive collection.
- **Why now:** The MASC step has applied since 3 Apr 2025. The suspension regime was repealed twice in early 2026 and re-created by RDL 29/2026 (pending around 12 Nov). The notes say "the new negotiation step adds a documented procedure that software can generate, log and time-stamp".
- **Alternatives today:**
  - Lawyers; rent-guarantee firms (Alquiler Seguro).
  - Rent-default insurance with legal defence, €306–658 a year (OCU; Arag, Allianz and Mapfre rated best).
  - Legálitas, €16.92–45.50/month, though one plan covers "vida familiar (no empresarial ni profesional)".
  - Administradores' software (unverified).
  - Weaknesses: insurance needs income ratios and can no longer be demanded from the tenant, and lawyers are expensive for an €8,000 debt.
- **Price hypothesis:** €29–49 per case file plus €3–5/month of monitoring per lease; €1 per community per month as an arrears module for administradores.
- **Channels:**
  - Integrations with administradores' software (Gesfincas, Fincas Pro, Comunidad Feliz, Fynkus) and the colegios de administradores.
  - Agencies; insurers and law firms as referral partners.
  - Search: "inquilino no paga MASC", "requerimiento previo desahucio".
- **Build notes:**
  - Integration with a certified-notification provider.
  - Templates reviewed by lawyers and versioned as rules change.
  - Debt and personal data are GDPR-sensitive.
  - Complexity: low to medium.
- **Moat in an AI-flooded market:** Low. Templates and workflow are easy to copy. Integrations with certified channels and admin ERPs, plus a lawyer network, help a little. This idea should be merged with the legal track's MASC idea (LEG-01).
- **Key risks and kill criteria:**
  - Law-firm or legal-tech MASC tools may already serve landlords cheaply.
  - Rules on what a valid MASC attempt contains are still being settled by the courts.
  - Reserved-profession limits apply.
  - Kill if the legal-track competitors already cover rental evictions at a similar price, or if insurers bundle the same workflow free.
- **Evidence strength:** Strong. Backed by CGPJ data, the BOE text of LO 1/2025 and the Observatorio del Alquiler figures. Community arrears are medium.
- **Validation questions:**
  1. What must a MASC attempt contain for an eviction claim for non-payment, and which channels count as proof? Look for case law since April 2025.
  2. Do courts require MASC before a community monitorio under LPH art. 21?
  3. Which MASC, burofax and legal-tech platforms already serve landlords (cross-check names in LEG-01 and FIN-06), and what do they charge? What do Arag, Mapfre and Legálitas business plans cost?
  4. After the vote around 12 Nov 2026, which eviction-procedure rules from RDL 29/2026 remain?
  5. Do Gesfincas, Fincas Pro, Comunidad Feliz or Fynkus already include arrears, monitorio and MASC workflows?
  6. What do lawyers charge for a non-payment eviction, and would small landlords pay €29–49 per case?
