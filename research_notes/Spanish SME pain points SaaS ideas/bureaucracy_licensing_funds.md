# Bureaucracy and public-administration friction for Spanish SMEs, autónomos and startups (licensing, company formation, digital administration, subsidies, public procurement), 2024–2026

Research context (read first): notes compiled on 23 Oct 2026. The shared web-search budget for this run ran out after the first round of 14 queries, so most evidence below comes from direct fetches of official documents (BOE, Defensor del Pueblo annual report 2025, OIReScon IAS 2025, Madrid Agencia de Actividades, CNMC, Red.es, FNMT, CEOE, INE) and from press pages surfaced in that first round. Facts marked **[search snippet only]** come from a search-engine summary whose page I could not open; treat them as unverified. City coverage beyond Madrid and Valencia is thin (see Gaps). Overlaps with other researchers (tax/e-invoicing, labour, late payment, hospitality, construction permits, startups law) are only flagged.

## Q1. Opening premises: licencia de actividad vs declaración responsable, urban-compatibility checks, processing times and costs by city

### Takeaway
Most small premises can now open on a *declaración responsable* (DR), but the municipal check comes months later and some cities have multi-year backlogs. Madrid's own 2026 service standards target an **average of 109 days** to check a DR and **137 days** for a prior licence. València averaged **370 days** for activity titles in 2022, with about 3,500 files pending. Businesses pay private certifiers for certainty: Madrid's ECUs charge **€600–€1,250 for premises up to 300 m²** (maximum prices for 2026, with discounts of up to 15%).

### Cited Findings
- **Madrid volume.** In 2025 the Agencia de Actividades handled **13,301 declaraciones responsables and licences**, carried out 3,805 disciplinary inspections, had 294 staff, served 22,695 people and scored 9.13 on satisfaction. — [Carta de Servicios Agencia de Actividades 2026, last evaluated 24 Feb 2026](https://www.madrid.es/UnidadesDescentralizadas/Calidad/CARTAS%20SERVICIOS/SistemaCartasServicio/41%20CS_de%20la%20Agencia%20de%20Actividades/DEFINITIVA/CS_ADA_2026.pdf)
- **Madrid service standards (targets, not reported results).**
  - DR material checks (*comprobación material*): average **109 days**; at least 40% done within 90 days and 60% within 100 days.
  - Prior activity licences: average **137 days**; 40% resolved within 60 days and 60% within 90 days, counted from registry entry.
  - Source: same charter.
- **Madrid in-person access.** Personalised attention, and help registering a DR at the office, both need *cita previa*. Fees (*tasas*) are charged for urban-planning consultations and certificates, DRs and licences. **ICIO is 4%** of the material execution budget and is due before works start. Legal basis: Ordenanza 6/2022, de 26 de abril, de Licencias y Declaraciones Responsables Urbanísticas. — same charter
- **What ECUs are.** *Entidades colaboradoras urbanísticas* are private entities authorised to verify and inspect compliance. Using one is **voluntary**. The Junta de Gobierno sets their maximum and minimum prices; 2027 prices were approved on 15 Oct 2026. — [Madrid ECU page, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.madrid.es/portales/munimadrid/es/Inicio/Buscador-Simple/Entidades-colaboradoras-urbanisticas-ECU-?vgnextfmt=default&vgnextoid=f0419ee34b257210VgnVCM2000000c205a0aRCRD&vgnextchannel=8db7566813946010VgnVCM100000dc0ca8c0RCRD)
- **ECU maximum prices for 2026** (Junta de Gobierno agreement of 16 Oct 2025). Source: [Anexos I–IV, ECU prices 2026](https://www.madrid.es/UnidadesDescentralizadas/AgenciaActividades/ECUs/ficheros/AnexosI_II_III_IV_PreciosECU2026.pdf)
  - DR for an activity, with or without works:
    - up to 50 m²: **€600**
    - 51–100 m²: €800
    - 101–150 m²: €950
    - 151–200 m²: €1,060
    - 201–250 m²: €1,140
    - 251–300 m²: €1,250
    - over 300 m²: €1,250 + €1.75/m²
    - new building with an activity: €1,750 + €2.25/m²
  - Licence for an activity: up to 50 m² **€575**, plus per-m² increments above that.
  - Inspection visits:
    - first-occupation/operation visit (LPOF/DRPOF), up to 200 m²: **€1,080**
    - DR check visit: from €190
    - visit at the applicant's request: €545
    - second and later visits: €325
  - *Certificado de adecuación urbanística*: **€500** (≤100 m²), €700 (≤200 m²), €1,000 (≤300 m²).
- **ECU competition.** 19 ECUs appear in the 2026 discount comparison, with total discounts of 5–15%. — [Cuadro comparativo descuentos ECU 2026](https://www.madrid.es/UnidadesDescentralizadas/AgenciaActividades/ECUs/ficheros/CuadroComparativoPreciosEcu_2026.pdf)
- **València backlog, 2022.** At the end of August 2022, **3,443 activity titles** were pending. Of 1,771 environmental files from 2015–2022, 1,637 were more than 90 days old. The PP opposition said 93% had been stalled for between 3 months and 7 years, and that *"Muchos de ellos desisten de abrir actividades"* ("many give up opening"). — [Valencia Plaza, 4 Sep 2022](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/pp-denuncia-un-atasco-de-mas-de-3400-licencias-de-actividad-en-el-ayuntamiento) (partisan source)
- **València processing times, 2022 municipal study.** The city's own Evaluación de Servicios study, as cited by the PP, gave these average days:
  - Actividades: **370 days**
  - Licencias: 135 days
  - Disciplina urbanística: 725 days
  - Other figures in the same article: 3,538 activity titles in process (75% of them filed in 2015–2021), and 1,470 environmental files stalled for 1–7 years. The then city government disputed the reading.
  - Source: [Valencia Plaza, 6 Feb 2023](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/el-pp-denuncia-que-urbanismo-de-valencia-tarda-725-dias-de-media-para-cerrar-un-informe-licencias-135-y-actividades-370)
- **València compatibility certificate (COGITI, the technical industrial engineers' college).** The *Certificado de Compatibilidad Urbanística* (CCU, required by Ley 6/2014 de la Comunitat Valenciana) used to take 15 days to 1.5 months. It now takes **a year or more** for large projects (coworkings, hotels, factories, nightclubs). A late answer can force changes after the works are done. Projects are moving to Málaga and Granada: *"está generando la fuga de empresas en la ciudad"* ("it is causing companies to leave the city"). — [Valencia Plaza, 24 May 2023](https://valenciaplaza.com/ingenieros-tecnicos-industriales-critican-embudo-valencialicencias-municipales)
- **València reorganisation, 2023.** The new mayor merged the Urbanismo and Actividades departments. Developers said licences took **nearly two years** on average. The PP estimated more than 10,000 building and activity licences were blocked. Two flagship projects (Corestate, and Zara's Boulevard Austria store) waited about 5 years. Plans included more staff, more use of the private collaborating entities (ECUV) and a 30-day *licencia básica*. — [Valencia Plaza, 19 Jun 2023](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/catala-unifica-las-concejalias-de-urbanismo-y-actividades-para-agilizar-las-licencias)
- **València new ordinance, 2025.** The city started drafting a procedural ordinance to *"unificar, clarificar y simplificar"* licensing for works and activities. It will also regulate the collaborating entities and require more parties to deal with Urbanismo electronically. It aligns with Ley 6/2024 de Simplificación Administrativa. — [valencia.es, 7 May 2025](https://www.valencia.es/cas/actualidad/-/content/nueva-ordenanza-de-obras)
- **Sevilla and Málaga.** Their urban-planning portals publish procedures, cita previa for licences (Sevilla) and an online licensing assistant, ALU (Málaga). I found no processing-time indicators on the pages I could access. — [Gerencia de Urbanismo Sevilla](https://www.urbanismosevilla.org/); [GMU Málaga](https://urbanismo.malaga.eu/)
- **Local decisions escalated to the competition authority, 2025–2026.** The CNMC issued market-unity reports involving:
  - tourist rentals in Málaga (UM/051/26) and Sevilla (UM/052/25)
  - a crematorium in Zaragoza (UM/039/26)
  - a food truck in Granada (UM/016/26)
  - tuk-tuks in Alicante (UM/022/26, UM/050/25)
  - a petrol station in Badajoz (UM/017/26, UM/051/25)
  - battery storage in Palma and Calvià (UM/049/26, UM/046/26)
  - vehicle re-stamping in Murcia (UM/047/25)
  - Source: [CNMC informes de unidad de mercado, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.cnmc.es/informes-unidad-de-mercado-2)
- **Municipal notification failures.** On 22 Jan 2025 the Ayuntamiento de Cuenca still had **200 notifications from 2024** unsent, because it lacked a postal contract. The Ombudsman (Defensor del Pueblo) warns that this makes *silencio administrativo* (often negative) operate on applications such as urban-planning licences. — [Defensor del Pueblo, Informe anual 2025, p. 312, published Mar 2026](https://www.defensordelpueblo.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Defensor-del-Pueblo_Informe-anual-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- The DR regime moves the delay to after opening. A Madrid business that has already spent on works and the lease operates for about 3–5 months, at the standard levels, before learning whether the check finds problems.
- The ECU market with regulated prices shows that businesses already pay €600–€1,250+ per file for speed and certainty, on top of architect or engineer fees and municipal *tasas*.
- València's backlog is well documented for 2022–2023 and was politically salient enough to trigger a reorganisation and a new ordinance. Whether it has improved by 2026 is unverified.
- Before signing a lease, a business needs a use-compatibility answer. In both València (CCU) and Madrid (paid *consulta urbanística* or ECU certificate) this step is slow or paid.

### Gaps
- Madrid's actual 2025 results against its standards, and the share of files handled through ECUs.
- No processing times or costs found for Barcelona, Sevilla, Málaga, Zaragoza or Bilbao, nor for Palma, Las Palmas, Murcia, Alicante, Valladolid, Vigo or A Coruña. The search budget was exhausted.
- No comparative local red-tape study retrieved (for example a World Bank subnational study or a Cámara or CEOE ranking).
- Professional fees for licence projects (architect, engineer, gestoría) were not found.
- València data after 2023, and the approval status of the 2025 ordinance, are unknown.

## Q2. Company formation (SL via CIRCE/PAE, notary, Registro Mercantil, €1 capital), autónomo registration and digital certificates

### Takeaway
Since Ley 18/2022 an SL can legally be formed with **€1 of capital**. Using CIRCE with model statutes, the law sets limits of **6–12 business hours** per step and promises completion in **24 hours**. Practitioner guides from 2025–2026 report **5–10 working days**, with the Registro Mercantil de Barcelona alone taking 5–10 working days, and about **€250–€450** if founders do it themselves. Founders get stuck coordinating the name certificate, bank deposit, notary, registry, NIE (foreigner ID number) and digital certificate, and on misinformation.

### Cited Findings
- **Ley 18/2022 (Crea y Crece), in force since 19 Oct 2022.**
  - Capital *"no podrá ser inferior a un euro"* (may not be below one euro).
  - While capital is below €3,000, at least 20% of profit goes to the legal reserve, and on liquidation the partners are jointly liable for the difference up to €3,000.
  - For CIRCE with model statutes: name reservation within **6 business hours**, notary appointment within **12 business hours**, registrar inscription within **6 business hours**, and BORME publication free.
  - The preamble says the process can be completed in **24 hours**.
  - Source: [BOE, Ley 18/2022 (BOE-A-2022-15818)](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-15818)
- **Official fees** [search snippet only]: CIRCE with model statutes and capital below €3,100 costs about **€60 notary + €40 registry**; without model statutes, about **€150 notary + €100 registry**. Legálitas warns that CIRCE statutes can make later changes more expensive. — [Legálitas](https://www.legalitas.com/actualidad/crear-sl-con-un-euro)
- **A 2025 do-it-yourself guide** (Strong Abogados, 17 Nov 2025):
  - Name certificate: €22, 1–2 business days.
  - Digital certificate: €20–30.
  - Notary: €150–300.
  - Registry: €40–100, with registration taking **3–5 working days**.
  - Total: **7–10 business days** and about €250–450.
  - Sticking points listed: names too similar to existing ones, no valid NIE, wrong activity codes.
  - The guide itself gives contradictory capital rules (a "€3,000 minimum" in its FAQ). — [Strong Abogados](https://www.strongabogados.com/articles/sl-self-registration)
- **Barcelona gestoría guide** (Billeo, updated 3 Aug 2026):
  - Name certificate: 1–2 days.
  - Bank account and capital deposit: 1–3 days.
  - Notary or CIRCE: 1–2 days.
  - **Registro Mercantil de Barcelona: 5–10 working days.**
  - Overall: *"no hay un plazo garantizable"* (no guaranteed timeframe). — [Billeo](https://www.billeo.es/blog/gestorias-especializadas-constitucion-sociedades-barcelona)
- **Totals with a gestor** [search snippet only]: €200–350 for notary plus registry with 5–10 working days via CIRCE-PAE ([Billeo 2026](https://www.billeo.es/crear-sociedad-limitada-espana-2026)); **€600–1,500 in total with a gestor**, 5–10 days ([Getrenn 2026](https://getrenn.com/blog/how-to-register-a-company-in-spain)).
- **FNMT certificate for a company's legal representative.**
  - Price: *"14 Euros, impuestos no incluidos"* (€14 before tax).
  - Valid for **2 years**.
  - Online identity accreditation only for entities whose NIF starts with A, B, C or D. Other entities must go **in person** to an AEAT office (appointment required), the CNMV or Navarra.
  - Download about 1 hour after accreditation. The page warns that the DNIe/NFC route "may not work correctly". — [FNMT, persona jurídica](https://www.sede.fnmt.gob.es/certificados/certificado-de-representante/persona-juridica)
- **FNMT personal certificate:** free; obtained by video identification, in person, with the DNIe or by mobile. The same NFC warning appears. — [FNMT, persona física](https://www.sede.fnmt.gob.es/certificados/persona-fisica)
- **Cl@ve registration** can be done by automatic video identification, invitation letter, manual review, certificate/DNIe, or in person. Variants: Cl@ve PIN, Móvil, Permanente and Firma. — [clave.gob.es](https://clave.gob.es/clave_Home/clave.html)
- **Company stock.** Spain had **3,310,824 active companies** on 1 Jan 2025, of which **1,859,925 (56.2%) were natural persons**. 54.4% had no employees. — [INE DIRCE, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/DIRCE2025.htm)
- **Autónomo contributions.** The Ombudsman received complaints about TGSS resolutions demanding differences after the annual regularisation of autónomo contribution bases, the adjustment introduced by RDL 13/2022. Prior claims are "generally not upheld". The Ombudsman says the calculation method **wrongly excludes months of activity** (for example during birth leave). — [Defensor del Pueblo, Informe anual 2025, pp. 226–227](https://www.defensordelpueblo.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Defensor-del-Pueblo_Informe-anual-2025.pdf)

### Inferences
- The legal "24 hours" is rarely the lived reality: 1–2 weeks is typical. The cost is low (€100–€450 official). The friction is coordination across bank, notary and registry, plus information quality, rather than fees.
- Willingness to pay is low. Law firms even offer free formation bundled with accounting (the Strong Abogados offer, not verified).
- About **1.45 million** non-natural-person businesses (3,310,824 − 1,859,925) must manage representative certificates and electronic interaction. This is my inference from DIRCE.

### Gaps
- No official CIRCE or Registradores statistics on actual formation times were retrieved, and no counts of new companies per year.
- Bank-account refusals and know-your-customer delays for new SLs and foreign founders are not documented here.
- Friction in autónomo registration (modelo 036 and RETA via Import@ss) was not researched in depth.
- The Ley de Startups and ENISA belong to another researcher.

## Q3. Digital administration: cita previa, certificado digital/Cl@ve, mandatory e-notifications (DEHú and many sedes), certificates of good standing

### Takeaway
The sharpest digital-administration pain is legal, not technical.
- All legal entities must deal with the administration electronically (Ley 39/2015, art. 14.2).
- An unopened electronic notification is **deemed rejected after 10 calendar days** (art. 43.2), so missed notices can end in enforcement and bank-account embargoes.
- The law calls the email or SMS alert mandatory, yet says its absence does not invalidate the notification, and courts are split on what that means.

On top of this come **9,000+ separate electronic offices** (a vendor's claim), 2-year representative certificates, appointment blockages in 2025 at Seguridad Social and SEPE (Madrid, Barcelona), and immigration-office delays the Ombudsman says harm **employers**.

### Cited Findings
- **Ley 39/2015, consolidated text updated 6 Nov 2024** — [BOE (BOE-A-2015-10565)](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2015-10565):
  - Art. 14.2: legal persons, entities without legal personality, professionals in compulsory colleges and their representatives *"estarán obligados a relacionarse a través de medios electrónicos"* (are obliged to deal with administrations electronically).
  - Art. 41.6: administrations *"enviarán un aviso al dispositivo electrónico y/o a la dirección de correo electrónico"* (will send an alert to the device or email), but *"La falta de práctica de este aviso no impedirá que la notificación sea considerada plenamente válida"* (a missing alert does not stop the notification being fully valid).
  - Art. 43.2: an electronic notification *"se entenderá rechazada cuando hayan transcurrido diez días naturales"* (is deemed rejected after ten calendar days) if not opened.
- **Strict line: Tribunal Supremo 1927/2017 (11 Dec 2017).** Ignoring a notification in the DEH (the electronic notification address) has the same effects as an unopened letter: *"no son diferentes de los que habría ocasionado… una carta… que no se abriera"*. The embargo against an economic interest grouping was upheld. — [TS 1927/2017 via Fiscal-Impuestos](https://www.fiscal-impuestos.com/sites/fiscal-impuestos.com/files/NFJ068943.pdf)
- **Protective line: TSJ Murcia 396/2025 (2 Oct 2025)** annulled a bank-account embargo against a company. The *providencia de apremio* (enforcement order) had been notified electronically without the alert, and the company had never accessed DEHú. GA-P (law firm) places this in a line of cases:
  - Tribunal Constitucional STC 84/2022.
  - Audiencia Nacional SAN 78/2022 (7 Apr 2022), which annulled a sanction because the email alert was missing.
  - Audiencia Nacional, 19 Apr 2023.
  - The administration cannot skip the alert *"para «pillar» sorpresivamente al administrado"* (to catch out the citizen by surprise).
  - The Supreme Court has not yet ruled on this line. Its STS 610/2022 declined to question the constitutionality of art. 41.6.
  - Source: [GA-P analysis, Nov 2025](https://ga-p.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Aviso_previo_condicion_validez.pdf)
- **Court notifications** [search snippet only]: since 20 Mar 2024 legal persons receive first court summonses electronically. If unopened for 3 days, they are published on the Tablón Judicial Edictal Único (single court notice board). — [Grupo2000](https://www.grupo2000.es/ya-es-posible-recibir-notificaciones-de-demandas-via-electronica)
- **Fragmentation (vendor claim).** IvNeos, an electronic-notification manager from Ivnosys, is marketed as *"Sincroniza más de 9.000 sedes y evita sanciones"* ("syncs more than 9,000 electronic offices and avoids sanctions"). IvSign centralises company certificates. No prices are published. — [Ivnosys, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.ivnosys.com/) (commercial, single source)
- **DEHú coverage.** The official DEHú page returned no readable content, so its coverage could not be confirmed. — [dehu.redsara.es](https://dehu.redsara.es/es)
- **Seguridad Social appointments.** In 2025 the Ombudsman received new complaints about the *"imposibilidad de obtener cita a través de los canales habilitados al efecto (sede electrónica, teléfono o atención presencial), por el bloqueo del sistema"* (impossible to get an appointment through any channel because the system was blocked), for example in **Madrid**. Security guards refused entry to people without appointments, including people over 65. The Ombudsman reopened its inquiry at the end of 2025. — [Defensor del Pueblo, Informe anual 2025, pp. 108–109 and 227–228](https://www.defensordelpueblo.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Defensor-del-Pueblo_Informe-anual-2025.pdf)
- **SEPE appointments.** There were "serious difficulties" appearing at offices, with complaints concentrated in **Madrid and Barcelona**. SEPE cites structural staff shortages: 712 offices in 586 localities and 1,429 new staff being added. In November 2025 it averaged **27,100 appointments per day**. — same report, pp. 231–232
- **DGT (traffic directorate).** At a meeting on 19 Mar 2025, appointments at provincial traffic offices were compulsory, with only a "perspective" of moving to recommended appointments. Sanction files cannot be consulted through the sede electrónica or the miDGT app. — same report, §3.5
- **Immigration offices.** The Ombudsman found "worrying delays" and diverging criteria between *oficinas de extranjería*, with *"graves consecuencias… no solo para las personas extranjeras, sino también para sus empleadores"* (serious consequences for foreigners and also for their employers). It asked for more staff. The new Reglamento de extranjería (RD 1155/2024) entered into force in **May 2025**; two interpretive criteria were issued a month apart, and legal challenges are pending before the Supreme Court. Asylum appointments have *"serias dificultades en el acceso y demoras acumuladas"* (serious access problems and accumulated delays). There were 144,396 new asylum applications in 2025. — same report, Part II ch. 4 (new regulation) and Part III §4.8–4.9, pp. 176–177
- **Foreign workers** [search snippet only]: 14.1% of Social Security contributors at the end of 2025. — [Ministerio de Inclusión](https://inclusion.gob.es/gl/web/guest/w/la-seguridad-social-suma-mas-de-800.000-afiliados-extranjeros-desde-la-reforma-laboral-y-cierra-2025-en-maximos-historicos)
- **Identity theft.** A citizen's identity, including his digital certificate, was stolen and two vehicles were registered in his name. The DGT would not deregister them without destruction certificates or a court order, and he struggled to revoke the certificate. — Defensor del Pueblo 2025, §3.5
- **Signatures misused in Kit Digital.** In a 2025–2026 fraud case, applications were filed in autónomos' names and their digital signatures were used without consent. — [Merca2, 14 Jun 2026](https://www.merca2.es/2026/06/14/fraude-kit-digital-supremo-las-palmas-2397192/)
- **Madrid licensing office.** In-person assistance at the Agencia de Actividades needs cita previa. — [Carta de Servicios ADA 2026](https://www.madrid.es/UnidadesDescentralizadas/Calidad/CARTAS%20SERVICIOS/SistemaCartasServicio/41%20CS_de%20la%20Agencia%20de%20Actividades/DEFINITIVA/CS_ADA_2026.pdf)

### Inferences
- The e-notification rule covers roughly 1.45M non-natural-person businesses (DIRCE inference). The penalty for missing a notice can be enforcement, and outcomes depend on contested case law. That combination makes notification monitoring a mission-critical, recurring and software-native need.
- Spain scores well on e-government supply, but these pains sit at the seams: many sedes, certificate lifecycles, and the in-person bottlenecks that remain.

### Gaps
- No statistics found on embargoes or sanctions caused by missed e-notifications, or on DEHú volumes and coverage.
- Certificates of being up to date with AEAT and Seguridad Social were not researched. They are believed to be instant online, but this is not verified here.
- No source found on resale of immigration appointments or appointment bots in 2024–2026.
- No Cl@ve outage statistics.
- No prices found for notification-management services or for gestorías acting as representatives (*apoderados*).

## Q4. Regulatory volume and fragmentation (normative inflation, regional divergence, market unity)

### Takeaway
Regulatory inflation and fragmentation are well documented:
- **1.32 million pages** of official bulletins in 2022, **80.8% of them regional** (CEOE).
- In 2025 the state alone approved **6 laws, 267 normative royal decrees and 17 decree-laws**.
- The CNMC issued about **50 market-unity reports in 13 months** (Sept 2025–Sept 2026). The most frequent topic is who counts as a *técnico competente* (qualified professional) to sign projects; others include fibre, EV charging, tourist rentals and training grants.

### Cited Findings
- **CEOE, "Producción normativa en 2022":**
  - 849 new state norms (851 in 2021).
  - BOE: 254,757 pages (+5.4%). Regional bulletins: **1,075,108 pages (+15%)**. Total over **1.32M pages (+22%)**.
  - 340 regional laws.
  - 20 decree-laws, about double the 40-year average.
  - A reader would need about **698 pages a day** to keep up with BOE legislation.
  - Regions produce **80.8%** of bulletin pages, which *"afecta significativamente a la unidad de mercado y a la seguridad jurídica"* (significantly affects market unity and legal certainty).
  - Source: [INAP La Administración al Día, summary of the CEOE report](https://laadministracionaldia.inap.es/noticia.asp?id=1232548) (2022 data; exact publication date unclear)
- **State output in 2025.** The Council of Ministers approved **6 laws and 267 royal decrees** (normative ones only) plus decree-laws RDL 1/2025 to RDL 17/2025. Of the 199 initiatives in the 2025 annual regulatory plan (PAN), 36.18% were approved, 46.23% were in progress and 17.59% had not started. — [Informe Anual de Evaluación Normativa 2025 (Gobierno, published 2026)](https://transparencia.gob.es/content/dam/transparencia_home/publicidadactiva/normativa/pan2025/IAEN%202025.pdf)
- **CNMC market-unity reports** (Ley 20/2013, arts. 26 and 28). The first page of 14 lists **50 items dated Sept 2025–Sept 2026**: 47 reports and 3 court rulings. The most frequent sector is engineering and technical services (NACE M.711, *técnico competente* disputes), with 10 reports. Others concern:
  - fibre deployment (Vinaròs, Nerja, Vejer)
  - EV charging (Cornellà)
  - tourist rentals (Canarias, Málaga, Sevilla)
  - training grants (Andalucía, Navarra, Valencia, Canarias)
  - archaeology rules (Andalucía)
  - food labelling (Euskadi)
  - Source: [CNMC, informes de unidad de mercado](https://www.cnmc.es/informes-unidad-de-mercado-2)
- **Recent CNMC actions:** 1 Oct 2026, appeal against the blocking of a petrol station in Badajoz; 31 Jul 2026, objection to an extra procedure for tourist homes in Canarias; 17 Jun 2026, objection to how Valencian training grants were allocated. — [CNMC unidad de mercado](https://www.cnmc.es/ambitos-de-actuacion/unidad-de-mercado)

## Q5. Subsidies: Kit Digital, Kit Consulting, NextGenerationEU absorption, regional grants, application and justification burden, consultants

### Takeaway
Subsidies are plentiful but costly to access.
- **Kit Digital** granted more than **900,000 aids** (budget €3,067M) and **ended in October 2025**. It left a trail of overpricing, slow payment to digitalisation agents and a fraud case in which SMEs' signatures were used without consent.
- **NextGenerationEU** reached **1.46M beneficiaries, 70% of them SMEs**, but SMEs received only **43% of awarded funds**. In 2025 payments were **18.7% of available credits**, and the loan tranche was cut by 73%.
- The Recovery Facility's milestone deadline (**31 Aug 2026**) has passed and Spain has filed its **final €25,861M payment request**. The pain now shifts to justification and repayment (*reintegro*) risk.
- Grant discovery is fragmented: one vendor tracks **81,665 grants from more than 8,500 administrations**.

### Cited Findings
- **Kit Digital, official figures.** More than 900,000 aids granted ("> 900 mil"), budget **€3,067M**, 5 calls, run from January 2021 to **October 2025**. — [Red.es Kit Digital page, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.red.es/es/iniciativas/proyectos/kit-digital)
- **CEOE:** Kit Digital has digitalised "más de 930.000 beneficiarios" (more than 930,000 beneficiaries). — [CEOE PRTR report, Jun 2026](https://www.ceoe.es/sites/ceoe-corporativo/files/content/file/2026/06/26/104/informe-ejecucion-fondos-europeos-junio-2026.pdf)
- **Ara (Catalan daily), 9 Jun 2025.**
  - Scale: more than 670,000 beneficiaries (over 20% in Catalonia) and more than **10,000 accredited agents**; the programme was extended to October 2025 only for autónomos.
  - Overpricing example: an invoicing program billed against a **€2,000** voucher cost **€150 to install plus €15 a month**, about €330 in total. Pimec (Catalan SME employers' group) says software worth €150–200 is "always more expensive" through the Kit.
  - Control: the main criticism is "lack of control" over how agents were chosen. Kitdigital.cat posed as the official site. Telcos and banks sold the Kit by phone. Contracts were hard to break, and one agent kept the VAT after a contract ended, which went to court.
  - Pimec also said justification "could have been made easier".
  - Source: [Ara](https://en.ara.cat/economy/the-ups-and-downs-of-the-digital-kit-overpricing-and-poor-control_1_5405825.amp.html)
- **Early Kit Digital, 29 Sep 2022 (Xataka).** Agents gave away iPhones. There were more than 9,300 agents. By early August 2022 there had been 68,000 applications and 21,000 grants. KPMG found only **2.4% of SMEs and 2.1% of autónomos** had tried to apply. Small IT firms lost clients to "free" Kit offers. — [Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/empresas-y-economia/regalar-iphones-no-digitalizacion-kit-digital-gobierno-cae-problemas-campanas-pasadas)
- **Agents' payment complaints, May 2023** (Afectados Kit Digital, about 400 agents):
  - Payment took **up to 10 months**.
  - *"se ha cobrado menos del 20% del trabajo realizado"* (less than 20% of the justified work had been paid).
  - The Cámaras de Comercio validating the work demanded excessive corrections (*subsanaciones*) using arbitrary criteria, with no deadline for validation and vague support. — [Press note via The Objective](https://theobjective.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/05/notadeprensa.afectadoskit-digital1.pdf)
- **Kit Digital fraud case, 2025–2026.**
  - The Supreme Court's criminal chamber (order of 1 Jun 2026) sent a case to a court in **Las Palmas**. It began with a complaint in August 2025 by a Málaga physiotherapist whose signature had been used.
  - TikTok ads claimed you *"podías acceder al Kit Digital aunque tuvieras deudas con Hacienda"* (could get the Kit even with tax debts). The court order calls agent-representative collusion a *"modus operandi sistémico"*.
  - The administration may demand repayment **even from people who never applied**.
  - The article cites a Tribunal de Cuentas (Court of Audit) report from February 2026 saying **only 25% of the €3,067M had been paid out**. This is single-source; I could not find the report on tcu.es.
  - Source: [Merca2, 14 Jun 2026](https://www.merca2.es/2026/06/14/fraude-kit-digital-supremo-las-palmas-2397192/)
- **Ombudsman on Kit Digital.** For a complaint about an agent, the Ombudsman referred the person to Red.es, which controls agents under art. 10.7 of Orden ETD/1498/2021. — [Defensor del Pueblo 2025, p. 278](https://www.defensordelpueblo.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Defensor-del-Pueblo_Informe-anual-2025.pdf)
- **Kit Digital design** (agent-industry blog, 2023). Maximum amounts per solution: e-invoicing €2,000; process management €6,000; customer management €4,000; web presence €2,000; e-commerce €2,000. To become an agent a firm needed €100,000 turnover over two years, or €50,000 in the previous year. — [STEL Order, 1 Jun 2023, updated 22 Aug 2023](https://www.stelorder.com/blog/agente-digitalizador/)
- **Kit Consulting.** Budget **€300M**. Vouchers of **€12,000** (10 to fewer than 50 employees), **€18,000** (50 to fewer than 100) and **€24,000** (100 to fewer than 250). Target: 15,000 SMEs. Dates: May 2024 to **February 2025**. — [Red.es Kit Consulting](https://www.red.es/es/iniciativas/proyectos/kit-consulting)
- **NextGenerationEU status at 31 Mar 2026** (CEOE report closed 31 May 2026):
  - Funds: €79,854M assigned to projects; €66,982M in resolved calls. EsadeEcPol (Esade's economic policy centre) puts the gap between funds called and funds awarded at **€27bn**. CEOE's table shows about €39bn of grant execution in national accounts (a Funcas estimate).
  - Who gets the money: 1,460,000 beneficiaries, 70% micro and SMEs, but **SMEs got 43%** of awarded volume.
  - Speed: in 2025 payments were **18.7%** of available credits, and only **8.6%** for financial operations.
  - Loans: the requested amount was cut from €83,200M to €22,700M (**73% renounced**). ICO-Verde and ICO-Empresas fell from €30,000M to €900M. Collateral requirements were "inalcanzables para la mayoría de las pymes" (out of reach for most SMEs).
  - PERTE strategic projects: €24,292M committed by December 2025, but only €703M of loans. PERTE Chip had 7.18% of €12,250M awarded.
  - Progress: 87% of reforms completed versus 35% of investments, with 223 milestones pending. Funcas sees about €32bn at risk.
  - Obstacles cited by companies: *"excesiva carga administrativa y la desalineación de los plazos del MRR con los ciclos reales de inversión"* (excessive administrative burden and recovery-fund deadlines out of step with real investment cycles).
  - CEOE proposes extending execution and justification to 2028 with no repayment when delays are the administration's fault, sampling instead of exhaustive reviews, earlier justification guides, less up-front paperwork, easier corrections and advance payments.
  - Source: [CEOE, Jun 2026](https://www.ceoe.es/sites/ceoe-corporativo/files/content/file/2026/06/26/104/informe-ejecucion-fondos-europeos-junio-2026.pdf)
- **Final payment request.** Spain submitted its **seventh and final payment request, for €25,861M**. The final milestones and targets document is dated 20 Aug 2026. — [planderecuperacion.gob.es, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://planderecuperacion.gob.es/)
- **Regional and intermediated grants.** The Ombudsman receives complaints about unresolved grant files, "mostly" MOVES III (EV and charging grants). These are managed by the region or through a *entidad colaboradora*. In Andalucía, the application, processing and justification belong **only to the participating installer (*empresa adherida*)**, so a beneficiary is stuck if that firm stops responding. — [Defensor del Pueblo 2025, pp. 277–278](https://www.defensordelpueblo.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Defensor-del-Pueblo_Informe-anual-2025.pdf)
- **Grant discovery.** Fandit claims **81,665 grants**, more than **8,500 public administrations** monitored and more than 25,000 companies as users.
  - Prices: Pro **€70/month** (€56/month billed annually); Premium €250 (€200); Corporate €600 (€480).
  - It charges no success fee and does not process applications itself; it refers users to consultancies through its marketplace.
  - Sources: [Fandit](https://www.fandit.es/); [Fandit precios](https://www.fandit.es/precios/) (vendor claims)

### Inferences
- Kit Digital's model (the state pays the agent and the SME signs) moved risk onto both sides. Small agents carried slow, arbitrary justification. SMEs carried overpricing and now identity and repayment exposure. A residual need remains (auditing past files, defence against repayment claims), but the programme market is closed.

- **What happened after the subsidised 12 months:** no evidence found on whether SMEs kept paying for Kit Digital websites or software. I did not verify the 12-month service duration in this session.
- Kit Consulting results (vouchers granted, satisfaction) and any extension after February 2025.

## Q6. Public procurement: SME participation, barriers, contratos menores, and tools SMEs pay for

### Takeaway
Spain's 2024 procurement market had **206,242 tenders with a €113.1bn base budget**, and competition was thin:
- **2.94 bidders on average** (3.99 in 2020).
- **40.7% of awarded lots had a single bidder.**
- Tenders are spread over the national platform (PLACSP) plus **seven regional platforms** with inconsistent data.
- There is **no aggregated data at all on contratos menores** (minor contracts below €15,000 for services and supplies, or €40,000 for works). Auditors flag misuse of these contracts through splitting (*fraccionamiento*).

### Cited Findings
- **Tender volume, 2024:** **206,242 tenders**, of which 99,013 local (48.01%), 55,753 state and 51,476 regional; base budget **€113,091.40M**. — [OIReScon IAS 2025, Module I](https://www.hacienda.gob.es/RSC/OIReScon/informe-anual-supervision-2025/ias2025-modulo1.pdf)
- **Competition, 2024:**
  - Average **2.94 bidders** per awarded contract (3.99 in 2020); regional 3.05; works 4.51.
  - **7.63% of lots were left without any award** (*desiertos*). The source gives 7.87% and 7.77% for 2023 in different places.
  - Average processing **117.47 days**; median 89.
  - Concessions: 2.03 bidders and 17.60% left unawarded.
  - Source: [OIReScon IAS 2025, executive summary, approved 22 Dec 2025](https://www.hacienda.gob.es/rsc/oirescon/informe-anual-supervision-2025/ias2025-resumen-ejecutivo-es.pdf)
- **Single bidders.** Excluding negotiated procedures without publicity, **40.69%** of awarded lots and contracts (85,029 of 208,962) had a single bidder, equal to 28.90% of awarded value (€20,025M of €69,284M). The state sector was highest at 43.99%. Single bidders are more common in smaller contracts. — [OIReScon Module I](https://www.hacienda.gob.es/RSC/OIReScon/informe-anual-supervision-2025/ias2025-modulo1.pdf)
- **Contratos menores.** *"no se dispone de datos agregados de la contratación menor"* (there is no aggregated data on minor contracts). Internal and external audit bodies find irregularities in minor contracts, splitting and chaining contracts to avoid open procedures. — OIReScon executive summary
- **Thresholds.** Art. 118 LCSP: contratos menores are those below **€40,000 (works)** or **€15,000 (supplies and services)**. Article last updated 2020. — [BOE, Ley 9/2017 (BOE-A-2017-12902)](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2017-12902)
- **Platform fragmentation.** Tenders sit on PLACSP plus seven regional platforms: Cataluña, País Vasco, La Rioja, Comunidad de Madrid, Galicia, Andalucía and Navarra. Their data lack homogeneity, and OIReScon says *"sería deseable contar con una única fuente de datos de contratación"* (a single source of procurement data would be desirable). — OIReScon Module I and executive summary
- **Transparency and planning.**
  - **95.38%** of contracting bodies fail to publish their contracting programme.
  - 58.72% of preliminary market consultations have no file or report.
  - The cost breakdown required by art. 100.2 is missing in 49.50% of service tenders and 58.75% of supply tenders.
  - Award criteria evaluated by formula weigh 81.36% on average. — OIReScon executive summary
- **Execution and disputes.**
  - "Modifications, extensions and other changes" made up 22.45% of 2024 consultations (+37.5%); execution-phase topics 32.65%.
  - **93.08%** of supervised works contracts above EU thresholds (SARA) had modifications not foreseen in the tender documents.
  - Subcontracting information is optional, so the payment of subcontractors (art. 217) cannot be supervised.
  - **5,053 special procurement appeals** (*recursos especiales*) in 2024; 27.96% were inadmissible or archived. — OIReScon executive summary
- **Tools businesses use.** Licitaciones.es sells alerts (including for contracts nearing expiry), an AI assistant for tender documents, competitor and buyer intelligence, expert consulting and **help registering in ROLECE** (the official register of bidders). It offers a free trial and publishes no prices. — [licitaciones.es, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.licitaciones.es/). Euroalert's tracking focuses on EU calls and publishes no prices. — [Euroalert](https://www.euroalert.net/)

### Inferences
- Low competition (single bidders in 40%, worst in small contracts) plus fragmented discovery suggest many SMEs simply do not see or attempt tenders. Matching and bid-preparation tools address real friction, but the market already has vendors.
- Execution-phase uncertainty (unforeseen modifications, appeals, missing subcontractor data) hits SME contractors and subcontractors after they win. Late payment is covered by another researcher.

### Gaps
- The share of contracts and value going to SMEs in Spain (the EU Single Market Scoreboard page returned 403).
- How much the European Single Procurement Document (DEUC), solvency or classification rules cost SMEs in time or euros.
- Prices of tender services and of consultants preparing bids.
- Payment periods of public administrations (overlap with the cash-flow researcher).

## Structured pain-point inventory (ADM entries)

### ADM-01: Madrid — months-long verification of declaraciones responsables and licences, with a paid ECU fast lane
- Who: any sector opening or changing premises (retail, hospitality, personal services, clinics, offices); autónomo, micro, small; chains and franchisees.
- Where: city of Madrid.
- Day-to-day reality: Owners open on a declaración responsable, but the council's material check comes months later (target average 109 days). They operate exposed to requirements, closure orders or sanctions after spending on works. Activities that still need a prior licence wait a target average of 137 days, with only 40% resolved within 60 days. For certainty, owners pay a private ECU and municipal fees, plus 4% ICIO on works.
- Evidence:
  - 109-day DR check standard (40% within 90 days); 137-day licence standard (40% within 60 days, 60% within 90 days); appointments needed for in-person help; ICIO 4% — [Carta de Servicios ADA 2026, evaluated 24 Feb 2026](https://www.madrid.es/UnidadesDescentralizadas/Calidad/CARTAS%20SERVICIOS/SistemaCartasServicio/41%20CS_de%20la%20Agencia%20de%20Actividades/DEFINITIVA/CS_ADA_2026.pdf)
  - ECU maximum prices for 2026: DR €600 (≤50 m²) up to €1,250 (≤300 m²) plus €1.75/m² above that; first-occupation visit €1,080 (≤200 m²); second visits €325 — [ECU prices 2026, Junta de Gobierno 16 Oct 2025](https://www.madrid.es/UnidadesDescentralizadas/AgenciaActividades/ECUs/ficheros/AnexosI_II_III_IV_PreciosECU2026.pdf)
  - 19 ECUs with 5–15% discounts — [Cuadro comparativo 2026](https://www.madrid.es/UnidadesDescentralizadas/AgenciaActividades/ECUs/ficheros/CuadroComparativoPreciosEcu_2026.pdf)
  - Second independent source on actual delays: none found (single-source, official targets).
- Scale: 13,301 DRs and licences handled in 2025; 3,805 inspections (same charter).
- Cost/impact: €600–€1,250+ per ECU file, plus visits (€190–€1,080), *tasas* (amounts not retrieved) and 4% ICIO. The standard timelines imply about 3–5 months of regulatory uncertainty per opening.
- Driver & status: Ordenanza 6/2022 de Licencias y Declaraciones Responsables Urbanísticas (in force; listed in the 2026 charter). ECU 2026 prices are in force; prices for 2027 were approved on 15 Oct 2026.
- How it is solved today: ECUs (voluntary, regulated prices); architects and engineers for technical projects (fees not found); gestorías. No documented complaints about ECUs were found.
- Software-solvable?: partly. Pre-checks (use compatibility, document completeness), file tracking and ECU comparison are software; the statutory verification is public.
- Confidence: medium-high (official targets; actual 2025 results not retrieved).

### ADM-04: Diverging local and regional requirements that block or fragment activities (market-unity cases)
- Who: engineering and technical services (who may sign projects), telecom and fibre deployers, EV charging and battery storage, tourist-rental operators, training providers, passenger transport, fuel stations, funeral services. Mostly small and medium firms working across municipalities.
- Where: Andalucía, Canarias, Comunitat Valenciana, Navarra, Illes Balears (Palma, Calvià, Formentera), Euskadi; cities including Málaga, Sevilla, Zaragoza, Granada, Alicante, Badajoz, Murcia and Cornellà.

- Evidence:
  - 50 CNMC reports and rulings dated Sept 2025–Sept 2026 on page 1 of 14; the top sector is engineering and technical services (10) — [CNMC informes UM](https://www.cnmc.es/informes-unidad-de-mercado-2)
  - CNMC appeals and objections in Badajoz (1 Oct 2026), Canarias (31 Jul 2026) and Valencian training grants (17 Jun 2026) — [CNMC](https://www.cnmc.es/ambitos-de-actuacion/unidad-de-mercado)
  - CEOE (2026) on "asimetría de criterios entre administraciones" — [CEOE PRTR report](https://www.ceoe.es/sites/ceoe-corporativo/files/content/file/2026/06/26/104/informe-ejecucion-fondos-europeos-junio-2026.pdf)
  - Regions produce 80.8% of bulletin pages — [INAP on CEOE 2022](https://laadministracionaldia.inap.es/noticia.asp?id=1232548)
- Scale: about 50 CNMC actions a year (my inference from the listing). This understates the problem, since most firms never complain.
- Cost/impact: blocked or delayed projects (for example the Badajoz petrol station, fibre deployments) and duplicate paperwork; no euro figures found.
- Driver & status: Ley 20/2013 de garantía de la unidad de mercado (in force and actively used by the CNMC in 2026).
- How it is solved today: lawyers, trade associations, free CNMC or SECUM complaints.
- Software-solvable?: partly. A municipality-by-municipality requirements map (who may sign, which procedure) helps; the root cause is legal.
- Confidence: medium.

### ADM-05: Regulatory inflation and the cost of keeping up

- Scale: universal (3,310,824 active companies — [INE DIRCE, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/DIRCE2025.htm)).

- Driver & status: structural. Of the 2025 annual regulatory plan, 36.18% was approved and 46.23% was still in progress.
- How it is solved today: gestorías and asesorías, trade associations, legal publishers (products and prices not verified).
- Software-solvable?: yes. Monitoring filtered by sector code (CNAE) and municipality, with plain-language impact summaries.
- Confidence: medium (volume data from 2022; newer CEOE editions not retrieved).

### ADM-06: SL formation — a "24-hour" legal promise versus 1–2 weeks of coordination
- Who: startups, new SMEs, autónomos incorporating, foreign founders.
- Where: national (Registro Mercantil; the Barcelona example).
- Day-to-day reality: Founders chain together the name certificate, bank account and deposit certificate, notary, registry and post-registration tax steps, often with a gestor. Foreign founders also need an NIE and a digital certificate, and online guides even get the capital rule wrong.
- Evidence:
  - Legal limits of 6 and 12 business hours and €1 capital — [BOE, Ley 18/2022](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-15818)
  - 7–10 business days and €250–450 doing it yourself; FAQ wrongly says "€3,000 minimum" — [Strong Abogados, 17 Nov 2025](https://www.strongabogados.com/articles/sl-self-registration)
  - Registro Mercantil de Barcelona 5–10 working days, "no hay un plazo garantizable" — [Billeo, 3 Aug 2026](https://www.billeo.es/blog/gestorias-especializadas-constitucion-sociedades-barcelona)
- Scale: new SLs per year not retrieved. About 1.45M non-natural-person businesses exist (DIRCE inference).
- Cost/impact: official fees about €100–€250 (search snippet, [Legálitas](https://www.legalitas.com/actualidad/crear-sl-con-un-euro)); €600–€1,500 with a gestor (search snippet, [Getrenn](https://getrenn.com/blog/how-to-register-a-company-in-spain)); 1–2 weeks.
- Driver & status: Ley 18/2022 (in force since 19 Oct 2022).
- How it is solved today: CIRCE/PAE (low fees), notaries, gestorías, law firms bundling free formation with accounting.
- Software-solvable?: partly. Orchestrating the single electronic form (DUE), bank and notary, plus post-formation compliance; but low willingness to pay and a crowded market.
- Confidence: medium.

### ADM-07: Digital identity for companies — representative certificates, renewals and safe custody
- Who: all legal entities (directors and administrators), gestorías holding many clients' certificates, autónomos (personal certificate or Cl@ve).
- Where: national (FNMT, AEAT offices).
- Day-to-day reality: Each company needs a representative certificate (€14 plus tax) and must renew it every 2 years. Entity types outside NIF A, B, C and D must accredit identity in person at AEAT, with an appointment. The DNIe/NFC route is unreliable. Certificates are installed per browser and shared among staff, which creates fraud risk; signatures were misused in the Kit Digital fraud.
- Evidence:

- Scale: about 1.45M legal entities and 1.86M natural-person businesses (DIRCE 2025).
- Cost/impact: €14 plus VAT every 2 years; office visits; fraud exposure (vehicles registered in victims' names, fraudulent grant applications).
- Driver & status: Ley 39/2015 art. 14.2 (in force).
- How it is solved today: FNMT; private certificate providers (prices not retrieved); centralised custody products such as IvSign ([Ivnosys](https://www.ivnosys.com/); no price shown); gestorías holding clients' certificates (practice not verified).

### ADM-08: Mandatory electronic notifications — the 10-day trap, embargoes and 9,000+ mailboxes
- Who: every SL, SA, cooperative, comunidad de bienes and other entity, of any size. Micro firms without an advisor are the most exposed.
- Where: national, regional and local (AEAT, Seguridad Social, DGT, regions, municipalities, courts).
- Day-to-day reality: Notices land in DEHú or in separate electronic offices. If one is not opened within 10 calendar days it is deemed rejected, and the procedure moves on (enforcement order, account embargo, sanctions). The email alert is "obligatory", but the law says its absence does not invalidate the notice, so firms end up litigating with uncertain results.
- Evidence:
  - Arts. 14.2, 41.6 and 43.2 — [BOE, Ley 39/2015, consolidated 6 Nov 2024](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2015-10565)
  - Embargo upheld: an unopened e-notification is like an unopened letter — [TS 1927/2017, 11 Dec 2017](https://www.fiscal-impuestos.com/sites/fiscal-impuestos.com/files/NFJ068943.pdf)
  - Embargo annulled for a missing alert (TSJ Murcia 396/2025, 2 Oct 2025; also STC 84/2022, SAN 78/2022, SAN 19 Apr 2023; no Supreme Court ruling yet) — [GA-P, Nov 2025](https://ga-p.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/Aviso_previo_condicion_validez.pdf)
  - "Sincroniza más de 9.000 sedes y evita sanciones" — [Ivnosys](https://www.ivnosys.com/) (vendor claim)
  - Court summonses electronic for legal persons since 20 Mar 2024 — [Grupo2000](https://www.grupo2000.es/ya-es-posible-recibir-notificaciones-de-demandas-via-electronica) (search snippet only)
- Scale: about 1.45M non-natural-person businesses (3,310,824 − 1,859,925; inference from [INE DIRCE, 11 Dec 2025](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/DIRCE2025.htm)).
- Cost/impact: bank-account embargoes, sanctions and enforcement surcharges (amounts not available), plus litigation costs. No national statistics found.
- Driver & status: Ley 39/2015 arts. 14.2, 41.6 and 43.2 (in force). Case law is evolving, with no Supreme Court ruling on the protective line as of Nov 2025.
- How it is solved today: gestorías and asesorías acting as representatives (prices not found); notification managers such as IvNeos (no price); manual daily checks of several offices.
- Software-solvable?: yes. Aggregation across DEHú, AEAT, Seguridad Social, regional and local offices, with alerts, legal-deadline calculation, triage, representative delegation and audit trail.
- Confidence: high on the mechanism and legal risk; medium on how often harm occurs.

### ADM-09: Scarce appointments (cita previa) at Seguridad Social, SEPE and DGT
- Who: autónomos and micro employers who handle their own procedures, and their staff; driving schools and transport firms (DGT); all sizes in Madrid and Barcelona.
- Where: national; worst reported in Madrid (Seguridad Social, SEPE) and Barcelona (SEPE).
- Day-to-day reality: In 2025 appointments could not be booked through any channel. Guards refused entry without one and people waited in the street. Traffic offices require compulsory appointments, and sanction files cannot be consulted online.
- Evidence:
  - Seguridad Social blockage and guards; SEPE complaints in Madrid and Barcelona with 712 offices and 27,100 appointments a day in November 2025; DGT compulsory appointments — [Defensor del Pueblo, Informe anual 2025, pp. 108–109, 227–228, 231–232 and §3.5](https://www.defensordelpueblo.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/03/Defensor-del-Pueblo_Informe-anual-2025.pdf) (single source; second source not found)
  - Madrid's Agencia de Actividades also requires appointments for in-person help — [ADA charter 2026](https://www.madrid.es/UnidadesDescentralizadas/Calidad/CARTAS%20SERVICIOS/SistemaCartasServicio/41%20CS_de%20la%20Agencia%20de%20Actividades/DEFINITIVA/CS_ADA_2026.pdf)
- Scale: SEPE averages 27,100 appointments a day (November 2025).
- Cost/impact: delays and lost working time (not quantified).
- Driver & status: administrative practice of compulsory appointments. The DGT was considering moving to "recommended" appointments (2025). The Ombudsman reopened its Seguridad Social inquiry at the end of 2025.
- How it is solved today: gestorías and *graduados sociales* using the Seguridad Social's online systems (prices not found); online procedures. Appointment resale is not verified.
- Software-solvable?: partly. Guiding users to online alternatives and to representation removes some visits. Appointment-sniping bots are abusive and carry legal risk.
- Confidence: medium.

### ADM-14: NextGenerationEU — complex access, slow payments and repayment risk for SMEs at the end of the Recovery Facility

## Top pains
1. **ADM-08, mandatory e-notifications:** applies to about 1.45M entities by law, a 10-day "deemed rejected" rule leads to embargoes and sanctions, the case law is split, there are 9,000+ separate offices, and the fit for software is clear.
2. **ADM-01, ADM-02 and ADM-03, opening premises:** Madrid's own standards are 109–137 days, València averaged 370 days in 2022, compatibility checks are uncertain, and businesses already pay €600–€1,250+ to ECUs for certainty. Severe for every new opening.
3. **ADM-17 and ADM-18, public procurement:** a €113bn base budget, 2.94 bidders on average, 40.7% single bidders, 8 platforms with heterogeneous data and invisible minor contracts. Real need but a crowded tool market.
4. **ADM-14, ADM-15 and ADM-16, grants:** the end of the Recovery Facility moves the risk to justification and repayment (2026–2028); SMEs got only 43% of awarded funds; discovery spans 81,665 calls and more than 8,500 administrations.
5. **ADM-07, company digital identity:** universal, recurring (2-year renewals), in-person accreditation for some entities, and linked to documented fraud. A cheap, clear software fit (custody, logs, renewals).
6. **ADM-05 and ADM-04, regulatory inflation and fragmentation:** 1.3M bulletin pages a year (80% regional) and about 50 CNMC market-unity actions a year. Universal but diffuse; monitoring is solvable, the divergence itself is not.
7. **ADM-09 and ADM-10, appointments and immigration offices:** widespread and severe in Madrid and Barcelona and for employers of foreign workers, but software can only partly help.
8. **ADM-11, autónomo regularisations:** 1.86M natural-person businesses face surprise bills; forecasting is software-solvable. Overlaps with the tax and labour researchers.

## Noise and dead ends
- **Kit Digital as a product market:** the programme closed in October 2025 (Red.es). Only residual needs remain (repayment defence, fraud victims, unpaid agents). Do not build "Kit Digital management".
- **Kit Consulting:** €300M run from May 2024 to February 2025 per Red.es. No results data found, and the programme appears closed (extension not verified).
- **The post-12-month "subsidy cliff":** asked explicitly, but no evidence was found on whether SMEs kept paying for Kit Digital websites or software. Weakly evidenced; Ara's €150 + €15/month example shows only the market price of one tool.
- **SL formation as a standalone pain:** legally fast (6–12 business hours per step) and cheap (about €100–€450 official). Gestorías and law firms bundle it, even for free. Real but moderate friction with low willingness to pay.
- **Appointment bots or resale:** not a legitimate product (abuse and legal risk), and no 2024–2026 source on resale was found in this session.
- **Market-unity divergence:** the root cause is legal and political. Software can map rules but cannot remove barriers; CNMC complaints are free.
- **NextGenerationEU loans:** 73% of the tranche was renounced and the products had no traction. Not a pain SaaS can solve, and the Recovery Facility window has passed.
- **Certificates of being up to date (AEAT and Seguridad Social):** believed to be instant online for certificate holders, but not verified in this session. Probably cheaply solved; verify before dismissing.
- **València backlog figures:** 2022–2023 data, partly from partisan sources and disputed by the then government. Reorganisation (2023) and a new ordinance (2025) may have changed things, so verify before using as a current-state claim.
