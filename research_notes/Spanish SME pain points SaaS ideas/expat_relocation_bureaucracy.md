# Expat and relocation bureaucracy in Spain (2024–2026): pain points, volumes, regulation and the service market

_Research date: 23 October 2026. Scope: national rules plus detail for Madrid, Barcelona, Málaga, Valencia, Alicante, Illes Balears and Canarias (Las Palmas / Santa Cruz de Tenerife). Notes in English with key Spanish terms kept. Labels: "via search snippet" means the fact comes from a search-engine summary of the cited page, not a full read of it, so it carries less weight. "Single-source" means I found only one source. The web-search budget shared by all research agents ran out partway through. After that I only fetched URLs I had already found, so Reddit, Expatica, The Local and Sur in English threads were not retrieved (see Gaps). Overlaps with other tracks are only touched on: the employer-side labour law of hiring foreigners (labour track), cita previa and e-government for businesses (bureaucracy track), foreign founders and ENISA (startups track), and padrón and rentals for remote workers (mid-term housing track)._

## 1. Core procedures and their real friction (NIE, TIE, padrón, NUSS, digital certificate, bank, canje, homologación, Beckham, DNV/UGE, EU certificate, family reunification)

### Takeaway
In 2025–2026 the bottleneck is mostly not the permit decision. By law the UGE decides in 20 days, with positive silence, and several Delegaciones resolve in 1–3 months. The bottleneck is the in-person steps that follow, above all the police fingerprint appointment for the TIE (3–6 months reported in Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia). There are also long-tail backlogs: homologación (72,000–100,000 pending files) and some office-specific queues (232 days for initial employee permits in Valencia, 184 days for family reunification in Baleares). Digital identity creates a chicken-and-egg problem. Without a TIE you cannot get an FNMT certificate, and without a certificate or Cl@ve most online shortcuts are closed.

### Cited Findings

**TIE and the fingerprint step (toma de huellas): national, worst in the big cities**
- After a favourable resolution, non-EU residents must book a Policía Nacional appointment for fingerprinting to get the TIE. One law firm says this must happen within "30 días hábiles", and elsewhere "un mes", from notification. It cites no article (single-source, law-firm blog) — [IG Abogados, early 2026 (undated)](https://igabogadosextranjeria.com/toma-de-huellas-en-extranjeria-2026-que-es-y-por-que-cuesta-tanto-conseguir-cita/)
- Barcelona: "Hay gente que lleva tres meses buscando una cita de huellas" ("there are people who have spent three months looking for a fingerprint appointment"). The source is Quim Clavaguera of ICAB, who also said slots are already insufficient for ordinary, non-regularisation procedures — [Crónica Global, 30 Jun 2026](https://cronicaglobal.elespanol.com/politica/20260630/avalancha-solicitudes-regularizacion-colapsar-servicios-extranjeria-barcelona/1003742774382_0.html)
- Realistic waits of "three to six months" in Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia — [RemoteFromSpain, 29 Jul 2026 (no sources given)](https://remotefromspain.com/tie-appointment-shortage-spain-2026/). A second law firm says 3–6 months or more, citing a case of 4 months in Madrid city against 3 days in Guadalajara — [IG Abogados, early 2026](https://igabogadosextranjeria.com/toma-de-huellas-en-extranjeria-2026-que-es-y-por-que-cuesta-tanto-conseguir-cita/). Both are commercial sources, not official data.
- A paid alert service estimates manual-search waits: Madrid 4–12 weeks, Barcelona 3–10, Málaga 3–8, Valencia 2–4, Alicante 2–4, Illes Balears 2–4, Las Palmas 2–3, with no estimate for Santa Cruz de Tenerife. By procedure: fingerprints (4010) from 1 week in small provinces to 12 weeks in Madrid; NIE assignment (4031) from 3–5 days to 6–8 weeks in Madrid and Barcelona; EU certificate (4038) 1–2 weeks inland and 4–6 weeks on the coast — [HayCita, data verified 23 Oct 2026](https://haycita.es/guia/tiempo-espera-cita-extranjeria). The vendor sells alerts, so it has an interest in reporting scarcity.
- From 8 Jun to 23 Oct 2026 (138 days), CitaEx's monitor detected these slots on the official ICP+ portal: Madrid 915, Barcelona 361, Alicante 204, Valencia 95, Illes Balears 57, Málaga 32, Santa Cruz de Tenerife 15, Las Palmas 3. Slots "sell out in under 2 minutes", often around 08:00. Caveats: counts measure portal activity, not unique appointments, and the detector was down from 17 Sep to 14 Oct — [CitaEx, updated 23 Oct 2026](https://citaex.com/disponibilidad-citas)
- For HR teams, Jobbatical describes backlogs "up to three months or more in high-demand areas", calls Catalonia "effectively blocked", and cites union vacancy rates "over 20%" in some offices. It gives no primary sources — [Jobbatical, updated 1 Sep 2026](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/spain-immigration-crisis-appointment-delays)
- After fingerprinting, the card typically takes 30–45 days — [XPath relocation guide 2026, via search snippet](https://xpath.global/blog/moving-to-spain-relocation-guide-expats-employers). Another source gives 4–6 weeks — [RemoteFromSpain, 29 Jul 2026](https://remotefromspain.com/tie-appointment-shortage-spain-2026/)
- One law firm, without a source, says the foreign population grew by more than 15% in three years while police staff grew by about 3% (unverified) — [IG Abogados, early 2026](https://igabogadosextranjeria.com/toma-de-huellas-en-extranjeria-2026-que-es-y-por-que-cuesta-tanto-conseguir-cita/)

**EU citizens' registration certificate (certificado de registro, "NIE verde")**
- Appointment waits for procedure 4038 are 1–2 weeks inland and 4–6 weeks on the coast — [HayCita, 23 Oct 2026](https://haycita.es/guia/tiempo-espera-cita-extranjeria). In Barcelona the certificate is handed over on the spot once the fee is paid and the documents are checked — [Barcelona International Welcome, via search snippet](https://www.barcelona.cat/internationalwelcome/en/procedure/certificate-registration-eu-national-green-nie). Saturation in Madrid and Barcelona is documented only anecdotally, for example a reader comment from Dec 2024 — [Parainmigrantes, via search snippet](https://www.parainmigrantes.info/certificado-de-registro-de-ciudadano-de-la-u-e/)

**Empadronamiento (city-specific)**
- Barcelona: 2–3 weeks for an appointment at the busiest OACs and 1–2 weeks at quieter ones such as Sant Andreu and Nou Barris — [Jobbatical Barcelona HR guide 2026, via search snippet](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/barcelona-empadronamiento-poblenou-gracia-eixample). Registering online needs a digital certificate or Cl@ve (search snippet of Barcelona guides).
- Madrid: according to a 2026 guide, appointments are released in batches at unpredictable times, with a limit of one appointment per person every 30 days. Another guide says most OACs accept walk-ins for padrón. The sources conflict, and I am not sure which of the two guides said what — [Vida en Madrid](https://vidaenmadrid.com/pedir-cita-para-empadronamiento-en-madrid/); [MJ Barba Consulting](https://www.mjbarbaconsulting.com/guias/cita-padron-madrid-sin-disponibilidad/) (both via search snippet)
- The padrón is needed for the TIE appointment — [XPath 2026, via search snippet](https://xpath.global/blog/moving-to-spain-relocation-guide-expats-employers) (single-source)

**Digital certificate and Cl@ve (national)**
- FNMT says the NIE "no es en ningún caso, elemento suficiente para realizar la acreditación de la persona que está solicitando el certificado" ("is in no case sufficient to accredit the identity of the person applying for the certificate"). Accepted identity documents are the EU Registration Certificate for EU/EEA citizens and the TIE for non-EU foreigners — [FNMT FAQ "Problemática del uso del NIE", undated, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.sede.fnmt.gob.es/registro-inicio/preguntas-frecuentes/-/asset_publisher/DKnDigPrgk4U/content/4-5-problematica-del-uso-del-nie)
- DGT's digital licence-exchange service "requiere que el ciudadano esté dado de alta en Cl@ve o disponga de certificado digital" ("requires the citizen to be registered in Cl@ve or to hold a digital certificate") — [DGT press release, 20 May 2025](https://www.dgt.es/comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/20250520-dgt-servicio-de-canje-digital-de-permisos-de-conduccion/)
- Conflicting: a migration law firm says Cl@ve accepts the NIE as identification — [Migralis, via search snippet](https://migralis.com/blog/clave-o-certificado-digital/)

**Seguridad Social number (NUSS/NAF)**
- Import@ss requests are described as resolved immediately and free, and the employer can request the number for a new worker — [De la Guía Luzón, via search snippet](https://delaguialuzon.com/blog/como-obtener-el-numero-de-la-seguridad-social/). Import@ss also offers a photo-plus-selfie route without Cl@ve — [Cohen y Aguirre, via search snippet](https://cohenyaguirre.es/numero-seguridad-social-requisitos-extranjeros). **Bank account**
- Banco de España describes the basic payment account (cuenta de pago básica) as a right for anyone legally resident in the EU, which banks must offer — [BdE leaflet, via search snippet](https://www.bde.es/f/webbe/INF/MenuHorizontal/AreasActuacion/conducta/ficheros/Sesion2Tripticocuentadepagobasica.pdf). Neobanks (Revolut, N26, Wise) open accounts online with a passport, while BBVA, CaixaBank and Santander usually require an in-person visit — [Rankia 2026, via search snippet](https://www.rankia.com/blog/cuentas-corrientes/6946269-abrir-cuenta-bancaria-pasaporte-online). The claim that "some banks require a TIE" is unsourced — [RemoteFromSpain, 29 Jul 2026](https://remotefromspain.com/tie-appointment-shortage-spain-2026/)

**Driving-licence exchange (canje)**
- Since 20 May 2025, DGT offers a digital exchange without cita previa, but only for third countries with bilateral agreements. These include the UK, Argentina, Andorra, Peru, Ukraine, Uruguay, Japan, Switzerland, Korea and Monaco, which need no prior verification code (localizador), and Morocco, Colombia, Brazil, Chile and others, which do. It requires Cl@ve or a certificate, or a representative registered in the Registro de apoderamientos — [DGT, 20 May 2025](https://www.dgt.es/comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/20250520-dgt-servicio-de-canje-digital-de-permisos-de-conduccion/)
- The US has no exchange agreement. US residents may drive on their state licence for up to six months after becoming resident and must then pass the theory test (available in English) and a practical test through an approved autoescuela — [Spainguru, 18 Dec 2025 (updated 2 May 2026)](https://spainguru.es/2025/12/18/spanish-drivers-license-road-test-us-residents-spain/); [Waypoint Sur, Mar 2026, via search snippet](https://guides.waypointsur.com/us-driving-license-spain/). The total cost of the practical route is about €700–1,200 — [Autoviatest, via search snippet](https://autoviatest.com/en-US/expat-license/spain) (single-source, commercial). Driving after the deadline risks a fine of up to €500 — [Costa Luz Lawyers, via search snippet](https://www.costaluzlawyers.com/exchange-uk-driving-licence-spain-2026/) (single-source)
- For in-person exchanges, waits for a Jefatura appointment "can be weeks or months" in high-demand cities — [CaixaBank new-residents page, via search snippet](https://www.caixabank.es/particular/nuevos-residentes/canje-de-permiso-de-conducir-extranjero-en-espana-p.html)

**Homologación / equivalencia of foreign degrees (national)**
- Ministry data presented on 14 Apr 2026: 85,564 files resolved in 2025 against 39,975 in 2024, and 72,337 still pending at the end of 2025. The ministry says the backlog has fallen by more than 40% since October 2024 — [Infomigrante, 14 Oct 2026](https://infomigrante.es/2026/10/14/la-reforma-de-homologacion-de-titulos-extranjeros-en-el-limbo-ante-las-elecciones-generales/)
- The Defensor del Pueblo estimates about 100,000 pending files and received more than 1,000 complaints in 2025. Its wording: "todos los expedientes se tramitan en un plazo muy superior a los seis meses previstos" ("all files are processed in far longer than the six months provided for"). In many cases more than a year passes before the documentation is even checked. It also found repeated requests to fix documents that had already been corrected (subsanación) and wrongful closures for abandonment (desistimiento) — [Diario Siglo XXI / Europa Press, 24 Mar 2026](https://www.diariosigloxxi.com/texto-ep/mostrar/20260324182602/defensor-avisa-colapso-homologacion-titulos-universitarios-pide-transparencia-ministerio)
- The Tribunal de Cuentas counted 80,648 pending files at 30 Jun 2025 — [Educación Gratuita, via search snippet](https://educaciongratuita.com/noticias-educacion/homologacion-de-titulos-extranjeros) (single-source)

**Beckham regime (régimen especial de impatriados, modelos 149/151)**
- Employment income is taxed at a flat 24% up to €600,000 and 47% above that, for the year of the move plus five more. Modelo 149 must be filed within six months of the Social Security registration (alta), and modelo 151 replaces modelo 100 for the annual return — [INEAF](https://www.ineaf.es/tribuna/la-ley-beckham-el-regimen-para-los-impatriados/); [Pellicer & Heredia](https://www.pellicerheredia.com/ley-beckham/) (via search snippet); [BM Consulting, 18 May 2026](https://bm.consulting/en/insights/beckham-law-court-rulings-aeat-rejections-2026/). BM Consulting cites art. 119.4 RIRPF and says late filings are rejected even with good reasons (unverified, and the firm sells Beckham advice).
- AEAT figures: about 37,000 taxpayers used the regime over the last decade, about 185 (0.5%) were inspected, and 55 of those checks led to a claim or appeal — [El Economista, May 2025, via search snippet; full page blocked (403)](https://www.eleconomista.es/economia/noticias/13349201/05/25/hacienda-responde-al-bufete-britanico-que-le-acusa-de-carterista-solo-se-inspecciona-al-05-de-los-beneficiarios-de-la-ley-beckham.html) (single-source). This came amid a public row with the firm Amsterdam & Partners, which accused AEAT of "un patrón de discriminación y persecución contra los extranjeros con altos ingresos" ("a pattern of discrimination and persecution against high-income foreigners") — [Infobae, 4 Jun 2025](https://www.infobae.com/espana/2025/06/05/las-13-preguntas-que-hacienda-se-niega-a-contestar-sobre-la-ley-beckham-del-bufete-que-acusa-de-carteristas-a-los-inspectores-espanoles/)

**Digital nomad visa and UGE permits (Unidad de Grandes Empresas y Colectivos Estratégicos)**
- Ley 14/2013 art. 76: "El plazo máximo de resolución será de veinte días desde la presentación electrónica de la solicitud" ("the maximum time to resolve is twenty days from electronic filing"). If that passes, "la autorización se entenderá estimada por silencio administrativo" ("the authorisation is deemed granted by administrative silence"). Art. 74 ter requires the company to have operated for at least one year and the worker to have at least three months of prior relationship with it — [BOE, Ley 14/2013 consolidated text, last update 9 Apr 2025](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2013-10074)
- In practice, the UGE-CE and consulates now ask for several months of bank statements, clear proof of remote work and established client relationships. Income must be at least 200% of the SMI, about €2,760–2,770 gross per month in 2025, plus 75% for the first family member and 25% per child — [Polaris Extranjería, published 21 Nov 2025, updated 29 Sep 2026](https://polarisextranjeria.com/nuevo-reglamento-extranjeria-rd-1155-2024-balance-2024-2025). Lexidy gives €2,763 per month "as of 2025" — [Lexidy, undated](https://www.lexidy.com/services/spain/immigration/digital-nomad/)
- Health policies with copayments or waiting periods are rejected, and a gap between the amounts in contracts or payslips and actual bank deposits is a frequent cause of requests for more documents (requerimientos) or denials — [Escalona Abogados](https://escalonaabogados.es/visado-para-nomadas-digitales-en-espana-guia-completa-2025-2026/); [IG Abogados DNV](https://igabogadosextranjeria.com/visado-de-nomada-digital-en-espana-requisitos-errores-comunes-y-que-documentos-presentar-correctamente-en-2025/) (both via search snippet). Criterio de Gestión 2/2025 of the DGGM (July 2025) allows authorisations shorter than three years when the contract is shorter (via search snippet, single-source).
- Typical UGE decisions take 16–18 working days, and complex cases 25–45 working days — [Klevvera](https://www.klevvera.com/blog/uge-application-spain/); [Nomadlex](https://www.nomadlex.io/blog/spain-digital-nomad-visa-processing-time) (via search snippet, commercial)
- Highly qualified professionals (PAC) and EU Blue Card: after INE published the 2024 average salary (€29,540.26) on 28 May 2026, the UGE updated the salary thresholds to €41,356.36 (general) and €33,085.09 (reduced, Blue Card only) — [Infoextranjeria, 11 Jun 2026, via search snippet](https://infoextranjeria.org/2026/06/11/actualizacion-del-umbral-salarial-para-profesionales-altamente-cualificados/). Sources disagree on whether the same threshold applies to the national PAC permit (Orden PJC/44/2026 refers to Blue Card holders) — [Machelin Díaz, via search snippet](https://machelindiaz.com/actualizacion-umbral-salarial-altamente-cualificado-2026/)
- Deel quotes 3–5 months for the highly qualified permit and the EU Blue Card and 10–20 working days for the DNV. That conflicts with the 20-day legal limit, probably because it counts door to door including the consulate (my reading) — [Deel, updated 9 Jun 2026](https://www.deel.com/blog/how-to-get-a-visa-and-work-permit-in-spain/). For intra-company transfers (ICT), Jobbatical gives "20 working days at UGE-CE, €89.34 in fees, 8–14 weeks door to door" — [Jobbatical ICT guide, via search snippet](https://www.jobbatical.com/blog/spain-intra-company-transfer-ict-permit-guide)

**Travel while the TIE is pending (national)**
- A renewal receipt (resguardo) lets you leave Spain but not re-enter. The return authorisation (autorización de regreso, form EX-13) is valid for at most 90 days, only through Spanish border posts, and is not available while an initial application is still unresolved — [España Abogados](https://espanaabogados.com/autorizacion-de-regreso/); [Immigration Lawyers Spain](https://immigrationlawyersspain.com/es/guias/autorizacion-de-regreso-viajar-renovacion) (via search snippet). The Comisaría General de Extranjería y Fronteras' Instrucción 1/2026 (2 Jun 2026) let people enter through Spanish land or sea border posts between 8 Jun and 30 Sep 2026 without a return authorisation, if they carried a passport, the expired permit and the renewal application. Practitioners warn: "La compañía aérea puede negarse a embarcarte" ("the airline may refuse to board you") — [Machelin Díaz, 2026](https://machelindiaz.com/autorizacion-de-regreso-verano-2026/)

**Consulates and outsourced visa centres (for hires recruited abroad)**
- The Defensor del Pueblo's 2025 report notes frequent complaints about visa appointments at consulates, and also differing criteria between Extranjería offices and processing delays — [Defensor del Pueblo, Informe anual 2025, via search snippet](https://www.defensordelpueblo.es/informe-anual/informe-anual-2025/)
- Anecdotal reviews of BLS International, the outsourced Spanish visa centre: "almost impossible to sign up on your own without bots", a 10-week wait, and being charged several times — [Tripadvisor](https://www.tripadvisor.com/Attraction_Review-g186338-d28055780-Reviews-Bls_International_Spain_Visa_Centre-London_England.html); [Trustpilot](https://uk.trustpilot.com/review/blsspainvisa.com) (via search snippet, unverified individual reviews). BLS "categorically" denies involvement. Its 2016 contract is reportedly worth €175M — [VisaVerge, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.visaverge.com/visa/spain-probes-bls-international-in-25000-schengen-visa-racket/)
- According to Polaris, a Supreme Court ruling treats filing at an outsourced centre (BLS, VFS) as filing with the Administration, so deadlines run from deposit — [Polaris Extranjería, updated 29 Sep 2026](https://polarisextranjeria.com/nuevo-reglamento-extranjeria-rd-1155-2024-balance-2024-2025) (single-source; ruling number not given)

### Inferences
- The time-critical path for a relocating non-EU employee is roughly: consulate visa or UGE decision → arrival → padrón → TIE fingerprint appointment → card (30–45 days) → FNMT certificate → self-service e-government. Every step before the TIE is low-digital and dependent on appointments, which is why HR teams and agencies outsource the period from week 0 to month 4.
- The "permit is fast, the card is slow" split means that the UGE's 20-day positive-silence advantage is cancelled out on the ground by police capacity in Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia.
- Official queue PDFs exist for only 3 of the 8 target offices. Expected-time-to-decision information is therefore scarce and valuable, and it could be built from public PDFs plus case data from professionals.

### Gaps
- No official TIE fingerprint wait-time data. The police do not publish it, so all wait figures are commercial or anecdotal.
- No official resolution-date data for Barcelona, Málaga, Alicante or Canarias.
- No official number of requerimientos or denials by procedure.
- No verified figures for US or other non-agreement drivers who need to retest.
- No AEAT statistics on modelo 149 rejections. The regulations behind the padrón, the EU certificate and the canje (LBRL, RD 240/2007, RD 818/2009) were not re-checked against the BOE during this research.

## 2. Cita previa: scarcity, bot-driven resale ("mafias de citas"), 2024–2026 countermeasures, and what it means for appointment software

### Takeaway
Appointment scarcity is structural, and partly caused by bots. Bot rings were dismantled in 2023 and 2024, yet resale at €15–500 per slot continued on TikTok, Telegram and in locutorios through 2025–2026. The state's response is to remove booking altogether: since 8 Oct 2026 a police pilot auto-assigns TIE fingerprint appointments for 10% of resolved files in the six biggest provinces. It also plans to tie appointments to identity: "personalised codes" were announced in Dec 2025, and I could not confirm they exist. Booking on behalf of third parties is the criminal-enforcement target. Monitoring and alerts are a crowded, cheap product (about €15 one-off) that the state is designing away.

### Cited Findings
- May 2023: 69 people arrested and 25 investigated across provinces, with the ringleaders arrested in Barcelona and Valencia. Applicants were charged €30–200 for a free procedure — [14ymedio](https://www.14ymedio.com/internacional/detenidas-espana-bloquear-extranjeros-revenderlas_1_1094744.html); [Metrópoli Abierta, 12 May 2023](https://metropoliabierta.elespanol.com/sucesos/20230512/detenidos-en-barcelona-los-principales-integrantes-de-una-mafia-que-revendia-citas-extranjeria/763173787_0.html) (via search snippet)
- November 2024: 21 people arrested (17 in Alicante, 1 in Murcia, 3 in Valencia) after an investigation that began in mid-2023. Bots "bloqueaban el servidor oficial" ("blocked the official server"), slots were sold for €15–30 each, and each member earned an estimated €9,000 a month. Charges were daños informáticos (computer damage), falsedad documental (document forgery), pertenencia a grupo criminal (membership of a criminal group) and favorecimiento de la inmigración ilegal (facilitating illegal immigration). The police said the scheme "vulneraban el principio de gratuidad" ("violated the principle that public services are free") — [Infobae / Europa Press, 27 Nov 2024](https://www.infobae.com/espana/agencias/2024/11/27/una-veintena-de-detenidos-por-la-venta-fraudulenta-de-citas-de-extranjeria-con-bots-bloqueando-el-servidor-oficial/); also summarised by [ConseguirCita, 12 Sep 2026](https://conseguircita.com/guia/robot-cita-previa/). One arrest in Teulada (Alicante) in Dec 2024 involved fake asylum appointments — [Friberd Legal, via search snippet](https://friberdlegal.com/citas-falsas-extranjeria-mafias-bots-reventa/) (single-source)
- In June 2025 a TikTok seller offered appointments for about €30 through a Telegram group, describing software that watches for openings and fills in the forms. ### Inferences
- For any SaaS, appointment booking is a dead end. The state is (a) criminalising capture for resale, (b) tying slots to identity, which would make booking by intermediaries impossible if done, and (c) replacing booking with push assignment, which moves the job to interpreting the SMS or email. The durable software job is tracking the 20-day window, ingesting the assignment notice (it can go to the representative), confirming or cancelling correctly ("a correct cancellation returns you to automatic assignment", per Málaga, quoted by Spainguru), and preparing the documents.
- Alert-only services are commoditised (about €15 one-off, with free Telegram alternatives) and will lose value as auto-assignment spreads.

### Gaps
- I did not retrieve the terms of use (aviso legal) of the ICP+ portal, so I cannot confirm an explicit ban on automated access.
- I found no official statistics on the share of slots captured by bots.
- I found no 2025–2026 Interior press notes; Interior pages returned HTTP 403 to automated fetching.

## 3. Regulatory changes 2024–2026 and their status (as of 23 Oct 2026)

### Takeaway
Three big changes landed. A new Reglamento de Extranjería (RD 1155/2024) took effect on 20 May 2025; it was amended by RD 316/2026 and partly annulled by the Supreme Court in July 2026. The golden visa was repealed from 3 Apr 2025. An extraordinary regularisation in Apr–Jun 2026 drew 1.17M applications. Around these came a steady drip of DGGM criteria, SEM instructions, threshold updates, police pilots and court rulings, published through fragmented channels: BOE, ministry pages, Delegación PDFs, and police notices circulated by bar associations. Neither the DNV nor the Beckham regime changed in law in 2025–2026, though administrative scrutiny tightened. The snap general election of 29 Nov 2026 puts pending drafts, such as the homologación reform, in limbo.

## 5. The service market: who handles it today and at what price

### Takeaway
Official fees are trivial, from €9.84 to €73.26 per step. Intermediaries charge:
- €60–200 per single procedure (gestorías)
- €400–3,900 per relocation or visa package
- €500–6,500+ for law-firm DNV work
- €500–1,500 for Beckham filings
- per case on corporate platforms (Deel's $250 documentation fee; Jobbatical and Localyze do not publish prices)

Professional software already exists: GestoLab offers AI validation and auto-fill of EX forms for gestorías, and the consumer app Extranjería App claims 300k downloads. An AI co-pilot would therefore enter a nascent but not empty field. Complaints centre on scarce appointments being monetised, unverifiable marketing claims and outsourced consular centres.

## 6. Legal constraints on automation (representation, data protection, portal rules, liability)

### Takeaway
Software cannot itself be the filer. Electronic filing on behalf of others is reserved to habilitated professionals (abogados, gestores administrativos and graduados sociales through convenios), habilitated civil servants, and, since March 2026, unions and NGOs that must act free of charge. Applicants can file for themselves only with their own certificate or Cl@ve. Booking appointments for third parties is the target of criminal enforcement. A co-pilot therefore has to be a tool used by a representative, or by the applicant, under strong data-protection controls.

### Cited Findings
- Who filed the 2026 regularisation e-applications: lawyers 58%, funcionarios habilitados 16.8%, gestores administrativos 8.4%, applicants with their own digital certificate 7.3%, entities in the Registro de Colaboradores 5.1%, graduados sociales 3.6% — [La Moncloa, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/serviciosdeprensa/notasprensa/inclusion/paginas/2026/020726-balance-regularizacion-extraordinaria.aspx)
- Professional access to Mercurio depends on adhering to the convenio between the State and the professional council. A simple signed mandate (hoja de encargo) from the client plus their ID is usually enough, which is separate from the APODERA power-of-attorney register — [Machelin Díaz, via search snippet](https://machelindiaz.com/mercurio-abogados-autorizacion-extranjeria/); the lawyers' agreement dates from April 2020 — [Abogacía Española, 25 Jan 2021](https://www.abogacia.es/actualidad/noticias/mas-del-60-de-las-solicitudes-telematicas-en-las-oficinas-de-extranjeria-a-traves-de-mercurio-las-realizan-abogados/)
- RD 1155/2024 art. 197: the personal-appearance requirement "se entenderá cumplida" ("is deemed met") whether filing is in person or electronic when the applicant is in Spain. Art. 197.4.c creates the "colaboradores de extranjería" route. The Reglamento also refers to habilitation agreements under art. 5 of Ley 39/2015, and the State may require adhered professionals to keep electronic representation registers. The fragment was cut off — [Noticias Jurídicas, via search snippet](https://noticias.juridicas.com/base_datos/Laboral/937904-rd-1155-2024-de-19-nov-reglamento-de-la-lo-4-2000-de-11-ene-sobre-derechos.html); [draft order on collaborators](https://expinterweb.inclusion.gob.es/participacion/downloadFile?blob=OM+REGISTRO+COLABORADORES+EXTRANJERIA.pdf&norma=2026003) (via search snippet)
- Orden ISM/164/2026: only the most representative unions and non-profits at least 3 years old may register. Representation must be free (art. 8.1.c). Each authorised person must have the necessary knowledge, no criminal conviction related to the activity, no sanctions in the previous three years under immigration, social-order or data-protection law, a certified electronic signature and residence in Spain. A sexual-offences certificate is also required (art. 8.1.b) — [BOE-A-2026-5128](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2026-5128)
- Appointments are free and personal, and the Secretary of State wants keys so that "nadie pueda representar a alguien e ir cobrando una comisión" — [El Correo Gallego / Europa Press, 28 Dec 2025](https://www.elcorreogallego.es/sociedad/2025/12/28/gobierno-estudia-implantar-2026-codigos-125198426.html). Resale rings are charged with daños informáticos and other offences — [Infobae / Europa Press, 27 Nov 2024](https://www.infobae.com/espana/agencias/2024/11/27/una-veintena-de-detenidos-por-la-venta-fraudulenta-de-citas-de-extranjeria-con-bots-bloqueando-el-servidor-oficial/). The ICP+ portal has its own technical requirements and a GDPR data-protection clause — [Sede Administraciones Públicas, ICP+, via search snippet](https://sede.administracionespublicas.gob.es/pagina/index/directorio/icpplus)
- The police auto-assignment runs "sin intervención humana", and notices can go to the designated representative — [DGP notice via ICAB](https://www.icab.es/export/sites/icab/.galleries/documents-noticies/2026-INICIO-PROYECTO-PILOTO-ASIGNACION-AUTOMATICA-DE-CITA-PARA-TOMA-DE-HUELLA.pdf); [Spainguru, 21 Oct 2026](https://spainguru.es/2026/10/21/tie-fingerprint-appointments/)
- What errors cost: each document-correction request (subsanación) gives 10 working days to respond and can add weeks — [GestoLab, 5 Jul 2026](https://www.gestolab.com/blog/formulario-ex-03-que-es-cuando-se-usa-y-como-rellenarlo-paso-a-paso). Missing the Beckham six-month deadline is described as final, with no extension — [BM Consulting, 18 May 2026](https://bm.consulting/en/insights/beckham-law-court-rulings-aeat-rejections-2026/) (vendor; legal basis unverified)

So relying on them carries legal risk: they guide office practice but do not bind courts (my interpretation).

### Inferences
- Go-to-market follows from the rules. Sell to the habilitated filer (law firms, gestorías, graduados sociales) as a co-pilot, or to HR and agencies as a preparation layer that hands off to a filer. Relocation agencies without a lawyer or gestor on staff already have to subcontract filing.
- Identity-bound appointment keys, if implemented, would end any intermediary booking model. Handling auto-assigned notices for the representative would remain legal.
- Data protection: immigration files contain passports, criminal-record certificates, health insurance and family documents. The colaboradores order even screens representatives for data-protection sanctions. A co-pilot will face buyer due diligence on security and processing agreements.

### Gaps
- I did not research AEPD guidance on processing passports and criminal-record certificates, how GDPR art. 10 applies to criminal records, or the EU AI Act's classification of private immigration assistants and its 2026 application status. All remain unverified.
- I found no source on liability when software-induced errors lead to denials, no official API for Mercurio or ICP+, and not the exact wording of the ICP+ terms of use.
- I did not check in the BOE text whether the employer files the UGE application for the worker (Ley 14/2013 art. 75).

## Pain-point inventory (structured entries)

### EXP-01: TIE fingerprint appointment bottleneck after approval
- Who: non-EU foreign workers, digital nomads, students and family members after a favourable resolution; the employer HR teams and relocation agencies that manage them (companies of any size; volumes per company not quantified).
- Where: National. Worst in Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia (city/province offices). Málaga is mid-range; Alicante, Illes Balears and Canarias are shorter but still weeks.
- Day-to-day reality: The permit is granted, but the card needs an in-person police fingerprint appointment. Slots appear at random times and disappear within minutes, so applicants and agencies refresh ICP+ for weeks, pay alert services, or travel to small-town stations. Without the TIE, people cannot re-enter Spain after travel (EXP-17) or get an FNMT certificate (EXP-12).
- Evidence:
  - Barcelona: "Hay gente que lleva tres meses buscando una cita de huellas" — [Crónica Global, 30 Jun 2026](https://cronicaglobal.elespanol.com/politica/20260630/avalancha-solicitudes-regularizacion-colapsar-servicios-extranjeria-barcelona/1003742774382_0.html)
  - 3–6 months in Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia — [RemoteFromSpain, 29 Jul 2026](https://remotefromspain.com/tie-appointment-shortage-spain-2026/); [IG Abogados, early 2026](https://igabogadosextranjeria.com/toma-de-huellas-en-extranjeria-2026-que-es-y-por-que-cuesta-tanto-conseguir-cita/) (both commercial)
  - Manual-search estimates: Madrid 4–12 weeks; Barcelona 3–10; Málaga 3–8; Valencia, Alicante and Baleares 2–4; Las Palmas 2–3 — [HayCita, 23 Oct 2026](https://haycita.es/guia/tiempo-espera-cita-extranjeria)
  - Slots detected over 138 days: Madrid 915, Barcelona 361, Alicante 204, Valencia 95, Baleares 57, Málaga 32, Santa Cruz de Tenerife 15, Las Palmas 3; "under 2 minutes" to sell out — [CitaEx, 23 Oct 2026](https://citaex.com/disponibilidad-citas)
- Scale: 3,497,284 general-regime residents with documents who hold or renew TIEs (31 Dec 2025) — [La Moncloa, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/serviciosdeprensa/notasprensa/inclusion/paginas/2026/200326-poblacion-extranjera-con-residencia.aspx). Up to 1,174,978 regularisation applicants who will need cards if granted — [La Moncloa, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/serviciosdeprensa/notasprensa/inclusion/paginas/2026/020726-balance-regularizacion-extraordinaria.aspx). Annual first-TIE issuance not found.
- Cost/impact: Weeks to six months of delay; black-market slots at €15–500 (EXP-02); travel blocked; a legal window of about one month or 30 working days after notification (single-source), with documented attempts accepted as justification (IG Abogados).
- Driver & status: Administrative practice: Policía Nacional capacity and the design of the ICP+ booking system. TIE obligations sit in RD 1155/2024 (in force since 20 May 2025). Countermeasure: police auto-assignment pilot since 8 Oct 2026 for 10% of files in Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Alicante, Málaga and Murcia (pilot). Personalised codes: announced Dec 2025, unconfirmed.
- How it is solved today: Refreshing ICP+ yourself; alert services (HayCita from €14.99; CitaEx deposit model); gestorías (full TIE service €90 incl. fee at gestoriadeextranjeria.es; €80–160 per Cirera); relocation admin packs (€400 Barcelona per Locallista; €1,200 + IVA Madrid per Relocation Angel); illegal resale. Complaints: resold slots get cancelled with no refund (ConseguirCita); vendors claim privileged channels without evidence (JuroSpain).
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Software cannot add police capacity, but it can track the 20-day auto-assignment window, read assignment notices, pre-check documents so the visit is not wasted, and suggest alternative stations. Automated booking is legally and strategically risky.
- Confidence: High that the pain exists; medium on the exact waits.

### EXP-02: Bot hoarding and resale of appointments ("mafias de citas")
- Who: Everyone who needs an ICP+ slot; low-income migrants are most exposed, but expats also pay intermediaries.
- Where: National. Prosecuted cases in Barcelona and Valencia (2023); Alicante (17 arrests), Murcia and Valencia (2024); Teulada, Alicante (Dec 2024).

## Top pains

1. **EXP-01, TIE fingerprint bottleneck.** National, it hits every non-EU newcomer, delays run to months and a black market has grown around it. The police pilot covers only 10% of files in six provinces.
2. **EXP-03, regularisation surge and the 2027 renewal cliff.** About 1.17M applications share the same offices and police units as expats, and one-year permits mean a renewal wave in 2027.
3. **EXP-04, unpredictable office-by-office backlogs.** Initial employee permits take 12 days in Baleares and 232 in Valencia, and Barcelona, Málaga, Alicante and Canarias publish nothing, so HR cannot promise start dates.
4. **EXP-07, form and data errors.** They are frequent and add weeks per correction request, they break the TIE stage, and they are directly fixable with document extraction and validation.
5. **EXP-06, fast-changing rules in scattered channels.** At least 20 changes in about 17 months, some known only through bar-association PDFs, make this the core of the "co-pilot" value for professionals.
6. **EXP-11, homologación backlog.** 72,000–100,000 pending files block regulated-profession hires, and the reform is in limbo because of the election.
7. **EXP-12, digital-identity chicken-and-egg.** It keeps newcomers out of every online shortcut during the months they most need one, which pushes them to appointments and paid representatives.
8. **EXP-08/09, UGE evidence scrutiny and moving thresholds.** This is the highest-value corporate segment (about 58,000 employer-sponsored permits plus 45,000 DNVs), and documentation mistakes are costly.

## Noise and dead ends

- **Golden visa (residence by investment).** Repealed from 3 Apr 2025 (LO 1/2025; arts. 63–67 of Ley 14/2013 "sin contenido"). Only renewals of the existing stock (54,930 holders in Sept 2025) remain. No new-flow opportunity — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2013-10074); [El Nacional](https://www.elnacional.cat/oneconomia/es/economia/barcelona-ya-supera-15000-nomadas-digitales-extracomunitarios-con-permiso-residencia_1549862_102.html)
- **Appointment-booking bots and resale.** Criminally prosecuted, and the state is designing them away through auto-assignment (pilot since 8 Oct 2026) and identity-bound codes (announced). Not a legitimate SaaS.
- **Appointment-alert services.** Already commoditised: HayCita from €14.99 one-off, free Telegram channels and a dozen competitors. Low willingness to pay, and they will lose value as auto-assignment spreads.
- **Personalised appointment codes as a design assumption.** Announced for Q1 2026 but unconfirmed in Oct 2026. Do not build on it either way.
- **Seguridad Social number (NUSS).** Largely solved: Import@ss online, immediate per one source, and employers can request it. Weakly evidenced as a pain.
- **Bank account without NIE or TIE.** There is a legal right to a basic payment account, and neobanks accept a passport. The evidence of systematic refusal is unsourced (RemoteFromSpain). Low value.
- **EU driving licences.** No exchange is required, so there is no pain. The canje pain is limited to non-agreement countries (EXP-13).
- **The homologación reform as a near-term fix.** The draft royal decree of 27 Apr 2026 is not approved and is likely stalled by the 29 Nov 2026 election. Do not count on it either removing or creating demand soon.
- **"Beckham Law changes in 2025–2026".** No legislative change was approved. Claims of "rising rejections" come from vendors' marketing with no data. The real pain is the deadline and eligibility (EXP-10), not new rules.
- **The "300,000 digital nomads" figure.** A promotional claim with no official table. Use 44,966 UGE teleworker permits (Sept 2025) instead.
- **Regularisation casework itself as a market.** Its users are mostly low-income, served free by unions and NGOs (collaborators must not charge) or by lawyers at €100–400 per file. It matters as a capacity shock (EXP-03) and a 2027 renewal wave, not as a premium SaaS segment.
- **Empadronamiento as a standalone product.** It is a municipal scheduling issue (walk-ins possible in many Madrid OACs, per one guide), the service is free, and address proof belongs to the mid-term housing track. Low value alone.
- **Nationality-by-residence backlog.** Mentioned only in passing (previously often more than 2–3 years, per Polaris; single-source). Weakly evidenced in this research and outside the relocation critical path.
- **The vendor survey "85% of HR expect mostly foreign hires".** A Remote-sponsored survey; do not use it for sizing.
