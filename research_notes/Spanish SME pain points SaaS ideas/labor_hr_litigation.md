## 1. Time recording (registro de jornada): current rules (RDL 8/2019), the planned digital time-recording royal decree, ITSS campaigns and fines, and what SMEs use

### Takeaway
Since 12 May 2019 every employer must keep a daily record of each worker's start and end times and retain it for 4 years. **Paper or a spreadsheet is still legally valid as of 23 Oct 2026**, because the royal decree that would make the record digital has not been approved. The Council of Ministers authorised urgent processing on 30 Sep 2025. The Consejo de Estado gave an unfavourable opinion on 23 Mar 2026, which estimated 1.35M companies affected at €867M a year. Approval was postponed on 24 Jul 2026, and the decree was not on the Council of Ministers agendas of 6, 13 or 20 Oct 2026, although the Government says it still wants it approved before the 29-N election. Enforcement and litigation already make the record matter. ITSS time-of-work fines run at about €15–17M a year and fines for the registro itself at about €3M a year. A 2026 Supreme Court ruling makes the record (or its absence) central to overtime claims.

### Cited Findings
- RDL 8/2019 amended art. 34.9 ET; it was published in the BOE on 12 Mar 2019 and has been mandatory since 12 May 2019 — [ControlHorarioLegal, rev. 23 Oct 2026](https://controlhorariolegal.com/normativa-fichaje-2026) (vendor guide).
- Current obligations: a daily record with start and end times, kept 4 years and available to workers, their representatives and the ITSS. "La norma general no impone hoy un soporte concreto" (the general rule imposes no specific medium today); "Puede utilizarse papel o un medio telemático" (paper or electronic means may be used) — [RegistraHora, 4 Aug 2026, upd. 13 Oct 2026](https://www.registrahora.es/noticias/registro-horario-digital-aplazado-septiembre-2026) (vendor).
- Current penalty: failing to keep the record is a grave infraction (art. 7.5 LISOS). Fines run €751–1,500 (minimum grade), €1,501–3,750 (medium) and €3,751–7,500 (maximum), **per infraction, not per worker**. The proposal of up to €10,000 per affected worker was in the 37.5-hour bill that Congress rejected, and the royal decree does not change LISOS amounts — [ControlHorarioLegal, 23 Oct 2026](https://controlhorariolegal.com/normativa-fichaje-2026); the €7,500 cap is confirmed by [Finantres Noticias, 25 Aug 2026](https://finantresnoticias.com/negocios-y-autonomos/multas-por-incumplir-horario-se-triplican-deben-revisar-ahora-pymes/).
- Timeline of the digital decree:
  - 30 Sep 2025: Council of Ministers authorises urgent processing.
  - Oct 2025: public consultation.
  - 23 Mar 2026: unfavourable Consejo de Estado opinion.
  - 21 Jun 2026: Trabajo's self-imposed deadline passes without publication.
  - Sources: [Aunte, upd. 6 Oct 2026](https://aunte.es/blog/decreto-fichaje-digital-2026-hosteleria) (vendor).
  - 24 Jul 2026: Trabajo and Economía agree to delay approval to September; the last Council of Ministers before summer was devoted to housing — [RegistraHora](https://www.registrahora.es/noticias/registro-horario-digital-aplazado-septiembre-2026), citing La Vanguardia and Valencia Plaza; headline "Yolanda Díaz suma otro revés: aparca registro horario hasta septiembre" — [El Debate, 24 Jul 2026](https://www.eldebate.com/economia/20260724/yolanda-diaz-suma-otro-reves-aparca-registro-horario-hasta-septiembre_443170.html) (search summary only).
- Consejo de Estado opinion, as reported:
  - Conclusion: "no procede aprobar el real decreto proyectado" (the draft royal decree should not be approved).
  - Scale and cost: the digital register would affect **1.35 million companies** at **€867M a year**. The claim of no significant economic impact "no se corresponde con la realidad empresarial" (does not match business reality).
  - Sectors: a single digital system is unfeasible for porteros de fincas (building caretakers), maquinistas (train drivers) and hostelería; it recommends a long transition period.

  - Legal basis: it says the draft encroaches on matters reserved to law.
  - Reactions: Economía and Función Pública filed critical reports; Trabajo called the opinion "incomprensible".
  - Source: [El Español, 23 Mar 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/invertia/economia/empleo/20260323/consejo-critica-tumba-registro-horario-yolanda-diaz/1003744181041_0.html).
- Yolanda Díaz said the decree would go ahead "aunque sea lo último que haga" (even if it is the last thing I do) — [Noticias de Navarra, 25 Mar 2026](https://www.noticiasdenavarra.com/economia/2026/03/25/diaz-asegura-nuevo-registro-horario-reforma-10867143.html) (search summary only). On 9 Sep 2026 she said it would be approved "con carácter inmediato" (immediately) once the legal changes were made, citing an agreement with Economía and the PSOE, and claimed 2.5 million unpaid overtime hours a week — [Onda Vasca, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.ondavasca.com/trabajo-asegura-que-el-registro-horario-se-aprobara-con-caracter-inmediato-tras-incorporar-cambios-juridicos/).
- Transition period under negotiation: Economía proposed 1 year for pymes, Trabajo 6 months. The draft would enter into force 20 days after BOE publication — [RegistraHora](https://www.registrahora.es/noticias/registro-horario-digital-aplazado-septiembre-2026); [ControlHorarioLegal](https://controlhorariolegal.com/normativa-fichaje-2026).
- Status on 23 Oct 2026: "no se ha aprobado ni publicado en el BOE" (not approved or published in the BOE). The 6, 13 and 20 Oct Council of Ministers meetings did not include it. Because it is a regulation, calling the 29-N election does not block it — [ControlHorarioLegal, rev. 23 Oct 2026](https://controlhorariolegal.com/normativa-fichaje-2026). A headline reads "La reforma del registro horario aún podría aprobarse antes de las elecciones del 29N" — [Demócrata](https://www.democrata.es/economia/la-reforma-del-registro-horario-aun-podria-aprobarse-antes-de-las-elecciones-del-29n/) (page returned 403; headline via search).

- Enforcement history:
  - 2019: €999,357 in registro fines from 564 infractions.
  - 2023: about €3M (provisional), with 8,355 actions, 2,083 requirements and 1,660 infractions — [El Independiente, 1 Apr 2024](https://www.elindependiente.com/economia/2024/04/01/multas-registro-jornada-horario-3-millones-euros/amp/).
  - 2024, registro fines: €2.99M (vs €3.06M in 2023), 1,869 infractions, 9,097 actions, 21,649 workers affected.
  - 2024, working time and overtime fines: €17.2M (vs €15.5M), 13,681 infractions, 38,686 actions — [El Derecho/Europa Press, 7 Feb 2025](https://elderecho.com/inspeccion-de-trabajo-elevo-un-9-las-multas-por-infracciones-por-tiempo-de-trabajo-en-2024).
  - 2025, working time: 14,728 infractions, 252,500 workers affected, fines of €15.7M (vs €5.3M in 2018) — [Finantres, 25 Aug 2026](https://finantresnoticias.com/negocios-y-autonomos/multas-por-incumplir-horario-se-triplican-deben-revisar-ahora-pymes/). This is single-source, unsourced within the article and not reconciled with the 2024 €17.2M figure; it may use a different scope.
- Euskadi, 2025: 1,490 working-time inspections, 417 infractions and 401 requirements; "más de un 60 % de irregularidades" (more than 60% of inspections found irregularities), a share similar to recent years — [Onda Vasca, 16 Feb 2026](https://www.ondavasca.com/trabajo-sigue-detectando-un-60-de-anomalias-en-las-inspecciones-por-horarios-de-trabajo-en-euskadi/).
- Unpaid overtime: in Q4 2023 (EPA labour force survey), 3.8M paid and 2.4M unpaid overtime hours a week — [El Independiente, 1 Apr 2024](https://www.elindependiente.com/economia/2024/04/01/multas-registro-jornada-horario-3-millones-euros/amp/); in Sep 2026 Díaz put unpaid hours at 2.5M a week — [Onda Vasca, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.ondavasca.com/trabajo-asegura-que-el-registro-horario-se-aprobara-con-caracter-inmediato-tras-incorporar-cambios-juridicos/).
- STS 372/2026 (15 Apr 2026):
  - A missing registro does not by itself prove all the overtime claimed.
  - Where there is no fixed, known schedule and no record, the worker's alleged hours can be taken as true if they are concrete and not absurd.
  - Where there is a fixed schedule, the worker must at least provide indications.
  - The case: a butcher's-shop assistant claimed €9,703.18 for 2021 and €2,012.19 for 2022, and the employer kept no continuous record.
  - Source: [RRHH Digital, 13 May 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/794593/el-tribunal-supremo-pone-limites-a-las-reclamaciones-de-horas-extra/).
- Tools SMEs use and what they cost:
  - Woffu Lite: "Desde 1,5€" per user per month, "Para autónomos o microempresas hasta 10 empleados", with app/PC clock-in and IP/geolocation restrictions — [Woffu pricing, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.woffu.com/es/precios).
  - Sesame HR: €4.75–8.00 per employee per month on annual billing (the comparison table shows €5.25–9.00), with control horario in every plan and a **minimum contract of 15 employees** — [Sesame pricing](https://www.sesamehr.es/precios/).
  - Factorial: "Desde 5€ al mes por persona" (the page also says from €5.5) — [Factorial ES pricing](https://factorial.es/pricing).
  - Timu (Nimbo Software): launched in July 2026 for pymes and autónomos, priced by number of employees, with optional NFC tablets for kiosk mode — [RRHH Digital, 4 Jul 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/pymes/798970/la-nueva-plataforma-que-prepara-a-pymes-y-autonomos-para-la-inminente-normativa-de-control-horario/).
- Complaint: a Factorial review (30 Sep 2026) says the app has failures "cada ciertos días" (every few days) and that clock-ins "tarda en registrarlos" (take a long time to register) — [Trustpilot Factorial](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/factorialhr.com).
- ASNALA (labour lawyers' association) president: companies need "periodos de transición razonables" (reasonable transition periods), not very short deadlines that overwhelm advisers and HR departments — [Law&Trends, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/abogacia/ana-gomez-presidenta-de-asnala-es-necesario-recuperar-el-dialogo-social-para-resolver-los-grandes-problemas-del-1.html).

### Inferences
- €867M a year spread over 1.35M companies averages about €640 per company per year (my arithmetic on the Consejo de Estado figures). That is the implied annual spend on compliant digital time-keeping if the mandate passes.
- Paper is legal and the fine is per infraction (maximum €7,500), so many micro firms rationally underinvest until something triggers them: an ITSS visit, an overtime lawsuit (where STS 372/2026 makes the record decisive) or the decree's approval.
- Generic fichaje apps fit poorly where the Consejo de Estado sees the decree as unworkable: building caretakers, hospitality, train drivers, plus mobile/field and split-shift staff. Vertical variants are a possible product angle.
- If the decree is approved in Oct–Nov 2026 with a 6–12-month transition for pymes, the effective deadline for small firms would fall around mid-to-late 2027.

### Gaps
- The ITSS 2025 annual report (published Oct 2026) was available only through a press summary, with no registro-specific breakdown.
- I found no survey quantifying the share of SMEs still on paper or Excel.
- The post-Consejo de Estado final text, and the agreed transition period, are not public.

## 2. Working-time and cost changes: the 37.5-hour bill and follow-up, SMI 2025/2026, contribution increases (MEI, solidarity quota), impact on micro-businesses

### Takeaway
The 37.5-hour bill died on 10 Sep 2025 (178–170 vote for the PP, Vox and Junts amendments to reject it outright, plus UPN). The legal maximum stays at 40 hours, and the follow-up was the time-recording decree. Labour costs nonetheless keep rising:
- **SMI 2026: €1,221 × 14 payments** (+3.1%, about 2.5M beneficiaries), set by RD 126/2026 on 18 Feb 2026, retroactive to 1 Jan and agreed with unions but not employers. The SMI is up 66% since 2018.
- **Contributions:** the MEI pension levy (Mecanismo de Equidad Intergeneracional) reaches 0.9%, the maximum contribution base rises 3.9% to €5,101.20, and the solidarity quota (a levy on pay above the maximum base) applies.
- **Overall labour cost:** INE puts it up 4.0% year on year in Q2 2026, with Social Security contributions up 4.2%.

### Cited Findings
- 37.5 hours: Congress passed the PP, Vox and Junts amendments to reject the bill outright, 178–170, with UPN also in favour. The text returned to the Government, which "tendrá que empezar de nuevo" (will have to start over), and Díaz said she would try again — [Periódico de Ibiza, 11 Sep 2025](https://www.periodicodeibiza.es/noticias/nacional/2025/09/11/2467647/congreso-tumba-reduccion-jornada-laboral-prosperar-veto-vox-junts.html). The vote was on 10 Sep 2025, the maximum stays at 40 hours a week and the Government could relaunch the bill in 2026 — [Grupo 2000, 17 Sep 2025](https://www.grupo2000.es/ayudas-de-hasta-150-000-euros-por-reducir-la-jornada-laboral-a-4-dias/). Trabajo signalled the regulatory route and a public consultation on the time-recording decree — [Genbeta](https://www.genbeta.com/laboral/congreso-tumba-reduccion-jornada-laboral-a-37-5-horas-esto-que-ocurrira-a-partir-ahora) (search summary only).
- SMI 2026 (RD 126/2026, 18 Feb, BOE 19 Feb 2026):
  - €1,221 a month × 14 payments, €40.70 a day, €17,094 a year.
  - +€37 a month and +€518 a year.
  - Temporary contracts under 120 days: at least €57.82 per working day; domestic workers: €9.55 an hour.
  - Retroactive from 1 Jan 2026, so the January payroll had to be regularised.
  - +66% since 2018 (from €735.90).
  - Sources: [PwC Periscopio, 19 Feb 2026](https://periscopiofiscalylegal.pwc.es/se-fija-la-cuantia-del-smi-el-problema-ahora-es-como-aplicarlo/); [LegalToday, 19 Feb 2026](https://www.legaltoday.com/actualidad-juridica/noticias-de-derecho/el-boe-publica-el-smi-para-2026-que-se-establece-en-1-221-euros-sin-tributacion-2026-02-19/) (search summary).
- Absorción y compensación (offsetting the SMI rise against existing pay supplements):
  - The comparison is made on an annual basis against at least €17,094.
  - Compensable income is what workers received under the rules in force on 20 Feb 2026.
  - The government–union agreement includes a specific regulation to stop absorption of certain supplements. It is not in RD 126/2026 and was expected in a separate decree-law that had not been approved as of Feb 2026.
  - The late approval and a lapsed 2025 extension decree-law reduced legal certainty.
  - Source: [PwC, 19 Feb 2026](https://periscopiofiscalylegal.pwc.es/se-fija-la-cuantia-del-smi-el-problema-ahora-es-como-aplicarlo/).
- The SMI was agreed with CCOO and UGT "pero sin la patronal" (but without the employers). About 2.5 million workers benefit, most of them in Andalucía, Madrid, Comunidad Valenciana and Cataluña — [Consejo General de Gestores Administrativos, Novedades destacadas 91, Feb 2026](https://www.consejogestores.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Novedades-destacadas-91.pdf).
- ASNALA president (Oct 2026): pymes may struggle to absorb SMI rises, and she opposes barring companies from offsetting them against existing supplements — [Law&Trends, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/abogacia/ana-gomez-presidenta-de-asnala-es-necesario-recuperar-el-dialogo-social-para-resolver-los-grandes-problemas-del-1.html).
- 2026 contribution bases and rates (official page, marked "being updated", which points to Orden PJC/297/2026):
  - Maximum base €5,101.20 a month; minimum base €1,989.30 for group 1 and €1,424.40 for groups 4–7.
  - Common contingencies: 23.60% employer + 4.70% worker.
  - Unemployment: 5.50% + 1.55% on permanent contracts; 6.70% + 1.60% on fixed-term contracts.
  - FOGASA (wage guarantee fund) 0.20%; vocational training 0.60% + 0.10%.
  - Additional contribution of **€33.62** when a contract of 30 days or less ends.
  - Source: [Seguridad Social, bases y tipos 2026](https://www.seg-social.es/wps/portal/wss/internet/Trabajadores/CotizacionRecaudacionTrabajadores/36537).
- **Conflict on the MEI:** that official page still shows the MEI at 0.80% (0.67/0.13). Two secondary sources give 0.90% for 2026 (0.75% employer, 0.15% worker) — [CopilotGestoria, 28 Mar 2026](https://copilotgestoria.com/blog/cuota-solidaridad-2026-calcular-nomina-tramos-ejemplos-guia-gestorias); [Gestoria247, 7 Sep 2026](https://gestoria247.com/blog/cuanto-cuesta-un-trabajador-a-la-empresa-2026). The official page appears not yet updated.
- Solidarity quota 2026 (charged on pay above the maximum base):
  - Rates by band: 1.15% on the first €510.12 above the base, 1.25% on €510.13–2,550.60 and 1.46% beyond €2,550.60 (2025: 1.07%, 1.17%, 1.37%).
  - The maximum base rose 3.9%.
  - Example: an €8,500-a-month executive costs the employer an extra €74.73 a month (MEI €38.26 plus solidarity quota €36.47).
  - Source: [CopilotGestoria, 28 Mar 2026](https://copilotgestoria.com/blog/cuota-solidaridad-2026-calcular-nomina-tramos-ejemplos-guia-gestorias) (single secondary source, vendor).
- Cost of an SMI worker in 2026 (permanent office job): employer contributions of 32.15% turn €17,094 gross into €5,495.72 of employer Social Security, a **total of €22,589.72 a year** (about €1,882 a month) — [Gestoria247, 7 Sep 2026](https://gestoria247.com/blog/cuanto-cuesta-un-trabajador-a-la-empresa-2026).
- INE quarterly labour cost survey (ETCL), Q2 2026:
  - Labour cost per worker per month €3,388.15 (+4.0%); wage cost €2,512.68 (+4.0%); other costs €875.47 (+4.2%, with Social Security contributions up 4.2%).
  - €26.18 per hour actually worked (+4.8%).
  - Among establishments with no vacancies, 5.8% cite high hiring costs.
  - Source: [INE, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/es/ETCL2T26.htm).

### Inferences
- SMI 2025 was €1,184 a month × 14 (derived as €1,221 − €37).
- For micro employers in low-wage sectors, the pain is mostly cost growth (SMI, MEI and contributions rising roughly 4% a year). The administrative pain is narrower: absorption calculations, retroactive regularisation and rate updates.
- The solidarity quota only touches salaries above €5,101.20 a month, so it is marginal for most micro firms (see Noise).
- With the Cortes dissolved, a new 37.5-hour attempt is unlikely before 2027.

### Gaps
- I could not verify whether the decree-law banning absorption of supplements was approved by Oct 2026.
- I found no verified data on micro-business closures or hiring freezes attributable to the SMI in 2025–26.
- I did not fetch Orden PJC/297/2026 itself.

## 3. Absenteeism (incapacidad temporal): rates, costs to SMEs, management burden with mutuas, proposed reforms

### Takeaway
Absenteeism reached a record 7.6% of agreed hours in 2025, of which temporary incapacity (incapacidad temporal, IT) was 5.95%, at a total cost of €59.1bn. Of the €33.3bn cost of IT for common contingencies, companies bore **€16.1bn directly**. About **1.41M workers** missed whole days. Mental-health leave has grown 111% in five years. Regional highs are in País Vasco, Canarias and Cantabria (9.6%), while Cataluña, Madrid and Andalucía bear the largest absolute costs. The only enacted changes found are Ley 2/2025 and a 2025–2028 INSS–INGESA control agreement. I found no evidence that a "flexible/partial IT" reform has been adopted.

### Cited Findings
- XV Adecco absenteeism report (14 Jul 2026):
  - Headline: the 2025 rate was 7.6% of agreed hours (+0.34 pp), a series record; IT is about 78% of absenteeism, with an IT rate of 5.95%.
  - Cost: €59,109M in 2025 (+11.7%), nearly double the €30,171M of 2019.
  - Regional rates: País Vasco, Canarias and Cantabria 9.6%; the lowest are Baleares 6.2%, Madrid 6.6% and La Rioja 6.7%.
  - Regional cost: Cataluña €11,557M, Madrid €10,290M, Andalucía €7,410M.
  - Sectors: industry 8.18%, services 7.62%, construction 6.25%.
  - Branches: postal 13.0%, building services and gardening 12.5%, gambling 12.2%; lowest are employment agencies 3.0%, legal/accounting 3.7% and IT consulting 4.0%.
  - Mental health: second cause of IT by number of episodes and first by duration; mental-health leave lasts 2–4 times longer than leave for physical causes and has grown 111% in five years.
  - Q1 2026: 7.6% overall, with IT at 5.9%.
  - Causes cited: an ageing workforce, saturated primary care and waiting lists, physically demanding jobs, and "una coordinación insuficiente entre sistema sanitario, mutuas y empresas" (insufficient coordination between health system, mutuas and companies).
  - Legal developments cited: Ley 2/2025 and the INSS-INGESA agreement 2025–2028.
  - Sources: [Adecco Group press release, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.adeccogroup.com/-/media/project/adecco-group/adeccogroup/spain-content/2026-press-releases/2026-07-14-ndp-xv-informe-anual-adecco-empresa-saludable-gestion-absentismo.pdf); [El Derecho/Europa Press, 14 Jul 2026](https://elderecho.com/el-absentismo-laboral-alcanza-un-maximo-historico-del-76-en-2025).
- 2025 IT figures from a debate organised by the Colegio de Graduados Sociales de Madrid and Unión de Mutuas:
  - Volume: 9.1M IT processes for common contingencies, plus 660,952 for work-related contingencies.
  - Cost: **€33,280M**, of which **€16,115M was borne directly by companies**.
  - Absence: 1,407,819 workers absent whole days (+7.73% vs 2024, +75.6% vs 2018).
  - Cataluña: 221,722 workers absent; IT cost €6,279M, of which companies paid €3,074M.
  - AMAT (the mutuas' association) and CEOE have started following up repeat claimants.
  - Monitoring workers on sick leave with private investigators requires a legitimate interest and proportionality (Ley 5/2014); "No se pueden hacer investigaciones prospectivas ni investigar a todo el mundo" (no fishing expeditions; you cannot investigate everyone).
  - Source: [Law&Trends, upd. 9 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/laboral/las-empresas-necesitan-herramientas-para-gestionar-mejor-sus-bajas-medicas-y-reducir-sus-niveles-de-absentismo-1.html) (single source; the article does not name the underlying dataset).
- ASNALA president: leave periods lengthen because of resource shortages and delays in medical treatment, and "la postura de los sindicatos de negar o minimizar el problema del absentismo no ayuda" (unions' stance of denying or minimising absenteeism does not help) — [Law&Trends, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/abogacia/ana-gomez-presidenta-de-asnala-es-necesario-recuperar-el-dialogo-social-para-resolver-los-grandes-problemas-del-1.html).
- A further article on absenteeism repeats the health-system saturation diagnosis and mentions no proposals on mutuas issuing discharges (altas), progressive return or partial IT — [RRHH Digital, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/mercado-laboral/799589/entre-el-colapso-sanitario-y-la-cuenta-de-resultados-que-hay-detras-de-las-cifras-de-absentismo/).
- Sector overlap: absenteeism in automotive reaches 8.9% at €720M a year — [RRHH Digital headline, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/mercado-laboral/798359/el-absentismo-laboral-en-la-automocion-escala-al-89-y-cuesta-720-millones-anuales/) (headline only; the industry researcher covers this).

### Inferences
- Companies carry about 48% of IT cost directly (€16.1bn of €33.3bn), mainly through statutory employer-paid days, convenio "complementos de IT" (supplements topping up sick pay) and replacement staff. The statutory split (employer pays days 4–15 under art. 173 LGSS) was not re-verified in this session.
- At a 5.95% IT rate, a 10-person firm has on average about 0.6 FTE off at any moment. For micro firms that is a disproportionate operational shock (no cover) as well as a cost.
- The software-addressable part is management: tracking leave, communication with the mutua, return-to-work planning, convenio supplement calculation and absence analytics. The root causes (health-system waits, ageing) are not software-solvable.

### Gaps
- I found no verified status (proposal, draft or rejected) for "reincorporación progresiva / IT flexible" in 2025–26.
- I found no SME-specific absenteeism rate, and no data on the cost to micro firms of administering IT supplements.

## 4. Hiring and talent shortages: vacancies by sector and city, hard-to-fill roles, housing as a hiring barrier, and friction hiring foreign workers

### Takeaway
INE counted **151,736 vacancies in Q2 2026**, 87% of them in services and concentrated in Cataluña, Madrid and Andalucía. The sharpest shortages are in technical trades: maintenance, refrigeration, welding, HGV/ADR drivers and CNC machinists. Hiring foreign workers is constrained by immigration-office (extranjería) capacity:
- The new Reglamento (RD 1155/2024, in force around 20 May 2025) raised workload 25% at the Zaragoza office, where files average 6-month delays.
- The 2026 extraordinary regularisation (RD 316/2026) drew **more than 1.17M applications**.
- The ITSS detected 18,655 foreign workers without authorisation in 2025 and proposed €187.7M in fines.

I could not evidence housing as a hiring barrier in this session (see Gaps).

### Cited Findings
- INE ETCL, Q2 2026:
  - 151,736 vacancies (+1,997 year on year).
  - By sector: services 132,555 (87.4%), construction 11,958 (7.9%), industry 7,224 (4.8%).
  - By region: most in Cataluña, Madrid and Andalucía; fewest in La Rioja, Extremadura and Cantabria.
  - Among establishments with no vacancies, 90.6% say they need no extra worker and 5.8% cite high hiring costs.
  - Source: [INE, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/es/ETCL2T26.htm).
- Hard-to-fill technical roles (Tecnio with Randstad):
  - Maintenance technicians/electromechanics account for more than 30% of technical vacancies identified.
  - Other in-demand roles: refrigeration technicians (heat waves), welders (TIG, MIG/MAG), mechanics, electricians/panel builders, drivers with C, C+E or ADR licences ("acusada escasez", marked shortage) and CNC machinists (coverage "especialmente compleja", especially hard).
  - Demand for technical training is up 20% in 2026.
  - Source: [RRHH Digital, 15 Aug 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/mercado-laboral/800825/los-siete-perfiles-tecnicos-mas-buscados-este-verano/).
- "El sector del transporte busca 1.000 chóferes" (transport seeks 1,000 drivers) — [RRHH Digital, 31 Aug 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/mercado-laboral/801076/el-sector-del-transporte-busca-1-000-choferes/) (headline only; the transport researcher covers this).
- Graduate employability perception (CEU/Adecco survey of more than 600 companies): software, computer engineering and renewable-energy engineering lead; "Entre el 22% y el 25% de los graduados universitarios desempeña funciones no alineadas con su formación" (22–25% of graduates work in jobs unrelated to their degree) — [RRHH Digital, 5 Jun 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/mercado-laboral/797217/que-buscan-las-empresas-en-2026-informatica-software-y-energias-renovables-lideran-la-empleabilidad/).
- RD 1155/2024 (BOE 20 Nov 2024; in force 6 months after publication, so about 20 May 2025):
  - Arraigo routes (residence permits for people already in Spain): segunda oportunidad, sociolaboral, social, socioformativo and familiar.
  - Arraigo sociolaboral requires a job contract "en las mismas condiciones" (on the same terms) as an initial work permit.
  - Arraigo holders are authorised to work, and the required residence period is cut to 2 years.
  - GECCO (collective hiring in the country of origin) covers batches of at least 10 permits, only for people outside Spain.
  - Source: [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2024/11/19/1155).
- Zaragoza immigration office:
  - Staffing: 53 staff, 5 of them on long-term leave with no replacement.
  - Workload: about 34,000 files a year, up 25% since the new reglamento took effect in May; "una demora media de seis meses" (an average delay of six months).
  - Outlook: more than 15,000 potential regularisation applicants in Zaragoza; CSIF says about 10 more staff are needed.
  - Source: [CSIF Aragón, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.csif.es/es/noticia/portada/aragon/articulo/85294).
- La Rioja: more than 1,000 files pending, some since July 2025 — [Nuevecuatrouno, 9 Mar 2026](https://nuevecuatrouno.com/2026/03/09/csif-rioja-colapso-extranjeria-mil-expedientes-pendientes-tramitacion/) (search summary; the page returned 403).
- RD 316/2026 extraordinary regularisation:
  - Announced 27 Jan 2026. Online applications opened 16 Apr 2026 and in-person ones 20 Apr; the deadline was 30 Jun 2026.
  - Requirements: in Spain before 31 Dec 2025, at least 5 months' proven stay, no criminal record; people with no job or dependent children need a vulnerability report.
  - The Government expected about 500,000 beneficiaries.
  - In Tarragona there were queues, with 786 people signing up in two days for the vulnerability report, and the Catalan bar council warned of difficulty getting appointments.
  - Source: [Diari de Tarragona, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.diaridetarragona.com/tarragona/257385/posible-invisibilidad-tarragona-vuelca-regularizacion-extraordinaria_amp.html).
- Regularisation outcome, ICAM (Madrid Bar) congress:
  - "More than 1.17 million" applications nationwide (Secretary of State Pilar Cancela); Madrid received 202,424.

  - 500 staff were added across 52 offices, and residence cards still need two appointments.
  - Source: [Law&Trends, upd. 4 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/abogacia/los-expertos-avalan-a-una-abogacia-leal-pero-firme-que-ha-ayudado-a-regularizar-a-mas-de-1-2-millones-de-inmigrantes-1.html).
- ITSS 2025: 18,655 foreign workers without authorisation (+53.1%), with proposed sanctions of €187.7M (+51.1%) — [El Derecho/Europa Press, 7 Oct 2026](https://elderecho.com/?p=489934).

### Inferences
- That works out at about €10,000 of proposed fines per unauthorised worker detected (€187.7M ÷ 18,655, my arithmetic). Checking work authorisation, and tracking expiry and renewal dates for a newly regularised workforce, is a real employer obligation with a high cost of error.
- The regularisation should widen the legal labour pool in 2026–27 for agriculture, hospitality, care and construction. Card issuance, renewals and employer paperwork (contracts on SMI terms for arraigo sociolaboral) create document-tracking work for gestorías and SMEs.
- The binding constraint on foreign hiring is public-office capacity, which software cannot fix. Software can help with case preparation, status tracking and permit-expiry alerts.

### Gaps
- **Housing as a hiring barrier (Madrid, Barcelona, Baleares, Málaga):** not evidenced in this session because the search budget ran out. The hospitality and city-conditions researchers may hold this evidence. I would not treat it as disproven.
- I did not retrieve the latest SEPE catalogue of hard-to-fill occupations (Catálogo de Ocupaciones de Difícil Cobertura) or the GECCO 2026 order.
- INE vacancy data is regional only; I found no vacancy data by city.
- I found no figures on how many regularisation files have been resolved.

## 5. Dismissals and labour lawsuits: CGPJ volumes, SMAC conciliation, costs, causes, recent rulings and reforms

### Takeaway
Dismissal lawsuits hit a **record 45,230 in Q2 2026 (+11.2%; the CGPJ series starts in 2007)**, after 42,572 in Q1 2026 (+3.5%), while wage claims run at 32–35k a quarter. The social courts are badly congested: Madrid trials are being set for 2029–2030 and Barcelona citations reach 2030. 2024–2026 case law tightened procedure:
- a hearing (audiencia previa) before disciplinary dismissals;
- nullity for retaliatory dismissals even before any formal complaint;
- Social Security contributions on payment in lieu of notice since July 2026.

At the same time, the Supreme Court refused extra compensation beyond the statutory tariff (STS 1350/2024; STS 736/2025).

My inference: the court-structure reform is in operation.

A defect can make the dismissal improcedente (unfair); "ya no basta con estudiar únicamente la carta de despido" (it is no longer enough to look only at the dismissal letter) — [Law&Trends, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/laboral/un-tramite-que-puede-cambiarlo-todo-cuando-un-despido-disciplinario-puede-ser-declarado-improcedente-1.html). The article does not give the ruling number. It is widely cited as STS of 18 Nov 2024, but I did not verify that in this session.
- Retaliation: STS 526/2026 (23 Jun 2026) declared null a dismissal six days after the worker objected to working Saturdays, under the garantía de indemnidad (protection against retaliation), even though no formal claim had been filed — [RRHH Digital, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/799602/el-supremo-refuerza-la-proteccion-frente-al-despido-represalia-incluso-antes-de-una-reclamacion-formal/).
- Notice pay: since Boletín RED 8/2026 (14 Jul 2026), the TGSS (Social Security treasury) treats payment in lieu of the 15-day notice in objective dismissals as salary that must be included in the contribution base. This requires a supplementary settlement (liquidación complementaria) and a renamed CRA code 0054, and makes immediate dismissals more expensive — [RRHH Digital, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/799952/la-tgss-cambia-el-criterio-la-compensacion-por-falta-de-preaviso-en-el-despido-objetivo-ya-cotiza/).
- Other 2026 rulings — [Grant Thornton, Jun 2026](https://www.grantthornton.es/contentassets/4730b6c010d04ea29770da8a29bc5b94/labour-newsjunio-retos-aun-pendientes-y-nuevos-limites-en-materia-laboralgrant-thornton.pdf):
  - The Supreme Court (May 2026) held that back pay for the period of the dismissal dispute (salarios de tramitación) is liquidated gross, but payment must apply income-tax (IRPF) withholding and the worker's Social Security share.
  - STS 491/2026 (20 May 2026): contract clauses allowing the employer to change working hours unilaterally are null; the art. 41 ET procedure for substantial modifications must be used.
- ITSS 2025: proposed sanctions of €459,587 for improper terminations during probation periods — [El Derecho/Europa Press, 7 Oct 2026](https://elderecho.com/?p=489934).
- Settlements: practical guidance articles on the validity of agreements signed on the day of dismissal (8 Oct 2026) and on settlement pacts (30 Sep 2026) — [RRHH Digital legal section](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/803062/es-valido-un-acuerdo-firmado-con-el-trabajador-el-mismo-dia-del-despido/) (headlines only).
- SMAC (pre-trial conciliation service) volumes: the Ministry publishes monthly MAC statistics, e.g. the Jan–Sep 2025 PDF. A search summary suggested about 350k individual conciliations in Jan–Sep 2025, about 61% of them dismissals, but the summary was internally inconsistent and the PDF returned HTTP 503, so **unverified** — [MITES MAC 09/25](https://www.mites.gob.es/estadisticas/Mac/mac25seppublicacion/mac_09_25.pdf).

### Inferences
- H1 2026 had 87,802 dismissal claims vs 81,796 in H1 2025 (+7.3%). H1 wage claims were 67,251 vs 70,030 (−4.0%). My arithmetic on CGPJ figures.
- Dismissal costs are capped by the tariff, but procedural risk has risen (audiencia previa, nullity for retaliation, contribution coding). For owner-managed SMEs without HR staff, the value lies in getting the process and paperwork right before the dismissal.
- Trials 3–4 years out raise uncertainty and contingent liabilities, especially in Madrid and Barcelona. Delay also raises settlement pressure at the SMAC stage.

### Gaps
- I found no verified data on the SME share of claims, average settlements, typical graduado social or lawyer fees, or how often claims cite time records, overtime or fijos discontinuos.
- I did not verify the status of the European Committee of Social Rights decisions or of any Government plan to reform dismissal compensation.
- The partial State payment of long-running salarios de tramitación (art. 116 LRJS) was not re-verified here.

## 6. Size-triggered and universal HR obligations: equality plans, pay register, harassment and LGTBI protocols, whistleblowing, disconnection, PRL, the Pay Transparency Directive, and RD 723/2026

### Takeaway
**From the first employee:**
- registro de jornada;
- registro retributivo (pay register, RD 902/2020);
- procedures to prevent and handle complaints of sexual harassment (LO 10/2022, art. 12);
- an occupational risk prevention (PRL) set-up (RD 39/1997);
- **since 5 Oct 2026, the RD 723/2026 written-information duties**, a new universal obligation.

**From 50 or more employees:**
- equality plan and pay audit;
- LGTBI measures and protocol (RD 1026/2024, more than 50 employees);
- internal whistleblowing channel (Ley 2/2023, 50+).

The EU Pay Transparency Directive deadline (7 Jun 2026) was missed. A draft royal decree amending RD 902/2020 went to consultation in Aug 2026 and, per one source, would apply from 50 employees (stricter than the Directive's 100). It is not in force.

### Cited Findings
- **RD 723/2026** (9 Sep 2026; BOE 15 Sep 2026; in force 5 Oct 2026):
  - Purpose and scope: transposes Directive 2019/1152 on transparent and predictable working conditions and applies to all employers under the Estatuto de los Trabajadores (ET). The general information chapter applies to relationships lasting more than 4 weeks.
  - When: information must be given before the job starts (art. 7.1); changes must be notified at the latest "el día en que el cambio surta efecto" (the day the change takes effect; art. 7.3).
  - Content (art. 3.2):
    - parties, workplace and remote-work centre;
    - duties and professional group;
    - the justification for a temporary contract;
    - pay items, working time, probation and training;
    - **the existence of algorithmic or automated decision systems and their rules (art. 3.2.k)**;
    - equality plan, harassment protocol and LGTBI measures;
    - termination procedure and notice;
    - applicable convenios, Social Security and pension plans.
  - Format: paper or electronic, with proof of delivery.
  - Existing staff: workers can request the information and must receive it "en el plazo de treinta días hábiles" (within 30 working days).
  - The SEPE must publish a model document within 20 days. The decree repeals RD 1659/1998 and its text sets no sanctions.
  - Sources: [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2026/09/09/723); [RRHH Digital, 18 Sep 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/802273/las-empresas-tendran-que-informar-con-mas-detalle-a-sus-empleados-sobre-sus-condiciones-de-trabajo-desde-octubre/).
- The Barcelona graduados sociales college warns of more complaints to the Labour Inspectorate and more court congestion. According to the Catalan ITSS, the European Commission took Spain to the Court of Justice of the EU for late transposition (due 2022), seeking a lump sum of at least €7.54M plus €266,772 a day — [Law&Trends, upd. 2 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/laboral/el-colegio-de-graduados-sociales-de-barcelona-girona-y-lleida-alerta-del-impacto-del-rd-723-2026-en-la-gestion-y-el-1.html) (single source).
- **RD 1026/2024** (BOE 9 Oct 2024; in force 10 Oct 2024):
  - Applies to companies with more than 50 workers; smaller ones may adopt it voluntarily.
  - The negotiating commission had to be set up within 3 months, or 6 months where there are no worker representatives.
  - If no agreement is reached within 3 months, the decree's own measures apply.
  - Seven minimum measures (Annex I) plus a mandatory LGTBI harassment and violence protocol (Annex II), which can be met by extending the general harassment protocol.
  - Source: [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2024/10/08/1026).
- ITSS LGTBI campaign 2025: 196 actions and 101 requirements — [El Derecho/Europa Press, 7 Oct 2026](https://elderecho.com/?p=489934).
- **LO 10/2022 art. 12:** "Las empresas deberán promover condiciones de trabajo que eviten la comisión de delitos" (companies must promote working conditions that prevent these offences) and other conduct against sexual freedom, including in the digital sphere. They must also "arbitrar procedimientos específicos para su prevención" (set up specific prevention procedures) and complaint channels — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/lo/2022/09/06/10).
- **Ley 2/2023 (whistleblowing):**
  - Private employers with 50 or more workers must have an internal reporting system; entities with 249 or fewer had until 1 Dec 2023, and those with 50–249 may share resources.
  - The responsible person (Responsable del Sistema) must be notified to the authority within 10 business days.
  - Fines for legal persons: up to €100,000 (minor), €100,001–600,000 (serious), €600,001–1,000,000 (very serious).
  - The Autoridad Independiente de Protección del Informante (AAI, the independent whistleblower-protection authority) holds sanctioning power.
  - Source: [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2023/02/20/2).
- **Equality plans and REGCON:** TS 519/2026 (28 May 2026), following STS 461/2025 (27 May 2025), holds that REGCON (the Ministry's register of collective agreements and equality plans) may only run formal checks. It must register plans and challenge them in court instead of refusing registration, and late adaptation does not void a registered plan — [Grant Thornton, Jun 2026](https://www.grantthornton.es/contentassets/4730b6c010d04ea29770da8a29bc5b94/labour-newsjunio-retos-aun-pendientes-y-nuevos-limites-en-materia-laboralgrant-thornton.pdf).
- SME equality survey (Hiscox, 2025 data):
  - 52% of SMEs apply equal-opportunity policies (42% in 2024), and 36% apply none.
  - Micro firms 51% vs larger pymes 61.5%.
  - The LGTBI harassment protocol is reportedly in place at 75% of companies, with 23% without one; the base is unclear and the figure seems high for firms of all sizes.
  - Source: [RRHH Digital, 1 Mar 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/pymes/790309/por-primera-vez-mas-de-la-mitad-de-las-pymes-espanolas-aplica-politicas-de-igualdad/).
- **Pay Transparency Directive 2023/970**:
  - Deadline missed: the 7 Jun 2026 deadline passed and Spain "ha incumplido el plazo" (has missed it); only the prior consultation had been opened — [Grant Thornton, Jun 2026](https://www.grantthornton.es/contentassets/4730b6c010d04ea29770da8a29bc5b94/labour-newsjunio-retos-aun-pendientes-y-nuevos-limites-en-materia-laboralgrant-thornton.pdf).
  - Ministry response: "de manera urgente, culminaremos el texto en los próximos días o semanas" (we will urgently finish the text in the coming days or weeks); 77% of workers are unaware of the Directive and more than a third of companies admit they are not ready — [RRHH Digital, 10 Jun 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/797519/la-directiva-de-transparencia-retributiva-el-plazo-vencio-ayer-y-espana-aun-no-la-ha-traspuesto/).
  - On hold: salary disclosure before interview, the ban on asking about pay history, and gap reports. The Directive's calendar still applies: companies with more than 150 staff file a first report before 7 Jun 2027, and those with more than 250 report annually from 2027. It is unclear whether the instrument will be a decree-law or a lower-rank norm, and a lower rank would limit sanctions — [Que.es, 9 Jun 2026](https://www.que.es/2026/06/09/transparencia-salarial-ofertas-pospuesta/).
  - Consultations: a first one ran 24 Apr–8 May 2026, and a second on a draft royal decree amending RD 902/2020 ran 4–24 Aug 2026.
  - Draft contents: integrated into the existing pay audit and equality-plan framework; duties **from 50 employees** (vs the Directive's 100); the 5% gap trigger would not act as a separate gateway; burden of proof on the employer.
  - Status: nothing published in the BOE and no approval date; 23 member states missed the deadline — [Talentup, upd. 10 Sep 2026](https://talentup.io/insights/es/pay-transparency/spain/) (vendor; single source for the draft details).
  - Stakeholders: CEOE asks that transposition "evite complicar las tareas burocráticas formales" (avoid complicating formal bureaucracy), especially for pymes. Supreme Court magistrate López Parada expects new disputes to end up "judicializados" (in court). The gender pay gap is €5,150 a year (INE wage structure survey, EAES 2024) — [Law&Trends, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/laboral/espana-ante-el-reto-de-la-igualdad-y-transparencia-salarial-las-mujeres-cobran-de-media-5-000-euros-anuales-menos-que-1.html).
- **PRL (occupational risk prevention):**
  - Four ways to organise it: the employer personally, designated workers, an in-house service, or an external SPA.
  - The employer may take on prevention personally (except health surveillance) in firms with up to 10 workers, or up to 25 with a single workplace, if the activity is not in Annex I — [BOE RD 39/1997, consolidated text](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1997-1853).
  - PRL is mandatory from the first worker — [Gestoria247, 7 Sep 2026](https://gestoria247.com/blog/cuanto-cuesta-un-trabajador-a-la-empresa-2026).
  - Heat: the Council of Ministers approved the Plan Estival 2026 on 9 Jun 2026, and the ITSS is e-mailing companies. RDL 8/2024 provides a 4-day paid leave when workers cannot reach work, and requires heat-episode protocols — [Grant Thornton, Jun 2026](https://www.grantthornton.es/contentassets/4730b6c010d04ea29770da8a29bc5b94/labour-newsjunio-retos-aun-pendientes-y-nuevos-limites-en-materia-laboralgrant-thornton.pdf).
  - ITSS 2025: 76 immediate work stoppages for serious and imminent risk — [El Derecho/Europa Press](https://elderecho.com/?p=489934).

### Inferences
- RD 723/2026 is the most universal new obligation of 2026: every employer, effective 5 Oct 2026, with a 30-working-day response clock for existing staff and an algorithmic-transparency clause that bites hardest on startups and platforms. It suits template and e-delivery automation well.
- With the Cortes dissolved, pay-transparency elements that need law-rank backing (sanctions, procedural burden-of-proof rules) probably wait for the new legislature. A decree amending RD 902/2020 could still be approved before 29-N. Treat pay transparency as **upcoming / delayed**.
- The universal stack (registro de jornada, pay register, harassment procedures, PRL, RD 723 documents) hits autónomo employers and micro firms from employee #1. That is the natural bundle for a micro-firm compliance product.

- I did not verify the AAI's 2026 operational and sanctioning activity.
- I found no evidence on enforcement of digital disconnection policies (art. 88 LOPDGDD) or on pain caused by them.
- I found no count of equality plans registered in REGCON.

## 7. Payroll administration: gestoría prices, Sistema RED/SILTRA, Contrat@, convenio complexity, FUNDAE

### Takeaway
Micro and small employers mostly outsource payroll to gestorías. Published market prices are about **€15–20 per payslip per month**, with minimum fees around €49–250 a month depending on the provider. The rulebook churns continuously:
- the retroactive SMI (Feb 2026);
- annual MEI and solidarity-quota changes;
- TGSS RED bulletins, such as the notice-pay contribution change of July 2026;
- new leave rules, such as 19-week birth leave and the per-event 5-day care leave;
- RD 723/2026 documents;
- convenio-specific rules on holidays and guard shifts.

FUNDAE (the state body for subsidised company training) shows 290,747 companies using their training credit, roughly one in five employers by my estimate.

### Cited Findings
- Gestoría prices (all three sources are vendors):
  - From about €20 per worker per month for small companies, with minimum fees from about €250 a month for pymes; extras such as legal advice and conflict handling cost more — [PayFit, upd. 17 Jun 2026](https://payfit.com/es/contenido-practico/software-de-nominas-vs-asesor-laboral/).
  - "20 €/nómina al mes más IVA" (€20 per payslip per month plus VAT), "desde 15 € por volumen" (from €15 at volume) — [Billeo, upd. 22 Jun 2026](https://www.billeo.es/blog/cuanto-te-cuesta-de-verdad-contratar-a-tu-primer-empleado).
  - An online gestoría "desde 49 €/mes + IVA" (from €49 a month plus VAT) with 2 workers included and no commitment — [Gestoria247, 7 Sep 2026](https://gestoria247.com/blog/cuanto-cuesta-un-trabajador-a-la-empresa-2026).
- The retroactive SMI forced regularisation of payroll already paid in January — [PwC, 19 Feb 2026](https://periscopiofiscalylegal.pwc.es/se-fija-la-cuantia-del-smi-el-problema-ahora-es-como-aplicarlo/).
- Payroll software must handle the three solidarity bands as a separate line. If it was not updated, January–March liquidations should be corrected. There is no annualisation, so variable pay must be reviewed month by month — [CopilotGestoria, 28 Mar 2026](https://copilotgestoria.com/blog/cuota-solidaridad-2026-calcular-nomina-tramos-ejemplos-guia-gestorias) (vendor).
- TGSS Boletín RED 8/2026 (14 Jul 2026): notice pay now contributes, through a supplementary settlement and the renamed CRA code 0054 — [RRHH Digital, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/799952/la-tgss-cambia-el-criterio-la-compensacion-por-falta-de-preaviso-en-el-despido-objetivo-ya-cotiza/).

  - Birth and childcare leave of **19 weeks** per parent, or 32 for single parents.

- Convenio-driven complexity — [Grant Thornton, Jun 2026](https://www.grantthornton.es/contentassets/4730b6c010d04ea29770da8a29bc5b94/labour-newsjunio-retos-aun-pendientes-y-nuevos-limites-en-materia-laboralgrant-thornton.pdf):

## Pain-point inventory (structured entries)

### LAB-01: Time-recording compliance in limbo (paper legal today, digital mandate pending)
- **Who:** All employers, from autónomos with one employee to medium firms. The pain is heaviest in hostelería, retail, building caretakers, field and mobile staff, and split shifts.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Owners keep paper sheets, Excel or a basic app. They don't know whether to invest now or wait for the decree, and they are exposed if an inspector or an ex-employee challenges the hours.
- **Evidence:**
  - Paper or electronic records are valid today; the fine is €751–7,500 per infraction — [RegistraHora, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.registrahora.es/noticias/registro-horario-digital-aplazado-septiembre-2026); [ControlHorarioLegal, 23 Oct 2026](https://controlhorariolegal.com/normativa-fichaje-2026).
  - The Consejo de Estado estimates 1.35M companies affected at €867M a year and says "no procede aprobar" (should not be approved) — [El Español, 23 Mar 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/invertia/economia/empleo/20260323/consejo-critica-tumba-registro-horario-yolanda-diaz/1003744181041_0.html).
  - Postponed on 24 Jul 2026; not approved as of 23 Oct 2026.
- **Scale:** 1.35M companies (Consejo de Estado figure, via El Español).
- **Cost/impact:**
  - €867M a year estimated compliance cost if the decree passes, about €640 per firm (my inference).
  - Fines up to €7,500 per infraction.
  - Registro-specific fines of €2.99M in 2024 (1,869 infractions) — [El Derecho/EP, 7 Feb 2025](https://elderecho.com/inspeccion-de-trabajo-elevo-un-9-las-multas-por-infracciones-por-tiempo-de-trabajo-en-2024).
- **Driver & status:**
  - Art. 34.9 ET (RDL 8/2019): **in force**.
  - Digital-registration royal decree (draft): **pending / postponed**. Urgent processing was authorised 30 Sep 2025, the Consejo de Estado opinion was unfavourable on 23 Mar 2026, and the decree was missing from the agendas of 6, 13 and 20 Oct 2026. The Government aims to approve it before 29-N, with a 6- or 12-month transition for pymes under discussion.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - Paper or Excel.
  - Apps: Woffu Lite from €1.5 per user per month (10 or fewer employees); Sesame €4.75–8 (15-seat minimum); Factorial from €5–5.5; Timu (Nimbo), priced by employees.
  - Complaint: Factorial clock-ins "tarda en registrarlos" (are slow to register) — [Trustpilot, 30 Sep 2026](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/factorialhr.com).
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Tamper-proof digital records, correction audit trails, worker access and ITSS export are exactly what software does; sector edge cases (caretakers, mobile staff) need tailored capture.
- **Confidence:** High on rules and status; medium on the cost estimate (one official-opinion figure reported by the press).

### LAB-02: Working-time and overtime inspections and unpaid-overtime lawsuits
- **Who:** Micro, small and medium firms with long or irregular hours: hostelería, retail, logistics, care, construction, and startups with "always-on" cultures.
- **Where:** National. Euskadi has its own inspection data showing more than 60% of inspections find irregularities.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Overtime is often unrecorded or paid informally. When a worker leaves, they claim back overtime; without a record the employer cannot rebut it if there was no fixed schedule.
- **Evidence:**
  - 2024 ITSS working-time and overtime fines €17.2M (13,681 infractions, 38,686 actions) — [El Derecho/EP, 7 Feb 2025](https://elderecho.com/inspeccion-de-trabajo-elevo-un-9-las-multas-por-infracciones-por-tiempo-de-trabajo-en-2024).
  - 2025: 14,728 infractions and 252,500 workers affected — [Finantres, 25 Aug 2026](https://finantresnoticias.com/negocios-y-autonomos/multas-por-incumplir-horario-se-triplican-deben-revisar-ahora-pymes/) (single source).
  - Euskadi 2025: 1,490 actions, 417 infractions — [Onda Vasca, 16 Feb 2026](https://www.ondavasca.com/trabajo-sigue-detectando-un-60-de-anomalias-en-las-inspecciones-por-horarios-de-trabajo-en-euskadi/).
  - STS 372/2026 sets the burden-of-proof rules; a butcher's-shop case involved €11.7k claimed — [RRHH Digital, 13 May 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/794593/el-tribunal-supremo-pone-limites-a-las-reclamaciones-de-horas-extra/).
- **Scale:** About 2.4–2.5M unpaid overtime hours a week nationally — [El Independiente, 1 Apr 2024](https://www.elindependiente.com/economia/2024/04/01/multas-registro-jornada-horario-3-millones-euros/amp/); [Onda Vasca, 9 Sep 2026](https://www.ondavasca.com/trabajo-asegura-que-el-registro-horario-se-aprobara-con-caracter-inmediato-tras-incorporar-cambios-juridicos/).
- **Cost/impact:** Back-pay claims in the thousands of euros per worker, plus grave-infraction fines. Wage claims (reclamaciones de cantidad) run at 32–35k a quarter nationally — [Law&Trends/EFE, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/laboral/las-demandas-por-despido-marcan-record-en-el-segundo-trimestre-con-45-230-procedimientos-1.html).
- **Driver & status:** Arts. 34–35 ET and LISOS (in force); STS 372/2026 (15 Apr 2026) case law.
- **How it is solved today:** Time-recording apps (see LAB-01), gestoría advice, and labour lawyers or graduados sociales once a claim arrives (fees not found).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Records, overtime alerts and compensation within 4 months (per Finantres) are automatable; disputes still need lawyers.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.

### LAB-03: SMI hikes, the absorption/compensation puzzle and retroactive payroll fixes
- **Who:** Micro and small employers in low-wage sectors (retail, hostelería, cleaning, care, agriculture, domestic employers) and the gestorías that run their payroll.
- **Where:** National. Most beneficiaries are in Andalucía, Madrid, C. Valenciana and Cataluña.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Each year the SMI arrives late and retroactively, so payroll must be re-run for months already paid. Firms must test annually whether supplements can absorb the rise, and a union-backed decree-law threatens to ban absorbing some supplements.
- **Evidence:**
  - RD 126/2026: €1,221 × 14, retroactive to 1 Jan, approved 18 Feb 2026; compensable income is measured as of 20 Feb 2026; an anti-absorption decree-law was pending — [PwC, 19 Feb 2026](https://periscopiofiscalylegal.pwc.es/se-fija-la-cuantia-del-smi-el-problema-ahora-es-como-aplicarlo/).
  - About 2.5M beneficiaries; approved without employers — [Consejo General de Gestores, Feb 2026](https://www.consejogestores.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Novedades-destacadas-91.pdf).
- **Scale:** About 2.5M workers; the SMI is up 66% since 2018.
- **Cost/impact:**
  - +€518 per worker per year in gross pay.
  - An SMI worker costs €22,589.72 a year in total — [Gestoria247, 7 Sep 2026](https://gestoria247.com/blog/cuanto-cuesta-un-trabajador-a-la-empresa-2026).
  - Re-processing January payroll.
  - ASNALA warns pymes may struggle to absorb the rises — [Law&Trends, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/abogacia/ana-gomez-presidenta-de-asnala-es-necesario-recuperar-el-dialogo-social-para-resolver-los-grandes-problemas-del-1.html).
- **Driver & status:** RD 126/2026 (in force, retroactive). The anti-absorption decree-law is announced but its status is unverified as of Oct 2026.
- **How it is solved today:** Gestorías (€15–20 per payslip) and payroll software (a3innuva and others) re-run payroll; employers' associations issue circulars, e.g. [FADE Circular laboral 1/2026](https://fade.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/Circular-laboral-1-2026.-SMI.-19Feb2026.pdf) (search result).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Annual absorption tests and retroactive recalculation can be automated; the cost itself cannot.
- **Confidence:** High.

### LAB-04: Rising employer contributions and total labour cost (MEI, solidarity quota, maximum base, short-contract surcharge)
- **Who:** All employers. In practice micro and small firms feel the total cost; the solidarity quota mainly hits startups and medium firms with high salaries.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Contribution rates and bases change every January, sometimes before the official order is published. Short contracts carry an extra charge. Total cost per employee rises about 4% a year.
- **Evidence:**
  - Maximum base €5,101.20; €33.62 surcharge for contracts of 30 days or less — [Seguridad Social, 2026](https://www.seg-social.es/wps/portal/wss/internet/Trabajadores/CotizacionRecaudacionTrabajadores/36537).
  - MEI 0.90% and solidarity quota at 1.15%, 1.25% and 1.46% by band — [CopilotGestoria, 28 Mar 2026](https://copilotgestoria.com/blog/cuota-solidaridad-2026-calcular-nomina-tramos-ejemplos-guia-gestorias), which conflicts with the official page's 0.80% (apparently not updated).
  - Labour cost +4.0% and Social Security contributions +4.2% year on year — [INE, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/es/ETCL2T26.htm).
- **Scale:** All employers. The maximum base matters only for salaries above about €61k a year.
- **Cost/impact:**
  - Employer rate about 32.15% for a permanent office job (33.35% temporary) — [Gestoria247](https://gestoria247.com/blog/cuanto-cuesta-un-trabajador-a-la-empresa-2026).
  - +€74.73 a month for an executive earning €8,500 a month — [CopilotGestoria](https://copilotgestoria.com/blog/cuota-solidaridad-2026-calcular-nomina-tramos-ejemplos-guia-gestorias).
  - 5.8% of establishments without vacancies cite high hiring costs — [INE](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/es/ETCL2T26.htm).
- **Driver & status:** Ley 21/2021 and RDL 2/2023 MEI schedule; DT 42ª LGSS solidarity quota (2025–2045); Orden PJC/297/2026. All **in force**.
- **How it is solved today:** Gestorías and payroll software updates; cost simulators on gestoría and vendor blogs.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Cost simulation and automatic rate updates are possible; the cost itself is not reducible.
- **Confidence:** High on direction; medium on exact 2026 MEI and solidarity rates (secondary sources).

### LAB-05: Record absenteeism (IT) cost and sick-leave management with mutuas
- **Who:** All sizes. The impact is acute for micro and small firms without cover staff; industry, postal, building services and care are worst hit.
- **Where:**
  - National.
  - Highest rates: País Vasco (Bilbao), Canarias (Las Palmas) and Cantabria, at 9.6%.
  - Largest costs: Cataluña (Barcelona) €11.6bn, Madrid €10.3bn, Andalucía (Sevilla, Málaga) €7.4bn.
  - Lowest rates: Baleares (Palma) 6.2% and Madrid 6.6%.
- **Day-to-day reality:**
  - The owner learns of a sick-leave note (parte de baja) the same morning and scrambles for cover.
  - The firm pays statutory days and convenio supplements, and juggles communication with the mutua and the INSS.
  - Long mental-health leaves are increasingly common.
  - Employers feel powerless on suspected abuse, since surveillance is legally limited.
- **Evidence:**
  - 7.6% absenteeism, 5.95% IT rate, €59.1bn cost, mental-health leave +111% in 5 years — [Adecco, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.adeccogroup.com/-/media/project/adecco-group/adeccogroup/spain-content/2026-press-releases/2026-07-14-ndp-xv-informe-anual-adecco-empresa-saludable-gestion-absentismo.pdf); [El Derecho/EP, 14 Jul 2026](https://elderecho.com/el-absentismo-laboral-alcanza-un-maximo-historico-del-76-en-2025).
  - 9.1M IT processes; €16,115M of €33,280M IT cost borne by companies; 1,407,819 absent — [Law&Trends, 9 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/laboral/las-empresas-necesitan-herramientas-para-gestionar-mejor-sus-bajas-medicas-y-reducir-sus-niveles-de-absentismo-1.html) (single source).
- **Scale:** About 1.4M workers absent; 9.1M IT processes a year.
- **Cost/impact:** €16.1bn borne directly by companies in 2025; Cataluña companies alone €3.07bn.
- **Driver & status:**
  - Structural (ageing, health-system waits).
  - Ley 2/2025 (in force 1 May 2025) and the INSS–INGESA 2025–2028 agreement (in force).
  - No adopted "flexible return / partial IT" reform found (status unknown).
- **How it is solved today:**
  - Mutuas (e.g., Unión de Mutuas) for monitoring.
  - AMAT/CEOE follow-up on repeat claimants.
  - Private investigators within legal limits.
  - Absence modules in HR suites (e.g., Sesame "Vacaciones y Ausencias" from €4.75 per employee per month — [Sesame](https://www.sesamehr.es/precios/)).
  - Graduados sociales and lawyers.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Absence tracking, return-to-work workflows, convenio supplement calculation, analytics and mutua paperwork can be automated; root causes cannot.
- **Confidence:** High on scale (Adecco plus multiple outlets); medium on the company-cost split (single source).

### LAB-06: Permanent-incapacity "reasonable adjustment" duty (Ley 2/2025)
- **Who:** All employers, especially micro and small firms (under 25 workers) in physically demanding sectors.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** When a worker is declared permanently incapacitated, the contract no longer ends automatically. The worker has 10 days to ask to stay. The employer then has 3 months to adapt the post or find a vacancy, or to justify in writing that this is an excessive burden before terminating.
- **Evidence:**
  - Ley 2/2025 (BOE 30 Apr 2025; in force 1 May 2025).
  - For firms under 25 workers, the burden is excessive if the adaptation cost (net of subsidies) exceeds the greater of the art. 56.1 ET severance or 6 months' salary.
  - The prevention service determines the scope of adjustments.
  - Source: [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2025/04/29/2); the change is also highlighted by [Adecco, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.adeccogroup.com/-/media/project/adecco-group/adeccogroup/spain-content/2026-press-releases/2026-07-14-ndp-xv-informe-anual-adecco-empresa-saludable-gestion-absentismo.pdf).
- **Scale:** Not quantified; number of permanent-incapacity declarations not found.
- **Cost/impact:** Adaptation costs, SPA involvement, and litigation risk if the procedure is mishandled (urgent procedure under art. 120 LRJS).
- **Driver & status:** Ley 2/2025, **in force**.
- **How it is solved today:** SPA plus labour lawyers; no dedicated tools found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Deadline tracking (10 days and 3 months), documentation, and a calculator comparing adaptation cost with the severance threshold.
- **Confidence:** High on rules; low on scale.

### LAB-07: Technical and trade talent shortages and persistent vacancies
- **Who:** Industrial SMEs, installers, maintenance, logistics and transport, energy, plus tech startups needing software and AI profiles.
- **Where:** National. Vacancies are concentrated in Cataluña (Barcelona), Madrid and Andalucía (Sevilla, Málaga), with sector campaigns elsewhere (e.g., Valencia agriculture, Pontevedra automotive — RRHH Digital headlines).
- **Day-to-day reality:** Positions stay open for months. SMEs compete with large firms and ETTs (temp agencies) for electromechanics, welders, refrigeration technicians and C+E drivers, and often train people in-house.
- **Evidence:**
  - 151,736 vacancies, 87.4% in services — [INE, 17 Sep 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/es/ETCL2T26.htm).

### LAB-11: Dismissal procedure traps (pre-dismissal hearing, retaliation nullity, notice-pay contributions, net back pay)
- **Who:** Owner-managed micro and small firms without HR staff; autónomo employers.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** The owner fires someone with a letter template. They skip the pre-dismissal hearing, or dismiss shortly after the worker complained, or pay notice in lieu without contributions. Each mistake turns a defensible dismissal into an unfair or null one, or into a Social Security debt.
- **Evidence:**
  - Hearing requirement since the 2024 change in case law — [Law&Trends, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/laboral/un-tramite-que-puede-cambiarlo-todo-cuando-un-despido-disciplinario-puede-ser-declarado-improcedente-1.html).
  - STS 526/2026 on retaliation nullity before any formal claim — [RRHH Digital, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/799602/el-supremo-refuerza-la-proteccion-frente-al-despido-represalia-incluso-antes-de-una-reclamacion-formal/).
  - TGSS RED 8/2026 makes notice pay contributable — [RRHH Digital, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/799952/la-tgss-cambia-el-criterio-la-compensacion-por-falta-de-preaviso-en-el-despido-objetivo-ya-cotiza/).
  - Back pay (salarios de tramitación) is subject to income-tax and Social Security withholding, and clauses allowing unilateral schedule changes are null (STS 491/2026) — [Grant Thornton, Jun 2026](https://www.grantthornton.es/contentassets/4730b6c010d04ea29770da8a29bc5b94/labour-newsjunio-retos-aun-pendientes-y-nuevos-limites-en-materia-laboralgrant-thornton.pdf).
- **Scale:** Applies to every disciplinary or objective dismissal; tens of thousands a quarter are litigated (LAB-09).
- **Cost/impact:** Unfair-dismissal compensation instead of none; nullity means readmission plus back pay; supplementary contributions on notice pay; ITSS proposed €459,587 for improper probation terminations in 2025 — [El Derecho/EP, 7 Oct 2026](https://elderecho.com/?p=489934).
- **Driver & status:** Case law 2024–2026 and TGSS criteria (July 2026). **In force.**
- **How it is solved today:** Gestorías and labour lawyers; articles from law firms (Augusta Abogados) serve as guidance. No dedicated SME tools found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes or partly. A guided dismissal workflow (hearing, letter, timing check against recent complaints, correct CRA coding of notice pay, net back-pay calculation) is very automatable.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.

  - 76 immediate stoppages in 2025 — [El Derecho/EP, 7 Oct 2026](https://elderecho.com/?p=489934).
  - PRL is mandatory from the first worker — [Gestoria247, 7 Sep 2026](https://gestoria247.com/blog/cuanto-cuesta-un-trabajador-a-la-empresa-2026).
- **Scale:** All employers (about 1.35M).
- **Cost/impact:** SPA prices not found. Sources only say "más costes de prevención" (plus prevention costs) — [Billeo, 22 Jun 2026](https://www.billeo.es/blog/cuanto-te-cuesta-de-verdad-contratar-a-tu-primer-empleado).
- **Driver & status:** Ley 31/1995, RD 39/1997 and RDL 8/2024: **in force**. A broader PRL reform was not verified.
- **How it is solved today:** SPA contracts (Quirónprevención, Cualtis, Preving and others; prices not found) or employer self-management for micro firms.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Risk assessment, training records, heat-alert protocols and SPA document management; health surveillance needs medical services.
- **Confidence:** Medium on obligations; low on cost data.

### LAB-21: Payroll administration complexity and gestoría dependence
- **Who:** Micro and small employers and autónomo employers (outsourced payroll); gestorías and asesorías laborales as intermediaries.
- **Where:** National. Convenios are often provincial, so the work is province-specific (e.g., Barcelona, Madrid, Valencia, Sevilla, Bizkaia).
- **Day-to-day reality:** The monthly cycle runs: gather incidents (absences, overtime, IT) → gestoría calculates payslips under the applicable convenio → Social Security filings through RED/SILTRA → contracts on Contrat@ → corrections whenever rules change mid-year.
- **Evidence:**
  - Gestoría prices: €20 per worker per month and minimum €250 a month — [PayFit, 17 Jun 2026](https://payfit.com/es/contenido-practico/software-de-nominas-vs-asesor-laboral/); €20 per payslip, from €15 at volume — [Billeo, 22 Jun 2026](https://www.billeo.es/blog/cuanto-te-cuesta-de-verdad-contratar-a-tu-primer-empleado); from €49 a month for 2 workers — [Gestoria247, 7 Sep 2026](https://gestoria247.com/blog/cuanto-cuesta-un-trabajador-a-la-empresa-2026).
  - Rule churn: retroactive SMI — [PwC](https://periscopiofiscalylegal.pwc.es/se-fija-la-cuantia-del-smi-el-problema-ahora-es-como-aplicarlo/); 2026 rate changes and correcting January–March filings — [CopilotGestoria](https://copilotgestoria.com/blog/cuota-solidaridad-2026-calcular-nomina-tramos-ejemplos-guia-gestorias); RED 8/2026 — [RRHH Digital, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/799952/la-tgss-cambia-el-criterio-la-compensacion-por-falta-de-preaviso-en-el-despido-objetivo-ya-cotiza/); convenio-specific rulings on shifts and holidays — [Grant Thornton, Jun 2026](https://www.grantthornton.es/contentassets/4730b6c010d04ea29770da8a29bc5b94/labour-newsjunio-retos-aun-pendientes-y-nuevos-limites-en-materia-laboralgrant-thornton.pdf).
- **Scale:** All employers that outsource payroll (count of gestoría clients not found).
- **Cost/impact:** About €900–1,200 a year for a 5-person firm at €15–20 per payslip (my inference); error and surcharge risk.
- **Driver & status:** LGSS, the annual contribution order, convenios and TGSS bulletins: **in force and continuously changing**.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - Gestorías on a3innuva and similar engines (Sesame integrates with a3innuva — [Sesame](https://www.sesamehr.es/precios/)).
  - PayFit software; complaints about ticket-only support — [Trustpilot](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/payfit.com).
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes or partly. Rules engines, convenio tables and change alerts for gestorías; automated incident capture from SMEs.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Prices come from three vendor sources, broadly consistent.

### LAB-22: Leave and permit entitlement tracking (19-week birth leave, per-event care leave, force-majeure and climate leave)
- **Who:** All employers. The pain is hardest for micro firms where one absence matters and for shift-based sectors.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Owners and gestorías must track a growing menu of entitlements that courts keep reinterpreting:
  - birth leave of 19 weeks, including 2 weeks usable until the child turns 8;
  - a 5-day care leave per event with no annual cap;
  - force-majeure hours;
  - a 4-day climate leave;
  - leave counted on the actual shift length.
- **Evidence:**
  - RDL 9/2025 — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rdl/2025/07/29/9).
  - TS 753/2026 (25 Sep 2026) — [RRHH Digital, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/804055/el-tribunal-supremo-confirma-que-el-permiso-por-cuidados-familiares-no-tiene-un-limite-anual-de-cinco-dias/).
  - Shift-staff leave counted on the real shift (TS, 27 May 2026) and RDL 8/2024 — [Grant Thornton, Jun 2026](https://www.grantthornton.es/contentassets/4730b6c010d04ea29770da8a29bc5b94/labour-newsjunio-retos-aun-pendientes-y-nuevos-limites-en-materia-laboralgrant-thornton.pdf).
  - A Supreme Court doctrine on the 8-week parental leave and a warning on force-majeure leave — [RRHH Digital, 1 Apr 2026 and 13 May 2026](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/legal/792157/el-tribunal-supremo-fija-doctrina-sobre-el-permiso-parental-semanas-completas-si-perdida-de-vacaciones-no/) (headlines).
- **Scale:** All workers with family-care events (not quantified).
- **Cost/impact:** Mis-applied caps lead to claims and conflicts (not quantified).
- **Driver & status:** ET arts. 37 and 48; RDL 5/2023, 8/2024 and 9/2025; TS case law 2026: **in force**.
- **How it is solved today:** HR suites with leave modules (Sesame, Factorial, Woffu) and gestorías.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. A per-event entitlement engine kept current with case law.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.

### LAB-23: FUNDAE training-credit bureaucracy and under-use
- **Who:** Micro and small firms, which have small credits and high administrative overhead.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Firms have a training credit, financed by the 0.60% vocational-training contribution, that they can recover through contribution rebates. Using it requires notifying courses, certifying and justifying costs, so many don't bother or delegate it to training providers.
- **Evidence:**
  - 290,747 companies using their credit (Oct 2026); new 2026 cost-justification application and guides (22 Oct 2026) — [FUNDAE](https://www.fundae.es/).
  - Vocational-training contribution: 0.60% employer + 0.10% worker — [Seguridad Social](https://www.seg-social.es/wps/portal/wss/internet/Trabajadores/CotizacionRecaudacionTrabajadores/36537).
- **Scale:** About 290k users vs about 1.35M employers, roughly 21% usage (my inference; bases differ).
- **Cost/impact:** Unused training credit (not quantified).
- **Driver & status:** Ley 30/2015 (in force; not re-verified here). Overlaps with the subsidies researcher.
- **How it is solved today:** Training companies acting as "entidad organizadora" (organising entity) handle the paperwork, often bundled with the course (prices not found).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Credit tracking, course notification and justification workflows.
- **Confidence:** Low-medium.

### LAB-24: HR and payroll software that fits micro firms poorly (minimum seats, support, gestoría hand-off)
- **Who:** Micro firms (under 10), autónomo employers and small firms (10–49).
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** The leading suites price per employee with minimums or annual commitments and leave payroll to the gestoría. Support is often ticket-only. Micro owners end up with paper, WhatsApp and a gestoría.
- **Evidence:**
  - Sesame 15-employee minimum — [Sesame](https://www.sesamehr.es/precios/).
  - Woffu Lite for 10 or fewer employees from €1.5, other plans annual-only — [Woffu](https://www.woffu.com/es/precios).
  - Factorial: slow support, unannounced changes — [Trustpilot](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/factorialhr.com).
  - PayFit: ticket-only support — [Trustpilot](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/payfit.com).
- **Scale:** Micro firms are the bulk of employers (count not retrieved here).
- **Cost/impact:** €1.5–9 per employee per month list prices; switching friction after annual commitments (e.g., a Sesame reviewer: "En cuanto pase el año me cambio" — as soon as the year is up I'm switching — [Trustpilot](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/sesamehr.es), tiny sample).
- **Driver & status:** Market structure, ongoing.
- **How it is solved today:** Woffu Lite, Factorial, Sesame, PayFit and gestorías.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. This is a product-design and pricing gap.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

## Top pains

1. **Labour litigation and dismissal risk (LAB-09, LAB-10, LAB-11).** Dismissal claims are at a record 45,230 in Q2 2026 (+11.2%). Trials in Madrid and Barcelona are set for 2029–2030. 2024–26 case law and TGSS criteria added procedural traps (pre-dismissal hearing, retaliation nullity, notice-pay contributions) that owner-managed SMEs routinely miss.
2. **Absenteeism / IT (LAB-05).** It runs at a record 7.6% and costs €59.1bn; companies bear €16.1bn of IT costs directly, and about 1.4M workers are absent. It hits every sector and region and is acute for micro firms with no cover staff.
3. **Time recording and working-time compliance (LAB-01, LAB-02).** It is universal (1.35M firms) and sits under a pending digital mandate estimated at €867M a year. ITSS working-time fines run at €15–17M a year, and the Supreme Court has made records decisive in overtime claims. It is the clearest deadline-driven software trigger if the decree passes.
4. **Labour-cost escalation plus SMI mechanics (LAB-03, LAB-04).** The SMI is up 66% since 2018 (€1,221 × 14 in 2026, retroactive), the MEI and the maximum base keep rising, and labour cost is up 4.0% a year. It is universal and hardest on low-margin micro employers.
5. **Payroll and leave administration churn (LAB-21, LAB-22, LAB-15).** RD 723/2026 has been in force since 5 Oct 2026 for all employers, alongside the RED bulletin changes, per-event leave rulings and the 19-week birth leave. SMEs pay gestorías about €15–20 per payslip to absorb the churn.
6. **Labour Inspectorate enforcement intensity (LAB-12, LAB-13, LAB-14).** Proposed sanctions hit a record €858M in 2025 (+58.6%). 47k bogus self-employed and 70k contracts were converted. Startups and seasonal SMEs are particularly exposed.
7. **Hiring friction: trades shortages and foreign-worker processing (LAB-07, LAB-08).** There are 151,736 vacancies, concentrated in technical trades, and extranjería delays run about 6 months. The 1.17M regularisation applications create a document-heavy onboarding wave, and fines average about €10k per unauthorised worker detected.
8. **The size-triggered compliance stack for 50+ firms (LAB-16 to LAB-19).** Equality plan, LGTBI measures, whistleblowing channel and imminent pay transparency affect fewer firms and enforcement is modest so far, but potential fines are high. The pay-transparency transposition is the next big wave, probably in 2027.

## Noise and dead ends

- **37.5-hour working week.** Rejected by Congress on 10 Sep 2025 (178–170). Not law, no current obligation, and unlikely before 2027 given the 29-N election ([Periódico de Ibiza](https://www.periodicodeibiza.es/noticias/nacional/2025/09/11/2467647/congreso-tumba-reduccion-jornada-laboral-prosperar-veto-vox-junts.html)). Also treat the "4-day-week subsidies up to €150,000" teaser as unverified: the Grupo 2000 page with that URL describes no programme.
- **"€10,000 per worker" fines for missing time records.** These were in the rejected bill. The current LISOS fine is €751–7,500 per infraction, and the royal decree cannot change it ([ControlHorarioLegal](https://controlhorariolegal.com/normativa-fichaje-2026)).
- **"Mandatory digital fichaje in force in 2026."** Many vendor blogs imply it, but it is **not in force** as of 23 Oct 2026. Treat it as an upcoming catalyst, not current law.
- **Pay transparency obligations (salary in job ads, gap reports).** Not yet in force in Spain; the draft decree is unpublished. Real but upcoming; first Directive report deadline 7 Jun 2027 ([Que.es](https://www.que.es/2026/06/09/transparencia-salarial-ofertas-pospuesta/)).
- **Solidarity quota.** Applies only to pay above €5,101.20 a month and costs about €36 a month at €8,500. Negligible for most micro firms ([CopilotGestoria](https://copilotgestoria.com/blog/cuota-solidaridad-2026-calcular-nomina-tramos-ejemplos-guia-gestorias)).
- **Whistleblowing channel (Ley 2/2023).** The deadlines passed in 2023, the software is commoditised, and I found no evidence of active AAI enforcement. A weak standalone SaaS opportunity.
- **LGTBI protocol enforcement.** Only 196 ITSS actions in 2025 ([El Derecho/EP](https://elderecho.com/?p=489934)). An obligation with low current pain, better bundled than sold alone.
- **"5-year waits for labour trials in Sevilla" (Público).** The article is from **Nov 2021** ([Público](https://www.publico.es/economia/bloqueo-justicia-cinco-anos-espera-juzgar-despido.html)), not current evidence; use the 2026 Madrid and Barcelona data instead.
- **"€47,300 in sanctions at 14 gestorías" (CopilotGestoria).** An unsourced vendor marketing claim with internal contradictions ([CopilotGestoria](https://copilotgestoria.com/blog/errores-nominas-sanciones-47300-euros-14-gestorias-checklist-validacion)). Do not use.
- **Billeo's SMI cost example (€1,323 gross a month "según SMI 2026").** Inconsistent with RD 126/2026 (€1,221 × 14); use Gestoria247's €22,589.72 a year instead.
- **Hiscox "75% have an LGTBI harassment protocol."** The base is unclear and the figure is implausible for all SMEs ([RRHH Digital](https://www.rrhhdigital.com/secciones/pymes/790309/por-primera-vez-mas-de-la-mitad-de-las-pymes-espanolas-aplica-politicas-de-igualdad/)). Use with caution.
- **Digital disconnection policy (art. 88 LOPDGDD).** I found no enforcement or complaint evidence; it looks like a low-pain paperwork item.
- **PRL for micro firms.** Partly solved cheaply already: owners of firms with up to 10 workers (or up to 25 with one workplace) may self-manage prevention, except health surveillance ([RD 39/1997](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1997-1853)). The SaaS opportunity is narrower than it looks (documentation, heat protocols), and SPA price data was not found.
- **Office capacity for immigration processing and court backlogs.** Real and severe pains, but not software-solvable at the core. Only adjacent tooling (case tracking, settlement support) is addressable.
- **Housing as a hiring barrier.** **Not a dead end, but unverified here:** the search budget ran out before any source was found. Check the hospitality and city-conditions researchers' notes before ranking.
