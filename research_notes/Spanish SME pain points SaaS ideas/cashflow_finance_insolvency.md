## Key question 1 — Late payment (morosidad): payment periods, share paid late, cost, worst sectors, and the status of sanctions (Ley 3/2004 / Ley 15/2010, Crea y Crece, 2025–2026 bills, EU Late Payment Regulation)

### Takeaway
On every measure, Spanish B2B payment periods are still above the 60-day legal maximum: 67 days in the PMcM survey for 2025, 80.5 days in the Cepyme observatory, and in Informa D&B's Q3 2026 data only 41.5% of payments arrived on time. Large companies are the main late payers, and the PMcM puts the share of large companies exceeding the legal deadline at 85%. There is still no general sanctions regime. The 2020 bill stalled for years, the Cortes are now dissolved, and the EU Late Payment Regulation is blocked in the Council. The only levers in force or scheduled are:
- **Subsidy eligibility:** art. 13.3 bis of the Ley General de Subvenciones (LGS), introduced by Crea y Crece.
- **E-invoicing:** mandatory B2B e-invoicing with payment-date reporting, from 6 October 2027 (turnover above €8M) and 6 October 2028 (everyone else).

**Legal framework and status as of October 2026**
- **Ley 3/2004:** the default payment term is 30 days and the maximum term the parties can agree is 60 days. The law contains no fines; one guide says the consolidated BOE text has had no changes since 2014 and has no sanctions — [itbid blog, 2026](https://itbid.com/blog/ley-morosidad-plazos-pago-proveedores/); [Ofidem, 2026](https://www.ofidem.es/la-ley-de-morosidad-en-2026-guia-de-supervivencia-para-empresas-y-autonomos/) (both via search summary). PMcM gives the legal maximums as 30 days for public administrations and 60 for private firms — [PMcM press release, 22 Oct 2026](https://pmcm.es/pmcm-cinco-compromisos-contra-morosidad/). **Not verified in BOE:** the consolidated-text fetch returned a 404, so I could not confirm Ley 15/2010's amendments.
- **The sanctions bill:**
  - Junts per Catalunya's proposición de ley to add a sanctions regime to Ley 3/2004 was admitted for debate (tomada en consideración) on 22 September 2020, with 344 votes in favour and 1 abstention.
  - It proposed fines of €6,000–100,000 for serious breaches and "desde un millón de euros" (from one million euros) for the most serious, and it would have banned agreeing terms longer than the legal limit — [CIC Construcción, 1 Oct 2020](https://www.cicconstruccion.com/texto-diario/mostrar/2742184/congreso-admite-tramite-ley-sanciones-morosidad)
  - From 21 October 2020 the bill sat in the Comisión de Industria while the deadline for amendments was extended every seven days — [Pere Brachfield, 30 Sep 2022](https://perebrachfield.com/blog/noticias-e-informaciones/la-reforma-de-la-ley-contra-la-morosidad-lleva-embarrancada-dos-anos-en-el-congreso/); [BOCG 14-B-60-1](https://www.congreso.es/public_oficiales/L14/CONG/BOCG/B/BOCG-14-B-60-1.PDF)
  - PMcM says a sanctions regime "has been blocked twice". It asks all parties for five commitments: effective sanctions; automatic detection of late payment using e-invoicing "sin obligar al proveedor a denunciar a su cliente" (without forcing the supplier to report its customer); making default interest enforceable; linking payment behaviour to public contracts and subsidies; and publishing real payment periods. It will publish a "Semáforo de Pago a Tiempo" (payment-on-time traffic light) on each party's commitments before the election. 91% of companies surveyed want a sanctions regime — [PMcM, 22 Oct 2026](https://pmcm.es/pmcm-cinco-compromisos-contra-morosidad/)
  - **Status: no sanctions regime is in force and no bill is pending, because the Cortes were dissolved on 6 October 2026** (see the context note at the top).
- **Crea y Crece (Ley 18/2022):** it added art. 13.3 bis to the Ley General de Subvenciones (Ley 38/2003). For subsidies above €30,000, for-profit companies must prove they meet Ley 3/2004 payment periods:

  - Other firms need an auditor's certificate or an "Informe de Procedimientos Acordados" (agreed-upon procedures report).
  - Art. 13.3 bis has been in force since 19 October 2022 and was amended by RDL 5/2023 — [BOE consolidated LGS](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2003-20977). **Status: in force.**

### Inferences
- Inference: the official PMP of 26–30 days and supplier surveys of about 70 days probably measure different things. For example, the official clock may start at invoice approval or registration rather than delivery, or surveys may over-sample aggrieved suppliers. Either way, public-sector late payment is best treated as **localised**: Balears, Murcia, Cantabria, Palma, Las Palmas and a tail of local councils.
- Inference: with no enforcement and around 90% of suppliers not using their statutory rights, solutions have to come from the private side. Examples are automated interest and compensation claims, evidence trails and analytics on customers' payment behaviour. From October 2027/2028, e-invoice payment-status data will create a national record of real payment dates. A future government could build automatic sanctions on it, and SaaS could build payer scoring on it.
- Inference: the three main data sources (PMcM, Cepyme, Informa) differ in method, but all point the same way: payment is structurally late, worst for micro firms, hospitality, construction chains and Canarias, and has improved little since 2023.

### Gaps
- I did not reach the Banco de España source for the 64-day figure. Cepyme's 2026 (H1/Q2) observatory could not be checked because cepyme.es returned 503 errors. The BOE resolution setting the H2 2026 default-interest rate was not verified. The Ley 3/2004 consolidated text could not be read (404).
- No official data links business closures to late payment. PMcM's headline "la morosidad aboca a la quiebra a empresas viables" (late payment pushes viable firms into bankruptcy) has no published share behind it.

## Key question 2 — Debt collection: how SMEs chase unpaid invoices, costs, success rates, court timelines, debtor registries, and the effect of the mandatory pre-litigation step (MASC, 2025)

### Takeaway
Since 3 April 2025, recovering an unpaid B2B invoice through the courts involves four steps:
1. Informal reminders.
2. A **documented MASC attempt** (the mandatory pre-litigation negotiation step), usually by burofax or certified email, followed by a wait of up to one month.
3. A monitorio (the fast court payment-order procedure) or a declarative claim.
4. For amounts above €2,000, enforcement with a lawyer and procurador (court representative).

Monitorio filings fell **20.7%** year-on-year in Q2 2026, and 31.3% in Madrid, while Madrid's court backlog grew. Collection agencies charge 5–25% of what they recover or flat fees from about €100 per file. Debtor registries (ASNEF Empresas, RAI) are controlled by credit bureaus and are reached through paid reports.

### Cited Findings
- **The MASC requirement:** Ley Orgánica 1/2025 entered into force on 3 April 2025 and made a prior MASC attempt a condition for filing civil claims (requisito de procedibilidad) — [Hay Derecho, 31 Mar 2025](https://www.hayderecho.com/2025/03/31/la-ley-de-eficiencia-procesal-y-los-masc-capitulo-i/); [BOE LO 1/2025](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2025-76)
- **It applies to the monitorio:**
  - Lower-court case law and judges' unification agreements have generally held that the monitorio is covered — [vLex](https://vlex.es/vid/proceso-monitorio-masc-1121357868); [Garón Abogados](https://www.garonabogados.es/blog/que-es-un-procedimiento-monitorio/)
  - On 6 June 2025 the Audiencia Provincial de Málaga (Section 6) confirmed the rejection of a homeowners' association's monitorio because no MASC had been attempted — [ETL Ilia](https://etl-ilia.es/blog-asesoria-legal/masc-monitorio-comunidades-propietarios/); [Duplo Abogados](https://www.duploabogados.com/monitorio-lph-masc-lo-1-2025/) (search summary)
  - Some authors disagree; the European order for payment procedure is expressly excluded — [Castrillo Alonso Abogados](https://www.castrilloalonsoabogados.es/aplicacion-del-masc-en-impago-facturas-mercantiles-2026-guia-completa-ley-organica-1-2025/) (search summary)
- **Practical rules for a valid MASC attempt** — [Grupo Intercobros, 2026](https://www.grupointercobros.com/masc-y-reclamacion-de-deudas/):
  - Valid methods: burofax with acknowledgment of receipt, BuroMail/BuroSMS, notarial deed, conciliation, mediation, a confidential binding offer (oferta vinculante confidencial), collaborative law, certified email and documented direct negotiation.
  - Not valid: WhatsApp, ordinary SMS, unrecorded calls and unsigned emails.
  - If the debtor rejects the offer or does not answer within one month, the requirement is met (art. 17). A failed attempt stays valid for one year.
  - Not having attempted a MASC is an incurable defect.
  - Starting a MASC interrupts the limitation period (art. 7).
  - Exceptions include the juicio cambiario (claims on bills of exchange and promissory notes), claims against a public entity and urgent precautionary measures.
- **Other procedural changes:**
  - Above €2,000, enforcing a monitorio that was not opposed requires a lawyer and a procurador — [BOE LO 1/2025](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2025-76) (search summary)
  - A law firm argues that after a failed MASC "no tiene sentido" (it makes no sense) to file a monitorio, and that creditors should go straight to a declarative claim. Its reasons: the monitorio prohibits notification by public notice (edictal), so files are archived when the debtor cannot be found, and any opposition converts the case into a declarative procedure, losing months. ### Inferences
- Inference: the fall in monitorio filings coincides with the MASC requirement, but the drop is not proven to be caused by it (the TSJ Madrid president urges caution). For creditors, the day-to-day effect is an extra mandatory step that must be documented and timed, can be done wrongly, and adds about a month before filing.
- Inference: the MASC step creates repeat demand for cheap, legally valid notifications and an evidence pack. Today this is sold piecemeal by burofax services, collection agencies and law firms. A tool built into invoicing or receivables software could automate the whole chain: dunning (reminders), MASC offer, evidence, monitorio filing and interest calculation.
- Inference: a minimum fee of about €100 per file, or a 10–25% success fee, makes chasing invoices of a few hundred euros uneconomic, so autónomos write them off.

## Key question 3 — Access to finance: bank credit conditions, ICO, SGR/CERSA, factoring, confirming, alternative lenders, bank fees and account opening

### Takeaway
Spanish firms currently report good availability of bank credit. In the ECB's Q2 2026 SAFE survey, availability is net +10, the financing gap is −4 and only 3% report financing obstacles. The cost is rising: across the euro area, a net 43% of SMEs reported higher loan rates. Demand for working-capital finance is up, with 51.2% of pymes needing finance in 2025. Public guarantee and lending schemes are large (SGR €3,084M in 2025; ICO €10,265M approved) but reach firms through the banks, which set the margins. I found little evidence on bank fees for businesses and none on difficulties opening accounts. For established SMEs, access to finance is not a top pain; its cost and the paperwork are.

## Key question 4 — Payments: card-acquiring (TPV) fees, Bizum for businesses, cash use, chargebacks and payment fraud

### Takeaway
Cash is still the main way people pay in physical shops: 57% of consumers in 2025, down from 59%. But cards are accepted by 94% of small retailers and hospitality businesses, and mobile payments by 88%. Fintechs have commoditised card acceptance (SumUp 0–1.49%; Qonto 0.8–1.2%; Stripe Terminal 1.4% + €0.10), while online sellers pay €20 per chargeback dispute at Stripe. Fraud in payment operations is now the Banco de España's main complaint focus, and INCIBE (Spain's national cybersecurity institute) logged 45,445 online-fraud incidents in 2025 (+19%).

### Cited Findings

## Key question 5 — Insolvency and closures: concursos 2023–2026, the Ley 16/2022 micro-enterprise procedure, Segunda Oportunidad, autónomo closures and causes

### Takeaway
Insolvency filings (concursos) rose about 40% in 2025 and 20.2% year-on-year in Q2 2026. But about 90% of the people filing are individuals, mostly non-entrepreneurs using Segunda Oportunidad (Spain's personal debt-discharge route). Company insolvencies grew more modestly (+15.5% year-on-year in Q4 2025) and are concentrated in tiny, young firms in commerce, construction and hospitality. The Ley 16/2022 micro-enterprise procedure handles 22.5% of company cases but works almost only as a liquidation route: 52 continuation cases out of 1,427 in 2025.   - Company procedures in 2025: 4,911 ordinary concursos plus **1,427 micro-enterprise special procedures**, a total of 6,338. The special procedure's share was **22.5%**, against 22.6% in 2024. The typical micro applicant had liabilities of €251,946.
  - Only **52 continuation procedures** were identified. 46 had not been converted to liquidation when the study was written; 6 had.
  - Liquidations: 107 had an insolvency administrator appointed (7.5%) and 1,268 did not (88.9%).
  - **970 special liquidations concluded in 2025**, with a median duration of **169.5 days** (P25 83, P75 294, P95 558). Twelve continuation procedures concluded, with a median of 378 days, and their outcome is unknown.
  - In ordinary concursos, the median common phase in 2025 was 126 days for debtors with liabilities under €5M and 169 days for €5M or more.
- **Micro-enterprise procedure rules:**
  - It must be a liquidation if at least 85% of the debts are owed to public creditors or the debtor is not up to date with tax or Social Security — [Registradores Anuario 2025](https://www.registradores.org/documents/d/guest/anuario-concursal-2025); [AEAT](https://www3.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/normativa-criterios-interpretativos/analisis/El_credito_publico_en_el_procedimiento_especial_para_microempresas.html)
  - Eligibility: fewer than 10 employees and turnover under €700k or liabilities under €350k — [Registradores Anuario 2025](https://www.registradores.org/documents/d/guest/anuario-concursal-2025)
  - The debtor's participation requires a lawyer and a procurador — [Cámara de Comercio de Toledo](https://camaratoledo.com/procedimiento-especial-para-micro-empresas/)
  - A 2024 University of Valladolid student thesis calls the procedure "un fracaso" (a failure) on its agility goals — [UVa TFG, 2024](https://uvadoc.uva.es/bitstream/handle/10324/73780/1-TFG-D_%2001773.pdf?sequence=1&isAllowed=y) (low weight)
- **Segunda Oportunidad and public debt:**
  - Supreme Court ruling STS 450/2025 (full chamber, 20 March 2025) held that art. 491.1 of the insolvency code (TRLC), which absolutely excludes public debts from discharge, exceeds its legal mandate (ultra vires). Non-privileged public debts can be discharged through a payment plan.
  - The TRLC has not been amended, and courts still disagree on how to apply it.
  - In practice, AEAT and TGSS (the tax and Social Security authorities) resist, and discharge can be revoked for five years.

  — [Economist & Jurist, 1 Oct 2025](https://www.economistjurist.es/articulos-juridicos-destacados/el-fin-del-blindaje-publico-el-reciente-viraje-jurisprudencial-sobre-la-exoneracion-de-deuda-publica-en-la-segunda-oportunidad/)
- **Directors' liability:**
  - Supreme Court ruling **STS 1477/2026**: directors who do not convene the shareholders within **two months** of a dissolution cause (losses reducing net equity below half of share capital) can be **personally liable** for company debts that arise during that period.
  - Not knowing about a specific debt does not restart the two months. What matters is when the obligation arose, not when it fell due.
  - The case concerned a €42,000 claim.

  — [AyE, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/pymes/supremo-confirma-que-autonomos-societarios-pueden-asumir-deudas-sl-actuan-tiempo/20261022150439056494.html) (single source; the article does not cite the Ley de Sociedades de Capital article numbers)
- **Autónomos (RETA, the self-employed social-security regime):**
  - 3,483,645 registered at the end of September 2026, +14,229 on the month.
  - Commerce lost **763** autónomos in the month (described as a continuing haemorrhage), and hospitality lost 2,315 (seasonal).
  - Regions losing autónomos: Castilla y León −380, Galicia −298, Aragón −84. Regions gaining most: Madrid +3,642, Andalucía +3,289, Cataluña +3,121, Comunitat Valenciana +2,653.

  — [AyE / ATA, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/info-ata/septiembre-sumaron-mas-400-autonomos-dia-sigue-sangria-sectores-como-comercio/20261002153557056144.html)
- **Why autónomos leave:** the Estudio Nacional del Autónomo 2024 (Infoautónomos and the University of Granada; 1,163 respondents; June–September 2024) found that **90%** of those leaving RETA did so for economic reasons. Most earn €900–1,166.7 a month. The main barriers named were taxation (27.1%), the monthly social-security contribution (25.6%) and bureaucracy (21.2%) — [AyE, 31 Oct 2024](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/noticias-de-empresa/cada-diez-autonomos-que-dan-baja-reta-hacen-motivos-economicos/20241030121727039138.html). Separately, 4.5% of autónomos just below 55 leave their business, against 1.2% of those over 55 — [AyE, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/emprendedores/autonomos-mayores-55-anos-abandonan-menos-negocios-emprenden-necesidad/20260603120009054161.html)
- **Tax and Social Security debts** (cash-flow aspect; tax overlap):
  - Social Security has made payment deferrals (aplazamientos) easier for small debts — [AyE, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/seguridad-social/autonomos-pequenas-deudas-tienen-ahora-mas-facil-lograr-aplazamientos-seguridad-social/20261019174524056408.html)
  - Hacienda can seize autónomos' tax refunds, and the TEAC (the central tax appeals tribunal) has opened a route to challenge this — [AyE, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/hacienda/hacienda-puede-embargar-devoluciones-impuestos-autonomos-teac-abre-via-reclamar/20261019170607056403.html) (headline level)

### Inferences
- Inference: the headline "concursos +40%" mostly reflects consumer over-indebtedness. Company insolvencies rose 15.5% year-on-year in Q4 2025, but SLs rose only 5.5% over the whole of 2025. Individual entrepreneurs fell 10.8% in the CGPJ Q2 2026 data. The business pain is **late detection**: micro firms enter the special procedure already insolvent (96% liquidations), and directors risk personal liability if they miss the two-month window.
- Inference: Madrid and Valencia use the micro-enterprise procedure heavily while Cataluña rarely does, despite having the most concursos. This likely reflects differences in local court and professional practice, which matters for any regional go-to-market.
- Inference: software could help by monitoring the losses-trigger ratio from the accounts, projecting cash runway and preparing filings for the micro procedure. A rescue outcome still needs negotiation with creditors and public bodies.

### Gaps
- I found no data on the share of business closures caused by late payment, no fee levels for concursos or the micro procedure (lawyers, administrators), no count of Segunda Oportunidad discharges granted in 2025–2026, and no national 2025 annual total of insolvent debtors (only quarterly figures and the cumulative growth rate).

## Key question 6 — Shocks and insurance: the Valencia DANA (October 2024), Consorcio claims and aid delays, underinsurance, cyber-fraud (INCIBE), April 2025 blackout

### Takeaway
The DANA of 26 October–4 November 2024 (a DANA is a cut-off low that brings torrential rain) produced 251,940 claims to the Consorcio de Compensación de Seguros (CCS), the state body that pays extraordinary-risk insurance claims. By 19 October 2026 it had paid €4.66bn against an estimated total of about €4.8bn. Business claims (23,348) settled more slowly than household ones, and in June 2026 3% were still pending. Aid to businesses was large but split across AEAT direct aid, ICO guarantees, ERTEs (temporary layoff schemes), extraordinary cessation benefit for autónomos, and regional first-come-first-served calls.

New catastrophes followed in 2026: floods in Andalucía and Extremadura (February), earthquakes around Granada (August) and DANA "Adam" (16–18 October 2026), which brought 6,528 claims in four days, mostly from Valencia and Alicante. The CCS pays only if a policy was in force, and covers lost profits only if they were insured. The CEOE (employers' confederation) estimated the April 2025 blackout's cost at €1.6bn; I found no compensation scheme.

Treasury and multi-bank aggregation tools exist at SME prices: Banktrack €59–119 a month, Qonto's cash forecasting from €19 a month, and Agicap on annual contracts priced on quote. ## Pain-point inventory (structured entries)

### FIN-01: Large customers impose long payment terms and pay late
- Who: Suppliers in all sectors that sell to large corporates. Hardest hit: autónomos, micro (<10) and small (10–49) firms.
- Where: National. On-time payment is worst in Canarias and Melilla and best in Navarra, País Vasco and La Rioja.
- Day-to-day reality: Large clients write 60–90-day terms into their contracts and then pay later still. The supplier finances the client, chases payment, and cannot push back for fear of losing the account. Reported verbatim: "Las empresas que más tardan en pagar son las grandes" (The companies that take longest to pay are the large ones).
- Evidence:
  - PMcM: private-sector PMP 67 days in 2025; only 15% of large clients pay within the legal period; 60% of suppliers have >60-day contracts; 33% face abusive clauses — [PMcM, Apr 2026](https://pmcm.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Informe-Morosidad-2025_PMcM-VF.pdf)
  - 85% of large companies exceed the legal deadline — [PMcM, 22 Oct 2026](https://pmcm.es/pmcm-cinco-compromisos-contra-morosidad/)
  - Cepyme: PMP 80.5 days in H2 2025 — [AyE, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/pymes/pymes-solo-cobran-cada-tres-facturas-tiempo-ultimo-observatorio-morosidad/20260410165245053056.html)
  - Informa: large firms 14.00 days late on average in Q3 2026 — [Empresa Exterior, 17 Oct 2026](https://empresaexterior.com/el-retraso-en-los-pagos-se-enquista-por-encima-de-los-14-dias-y-lastra-al-tejido-empresarial-espanol/)
- Scale: More than 2.9M pymes and autónomos (Cámara de Comercio, cited by [AyE, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/pymes/reglamento-que-sancionara-empresas-pagar-tarde-autonomos-podria-estar-listo-2026/20251119125557046617.html)); 60% of surveyed suppliers have contracts beyond the legal term.
- Cost/impact:
  - €1,957M financial burden on pymes in 2025 — [Cepyme](https://cepyme.es/el-esfuerzo-financiero-de-las-pymes-como-consecuencia-de-la-morosidad-se-cifra-en-1-957-millones-de-euros/)
  - About €3,100M direct cost — [Informa via Empresa Exterior](https://empresaexterior.com/el-retraso-en-los-pagos-se-enquista-por-encima-de-los-14-dias-y-lastra-al-tejido-empresarial-espanol/)
  - Unpaid invoices equal to 3.3% of sales — [PMcM](https://pmcm.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Informe-Morosidad-2025_PMcM-VF.pdf)
- Driver & status:
  - Ley 3/2004 as amended by Ley 15/2010 (60-day maximum): **in force, no sanctions**.
  - The 2020 Junts bill on sanctions: **stalled**. No bill is pending after the Cortes were dissolved on 6 October 2026.
  - EU Late Payment Regulation COM(2023) 533: **blocked in the Council** (formal withdrawal not verified).
  - Crea y Crece: the subsidy condition for grants above €30k is in force, and e-invoice payment-status reporting arrives 2027–2029 (upcoming).
- How it is solved today:
  - External financing is the first choice of 71% (credit lines, factoring and confirming; Novicap etc., prices on quote).
  - 19% stretch their own payables.
  - Collection agencies (5–25%), gestorías and lawyers.
  - Complaint: 43% of suppliers think even an EU 30-day cap "won't be complied with" — [PMcM](https://pmcm.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Informe-Morosidad-2025_PMcM-VF.pdf)
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Software cannot change bargaining power, but it can monitor contract terms against the law, document breaches, automate claims, and score payers.
- Confidence: High.

### FIN-02: Late payment along subcontracting chains (construction and industrial services)
- Who: Construction subcontractors and trades, plus installation and maintenance firms. Mostly autónomos, micro and small (10–49) firms. Overlaps with the construction researcher.
- Where: National.
- Day-to-day reality: The main contractor waits to be paid by its client, sometimes a public body, and only then pays subcontractors. Subcontractors carry wages and materials for about three months.
- Evidence:
  - Contractor-to-subcontractor PMP 87 days in 2025 — [PMcM](https://pmcm.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Informe-Morosidad-2025_PMcM-VF.pdf); 88 days in 2024 — [AyE, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/pymes/reglamento-que-sancionara-empresas-pagar-tarde-autonomos-podria-estar-listo-2026/20251119125557046617.html)
  - Construction PMP 96.5 days in H2 2025 — [AyE / Cepyme, 12 Apr 2026](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/pymes/pymes-solo-cobran-cada-tres-facturas-tiempo-ultimo-observatorio-morosidad/20260410165245053056.html)
- Scale: Construction accounted for 15.4% of insolvent companies in Q4 2025 — [Registradores](https://www.registradores.org/documents/d/guest/np-epc-4t-2025b). I found no count of subcontractors affected.
- Cost/impact: Not quantified separately.
- Driver & status: Ley 3/2004, in force without sanctions. The blocked EU proposal would have required administrations to check that contractors pay subcontractors on time — [AyE, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/pymes/reglamento-que-sancionara-empresas-pagar-tarde-autonomos-podria-estar-listo-2026/20251119125557046617.html). Public-procurement rules on paying subcontractors (LCSP) were not verified in this session.
- How it is solved today: Confirming offered by the main contractor (the subcontractor pays to be paid early), factoring, lawyers.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Payment tracking along the chain and early-payment marketplaces are feasible. Whether subcontractors get paid sooner still depends on main contractors.
- Confidence: Medium-high.

### FIN-03: Statutory late-payment interest and the €40 compensation go unclaimed
- Who: All suppliers, especially autónomos and micro firms.
- Where: National.
- Day-to-day reality: Overdue invoices are chased for the principal only. Firms rarely calculate or add the statutory interest or the €40 per-invoice compensation, out of fear of upsetting the customer, lack of awareness, or the hassle.
- Evidence:
  - 89% never or almost never claim interest; 94% don't claim the €40; 30% don't know the law — [PMcM 2025](https://pmcm.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Informe-Morosidad-2025_PMcM-VF.pdf)
  - In 2024 the figures were 84% and 92% — [AyE, 20 Nov 2025](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/pymes/reglamento-que-sancionara-empresas-pagar-tarde-autonomos-podria-estar-listo-2026/20251119125557046617.html)
- Scale: About 9 in 10 suppliers surveyed.
- Cost/impact: Money left uncollected. Illustration: about €168 on a €10,000 invoice paid 45 days late, at 10.40% interest plus €40 — [Novicap, 15 Oct 2026](https://novicap.com/blog/morosidad-en-espana-2026/) (single source for the rate).
- Driver & status: Ley 3/2004, arts. 7–8 (interest and €40 compensation), **in force**. The rate is reset each half-year by BOE resolution (H2 2026 resolution not verified).
- How it is solved today: Manually by lawyers and collection agencies once a claim is under way. I found no evidence that invoicing tools automate it.
- Software-solvable?: Yes. Calculation, notices and evidence can be fully automated. The barrier is behavioural (fear for the relationship), so the feature should be optional and offered as a settlement lever.
- Confidence: High for the data; medium for willingness to use it.

### FIN-04: Public-sector late payment concentrated in specific regions and cities

### FIN-05: Collection dates are unpredictable, which breaks cash planning
- Who: Everyone, most acutely micro firms and autónomos, hospitality, agriculture and energy.
- Where: National. Worst in Canarias (23.16 days late) and Melilla (23.56).
- Day-to-day reality: Fewer than half of invoices are paid on the due date, so owners cannot know whether they will cover payroll, Social Security contributions and VAT. They juggle overdrafts and delay their own suppliers.
- Evidence:

- Scale: National. The Informa sample covered invoices worth €18,617M.
- Cost/impact: About €3,100M direct cost (Informa); 5.23% of payments more than 60 days late.
- Driver & status: Market practice; no specific regulation.
- How it is solved today: Excel and gestorías (assumed; no survey found), credit lines, and treasury tools: Banktrack €59–119 a month, Qonto from €9–19 a month, Agicap on quote.
- Software-solvable?: Yes. Forecasting each customer's payment date from its history, plus automatic reminders. The data advantage grows once e-invoice payment statuses arrive in 2027–2029.
- Confidence: High for the data; medium for the solution fit.

### FIN-06: The mandatory pre-litigation step (MASC) before claiming unpaid invoices
- Who: All creditors, especially autónomos and micro firms with small claims, and homeowners' associations.
- Where: National. Madrid monitorios −31.3%; Audiencia Provincial de Málaga ruling of June 2025.
- Day-to-day reality: Before filing a monitorio or a verbal claim, the creditor must send a documented negotiation offer that can be proven (burofax, certified email or SMS, notary or mediation) and wait up to a month. A WhatsApp message or phone call does not count. Filing without a valid attempt is an incurable defect and the claim is rejected.
- Evidence:
  - LO 1/2025 in force since 3 April 2025 — [Hay Derecho, 31 Mar 2025](https://www.hayderecho.com/2025/03/31/la-ley-de-eficiencia-procesal-y-los-masc-capitulo-i/); [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2025-76)
  - Valid and invalid methods, the one-month rule, incurable defect — [Grupo Intercobros, 2026](https://www.grupointercobros.com/masc-y-reclamacion-de-deudas/)
  - Courts apply the requirement to the monitorio — [vLex](https://vlex.es/vid/proceso-monitorio-masc-1121357868)
  - Monitorios −20.7% year-on-year in Q2 2026 — [Moncloa.com / CGPJ, 16 Oct 2026](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/10/16/cgpj-desahucios-lanzamientos-concursos-acreedores-3449594/)
- Scale: 179,846 monitorios in Q2 2026 alone, each of which now needs a MASC attempt.
- Cost/impact: An extra certified communication (burofax price not verified), legal fees (RedClaimer from €99.90 per file; a GW Abogados consultation €110), and about a month of delay. Rejections when the attempt is not valid.
- Driver & status: Ley Orgánica 1/2025, **in force since 3 April 2025**.
- How it is solved today: Lawyers, collection agencies (Intercobros, RedClaimer), certified-communication providers (BuroMail/BuroSMS). Complaint: confusion over which methods are valid, and claims rejected for a missing MASC — [ETL Ilia](https://etl-ilia.es/blog-asesoria-legal/masc-monitorio-comunidades-propietarios/).
- Software-solvable?: Yes. Software can generate a compliant offer, send it by a certified channel, track deadlines (one month to qualify, one year of validity), assemble the evidence pack and pass the file straight to the monitorio.
- Confidence: High for the law; medium for the size of its effect.

### FIN-07: Monitorio and court friction, and backlogs during the efficiency reform
- Who: Creditors in all sectors, especially micro and small firms.
- Where: National. Madrid's backlog grew 10.4% in 2025.
- Day-to-day reality:
  - In a monitorio, a debtor who cannot be located cannot be notified by public notice, so the file is archived.
  - Any opposition turns the case into a declarative procedure.
  - Enforcing more than €2,000 needs a lawyer and a procurador.
  - Courts are being reorganised (tribunales de instancia), and lawyers are holding back filings.
- Evidence:
  - No notice by publication; opposition converts the case — [GW Abogados, 20 Sep 2026](https://www.gwabogados.es/blog/morosidad-y-cobro/la-muerte-del-procedimiento-monitorio)
  - Lawyer and procurador required above €2,000 — [BOE LO 1/2025](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2025-76) (search summary)
  - Madrid: incoming cases −5.3%, backlog +10.4%, the president's caution about the fall — [Moncloa.com, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/07/15/tsj-madrid-ley-eficiencia-descenso-asuntos-3399983)
- Scale: About 180,000 monitorios a quarter; 825,114 pending cases in Madrid.
- Cost/impact: Lawyer and procurador fees (not verified), and months of delay.
- Driver & status: LO 1/2025, in force. The tribunales de instancia rollout happened in 2025–2026.
- How it is solved today: Lawyers, collection agencies, skipping the monitorio for a declarative claim, writing debts off.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Software can prepare the case, find the debtor's address and send status alerts. Court times are outside its control.
- Confidence: Medium.

- Day-to-day reality: Thin margins and fixed costs (rent, the monthly social-security contribution, taxes) exhaust cash within a few years. Owners exit, often through a no-asset concurso or simply by deregistering.
- Evidence:
  - Hospitality: 59.1% of insolvent firms under four years old; 61.7% of all insolvent firms eight years or younger; 46.8% with turnover of €250k or less — [Registradores Q4 2025](https://www.registradores.org/documents/d/guest/np-epc-4t-2025b)
  - 90% of those leaving RETA do so for economic reasons, earning €900–1,166.7 a month — [AyE / ENA 2024, 31 Oct 2024](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/noticias-de-empresa/cada-diez-autonomos-que-dan-baja-reta-hacen-motivos-economicos/20241030121727039138.html)
  - September 2026 RETA flows — [AyE / ATA, 23 Oct 2026](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/info-ata/septiembre-sumaron-mas-400-autonomos-dia-sigue-sangria-sectores-como-comercio/20261002153557056144.html)
- Scale: 3,483,645 autónomos; about 6,300 company insolvency procedures in 2025.
- Cost/impact: Closures; personal debts.
- Driver & status: Market forces (costs, demand). Tax and social contributions were cited as barriers by 27.1% and 25.6%.
- How it is solved today: Gestorías; no dedicated tool found.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Cash runway and break-even tools are feasible; weak demand is a structural problem.
- Confidence: Medium.

### FIN-19: DANA 2024 business recovery — business claims settle slowly and aid is fragmented
- Who: Shops and warehouses, industrial firms on industrial estates, offices and autónomos. Micro, small and medium firms.
- Where: Valencia province: Paiporta, Catarroja, Algemesí, Aldaia, Alfafar, València city, Benetússer, Massanassa, Sedaví, Picanya, Riba-roja de Túria, Albal, Beniparrell, Torrent, Chiva, among others.
- Day-to-day reality: Two years on, some business claims are still being assessed, especially industrial ones. Firms deal with several aid channels at once (AEAT, ICO, ERTE, cessation benefit, regional calls), each with its own procedure, and some calls are first come, first served.
- Evidence:
  - 23,348 business claims, 3.0% still pending, 4.6% of industrial claims pending (June 2026) — [CCS, 30th note](https://www.consorseguros.es/documents/10184/0/TRIGESIMA_nota_informativa_DEFINITIVA.pdf/e156c23e-f3a8-a031-0c46-bbd5c9d47001?t=1780910871713)
  - Aid figures: 27,466 AEAT direct-aid payments; 4,169 ERTE companies; 5,295 cessation benefits — [Moncloa, 19 Oct 2026](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/info-dana/Paginas/2026/191026-datos-seguimiento-actuaciones-gobierno.aspx)
  - EMDANA second call, €3.26M, first come, first served — [AyE, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/ayudas-subvenciones/comunidad-valenciana-abre-nuevas-ayudas-20000-euros-autonomos-afectados-dana/20260914161820055856.html)
- Scale:
  - CCS business claims: 16,861 shops and warehouses, 5,336 industrial, 1,076 offices; 4,685 lost-profit claims.
  - AEAT aid to 16,035 autónomos and 11,431 companies.
- Cost/impact:
  - CCS paid €1,148.5M to shops and warehouses, €928.0M to industrial risks and €40.9M to offices.
  - AEAT direct aid totalled €347.5M (about €5k per autónomo and €23.4k per company; my calculation).
- Driver & status:
  - CCS extraordinary-risk insurance: in force.
  - State aid decrees: in force; their numbers were not verified.
  - RDL 10/2026 tax exemption of aid: reported (single source).
- How it is solved today: Insurers, brokers and CCS loss adjusters; gestorías for aid applications; banks for ICO loans. I found no documented complaints about these services.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Claim documentation and an aid-eligibility and deadline tracker are feasible; settlement speed depends on loss adjusters.
- Confidence: High for the data; medium for the size of the residual pain.

### FIN-20: Catastrophes keep recurring in 2026, and each brings a claims burden
- Who: Businesses in areas exposed to floods and earthquakes, mostly micro and small firms.
- Where:
  - DANA Adam (16–18 October 2026): Valencia province (Oliva 2,760 claims), Alicante 847, Almería 163, Málaga 136, Illes Balears 127.
  - Granada metropolitan area: earthquakes in August 2026.
  - Andalucía and Extremadura: floods in February 2026.
- Day-to-day reality: After each event, owners must report the policy and bank details, photograph or keep damaged goods, keep repair invoices and wait for a loss adjuster. Lost profits are paid only if insured.
- Evidence:
  - 6,528 claims by 22 October and the CCS's conditions — [CCS DANA Adam note, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.consorseguros.es/documents/10184/0/PRIMERA_NOTA_DANA_ADAM_Def.pdf/6910f08d-ae58-d3d5-4a79-f780620b21a3?t=1792654884208)
  - Granada notes — [CCS news](https://www.consorseguros.es/noticias)
  - RDL 5/2026 emergency line — [ICO](https://www.ico.es/web/guest/linea-avales-emergencias-andalucia-y-extremadura)
- Scale: 6,528 claims within four days of DANA Adam, and still rising.
- Cost/impact: Not yet known for 2026 events.
- Driver & status: CCS regime **in force**. ICO emergency line **deadline 30 November 2026**.
- How it is solved today: Insurers and brokers; the CCS phone line 900 222 665 and website.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Claims documentation, guidance on which events are covered, and business-continuity checklists.
- Confidence: High for the facts; medium for the pain.

### FIN-21: Underinsurance and missing business-interruption cover
- Who: Micro and small firms, especially in commerce and hospitality.
- Where: National; the evidence comes from Valencia.
- Day-to-day reality: Firms without a policy get nothing from the CCS. Firms whose policy lacks lost-profit cover absorb the closure period themselves.
- Evidence:
  - The CCS pays only if a policy is in force, and lost profits only if insured — [CCS, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.consorseguros.es/documents/10184/0/PRIMERA_NOTA_DANA_ADAM_Def.pdf/6910f08d-ae58-d3d5-4a79-f780620b21a3?t=1792654884208)
  - 37,384 DANA claims denied as duplicates or for lack of cover; 4,685 lost-profit claims — [CCS, 30th note](https://www.consorseguros.es/documents/10184/0/TRIGESIMA_nota_informativa_DEFINITIVA.pdf/e156c23e-f3a8-a031-0c46-bbd5c9d47001?t=1780910871713)
  - Munich Re: €10.7bn damage, €4.1bn insured — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2024_Spanish_floods) (aggregator)
- Scale: No SME-specific statistic was found.
- Cost/impact: My derivation: about 60% of DANA damage was uninsured (including public assets).
- Driver & status: Market (voluntary insurance) and the CCS rules, in force.
- How it is solved today: Brokers and insurers. I found no evidence on documented complaints or on price levels.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Coverage-gap checks and broker leads; the policy itself is still sold by insurers.
- Confidence: Medium-low.

### FIN-22: The April 2025 blackout left business losses uncompensated
- Who: Hospitality, retail and industry, nationally.
- Where: All of mainland Spain.
- Day-to-day reality: About 10 hours without power. Card terminals stopped working, perishable stock was lost and production stopped. No compensation channel was identified.
- Evidence: CEOE estimate of €1.6bn; RDL 7/2025 revoked in July 2025; ENTSO-E final report of 20 March 2026 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/2025_Iberian_Peninsula_blackout) (single aggregator source).
- Scale: National.
- Cost/impact: €1.6bn (CEOE estimate).
- Driver & status: One-off grid failure. RDL 7/2025 **revoked**; no compensation scheme found.
- How it is solved today: Firms absorbed the losses. Whether insurance applied was not verified.
- Software-solvable?: Mostly no. Offline-payment and continuity planning help only at the margin.
- Confidence: Low.

### FIN-23: Cash forecasting and seeing all bank accounts in one place
- Who: Micro and small (10–49) firms with several bank accounts; startups.
- Where: National.
- Day-to-day reality: Cash positions are pieced together from bank apps and spreadsheets, or sent by the gestoría after the fact. Collections are unpredictable (see FIN-05).
- Evidence:
  - Tool prices: Banktrack €59 and €119 a month — [Banktrack](https://www.banktrack.com/precios); Qonto forecasting from €9–19 a month — [Qonto](https://qonto.com/es/pricing); Agicap annual contracts priced on quote — [Agicap](https://agicap.com/es/precios/)
  - Usage of Excel or gestorías: not found.
- Scale: Unknown.
- Cost/impact: €700–1,400 a year for Banktrack, at monthly list price.
- Driver & status: Market.
- How it is solved today: Excel (assumed), gestorías, neobanks, Banktrack, Agicap, Embat (for mid-sized firms and above).
- Software-solvable?: Yes, but the market is already served. Differentiation would come from predicting payment dates and linking forecasts to collection actions.
- Confidence: Low-medium.

### FIN-24: Cash crunches from tax and Social Security debts (deferrals, seizures)
- Who: Autónomos and micro firms. Overlaps with the tax researcher.
- Where: National.
- Day-to-day reality: When collections are late, owners defer VAT or Social Security payments, run into seizures of refunds, and fall out of good standing. That then blocks subsidies and forces liquidation in the micro-enterprise procedure.
- Evidence:
  - Social Security has eased deferrals for small debts — [AyE, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/seguridad-social/autonomos-pequenas-deudas-tienen-ahora-mas-facil-lograr-aplazamientos-seguridad-social/20261019174524056408.html)
  - Seizure of tax refunds — [AyE, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/hacienda/hacienda-puede-embargar-devoluciones-impuestos-autonomos-teac-abre-via-reclamar/20261019170607056403.html)
  - Forced liquidation if not up to date with tax and Social Security — [Registradores Anuario 2025](https://www.registradores.org/documents/d/guest/anuario-concursal-2025)
- Scale: Unknown.
- Cost/impact: Default surcharges and seizures (not quantified).
- Driver & status: General Tax Law (LGT) and Social Security collection rules, in force. The October 2026 easing is reported but not verified in the BOE.
- How it is solved today: Gestorías.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Deferral-request assistants and tax-cash planning.
- Confidence: Low-medium (headline-level sources).

### FIN-25: Mandatory B2B e-invoicing with payment-status reporting (upcoming)
- Who: All businesses. First those with turnover above €8M, then the rest, including autónomos. Overlaps with the invoicing researcher.
- Where: National.
- Day-to-day reality: From 2027 to 2029, invoice recipients must report acceptance or rejection and the actual payment date of each invoice. Accounts-payable teams take on a new reporting task, and the government gains real data on payment dates.
- Evidence:
  - RD 238/2026 and Orden HAC/1028/2026 (BOE 5 Oct 2026); deadlines 6 October 2027, 6 October 2028 and 6 October 2029 — [Sánchez Garrido Abogados](https://www.sanchezgarridoabogados.com/factura-electronica-obligatoria-plazos)
  - Status reporting framed "para reforzar la lucha contra la morosidad" (to reinforce the fight against late payment) — [Lealtadis](https://www.lealtadis.es/factura-electronica-b2b-estados-pago-pymes/)
- Scale: All B2B invoice issuers and recipients.
- Cost/impact: The cost of adapting software and processes (not quantified).
- Driver & status: Ley 18/2022 (Crea y Crece), RD 238/2026 and Orden HAC/1028/2026. **Upcoming deadlines:** 6 October 2027 (above €8M) and 6 October 2028 (rest), with status reporting required for smaller filers from 6 October 2029.
- How it is solved today: Not yet in force. ERP and invoicing vendors are preparing.
- Software-solvable?: Yes, and it is a data source for late-payment analytics.
- Confidence: High on the dates (two law-firm sources; the BOE was not read directly).

### FIN-26: Proving payment-period compliance to get subsidies above €30k
- Who: Small and medium (50–249) companies applying for subsidies, especially those that must file a full profit-and-loss account and therefore need an auditor. Overlaps with the subsidies researcher.
- Where: National.
- Day-to-day reality: To receive a subsidy above €30k, a firm must certify that it meets Ley 3/2004 payment periods. Firms that file abbreviated accounts self-certify; others need an auditor's certificate or an agreed-upon procedures report, usually within 10 working days of the provisional award.
- Evidence: Art. 13.3 bis of the LGS, added by Ley 18/2022 and amended by RDL 5/2023 — [BOE consolidated LGS](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2003-20977)
- Scale: Unknown number of applicants.
- Cost/impact: Auditor fees (not found). Risk of losing the subsidy.
- Driver & status: Ley 18/2022, art. 11 (amending the LGS). **In force since 19 October 2022.**
- How it is solved today: Auditors and gestorías.
- Software-solvable?: Yes. Calculating the payment-period ratio continuously from the payables ledger.
- Confidence: Medium.

## Top pains
Ranked by how severe and how widespread each pain is, using the evidence above:

1. **FIN-01: Structural late payment by large customers.** It has the widest reach (more than 2.9M pymes and autónomos; 85% of large firms breach the deadline; 60% of suppliers have contracts beyond 60 days) and the largest cost (€1.96bn for pymes per Cepyme; about €3.1bn per Informa). With the Cortes dissolved and the EU regulation blocked, no enforcement is coming before 2027.
2. **FIN-05: Unpredictable collection dates.** Only about 41–44% of payments arrive on time (Informa) and 30% of invoiced value (Cepyme). This is the root of day-to-day cash stress, worst for micro firms, hospitality and Canarias.
3. **FIN-06 and FIN-07: Collection friction after the MASC requirement.** A mandatory, easily botched pre-litigation step plus monitorio limits. Monitorios fell 20.7% (31.3% in Madrid) while the Madrid backlog grew 10.4%. This affects every creditor taking legal action.
4. **FIN-03: Statutory interest and the €40 compensation go unclaimed.** About 90% of suppliers forgo money they are legally owed. It is directly automatable and does not depend on any new law.
5. **FIN-12: Payment and invoice fraud.** It is the fastest-growing money-loss channel (INCIBE online fraud +19%, 017 queries +45%, fraud the BdE's top complaint focus), and losses are usually irreversible.
6. **FIN-10: Working-capital squeeze.** 51.2% of pymes needed finance in 2025 and euro-area bank rates are rising (net 43%). The cost of carrying late customers is the real burden, not access to credit.
7. **FIN-16 and FIN-17: Late detection of insolvency in micro firms.** Only 3.6% of micro procedures aim at continuation, and the October 2026 Supreme Court ruling exposes SL directors who miss the two-month window to personal liability.
8. **FIN-19, FIN-20 and FIN-21: Recurring catastrophes and insurance gaps.** €4.66bn of DANA 2024 claims, a slower business tail, and new 2026 events (DANA Adam, Granada, Andalucía/Extremadura). This pain is regional (Valencia, Alicante, Andalucía) but recurs.

## Noise and dead ends
- **EU Late Payment Regulation (30-day cap with automatic sanctions):** blocked in the Council since the 2025 compromise failed, and 43% of suppliers expect it would not be complied with anyway. No product should assume it arrives before about 2028; a formal withdrawal was not verified.
- **Spanish late-payment sanctions regime:** the 2020 Junts bill (fines of €6k–100k and above €1M) stalled for years in committee, and no bill is pending since the 6 October 2026 dissolution. Any SaaS premised on imminent fines is speculative. Watch the PMcM's party "traffic light" and the post-election programme instead.
- **Bank credit access for established SMEs:** SAFE Q2 2026 shows Spain with improving availability (+10), a negative financing gap (−4) and only 3% reporting obstacles. Not a top pain now, although cost is rising. Evidence for autónomos and startups is missing rather than negative.
- **Card-acceptance fees, POS terminals and Bizum:** already commoditised and cheap (SumUp 0–1.49%, Qonto 0.8%, Stripe 1.4% + €0.10 in person), and cards are accepted in 94% of small shops. Low opportunity, crowded market.
- **Official public-sector payment periods:** nationally they are within the legal limit (CCAA 27.05 days, central 26.46). The "public sector pays at 70 days" survey figure conflicts with official data. The real pain is local (Balears, Murcia, Palma, Las Palmas) and should not be generalised.
- **"Concursos +40%" headlines:** about 82% of insolvent debtors in Q4 2025 were individuals without business activity (14,414 of 17,632), and individuals of all kinds were about 89–91%. Most of the growth is consumer over-indebtedness. Company insolvencies rose 15.5% year-on-year in Q4 2025, but limited companies (SLs) rose only 5.5% over the whole of 2025. CGPJ filings by individual entrepreneurs fell 10.8% in Q2 2026. Do not read these headlines as an SME-distress boom.
- **DANA 2024 claims processing as a mass pain:** the CCS had handled 99.5–99.7% of claims by mid-to-late 2026. What remains is a business and industrial tail plus aid fragmentation, not a general backlog.
- **The April 2025 blackout:** a one-off event, with a single-source €1.6bn estimate and no compensation channel. Little that software can solve.
- **Debtor registries (ASNEF Empresas, RAI, EBE):** controlled by a few bureaus (Equifax, Experian, CCI), with no public prices. Hard for a newcomer to enter; better as an integration than a product.
- **Vendor statistics:** collection agencies' success rates (77%, 60–70%) and Kaspersky's "86% of SMEs attacked" are self-reported marketing figures, not evidence of scale.
- **INE insolvency statistics:** discontinued. The Registradores' Portal Estadístico Registral and the CGPJ are the live sources, and they count differently: about 14.6k debtors (Registradores) against about 19.0k filings (CGPJ) in Q2 2026.
- **Factoring, confirming, revenue-based financing and crowdlending prices:** could not be evidenced. Prices are quote-based and not public, so this stays a gap rather than a ranked pain.
