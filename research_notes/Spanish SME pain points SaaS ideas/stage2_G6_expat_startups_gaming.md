## 1. Summary

- **Reviewed:** 70 entries (23 EXP, 22 STA, 25 GAM), plus the user ideas U2, U4 and U5 and 18 sub-claims from the regional table.
- **Carried forward:** 35 entries (17 EXP, 7 STA, 11 GAM), grouped into 12 pain clusters. **Filtered out:** 35 entries: N1 weak evidence 9, N2 not software-solvable or context only 16, N3 postponed or likely to lapse 2, N4 saturated 2, N5 too niche 3, N6 legally risky 1, N7 duplicate 2.
- **Strongest expat theme:** the permit decision itself is fast (the UGE has 20 days, and silence counts as approval). The pain is everything around it: the TIE fingerprint appointment, hard personal deadlines, office queues that most offices do not publish, at least 20 rule changes in 17 months, and a 2027 renewal wave from 1,174,978 regularisation applications whose permits last one year. Software can prepare, validate, track and hand off. It cannot book appointments (resale is prosecuted) or file for others (filing is reserved to habilitated professionals). So the buyers are the filers, employers and HR teams.
- **Startups:** the pains closest to money are keeping the startup certification and its tax perks (483 of 2,529 certifications already lost), slow and conditional public funding, and R&D credits that loss-making startups leave unclaimed. Court-ordered IP blocking by ISPs during football broadcasts is a clean case for a low-touch monitor.
- **Gaming:** indie discoverability is the best-evidenced pain. The user's gaming pitches fail on three evidenced points: price (€30 against €5–10 for the genre's hits), the "shift away from shooters" (not seen in Spain), and the legality of real-money transfer markets (Ley 13/2011 and Twitch's rules).
- **Ideas:** 14 in total, 11 from the notes plus 3 reshaped user ideas. Primary types: A ×1, B ×9 (including one game, one gaming platform and one SaaS tool for game studios) and C ×4.
- **Verdicts:** U2 reshape (sell to habilitated filers, drop appointment booking, track national sources rather than "regional portals"). U4 reshape (an €8–10 streamer-vs-chat co-op game rather than a €30 premium one). U5 flagged for likely discard as specified, and reshaped into a points-only cross-platform companion for creator events. Regional claims are mostly partly supported or unverified; Valencia's "low-emission-zone logistics API" fit is contradicted and Madrid's "GovTech" fit is weak.

## 2. Pain clusters

### CL1. The critical path after approval, and personal deadlines, for relocating non-EU talent
- **Entries:** EXP-01, EXP-10, EXP-12, EXP-13, EXP-16, EXP-17 (STA-11 merged into EXP-10).
- **Problem:** This affects non-EU employees, digital nomads, students and their families, and the HR teams and agencies that manage them. It is national but worst in Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia. Once the permit is granted, everyone still needs a police fingerprint appointment for the TIE (the residence card).
  - Commercial sources report waits of 3–6 months (EXP-01). One monitor detected only 915 slots in Madrid, 361 in Barcelona, 95 in Valencia and 32 in Málaga over 138 days, and they "sell out in under 2 minutes" ([CitaEx](https://citaex.com/disponibilidad-citas)). The card then takes another 30–45 days.
  - Until the TIE arrives the person cannot get an FNMT certificate: the NIE "no es en ningún caso, elemento suficiente" ([FNMT FAQ](https://www.sede.fnmt.gob.es/registro-inicio/preguntas-frecuentes/-/asset_publisher/DKnDigPrgk4U/content/4-5-problematica-del-uso-del-nie)). That closes most online shortcuts (EXP-12).
  - Travel is blocked: a renewal receipt lets you leave but not re-enter, the EX-13 return authorisation lasts at most 90 days, and it cannot be issued while an initial application is unresolved (EXP-17).
  - Personal clocks keep running. Modelo 149 must be filed within six months of Social Security registration to keep a flat 24% rate instead of rates up to 47% for six years (EXP-10). US and other drivers whose countries have no exchange agreement have six months before they must retest, at €700–1,200 (EXP-13, single-source).
  - Family members queue separately: reunification takes 62–184 days and EU family-member cards 47–96 days (EXP-16).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):**
  - Police capacity and the design of the ICP+ booking system; RD 1155/2024 is in force, partly annulled by STS 868/2026 (BOE 22 Sep 2026).
  - Since 8 Oct 2026 a police pilot auto-assigns fingerprint appointments for 10% of resolved files in six big provinces. People who get nothing within 20 days must book themselves, and notices can go to the representative ([DGP notice via ICAB](https://www.icab.es/export/sites/icab/.galleries/documents-noticies/2026-INICIO-PROYECTO-PILOTO-ASIGNACION-AUTOMATICA-DE-CITA-PARA-TOMA-DE-HUELLA.pdf)).
  - Personalised appointment codes were announced but are unconfirmed. The travel relief in Instrucción 1/2026 expired on 30 Sep 2026.
  - The Beckham rules did not change in law in 2025–26; a TEAC criterion added deemed rental income on the main home. DGT's digital licence exchange has been live since 20 May 2025.
- **Used today, and price:**
  - Refreshing ICP+ yourself, or alerts (HayCita from €14.99 one-off).
  - Gestorías: full TIE service €90 including the fee (gestoriadeextranjeria.es), €80–160 at market rates (Cirera).
  - Relocation admin packs: €400 in Barcelona (Locallista), €1,200 + IVA in Madrid (Relocation Angel).
  - Lawyers for the EX-13; Beckham advisers at €500–1,500 for a single person or €800–2,200 for a family ([Klevvera](https://www.klevvera.com/blog/spain-digital-nomad-visa-cost-breakdown/)); TaxDown's Beckham calculator; gestorías and driving schools for the licence exchange.
- **Conflicts in the notes:** all TIE wait figures are commercial or anecdotal. Migralis says Cl@ve accepts the NIE, while FNMT requires a TIE for its certificate (these are different credentials). The legal window to book fingerprints ("30 días hábiles" or "un mes") is single-source.
- **Feeds:** G6-I04, G6-I05, G6-I06.

### CL2. Office queues nobody can see
- **Entries:** EXP-04, EXP-16, EXP-19.
- **Problem:** This hits HR teams setting start dates, relocation agencies, families and students, and it varies office by office. The official monthly PDFs from three Delegaciones show the same procedure taking 12 days or 232 days: an initial employee permit takes 12 days in Baleares and 232 in Valencia, where files from 15 Feb 2026 were still being resolved. Reunification takes 184 days in Baleares and study stays 75 days in Madrid ([España Abogados, 12 Oct 2026](https://espanaabogados.com/fechas-de-resolucion-de-tramites-de-extranjeria-en-espana/)).
  - Barcelona, Málaga, Alicante, Las Palmas and Santa Cruz de Tenerife publish no resolution dates (EXP-04).
  - The population affected includes 3,497,284 general-regime residents, 85,690 students and family members (up 52% in a quarter) and 523,821 EU family-member cardholders (EXP-16, EXP-19; [La Moncloa, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/serviciosdeprensa/notasprensa/inclusion/paginas/2026/200326-poblacion-extranjera-con-residencia.aspx)).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):** office capacity against legal deadlines under RD 1155/2024 (in force), plus the regularisation load. The deadlines themselves conflict in the notes: two months with negative silence, or three months or 45 working days with positive silence. Counter-evidence: Catalonia's emergency plan (plan de choque) cleared 99.8% of the ordinary files stuck before 16 Apr 2026 (expat §3).
- **Used today, and price:** España Abogados' compiled table and Parainmigrantes for Baleares (both free and static), lawyers' own trackers, complaints to the Defensor del Pueblo, and university international offices. No paid product.
- **Feeds:** G6-I03, G6-I04.

### CL3. Fast-changing rules and inconsistent office practice
- **Entries:** EXP-05, EXP-06, EXP-09.
- **Problem:** This affects lawyers, gestorías, relocation agencies and mobility teams nationally.
  - At least 20 rule and practice changes landed between May 2025 and Oct 2026 (EXP-06 count), through BOE decrees and orders, DGGM interpretive criteria, SEM instructions, UGE threshold notes and two Supreme Court rulings. The police fingerprint pilot was only findable as a bar-association PDF.
  - Outdated EX forms are a common cause of correction requests (subsanaciones), each giving 10 working days to respond ([GestoLab](https://www.gestolab.com/blog/formulario-ex-03-que-es-cuando-se-usa-y-como-rellenarlo-paso-a-paso)).
  - Job offers below the June 2026 thresholds (€41,356.36 general, €33,085.09 for the Blue Card) are a frequent cause of denials for highly qualified permits (EXP-09; [Infoextranjeria](https://infoextranjeria.org/2026/06/11/actualizacion-del-umbral-salarial-para-profesionales-altamente-cualificados/)).
  - Offices and police units ask for different documents, which led the DGGM to issue Criterio 1/2025 (EXP-05).
  - The professional audience is sizeable: 5,925 ICAM lawyers are registered on Mercurio, and Catalonia has about 2,000 immigration lawyers ([Law&Trends](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/administrativo/largas-colas-en-los-consulados-y-las-caidas-de-la-plataforma-mercurio-protagonistas-de-la-primera-jornada-de-1.html)).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):** all the norms are in force. The election slows new decrees but not new criteria (expat §3). The gestores' council says DGGM criteria "carecen de valor normativo" (have no regulatory force).
- **Used today, and price:** free sources only: ICAB and ICAM notices, the gestores' councils, and blogs and newsletters (Infoextranjeria, Machelin Díaz, Parainmigrantes, Extranjería Legal on Substack). Willingness to pay for tracking alone is unproven (EXP-06).
- **Conflict:** sources disagree on whether the June 2026 thresholds also apply to the national highly qualified permit (EXP-09).
- **Feeds:** G6-I02, G6-I12.

### CL4. The 2026 regularisation surge and the 2027 renewal wave
- **Entries:** EXP-03, EXP-07, EXP-22, EXP-23.
- **Problem:** The extraordinary regularisation drew 1,174,978 applications between 16 Apr and 30 Jun 2026: Cataluña 257,602, Madrid 202,424, C. Valenciana 167,286, Andalucía 161,557.
  - 83.2% (977,876) were filed electronically ([La Moncloa, 2 Jul 2026](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/serviciosdeprensa/notasprensa/inclusion/paginas/2026/020726-balance-regularizacion-extraordinaria.aspx)). By filer: lawyers 58%, habilitated civil servants 16.8%, gestores 8.4%, applicants with their own certificate 7.3%, registered union and NGO collaborators 5.1%, graduados sociales 3.6%.
  - The load landed on about 180,000 pending files, during a CCOO strike and Mercurio outages on day one (EXP-03, EXP-23).
  - Initial permits last one year, so renewals bunch up in 2027: up to roughly 1M, by the notes' inference. A Barcelona source warned: "El volumen de expedientes que nos puede venir va a ser inmenso".
  - Data errors (names, transliterations, inverted dates, NIE and passport mismatches, wrong 790 fee receipts) trigger 10-working-day correction requests and break the TIE stage, and CCOO reports recording errors and duplicated NIEs (EXP-07).
  - Lawyers charge €100–400 per file, and union and NGO collaborators must represent for free (EXP-22).
  - 337,917 regularised workers were Social Security affiliates by end-Aug 2026 (Funcas, search snippet). Hostelería accounted for 38,776 of the first 159,097 (EXP-03).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):** RD 316/2026 is in force. Filing closed on 30 Jun and in-person corrections on 30 Sep 2026; resolutions are still being issued. A Baleares lawyer estimates about 30% of early-filed cases still lacked provisional authorisation in October. There is no official API for Mercurio or ICP+.
- **Used today, and price:** manual review; GestoLab (AI validation, EX forms as editable PDFs, pricing "from 1€"); Cicerai (unverified); gestoría renewal €90; free help from NGOs and unions.
- **Feeds:** G6-I01, G6-I05, G6-I12.

### CL5. High-value UGE files for nomads, executives and founders
- **Entries:** EXP-08, EXP-09, STA-09 (STA-10 merged).
- **Problem:** The UGE held 174,357 permits in Sept 2025, including 44,966 digital nomads (DNV), 47,190 executives and highly qualified professionals, 6,083 Blue Card holders, 4,592 intra-company transferees and 4,818 entrepreneurs. Barcelona had 53,700 (15,073 DNV), Madrid 41,680 (8,087) and Málaga 19,056 (4,916) ([El Nacional](https://www.elnacional.cat/oneconomia/es/economia/barcelona-ya-supera-15000-nomadas-digitales-extracomunitarios-con-permiso-residencia_1549862_102.html)).
  - **Digital nomads (EXP-08):** applicants must show a company at least one year old, at least three months of prior relationship with it, months of bank statements that match payslips or invoices, insurance without copayments, and income of at least 200% of the minimum wage (SMI) plus 75% for the first family member and 25% per child. That is €2,760–2,770 a month in 2025 (Polaris) or €2,849 a month in 2026 (Anchorless, STA-10 merged). Gaps between contracts and deposits often trigger document requests (requerimientos) or denials. One commercial source says the UGE reorganised in H1 2026 and now often rejects outright (STA-10, single-source).
  - **Corporate permits (EXP-09):** thresholds reset when INE publishes the average salary. Door to door takes 8–14 weeks for intra-company transfers and 3–5 months for highly qualified and Blue Card permits (Deel).
  - **Founders (STA-09):** "Sin un informe favorable no hay autorización": no residence without ENISA's favourable report. 28% of Catalan startups have a foreign founder.
- **Cost:** a single applicant's first-year DNV cost is €2,940–7,850 (Klevvera). Law-firm packages run €900–3,900 (Lexidy), €1,899 + €999 per renewal (Platinum Legal) and €2,000–5,000 (Latitude Law); a gestoría charges €498. Intra-company transfers carry €430–980 in hard costs. Deel charges a US$250 documentation fee, and Tarmack charges from $999 per permit (single-source).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):** Ley 14/2013, as amended by Ley 28/2022, is in force with no 2025–26 change in law. Tightening has come through criteria and practice (Criterio 2/2025), and thresholds were updated in June 2026.
- **Conflict:** the UGE fee is €73.26 in the expat notes (2026 amounts) but €70.40 initial and €75.60 renewal in the startup notes (Anchorless).
- **Feeds:** G6-I12.

### CL6. A fragmented relocation service market, and the filing reservation
- **Entries:** EXP-21, EXP-22.
- **Problem:** HR teams (SMEs to large companies) and people relocating themselves stitch together an agency, a lawyer or gestoría, a tax adviser and translators. Prices run from €181.50 per procedure to €3,900 packages and €7,500 white-glove moves, adding up to about €2,000–6,000 of admin per non-EU move before housing (notes' synthesis). Inclusions and IVA are unclear, and some claims cannot be checked (Lexidy states both 10K and 7K clients; JuroSpain cites unexplained "internal channels") (EXP-21).
  - Only habilitated professionals under convenios, habilitated civil servants, free union and NGO collaborators, or applicants with their own certificate or Cl@ve can file electronically. Agencies must therefore subcontract filing (EXP-22).
  - About 110 companies are registered under "Relocation" (Empresite, search snippet).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):** market structure, plus Ley 39/2015 art. 5, the professional convenios, RD 1155/2024 art. 197 and Orden ISM/164/2026, all in force.
- **Used today, and price:** bundles from Deel and Jobbatical (Jobbatical's prices are not public); partnerships between agencies and law firms; the free Barcelona International Welcome Desk (more than 1,200 requests in 2024).
- **Feeds:** G6-I04, G6-I12 (as a constraint: sell to filers or hand off to them).

### CL7. Keeping the Ley de Startups benefits
- **Entries:** STA-01, STA-02, STA-20.
- **Problem:** By 11 Oct 2026, 2,529 Spanish startups had ever been certified, 2,046 still held the status and 483 (about 19%) had lost it ([Economía 3](https://economia3.com/2026/10/15/762915-certificado-de-empresa-emergente-2-529-startups-lo-tienen-pero-no-garantiza-el-visado/)). That compares with more than 5,000 active startups, and with ENISA's forecast of 5,000–7,000 certifications in 2023 alone.
  - Every perk depends on keeping the status: at most 5 or 7 years old, turnover up to €10M, at least 60% of staff on Spanish contracts, no dividends.
  - The perks are a 15% corporate tax rate instead of 25%, a 50% angel deduction on a €100k base, a €50k ESOP exemption instead of €12k, relief from forced dissolution, and points in council calls in Madrid, Valencia and Málaga. ENISA's CEO: "Los inversores cada vez lo piden más" ("investors increasingly ask for it") (STA-01).
  - ESOP tax falls due after 10 years even without an exit, and H1 2026 saw 19 exits, none with a disclosed value (STA-02).
  - Each angel needs a certificate from the company and must hold the shares for 3–12 years. Misinformation circulates: Venza claims a €250k limit, and Legalitas says ENISA's silence means rejection (STA-20, STA-01).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):** Ley 28/2022 is in force and unchanged since 22 Dec 2022, with no reform bill found. ENISA's silence counts as approval after three months (BOE).
- **Used today, and price:** ENISA's free portal and Guía de Certificación 2026; consultants such as Premoney and Managora (no published fees); lawyers and gestorías for certificates (no prices); Ledgy and Carta for equity (Spanish pricing not verified).
- **Feeds:** G6-I07. The founders' Beckham route (STA-11, merged into EXP-10) feeds G6-I06.

### CL8. Non-dilutive money that is slow to arrive or never claimed
- **Entries:** STA-04, STA-05.
- **Problem:**
  - **Public funding (STA-04):** ENISA lends €25k–€1.5M, but own funds must at least equal the loan, which is the main reason for refusal, and money takes 3–6 months to arrive. Neotec gives up to €250k in a single annual call, a company can win it only once, and payment takes 6–9 months. CDTI loans are often refused because the technical report does not explain the technology's value. The EIC takes about 12 months ([Tetuan Valley](https://tetuanvalley.com/blog-financiacion-publica-para-startups/)). By end-2024 ENISA had made about 9,000 loans to 7,800 companies, worth more than €1,400M.
  - **R&D credits (STA-05):** credits of 25–42% for R&D, plus 17% for researcher staff (up to 59% in total), can be paid in cash at a 20% discount when the company is loss-making. Yet a common mistake is to "descartar las deducciones por estar en pérdidas" (drop the credits because the company is losing money). 89% of Catalan startups invest in R&D, and 39% spend more than half their revenue on it ([ACCIÓ](https://www.accio.gencat.cat/web/.content/bancconeixement/documents/pindoles/ACCIO-analisi-ecosistema-startup-catalunya-2026-pindola-en.pdf)).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):** ENISA's 2026 offer has been restructured into five lines. The €303M FEPYME fund's window closed on 31 Aug 2026, and its status afterwards is not verified; Neotec's 2026 call details are not verified either. The R&D credit rules (Ley 27/2014, RD 1432/2003, RD 475/2007) are presumed in force but were not re-checked.
- **Used today, and price:** consultancies (fees not public), accelerators, fractional CFOs at €750, €1,250 or €2,000 a month ([Premoney](https://www.premoney.es/blog/cfo-externo)) and ENISA's client portal; R&D tax consultancies (fees not verified).
- **Feeds:** G6-I08, G6-I09.

### CL9. Collateral damage from court-ordered ISP blocking
- **Entries:** STA-16.
- **Problem:** This hits startups and online shops hosted on shared content-delivery networks, across Movistar, O2, MásOrange, Vodafone, Digi and smaller ISPs, during every blocked live broadcast. Since Feb 2025, ISPs and Telefónica have dynamically blocked IP addresses used by pirate LaLiga streams, and an order from Juzgado Mercantil nº 9 de Barcelona extends blocking to other sports, films and series. ISPs must apply the block lists within 30 minutes, so legitimate sites that share the same IPs go dark at peak hours, and the government has acknowledged the collateral damage ([Qué!, 17 Apr 2026](https://www.que.es/2026/04/17/bloqueos-futbol-operadoras/)). Startups say they lack the evidence to claim compensation (AES-tracked headline, 26 Aug 2026).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):** court-ordered blocking was in force and ongoing as of Apr–Aug 2026, with no appeals reported.
- **Used today, and price:** changing IPs or CDN settings, and complaints. No tool was verified and none is priced.
- **Feeds:** G6-I10.

### CL10. Indie discoverability depends on streamers
- **Entries:** GAM-03, GAM-06, GAM-08, GAM-09, GAM-10, GAM-24.
- **Problem:** Spain has 815 active studios. 54% bill under €200k (about 440 micro-studios), 55% self-publish, 82% of PC-focused studios rely on Steam, 39% need €50k–150k for their next project, and employment growth of 2.4% is the lowest on record (GAM-03).
  - Steam receives about 19–20k releases a year. Half of 2025's releases got fewer than 10 reviews and 40% earned under $100 ([MP1st](https://mp1st.com/news/40-of-steam-games-in-2025-earned-less-than-100)) (GAM-24).
  - The 2024–26 breakout hits spread through streamers (GAM-10).
  - Spain has about 10.5M monthly Twitch users, and Spanish is Twitch's fifth language at about 19.5M hours a week across the Spanish-speaking world ([Kolsquare](https://www.kolsquare.com/es/informes-y-guias/guia-marketing-influencers-twitch)). But 90% of streams average three or fewer viewers ([Que.es](https://www.que.es/2026/01/09/fenomeno-streaming-espana-videojuegos/)) (GAM-06, GAM-09).
  - Kick's Spanish-language growth comes from Latin America (GAM-08).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):** easy publishing plus scarce attention; 2026 is on pace for another release record. Hours watched on indie-tagged Twitch channels rose 51% (global).
- **Used today, and price:** Twitch Drops (mostly used by large publishers), manual key distribution, publishers, events (Mad Games Show 2026 had a €481,090 budget, including €88,000 for the "Meet to Match" B2B marketplace) and grants of up to €80k (GAM-24, GAM-04).
- **Feeds:** G6-I11, G6-I13.

### CL11. Cheap co-op party games: a real hit genre, but the user's pitch gets the price wrong
- **Entries:** GAM-10, GAM-11, GAM-13, GAM-18.
- **Problem:**
  - Cheap co-op "friendslop" titles were 4 of Steam's 10 best sellers by units in 2025 (R.E.P.O. about 18.5M copies, PEAK about 15.4M). They cost $5–20, and group play at least doubles effective spend. These figures are global and secondary, and PEAK sells best in the USA and Russia (GAM-10).
  - Spanish Steam prices on 23 Oct 2026: €2.99–9.99 for the proximity-chat hits, €12.99–22.99 for physics party games, and €39.99–49.99 only for Hazelight's story-driven co-op (GAM-13).
  - Spain still mostly watches League of Legends, Valorant and Fortnite (GAM-11).
  - Viewers may vote through chat, Channel Points or Bits as long as there is no wagering and no item-for-money exchange (Twitch Extension rules 5.2 and 6.2.x). Kick's API supports chat and webhooks but has no rewards or predictions endpoints (GAM-18; [Twitch rules](https://dev.twitch.tv/docs/extensions/guidelines-and-policies/)).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):** streamer virality plus groups of friends buying together. New titles keep arriving in 2026, so saturation risk is rising. The minors' digital protection bill is pending and likely to lapse (GAM-23, filtered N3), and 86% of 12–17-year-olds play (GAM-02, context), so voice chat needs safety by design.
- **Used today, and price:** more than 20 titles in the €3–23 band. Crowd Control (US$7.99 or $19.99 a month) already adds chat-driven chaos to more than 200 existing games (GAM-19, context).
- **Feeds:** G6-I13.

### CL12. Creator-led competitions and audience participation, within gambling and platform rules
- **Entries:** GAM-07, GAM-09, GAM-18, GAM-21.
- **Problem:**
  - Creator mega-events work. La Velada V peaked at 9.2–9.33M concurrent viewers, and La Velada VI reached about 8M on Twitch after being simulcast on Twitch, YouTube and TikTok (single-source) (GAM-07).
  - Native interactivity is tied to one platform: Twitch Predictions use Channel Points on a single channel, and Kick has no predictions endpoint (GAM-18).
  - Mid-tier creators earn little: the standard split is 50/50 and 90% of streams average three or fewer viewers (GAM-09).
  - Under Ley 13/2011, staking "objetos económicamente evaluables" (objects of economic value) that can be transferred between participants is gambling ("juego") (art. 3.a). Operating without a licence is prohibited (art. 9.2). Leisure games are excluded only if they produce no transfers of economic value (art. 2.2.a) ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2011-9280)) (GAM-21).
  - Context from filtered entries: the Kings League had €51M revenue but is loss-making and about 90% sponsor-funded. Its Spanish audience fell 54%, and in 2026 it opened a collective dismissal (ERE) and paused the Spanish league (GAM-14, GAM-15). There is no evidence that mid-tier creators want to run leagues (GAM-17).
- **Driver and status (Oct 2026):** Ley 13/2011 is in force (consolidated text to 31 Dec 2022). Twitch's rules are current as fetched, and simulcasting is becoming the norm for big events.
- **Used today, and price:** Twitch Predictions and Polls (free); fantasy apps (Biwenger, Comunio, LaLiga Fantasy; free with premium tiers); start.gg (6% fee on paid registrations), Toornament ($5 or $15 a month), Battlefy ($10 a month) and Crowd Control ($7.99 a month).
- **Feeds:** G6-I14.

### Conflicts noted across the G6 notes
- **TIE waits:** only commercial or anecdotal figures exist, with no official data (EXP-01).
- **Cl@ve and the NIE:** Migralis and FNMT disagree (EXP-12).
- **Reunification deadline:** two months with negative silence, or three months or 45 working days with positive silence (EXP-04, EXP-16).
- **Threshold scope:** whether the June 2026 thresholds cover the national highly qualified permit (EXP-09).
- **UGE fee:** €73.26 against €70.40 / €75.60 (expat §3 against STA §1).
- **Padrón in Madrid:** batch appointment releases against walk-ins (EXP-15).
- **ENISA:**
  - certified startups in Valencia: 275 against "en torno a 200"; nationally, 2,527 against 2,529 (STA-01);
  - what silence means: approval (BOE) or rejection (Legalitas) (STA-01);

### G6-I01: Renewal-wave case manager for immigration filers (2027 regularisation renewals)
- Type: B (primary), a new specialised tool. A (secondary): a narrower and possibly cheaper alternative to general co-pilots and manual paralegal work for high-volume, low-fee files.
- Buyer: the habilitated filers who carried the 2026 regularisation:
  - law firms (58% of 977,876 e-filings), gestores administrativos (8.4%) and graduados sociales (3.6%);
  - union and NGO collaborators (5.1%), who must represent for free and so would pay from their own budgets or grants (EXP-03, EXP-22).
  - Known counts: 5,925 ICAM lawyers on Mercurio, about 2,000 immigration lawyers in Catalonia (EXP-06) and 495 third-sector and union entities that advised in the regularisation (expat §5). The number of gestorías working in extranjería is unknown — validate.
- Pains addressed: EXP-03, EXP-07, EXP-22, EXP-23
- Problem: The regularisation brought 1,174,978 applications in ten weeks, and every initial permit lasts one year. Renewals therefore bunch up in 2027 (up to about 1M by the notes' inference) on the same offices, police units and fragile Mercurio platform (EXP-03, EXP-23). Filers earn €100–400 per file, and data errors trigger 10-working-day correction requests or break the TIE stage (EXP-07).
- Product:
  - **What it does:** imports a filer's 2026 client roster (spreadsheet plus filing receipts) and computes each client's renewal date and window. It sends clients a mobile link (WhatsApp or SMS, in their language) to upload documents. It extracts data from passports, TIEs, contracts, payslips and Social Security records, and cross-checks names, dates and numbers against the 2026 file and the EX form. It flags outdated form versions and outputs an editable EX form plus a numbered package and checklist for the filer to submit on Mercurio, logging receipts per client.
  - **MVP in weeks:** roster import, renewal calendar, document-request links, consistency checks on passport, NIE and names, and EX form PDFs for the renewal route that applies to the regularisation permits (DA 20 and 21).
  - **It does not:** file or sign (the habilitated professional does), book or monitor ICP+ appointments, scrape government portals, or advise applicants directly.
- Why now:
  - RD 316/2026 is in force. Filing closed on 30 Jun 2026 and resolutions are still being issued, so the first renewals fall due from mid-2027 (to confirm).
  - The 29 Nov 2026 election slows new decrees, but DGGM criteria keep arriving (expat §3). A new government could change the renewal rules, so the rules layer must be easy to edit.
- Alternatives today:
  - GestoLab is the main threat. Its AI checks that a file "esté completa y sea coherente" (is complete and consistent), and it generates EX forms (EX-01, 02, 03, 07 and others) as editable PDFs before Mercurio filing, priced "from 1€" with a demo.
  - Cicerai (AI form-filling for immigration lawyers; Spain focus unverified), manual review, gestoría renewals at €90 (gestoriadeextranjeria.es) and free NGO help.
  - Gap: the notes show no product with bulk renewal campaigns, client-side document chasing or roster import.
- Price hypothesis: €3–5 per active renewal case, or €49 a month (solo) to €149 a month (office) with case quotas; an at-cost tier for NGOs.
- Channels: the extranjería sections of ICAM, ICAB and CICAC; the Consejo General de Gestores and provincial colegios (Gestores Alicante circulated Criterio 1/2025); colegios of graduados sociales; union and NGO networks in the Registro de Colaboradores. Search terms: "renovación arraigo extraordinario", "renovación regularización 2027".
- Build notes: OCR and ID-document extraction; EX form templates with version control; WhatsApp Business API. There is no Mercurio or ICP+ API, so the output is a package for manual upload. The data is highly sensitive (passports, criminal-record certificates, family documents): EU hosting, processor agreements and filers' security due diligence are needed, and the GDPR art. 10 treatment of criminal records was not researched. Complexity: medium.
- Moat in an AI-flooded market: low to moderate. Switching costs once a filer's roster lives in the tool, distribution through the colegios, and an outcome dataset (which errors trigger corrections at which office). The engine is copyable, and GestoLab could add renewals quickly.
- Key risks and kill criteria:
  - **Kill or pivot triggers:** renewal of the DA 20/21 permits turns out to be simplified or automatic; GestoLab ships bulk renewals at around €1 per case (pivot to the NGO tier).
  - **Other risks:** filers on €100–400 fees may refuse to pay per case. Legal: liability as a processor of criminal-record data, and any feature that drifts into representation would breach the reservation (EXP-22).
  - **Kill criterion:** fewer than 10 of 30 interviewed filers report a 2027 renewal book above 100 clients, or more than one hour of work per renewal file.
- Evidence strength: medium. The size of the surge, the filer shares and the one-year validity are official (La Moncloa); the 2027 volume is the notes' inference and error rates are not published.
- Validation questions:
  1. In the BOE text of RD 316/2026 (DA 20 and 21): permit validity, renewal route, renewal window and required documents. Is any renewal automatic?
  2. GestoLab: real pricing beyond "from 1€", support for renewals and batch work, number of clients.
  3. Cicerai: is it active in Spain, for which procedures and at what price?
  4. How many gestorías and graduados sociales filed regularisation files (DGGM or Consejo General de Gestores data)?
  5. Interview 20–30 filers: size of their renewal book, hours per file, share of files with a correction request, current tools, willingness to pay per case.
  6. Does the police auto-assignment pilot (8 Oct 2026) cover renewal TIEs, and who receives the notices?
  7. What AEPD guidance applies to processors that handle criminal-record certificates and passports for law firms?

- Channels: the same colegios as G6-I01; LinkedIn groups of extranjería lawyers; search traffic on specific changes ("criterio de gestión 2/2026 DGGM", "EX-00 sustituye EX-12"); co-marketing with an existing newsletter.

- Moat in an AI-flooded market: none — speed only. Anyone can scrape the same sources, and GestoLab or a newsletter could copy it. The only lasting assets would be a curated archive of office practice and a professional community.
- Key risks and kill criteria:
  - **Risks:** free newsletters may be enough (the notes say willingness to pay for tracking alone is unproven); source access may be blocked. Legal: a professional relies on a wrong summary (disclaimers and human review needed); DGGM criteria "carecen de valor normativo", so they must be presented as practice, not law.
  - **Kill criterion:** fewer than 5% of free-digest subscribers convert to paid within three months, or interviews show colegio circulars already cover every change in time.
- Evidence strength: strong for the pace of rule changes (EXP-06 list); weak for willingness to pay.
- Validation questions:
  1. How many readers do Infoextranjeria, Machelin Díaz, Parainmigrantes and Extranjería Legal have, and do any sell paid tiers?
  2. What do ICAM and ICAB send members, how fast and in what format?
  3. Do legal databases offer extranjería alerts tagged by procedure, and at what price? vLex is named in the notes; Aranzadi and Lefebvre are not — check them.
  4. Is there a machine-readable feed for DGGM criteria and SEM instructions, or must the ministry site be scraped (403 risk)?
