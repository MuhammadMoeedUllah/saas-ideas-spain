# Spain's video game, streaming and creator-led gaming/sports-entertainment market (2024–2026)

Research date: 23 October 2026. Scope labels used below: **[ES]** = Spain-only data; **[ES-world]** = Spanish-speaking world (Spain + Latin America, not separable); **[GLOBAL]** = worldwide data with no Spain split. Steam prices were pulled live from the Steam store API with country code `cc=es` on 23 Oct 2026, so they are Spanish shelf prices in euros. Facts carry inline sources. Interpretation is kept to the "Inferences" sections and the "Opportunity type" lines. The session's shared web-search budget ran out at the end of the research, so three planned checks are listed under Gaps.

## 1. Market: consumer market, players, development industry, funding and notable studios

### Takeaway
Spain's consumer market **shrank 3.2% to €2,293M in 2025** while players grew to **22.8M**. Mobile is the preferred platform (43%) and PC only 28%. The development industry (**€1,464M in 2024**, 815 studios) is fragmented: 54% of studios bill under €200k. It is slow-growing (employment +2.4%, the lowest on record), centred on Catalonia, and dependent on Steam. It relies on small, EU-recovery-funded grants (around €80k maximum per applicant). 2025–26 brought layoffs and closures in Barcelona and Madrid, and Spanish breakout hits remain rare (Blasphemous passed 5M copies).

### Cited Findings
- AEVI calls Spain the third-largest video game market in Europe — [AEVI Anuario 2024, May 2025](https://www.aevi.org.es/web/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/VDIGITAL-ANUARIO2024-comprimido.pdf)
- The 2023 market grew about 16%, making Spain third in the EU — [El Observador (Europa Press), 22 May 2024](https://www.elobservador.com.uy/espana/cine-series-y-musica/videojuegos-espana-el-tercer-puesto-la-union-europea-un-crecimiento-del-16-n5941476)
- The latest GAMERGY (consumer esports/gaming fair) drew more than 63,000 attendees — [merca2, 28 Jul 2026](https://www.merca2.es/2026/07/28/espanoles-juega-videojuegos-2425797/)
- The DEV executive summary PDF (uploaded June 2026) returned HTTP 403 and could not be read directly — [dev.org.es PDF](https://dev.org.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/LibroBlancoDEV-Resumen-Ejecutivo.pdf)
- Structured entries GAM-01 to GAM-05 follow.

### GAM-01: Consumer market contracted in 2025 despite a record player base
- Who: Players (22.8M) plus publishers, platform holders and retailers selling into Spain.
- Where: National [ES].
- Reality: After a record 2024, AEVI's 2025 yearbook (published July 2026) shows revenue **down 3.2%**. Digital spending fell 4% and DLC fell 27.8%, while mobile apps and subscriptions grew 15.3%. AEVI also **restated 2024 downward** from €2,408M to €2,369M, so year-on-year comparisons must use the restated base.
- Evidence: (1) 2025 revenue was €2,293M, −3.2% vs €2,369M in 2024. Digital was €1,419M (−4%), including mobile apps at €567M and full games plus DLC at €509M. Physical was €873M (−1.3%), including hardware at €413M. Units sold: 999,197 consoles, 5,554,203 console games and 30,772 PC games — [Menorca.info (Europa Press), 20 Jul 2026](https://www.menorca.info/actualidad/tecnologia-videojuegos-1/2026/07/20/2673835/espana-tiene-millones-personas-juegan-videojuegos-dedican-media-horas-semana-jugar.html). (2) Digital was 61.9% of revenue; apps and subscriptions grew 15.3%; DLC fell 27.8%, which the coverage attributes to "the lack of major releases compared with 2024" — [merca2, 28 Jul 2026](https://www.merca2.es/2026/07/28/espanoles-juega-videojuegos-2425797/). The same total ("casi 2.300 millones", "almost 2,300 million") also appears in [Que.es, 29 Aug 2026](https://www.que.es/2026/08/29/dia-internacional-del-videojuego/). (3) As originally published, 2024 revenue was €2,408M (+3%) — [AEVI Anuario 2024, May 2025](https://www.aevi.org.es/web/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/VDIGITAL-ANUARIO2024-comprimido.pdf). Press coverage of that yearbook gave digital €1,558M (+19.75%) and physical €850M (−18.11%) — [euribor.com.es, 5 Jun 2025](https://www.euribor.com.es/2025/06/05/la-industria-del-videojuego-en-espana-no-solo-entretiene-sino-que-tambien-impulsa-la-economia/). The outlet was identified from a search summary; verify the split, which the 2025 yearbook has also restated.
- Scale: €2.29bn consumer spend in 2025 and 22.8M players — [Menorca.info, 20 Jul 2026](https://www.menorca.info/actualidad/tecnologia-videojuegos-1/2026/07/20/2673835/espana-tiene-millones-personas-juegan-videojuegos-dedican-media-horas-semana-jugar.html).
- Money: The split is 61.9% digital and 38.1% physical. Mobile apps (€567M) are the largest single digital line; hardware is €413M — [merca2](https://www.merca2.es/2026/07/28/espanoles-juega-videojuegos-2425797/); [Menorca.info](https://www.menorca.info/actualidad/tecnologia-videojuegos-1/2026/07/20/2673835/espana-tiene-millones-personas-juegan-videojuegos-dedican-media-horas-semana-jugar.html).
- Driver & status: Coverage attributes the fall to a weaker release slate after 2024, with DLC collapsing. **Status in Oct 2026:** the 2025 data (published July 2026) is the latest; 2026 data is expected around mid-2027.
- How it is served today: Retail is led by sports and mainstream franchises. GAME Spain's 2025 top sellers across all platforms were EA Sports FC 26, Pokémon Legends Z-A, EA Sports FC 25, GTA V, Minecraft and Assassin's Creed Shadows — [Vandal, early 2026](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia/1350788363/game-espana-revela-los-juegos-mas-vendidos-en-espana-durante-2025-con-un-claro-ganador/). The H1 2025 GfK top three for Spain were EA FC 25, Minecraft and AC Shadows — [Vandal, 2025](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia/1350782147/no-hay-duda-estos-son-los-videojuegos-mas-vendidos-de-lo-que-llevamos-de-2025-en-espana-y-europa/).
- Opportunity type: none (context only). The topline is flat to declining, so a new game must take share rather than ride market growth.
- Confidence: high. Two outlets reproduce the same AEVI 2025 figures, and the restatement is documented.

### GAM-02: Player base is broad, gender-balanced, mobile-first and near-universal among teens
- Who: 22.8M players, 47.5% of the population.
- Where: National [ES].
- Reality: Gaming is mass-market in Spain. Teen penetration is very high (86% of 12–17-year-olds) and seniors are a growing share. Mobile is the preferred platform, so the PC (Steam) audience is a minority of players.
- Evidence: (1) 54% of people aged 6–75 play; 86% of 12–17-year-olds; 28% of 65–75-year-olds. The gender split is 51% men, 48.5% women and 0.5% non-binary. Preferred platform: mobile/tablet 43%, console 29%, PC 28% — [merca2, 28 Jul 2026](https://www.merca2.es/2026/07/28/espanoles-juega-videojuegos-2425797/). (2) Players average 8.5 hours a week. The 2025 study extended its age range to 75 for the first time — [Menorca.info, 20 Jul 2026](https://www.menorca.info/actualidad/tecnologia-videojuegos-1/2026/07/20/2673835/espana-tiene-millones-personas-juegan-videojuegos-dedican-media-horas-semana-jugar.html). (3) 2024 had 22.1M players (+10.22%) — [AEVI Anuario 2024, May 2025](https://www.aevi.org.es/web/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/VDIGITAL-ANUARIO2024-comprimido.pdf). Coverage of the 2024 yearbook said more women than men played — [Que.es, 27 May 2025](https://www.que.es/2025/05/27/mas-jugadoras-videojuegos-espana/). That conflicts with the 2025 split, where women are 48.5%.
- Scale: 22.8M players — [merca2](https://www.merca2.es/2026/07/28/espanoles-juega-videojuegos-2425797/).
- Money: The sources found give no per-player spend.
- Driver & status: Smartphone ubiquity, and a methodology change (age cap raised to 75) that inflates the year-on-year growth. **Status in Oct 2026:** this is the latest data (July 2026).
- How it is served today: Mobile free-to-play leads usage. Console and PC are each under 30% preference.
- Opportunity type: none (context only). The 86% teen penetration makes minors' safety rules (GAM-23) central for any social or voice-chat game.
- Confidence: high.

### GAM-03: Development industry: €1.46bn, fragmented, slowing, Catalonia-centred and dependent on Steam
- Who: 815 active studios (495 incorporated) and about 10,500 workers.
- Where: Catalonia has 53% of revenue, 50% of employment and 32% of studios. Madrid has 23% of studios, Andalucía 13% and Comunidad Valenciana 10% [ES].
- Reality: Revenue growth has settled at about 3% a year since the pandemic boom. Employment growth hit a record low in 2024. Most studios are micro-businesses that self-publish on PC/Steam, while a handful of large firms hold half the jobs.
- Evidence: (1) **2023 data (DEV white paper, 11th edition, published May 2025):** revenue €1,425M (+3.1%) and 10,259 direct jobs. In 2024 there were 815 active studios, 495 of them incorporated. 54% of studios bill under €200k a year; 2% bill over €50M and account for 49% of employment. Business models: digital premium sales 41%, services 20%, work for third parties 16%. 88% develop their own IP and 55% self-publish. Platforms: PC 89%, Android 52%, Switch 49%. 82% of PC-focused studios use Steam as their main store. 39% need €50k–€150k for their next project, and 83% say they would be at risk if the crisis continues. Markets: Spain 44%, Europe 26%, North America 22%. DEV's president said "Los números no son para nada catastróficos" ("the numbers are not at all catastrophic") — [Forbes España (Europa Press), 27 May 2025](https://forbes.es/economia/722211/el-videojuego-espanol-facturo-1-425-millones-en-2023-y-empleo-a-mas-de-10-000-personas-de-manera-directa/). (2) **2024 data (white paper published July 2026, called the "2025 edition"):** revenue €1,464M (+2.7%) and employment +2.4%. DEV: "Registramos el menor crecimiento desde que tenemos datos, solo un 2,4%" ("we recorded the lowest growth since we have data, only 2.4%"). There are about 10,500 workers, women are 27% of them, and the forecast is over €1,650M by 2028. About half of studios say their viability is at risk if the crisis lasts another year — [Vandal, 8 Jul 2026](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia/1350791499/el-videojuego-espanol-factura-1464-millones-de-euros-pero-registra-su-peor-dato-de-crecimiento-de-empleo/).
- Scale: €1.464bn revenue (2024), 815 studios and about 10,500 jobs — [Vandal](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia/1350791499/el-videojuego-espanol-factura-1464-millones-de-euros-pero-registra-su-peor-dato-de-crecimiento-de-empleo/); [Forbes](https://forbes.es/economia/722211/el-videojuego-espanol-facturo-1-425-millones-en-2023-y-empleo-a-mas-de-10-000-personas-de-manera-directa/).
- Money: Premium digital sales are the leading model at 41% — [Forbes](https://forbes.es/economia/722211/el-videojuego-espanol-facturo-1-425-millones-en-2023-y-empleo-a-mas-de-10-000-personas-de-manera-directa/). A Vandal sidebar headline says only 6% of studios monetise subscriptions; the article body was not read, so this is unverified.
- Driver & status: Post-Covid correction, rising costs and a funding squeeze. **Status in Oct 2026:** the latest white paper (2024 data) was published in July 2026.
- How it is served today: Self-publishing on Steam, work-for-hire for foreign publishers, and public grants (GAM-04).
- Opportunity type: SaaS tool (possible). Roughly 440 micro-studios bill under €200k (54% of 815, an inference) and self-publish on Steam, which makes a defined but low-budget customer segment.
- Confidence: high. Two editions of DEV data were reported by two outlets.

### GAM-04: Public funding comes as small national grants plus regional calls, and depends on EU recovery funds
- Who: Indie studios and digital creators; AEVI and DEV as co-funded event organisers.
- Where: National (Ministerio de Cultura) and the Madrid region; Málaga has small prizes. Catalonia (ICEC) and Valencia (IVC) could not be verified.
- Reality: The national "ayudas para la promoción del sector del videojuego, del pódcast y de otras formas de creación digital" (grants promoting video games, podcasts and other digital creation) are capped at about €80k per applicant and funded by the EU Recovery Plan (PRTR / NextGenerationEU). The state also co-funds B2B market access through events.
- Evidence: (1) The 2024 call totalled €8M, funding up to 80% of project cost with a maximum of €80k per applicant. This figure comes from a search summary of the 2024 call extract, is single-source and should be verified in the BDNS (national grants database) — [BOE-B-2024-243, 4 Jan 2024](https://www.boe.es/boe/dias/2024/01/04/pdfs/BOE-B-2024-243.pdf). (2) The 2025 call extract was published on 16 Nov 2024 (BDNS 796539), is PRTR-funded, and excludes beneficiaries of the three previous calls — [BOE-B-2024-41842, 16 Nov 2024](https://www.boe.es/boe/dias/2024/11/16/pdfs/BOE-B-2024-41842.pdf). (3) Mad Games Show 2026 (Naves de Matadero, Madrid, 28–29 Apr 2026) ran under a Ministry–AEVI–DEV agreement with a budget of €481,090. The Ministry put in up to €240,350 and AEVI up to €240,740. The budget includes €88,000 for the "Meet to Match" B2B marketplace, and is funded by PRTR component 24 — [BOE-A-2026-928, published 15 Jan 2026](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2026-928). (4) Comunidad de Madrid video-game project grants were resolved by Orden 2405/2025 of 9 Oct 2025 — [BOCM, 22 Oct 2025](https://www.bocm.es/boletin/CM_Orden_BOCM/2025/10/22/BOCM-20251022-21.PDF). A new 2026 call (Orden 411/2026, corrected by Orden 505/2026; BDNS 892428) was found, but its amount was not — [BOCM, 19 Mar 2026](https://www.bocm.es/boletin/CM_Orden_BOCM/2026/03/19/BOCM-20260319-25.PDF). (5) In Málaga, the Premios Iris Games 2026 handed out €44,000 in prizes (single source) — [Ayuntamiento de Málaga communiqué, 2026](https://www.malaga.eu/visorcontenido/ANUDocumentDisplayer/179107/COMUNICADO.pdf).
- Scale: The 2024 national pot was €8M — [BOE-B-2024-243](https://www.boe.es/boe/dias/2024/01/04/pdfs/BOE-B-2024-243.pdf). That is about 0.5% of the industry's €1.46bn revenue (inference).
- Money: Grants of up to €80k compare with 39% of studios needing €50k–€150k for their next project — [Forbes/DEV, 27 May 2025](https://forbes.es/economia/722211/el-videojuego-espanol-facturo-1-425-millones-en-2023-y-empleo-a-mas-de-10-000-personas-de-manera-directa/).
- Driver & status: PRTR/NextGenEU funding. **Status in Oct 2026:** regional 2026 calls are active (Madrid), and the national 2026 call amount is not verified. Continuity after PRTR funding winds down is an unverified risk (inference).
- How it is served today: Grants, trade events (Mad Games Show, GAMERGY), and ICEX internationalisation support. ICEX's representative described the sector as "resiliente, internacionalizado, con empleo cualificado y joven" ("resilient, internationalised, with skilled and young employment") — [Forbes, 27 May 2025](https://forbes.es/economia/722211/el-videojuego-espanol-facturo-1-425-millones-en-2023-y-empleo-a-mas-de-10-000-personas-de-manera-directa/). The specific ICEX programmes were not verified. DEV maintains a grants directory — [DEV ayudas](https://dev.org.es/es/servicios/ayudas).
- Opportunity type: none (funding context). The ticket sizes fit a small game prototype, not a platform.
- Confidence: medium. Amounts are partly single-source, and Catalonia and Valencia are unverified.

### GAM-05: Spanish hits are rare, and 2025–26 brought layoffs and closures
- Who: Studios including The Game Kitchen, MercurySteam, Ubisoft Barcelona, King Barcelona, Secret 6, Aheartfulofgames and Tequila Works.
- Where: Seville, Barcelona and Madrid [ES].
- Reality: Spain has a few durable successes but saw a wave of cuts in 2025–26, from multinational studios in Barcelona to Madrid studios that closed after being acquired.
- Evidence: (1) Blasphemous (The Game Kitchen, Seville, released 2019) passed 5M copies sold — [Vandal, Jun 2026](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia/1350791155/blasphemous-celebra-sus-mas-de-5-millones-en-ventas-y-se-confirma-como-uno-de-los-grandes-exitos-espanoles/). (2) MercurySteam (Metroid Dread) began "un proceso de ajuste de nuestra mano de obra" ("a process of adjusting our workforce") in May 2026 without disclosing the scale. Press linked this to the commercial failure of Blades of Fire and NetEase withdrawing funding — [Vandal, 13 May 2026](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia/1350789773/dia-negro-para-la-industria-espanola-mercurysteam-el-estudio-de-metroid-dread-anuncia-despidos/). (3) Ubisoft Barcelona lost 51 employees in Ubisoft's 2026 restructuring and remains the main hub for Rainbow Six Siege — [VidaExtra, 2026](https://www.vidaextra.com/industria/ubisoft-va-camino-quedarse-vacia-cierran-dos-estudios-despiden-a-51-empleados-barcelona-380-puestos-corren-peligro); [Menorca.info, 11 Jun 2026](https://www.menorca.info/actualidad/tecnologia-videojuegos-1/2026/06/11/2648115/ubisoft-realiza-nuevos-despidos-afectan-equipo-barcelona-centra-desarrollo-rainbow-six-siege.html). (4) Secret 6 (Madrid) closed on 31 July with 42 staff, and Aheartfulofgames (28 staff) also closed. elDiario.es says Tequila Works "ya no existe" ("no longer exists") and that these studios were bought by larger companies before disappearing. King Barcelona faced more than 50 layoffs. This is single-source, and the article date (2025) was inferred from context — [elDiario.es](https://www.eldiario.es/1_bf4169).
- Scale: The layoff and closure items above total over 160 named jobs (51 + 42 + 28 + 50+), an inference that excludes MercurySteam's undisclosed number.
- Money: No sales figures were found for 2024–26 Spanish releases other than Blasphemous.
- Driver & status: Global industry contraction, rising development costs and publisher funding pullbacks. **Status in Oct 2026:** ongoing, with the latest items in May–June 2026.
- How it is served today: n/a.
- Opportunity type: none directly. It signals a pool of experienced developers in Barcelona and Madrid (inference).
- Confidence: medium.

### Inferences
- The Spanish consumer market is mature and flat, and DEV reports only 44% of studio revenue comes from Spain. Spain works as a launch market and talent base for a PC party game, not as its main revenue pool.
- PC is the preferred platform for 28% of 22.8M players, roughly 6.4M people. That is a generous upper bound on Spain's Steam-addressable audience for a PC-first party game.
- The typical Spanish indie scope (39% need €50k–€150k per project) matches a small co-op party game. A platform business would need private capital, because grants top out around €80k.
- City hubs: Barcelona/Catalonia (53% of industry revenue; Ubisoft and King layoffs), Madrid (23% of studios, Mad Games Show, regional grants, Secret 6 closure), Seville (The Game Kitchen; La Velada venue, GAM-07), Valencia (10% of studios) and Málaga (Iris Games prizes).

### Gaps
- Grant amounts from ICEC (Catalonia), IVC (Valencia) and the Junta de Andalucía, plus Málaga programmes, could not be verified. ICEX programme specifics ("Games from Spain" and others) were not found.
- The total for the national 2025/2026 video game grant calls was not confirmed beyond the €8M figure for 2024.
- The DEV executive summary PDF (403) was not readable, so funding sources, the share of loss-making studios, and DEV's tax-incentive asks are missing.
- No sales figures were found for other Spanish 2024–26 releases (Ninja Gaiden: Ragebound, Moonlighter 2, Neva, Blades of Fire).

## 2. Streaming culture: audiences, platforms, top creators and what goes viral

### Takeaway
Spain is a heavyweight in live streaming. It has about 10.5M monthly Twitch users, four of the world's ten most-followed Twitch channels, and the largest creator-led live event ever streamed (La Velada V, about 9.2–9.3M concurrent viewers). The audience is **superstar-concentrated**: 90% of streams average three or fewer viewers. Kick's Spanish-language growth is **driven by Latin America**, while Spain-based stars (Ibai, Rubius) stay on Twitch. Spain's most-watched games in 2026 are **League of Legends, Valorant and Fortnite**.

### Cited Findings
- A La Gaceta headline (undated) says Ibai passed Ninja and Kai Cenat to become the most-followed Twitch streamer in the world — [La Gaceta](https://www.lagaceta.com.ar/nota/1096448/mundo/ibai-llanos-hace-historia-twitch-supera-ninja-kai-cenat-como-streamer-mas-seguido-mundo.html)
- The concurrent-viewer record history: TheGrefg held it with about 2.5M (Fortnite skin, 2021) until Ibai passed it in 2022 with almost 3.4M — [Sherwood News, undated](https://sherwood.news/tech/how-twitch-became-a-spanish-language-platform)
- Structured entries GAM-06 to GAM-10 follow.

### GAM-06: Spain's Twitch footprint and what Spain watches
- Who: About 10.5M monthly Twitch users in Spain (third-party estimate) and over 1.1M active Spanish-language channels.
- Where: Spain for user counts and Twitch's Spain ranking; [ES-world] for language-level watch time.
- Reality: Twitch remains the centre of Spain's streaming culture. Twitch's own 2026 data ranks competitive titles first in Spain, while top creators mix Minecraft, simulators and new releases. The long tail is tiny.
- Evidence: (1) Spain is Twitch's sixth-largest market with 10.5M monthly users, 4.1% of the global audience (worldpopulationreview plus StreamsCharts, Mar 2026). Spanish is Twitch's fifth language by watch time at about 6%, or 19.5M hours a week [ES-world] (TwitchTracker/SullyGnome, Jun 2026). Four of the ten most-followed channels worldwide are Spanish — [Kolsquare, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.kolsquare.com/es/informes-y-guias/guia-marketing-influencers-twitch). (2) Twitch State of Gaming 2026 (1 Jan–1 Sep 2026): Spain's top three games are League of Legends, Valorant and Fortnite. Rubius passed 21M hours watched, and his most-streamed categories were Minecraft, TCG Card Shop Simulator and Marvel Rivals. Globally, hours watched on indie-tagged channels rose 51% year on year — [Vandal, 14 Sep 2026](https://vandal.elespanol.com/noticia/2026257023/twitch-supera-los-8600-millones-de-horas-de-gaming-vistas-en-2026-y-revela-los-juegos-y-streamers-mas-populares/). (3) 18% of Spanish social-media users use Twitch daily (2024 data, source not named). There are over 1.1M active Spanish-language channels, and **90% of streams average three or fewer viewers** — [Que.es, 9 Jan 2026](https://www.que.es/2026/01/09/fenomeno-streaming-espana-videojuegos/). (4) Twitch's Spanish web traffic was "relatively stable" while YouTube's was volatile (Semrush) — [PuroMarketing, 2025](https://www.puromarketing.com/163/215300/youtube-recupera-terreno-mientras-twitch-resiste-cambiado-trafico-digital-espana-2025).
- Scale: 10.5M monthly users [ES] and 19.5M hours a week [ES-world] — [Kolsquare](https://www.kolsquare.com/es/informes-y-guias/guia-marketing-influencers-twitch).
- Money: See GAM-09.
- Driver & status: A creator-centric culture that formed during 2020–22. Que.es describes it as "en una fase de consolidación tras el boom pandémico" ("in a consolidation phase after the pandemic boom") — [Que.es](https://www.que.es/2026/01/09/fenomeno-streaming-espana-videojuegos/). **Status in Oct 2026:** Twitch remains the default platform for Spain-based top creators.
- How it is served today: Twitch, with simulcasts to YouTube and TikTok for big events (GAM-07).
- Opportunity type: game (streamer integration). Spanish creators are a credible distribution channel; for Spain, a Twitch integration matters more than Kick.
- Confidence: medium. The top games are first-party Twitch data; the user counts are third-party estimates.

### GAM-07: La Velada del Año is the largest creator-led live event in streaming, but its peak fell in 2026
- Who: Ibai Llanos and invited creators/celebrities. Production by Telefónica's TBS in 2025.
- Where: Seville (Estadio La Cartuja in 2025 and 2026); audience [ES-world].
- Reality: Peak concurrent viewers rose from about 3.3M (2022) to about 9.2–9.3M (2025). In 2026 the show was simulcast on Twitch, YouTube and TikTok, and the Twitch peak fell to about 8M.
- Evidence: (1) Edition II (2022) peaked at 3.3M — [LevelUp, 2022](https://www.levelup.com/noticias/la-velada-del-ano-ii-ibai-la-rompe-en-twitch-con-3-millones-de-espectadores-supera-record-de-thegrefg/). Edition III (2023) peaked at about 3.45M — [LevelUp, 2023](https://www.levelup.com/noticias/ibai-vuelve-a-hacer-historia-la-velada-del-ano-iii-rompe-records-de-twitch/). Edition IV (2024) peaked at 3.7M — [LevelUp, 2024](https://www.levelup.com/noticias/ibai-llanos-y-la-velada-del-ano-4-rompieron-un-record-historico-de-audiencia-en-twitch/). ## 3. Fact-checks for Spain: shooter shift, esports decline, ~$30 price

### Takeaway
(1) The "massive shift away from competitive shooters toward premium comedic co-op" is **not supported for Spain**. Valorant and Fortnite are Spain's #2 and #3 most-watched Twitch games in 2026, and the co-op hits are cheap, not premium. ## 4. Creator-led sports entertainment: Kings League/Queens League, copycats and mid-tier demand

### Takeaway
The Kings League proved creator-led sport can reach **€51M revenue (2024–25)** with sponsorship as roughly 90% of income. It is **not profitable**, has burned €60M on international expansion, saw its **Spanish audience fall 54%** between early 2023 and 2024, and in June–July 2026 began an **ERE (collective dismissal)** and **paused the Spanish league for six months**. Funded copycats exist in Germany (Baller League, Icon League). I found **no evidence** that mid-tier Spanish streamers want to run their own leagues.

### Cited Findings
- Structured entries GAM-14 to GAM-17 follow.

### GAM-14: Kings League has fast revenue growth, no profit, and depends on sponsors
- Who: Kings League / Queens League (Kosmos/Piqué, Ibai). Team presidents include streamers such as Ibai, TheGrefg and Guarnizo — [Xataka, 14 Jul 2026](https://www.xataka.com/magnet/kings-league-nacio-2023-para-acabar-futbol-tradicional-tres-anos-despues-ha-declarado-ere).
- Where: HQ in Barcelona; leagues in Spain, Italy, France, Germany, Mexico and Brazil.
- Reality: Revenue is growing quickly, but international expansion pushed the company into losses, and almost all income comes from sponsors.
- Evidence: (1) Revenue was €51M in 2024–25, with €66M (+35%) expected for 2025–26. ## 6. Regulation and risk (gambling law, advertising, influencers, loot boxes, minors, platform terms, IP)

### Takeaway
Under **Ley 13/2011**, an activity is gambling ("juego") when money or **economically valuable objects** are staked on uncertain outcomes and **can be transferred between participants**, whatever the share of skill. Pure leisure is excluded only if it produces **no economically valuable transfers**. A "transfer market that mirrors real-money transactions" therefore sits squarely in licensing territory. The Supreme Court (**STS 527/2024**) struck down RD 958/2020's bans on celebrity gambling ads and on social-media and video-platform gambling ads. **RD 444/2024** brings only large influencers (at least €300k income and 1M followers) under audiovisual rules. The **minors' digital protection bill**, which bans loot boxes for under-18s and adds age verification and default parental controls, was still in Congress as of mid-2026. **I could not verify final passage by 23 Oct 2026.**

### Cited Findings
- The Ley 13/2011 consolidated text shows "Última actualización publicada: 31/12/2022" (last published update 31 Dec 2022) — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2011-9280)
- The DGOJ issues Spain's gambling licences (trade press) — [Focus Gaming News](https://focusgn.com/latinoamerica/dgoj-entrega-nueva-licencia-de-juego). Licensed operators earned about €1,700M gross gaming revenue in 2025, about 41% from sports betting (single source, via search summary) — [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/es/?p=105043)
- Structured entries GAM-21 to GAM-23 follow.

### GAM-21: Ley 13/2011 defines what turns predictions, virtual currencies or transfer markets into regulated gambling
- Who: Any operator of predictions, contests or markets with real-money value (idea b); game studios with paid randomised items.
- Where: National [ES] (state-level online activity).
- Reality: The definition of gambling turns on (a) staking money or economically valuable objects, (b) uncertain outcomes partly dependent on chance, and (c) transferability between participants. Leisure games are excluded only when they produce no economically valuable transfers. Unlicensed operation is prohibited.
- Evidence: All from the [BOE consolidated text (last update shown 31 Dec 2022)](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2011-9280). (1) Art. 3.a: "juego" is "toda actividad en la que se arriesguen cantidades de dinero u objetos económicamente evaluables en cualquier forma" ("any activity in which sums of money or economically valuable objects are risked in any form") on future, uncertain outcomes dependent to some degree on chance, "y que permitan su transferencia entre los participantes" ("and which allow their transfer between participants"). This applies whether skill or chance predominates, and prizes may be cash or in kind. (2) Art. 3.c: a bet ("apuesta") stakes money on the outcome of a predetermined event that is uncertain and outside the participants' control. (3) Art. 3.e: a "concurso" (contest) is offered, run and resolved through a media outlet, tied to its main activity, and requires payment or premium-rate participation. Free participation is not a concurso. (4) Art. 2.2.a excludes "juegos o competiciones de puro ocio, pasatiempo o recreo que constituyan usos sociales" ("games or competitions of pure leisure, pastime or recreation that constitute social customs") "siempre que éstas no produzcan transferencias económicamente evaluables" ("provided they produce no economically valuable transfers"). A price for the means to play is allowed "cuando éste no constituya en medida alguna beneficio económico para el promotor o los operadores" ("when it does not constitute any economic benefit for the promoter or operators"). (5) Art. 2.2.c excludes "combinaciones aleatorias con fines publicitarios o promocionales" (promotional prize draws), subject to Title VII. (6) Art. 10.1 requires a general licence (licencia general) per game category before operating; Art. 11.1 requires a singular licence (licencia singular) per game type. The text names the Comisión Nacional del Juego; in practice the DGOJ issues licences ([Focus Gaming News](https://focusgn.com/latinoamerica/dgoj-entrega-nueva-licencia-de-juego)). Art. 9.2 makes activity without a licence "prohibida" (prohibited) and sanctionable under Title VI.
- Scale: The licensed market is about €1.7bn in gross gaming revenue (2025; single source) — [Gambling Insider](https://www.gamblinginsider.com/es/?p=105043).
- Money: Licensing costs and fine amounts were not read (Title VI is beyond the portion of the text retrieved).
- Driver & status: Law in force since 2011. **Status in Oct 2026:** in force (BOE consolidated text). DGOJ guidance on fantasy, prediction or creator products was not found.
- How it is served today: Licensed operators only. Free fantasy apps and Twitch Predictions use points with no cash value or transferability (GAM-18, GAM-20).
- Opportunity type: none for real-money versions. A points-only design (non-purchasable or non-cashable, non-transferable, no prizes of economic value) is the viable path (inference; legal advice needed).
- Confidence: high for the legal text; medium for how it applies to specific designs.

### GAM-22: Gambling advertising after STS 527/2024, and the influencer decree RD 444/2024
- Who: Gambling operators and the creators they sponsor; large influencers ("usuarios de especial relevancia", users of special relevance).
- Where: National [ES].
- Reality: The Supreme Court annulled key parts of RD 958/2020, including the celebrity ban, new-customer promotions, and restrictions on information-society services, video-sharing platforms and social networks, because they lacked legal-rank backing. Separately, RD 444/2024 treats only very large creators as audiovisual providers for advertising and minors' protection duties.
- Evidence: (1) STS 527/2024 of 2 Apr 2024, on an appeal by Jdigital, annulled art. 15 (celebrities in ads), art. 13.1 and 13.3 (new-customer promotions), art. 23.1 (a general ban on commercial communications in information-society services), art. 25.3 (video-sharing platforms) and art. 26.2–26.3 (social networks). Other articles were upheld — [Noticias de Navarra, 10 Apr 2024](https://www.noticiasdenavarra.com/sociedad/2024/04/10/supremo-anula-parte-real-decreto-8099748.html); [LegalToday, 11 Apr 2024](https://www.legaltoday.com/practica-juridica/derecho-publico/publico/el-tribunal-supremo-anula-varios-articulos-del-real-decreto-958-2020-de-comunicaciones-comerciales-de-las-actividades-de-juego-2024-04-11/); [vLex](https://vlex.com/vid/tribunal-supremo-anula-varios-1029883895); [ECIJA note, Apr 2024](https://ecija.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/ECIJA_Nota-Informativa-STS-RD-958-2020.pdf). Jdigital welcomed it — [Yogonet, 12 Apr 2024](https://www.yogonet.com/latinoamerica/noticias/2024/04/12/98729-). (2) Under RD 444/2024 (BOE 1 May 2024, in force 2 May 2024), a creator is a "usuario de especial relevancia" if they meet **all** of: at least €300,000 gross income from video-sharing activity in the previous year; at least 1M followers on one service or 2M in aggregate; and at least 24 videos a year. Such creators had two months to register in the State Registry (Ley 13/2022 art. 39) and become subject to that law's minors' protection and audiovisual commercial-communication rules — [BOE RD 444/2024](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2024/04/30/444). (3) Betting and casino brands sponsor Spanish streamers, and some creators work with unlicensed casinos (no figures) — [Que.es, 9 Jan 2026](https://www.que.es/2026/01/09/fenomeno-streaming-espana-videojuegos/).
- Scale: Only top creators meet RD 444/2024 (€300k and 1M followers); the number registered was not found.
- Money: n/a.
- Driver & status: Litigation by the gambling industry, and transposition of the audiovisual directive. **Status in Oct 2026:** the annulments stand. I found no evidence that the annulled provisions were re-enacted by law; this is unverified.
- How it is served today: Agencies and self-regulation (not verified).
- Opportunity type: none. This is a compliance factor: gambling-linked sponsorship of a creator league would draw scrutiny.
- Confidence: high (BOE plus several legal outlets).

### GAM-23: The minors' digital protection bill bans loot boxes for under-18s and adds age verification, but its passage is unverified
- Who: Game studios, platforms, device makers and large influencers; minors (86% of 12–17-year-olds play, GAM-02).
- Where: National [ES]; an EU draft is in parallel.
- Reality: The government's organic bill would ban minors' access to random reward mechanisms ("cajas botín", loot boxes), raise the digital-consent age from 14 to 16, require default parental controls on devices, and require age verification from large influencers. It was admitted for processing in September 2025 under the urgency procedure and was still in Congress in mid-2026.
- Evidence: (1) The draft first approved on 4 Jun 2024 includes a "prohibición expresa a que las niñas, niños y adolescentes accedan a los mecanismos aleatorios de recompensa" ("an express ban on children and adolescents accessing random reward mechanisms"). The DGOJ says loot boxes "funcionan con la lógica de las máquinas tragaperras" ("work with the logic of slot machines"), and that almost 24% of 15–17-year-olds bought loot boxes in the past year — [Ministerio de Derechos Sociales, Consumo y Agenda 2030, 4 Jun 2024](https://www.dsca.gob.es/es/comunicacion/notas-prensa/ministerio-consumo-prohibe-acceso-menores-cajas-botin-videojuegos-evitar). (2) The Council of Ministers gave second-round approval on 25 Mar 2025. The bill bars under-18s from accessing or activating random reward mechanisms in video games, raises the age of consent from 14 to 16, requires free parental controls "activated by default", and requires large influencers to warn about harmful content and to "implement effective age-verification systems" — [Osborne Clarke, 25 Apr 2025](https://www.osborneclarke.com/insights/spain-seeks-protect-minors-digital-environment-key-points-new-version-draft-law); [La Moncloa, 25 Mar 2025](https://www.lamoncloa.gob.es/lang/en/gobierno/councilministers/Paginas/2025/20250325-council-press-conference.aspx). (3) Congress rejected Vox's amendment to the whole bill (Sep 2025), allowing it to proceed, and it is processed by urgency — [Ministerio de Justicia, 10 Sep 2025](https://www.mjusticia.gob.es/es/institucional/gabinete-comunicacion/noticias-ministerio/100925-tramitacion-lo-proteccion-menores-entornos); [BOCG 15-A-52-2](https://www.congreso.es/public_oficiales/L15/CONG/BOCG/A/BOCG-15-A-52-2.PDF). (4) As an organic law it needs an absolute majority, and Junts' votes were reported as key (Dec 2025) — [The Local, 1 Dec 2025](https://www.thelocal.es/20251201/today-in-spain-a-roundup-of-the-latest-news-on-monday-58). The EU Better Internet for Kids profile (June 2026) says the bill is "currently under parliamentary consideration in the Congress of Deputies" — [BIK Spain Country Profile 2026](https://better-internet-for-kids.europa.eu/sites/default/files/2026-06/ES%20Country%20Profile%202026_final.pdf). Minister Sira Rego was reported to expect approval in autumn 2026 (seen only in a search summary; attribution uncertain). (5) EU level: a **leaked draft** "EU Kids Act" would require age verification for new accounts in online games, social media and AI — [Profesional Review, 15 Sep 2026](https://www.profesionalreview.com/2026/09/15/union-europea-eu-kids-act-borrador-filtrado/).
- Scale: About 24% of 15–17-year-olds bought loot boxes — [DSCA, 4 Jun 2024](https://www.dsca.gob.es/es/comunicacion/notas-prensa/ministerio-consumo-prohibe-acceso-menores-cajas-botin-videojuegos-evitar).
- Money: n/a.
- Driver & status: Child-protection politics. **Status in Oct 2026: final passage and BOE publication NOT verified.** Congress bulletins (BOCG-15-A-52-1 to 10) returned 403 through the research proxy, and searches found no final vote. Treat it as pending.
- How it is served today: Platform age gates and PEGI (not researched).
- Opportunity type: none (constraint). A Spain-launched game should avoid loot boxes and plan age assurance and voice-chat safety for minors. A league product should avoid any randomised paid mechanics.
- Confidence: medium. The content is verified; the current stage is not.

### Inferences
- **Idea (b):** a transfer market where users pay money for value, stake it on uncertain real or in-game outcomes, and can transfer or cash out that value meets all three elements of art. 3.a. Running it without a DGOJ licence is prohibited (art. 9.2), and Twitch's clause 5.2 bans the same mechanic inside extensions. A "mirror" with **non-purchasable, non-transferable points and no prizes of economic value** is the only low-risk version, and it is what free fantasy apps and Twitch Predictions already provide.
- **Idea (a):** viewer voting on rule changes is not gambling when no stake or prize exists. Paid Bits votes appear permissible if they are not wagering (verify). **Proximity voice chat with strangers**, where 86% of 12–17-year-olds play, will attract minors' safety duties (age assurance, moderation, parental controls) under the pending Spanish law and the EU draft.
- RD 444/2024 covers only top creators. Most mid-tier streamers fall outside its registry duties, though the remaining RD 958/2020 provisions and the general advertising rules still apply to gambling promotions (inference).

### Gaps
- Ley 13/2011 Title VI fine amounts were not read (beyond the retrieved portion).
- The DGOJ's position on fantasy sports with paid entry, creator prediction products, or Sorare-style tradable cards in Spain was not found and needs a Spanish gaming lawyer.
- The final status of the minors' bill as of 23 Oct 2026 is unverified (Congress PDFs 403; no final-vote coverage found).
- Whether the government re-legislated the provisions annulled in RD 958/2020 was not verified.
- IP rules for using football club or player names (LaLiga, club trademarks, AFE image rights) were not researched beyond LaLiga's Sorare licence. This is open.
- Kick's developer terms and rate limits were not found on its docs landing page.

## 7. Pains of the actors: indie studios, streamers, esports organisations and brands

### Takeaway
**Studios:** the binding pain is **discoverability and funding**. Steam receives about 19–20k releases a year, half of 2025's releases got fewer than 10 reviews, and Spanish studios are micro-sized and self-publishing on Steam. **Streamers:** monetisation is thin and concentrated (GAM-09). **Esports organisations:** the national league economy contracted (GAM-12). **Brands:** sponsorship funds creator entertainment (about 90% of Kings League revenue), but spend concentrates on mega-events and English-language creators.

### Cited Findings
- Structured entries GAM-24 and GAM-25 follow. Streamer pains are in GAM-09 and esports organisations in GAM-12.

### GAM-24: Indie discoverability: a Steam glut and dependence on streamers
- Who: Spanish indie studios (54% bill under €200k; 55% self-publish; 82% of PC studios rely on Steam).
- Where: Spain (studios) on a [GLOBAL] storefront.

- Opportunity type: SaaS tool. A Spanish-language streamer-campaign or discovery tool for micro-studios fits the evidenced pain, though budgets are small (inference). Designing virality into the game, as idea (a) does, addresses the same pain from the product side.

- Confidence: low to medium. The brand-gap claim is unquantified.

### Inferences
- The studio pain (discoverability) is the best-evidenced pain in this scope, and streamer integration is a credible answer. The biggest friendslop moments were driven by US creators, so Spanish creators mostly unlock Spanish-speaking-world reach.
- Mid-tier streamers' pains (low revenue per sub, few viewers, a thin brand market) are real, but they also limit what those creators can pay for a SaaS league tool.
- Esports organisations in Spain are consolidating around media groups (Atresmedia/LastLap) and creator-owned brands (KOI). This is not a growth customer segment for new tooling (inference).

### Gaps

## Top findings (ranked by decision relevance)

1. **GAM-13 — €30 is off-band for this genre in Spain.** The viral proximity-chat co-op hits sell for €4.99–€9.99 and the best-known physics party games for €17.99–€22.99. Only story-driven co-op from Hazelight sells at €39.99–€49.99. Group buying multiplies the price friction. This decides idea (a)'s pricing.
2. **GAM-21 plus GAM-18 — a real-money transfer market or predictions layer is regulated gambling territory and is banned on Twitch.** Ley 13/2011 art. 3.a catches stakes of "objetos económicamente evaluables" that can be transferred between participants, and unlicensed operation is prohibited (art. 9.2). Twitch's Extension rules ban item-for-money exchange (5.2) and Bits wagering or contests (6.2.x). This decides idea (b)'s design: points only.
3. **GAM-15 — the category leader is retrenching.** Kings League Spanish viewership fell 54% from early 2023 to 2024. In June–July 2026 it opened an ERE for about 30% of staff and paused the Spanish league for six months, despite €51M revenue and €110M+ raised (GAM-14).
4. **GAM-11 — Spain did not "shift away from shooters".** Spain's top Twitch games in 2026 are League of Legends, Valorant and Fortnite, and the 2025 retail chart is led by EA FC and Pokémon. Cheap co-op games coexist with shooters rather than replacing them.
5. **GAM-06 plus GAM-09 — Spanish streaming is superstar-concentrated.** There are about 10.5M Twitch users in Spain and four of the world's top-ten followed channels, yet 90% of streams average three or fewer viewers. The mid-tier a league platform would serve is thin, and demand is unproven (GAM-17).
6. **GAM-10 — friendslop is a real hit genre.** Four of Steam's 2025 top ten by units were friendslop titles (R.E.P.O. about 18.5M, PEAK about 15.4M), driven by streamers. The data is global and cheap-priced, with no Spain split.
7. **GAM-23 — minors' rules are tightening.** The bill banning loot boxes for under-18s and adding age verification and default parental controls was in Congress as of mid-2026; final passage is unverified. With 86% of teens playing, proximity voice chat needs safety-by-design.
8. **GAM-24 plus GAM-03 — Spanish studios' core pain is discoverability.** The industry is fragmented (54% of studios bill under €200k) and employment growth hit a record low of 2.4%. Steam takes about 19–20k releases a year, and half of 2025's releases got fewer than 10 reviews. This is the clearest SaaS-shaped pain in this scope.
