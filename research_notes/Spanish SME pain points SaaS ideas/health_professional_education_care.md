# Pain points of Spanish private clinics, professional-services firms, education/training providers and care-service businesses (2024–2026)

Status date for all regulations: 23 October 2026. Method note for the report writer: 24 web searches were completed (Spanish and English), and then the shared search budget for this run was used up; five further searches were refused. After that, evidence came only from fetching pages that earlier searches had surfaced, plus four official texts fetched directly from the BOE to check their status. As a result, several sub-topics in the brief have no evidence and are listed under Gaps rather than guessed: FUNDAE, academies, SEPA receipt returns, parent-communication apps, cleaning/childcare agencies, architects, translators, marketing agencies, Top Doctors, telehealth and most clinic/practice-software prices. Where a fact comes only from a search-engine summary of a page that could not be opened, it is flagged "(search summary; page not fetched)". "Single-source" marks claims with only one source behind them.

## 1. Private health and wellness clinics (incl. veterinarians and pharmacies)

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced clinic pains are structural rather than operational. Self-employed health professionals are paid insurer fee scales that have been frozen for decades: about €17–19 for a specialist consultation and €5–20 for a psychology session. They have no collective bargaining power, and the CNMC (competition regulator) was formally asked for help in March 2026. Veterinarians face the RD 666/2023 regime: PRESVET antibiotic reporting at least every 15 days since 2 January 2025, fines up to €1.2M and limits on dispensing. RD 767/2025 eased the regime only partly. The operational pains are no-shows (12–19% of appointments, vendor-sourced), shortage management in pharmacies (about 11–12 h/week, EU average) and a new staff-credential record duty for every health centre (RD 239/2026, due about 1 January 2027). Cheap tools already exist for these.

### Cited Findings
- Specialist consultation fees paid by Adeslas rose from €18 to €19 in 2025, a 3.5% rise for private policies only. Asisa raised all acts by 6.5%, backdated to 1 January 2025 — [The Objective, 6 May 2025](https://theobjective.com/economia/2025-05-06/adeslas-asisa-suben-tarifa-medicos/)
- 88.76% of psychologists surveyed said insurers impose terms unilaterally. Insurers pay "desde 5 a 20 euros la sesión" (from €5 to €20 a session) — [Infocop (Consejo General de la Psicología), 13 Jun 2023](https://www.infocop.es/?p=50249)
- In March 2026 the physiotherapists' general council, with ATA and the Andalusian councils of doctors, podiatrists, psychologists, dietitian-nutritionists, dentists and speech therapists, met the CNMC about self-employed professionals' inability to negotiate with insurers — [Redacción Médica, 24 Mar 2026](https://www.redaccionmedica.com/profesionales/20260324/fisioterapia-eleva-competencia-las-demandas-del-colegiado-autonomo/260131_0.html)
- About 16,460 companion-animal clinicians had registered about 3.22M antibiotic prescriptions in PRESVET since January 2025, and 80% report automatically through web services (MAPA report, May 2026) — [MAPA PRESVET page](https://www.mapa.gob.es/es/ganaderia/temas/sanidad-animal-higiene-ganadera/Higiene-de-la-produccion-primaria-ganadera/medicamentos-veterinarios/Presvet.aspx) (search summary; the page returned HTTP 503 when fetched on 23 Oct 2026)
- 12–19% of private-clinic appointments go unattended, costing €2,500–7,500 per centre per month (studies not named, figures pushed by a vendor) — [El Independiente/Servimedia, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.elindependiente.com/sociedad/2025/09/29/las-ausencias-a-citas-medicas-cuestan-hasta-7-500-euros-al-mes-a-las-clinicas-privadas/)
- RD 239/2026 (BOE 26 Mar 2026, in force 1 Jul 2026) obliges every health centre to keep up-to-date records of all health staff and their official qualifications, within six months — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2026/03/25/239)
- Full detail is in PRO-01 to PRO-09 below.

### PRO-01: Frozen insurer fee scales (baremos) squeeze self-employed doctors and the clinics that depend on insurers
- Who: Self-employed specialist doctors (autónomos) and micro clinics on insurer panels (Adeslas, Asisa, DKV, Sanitas, Mapfre). Also private hospitals.
- Where: National. Mobilisation was strongest in Sevilla in 2023, then spread to other provinces. Also Andalusia (Consejo Andaluz de Colegios de Médicos) and Madrid.
- Day-to-day reality: Insurers send fee tables by email on a take-it-or-leave-it basis and refuse collective negotiation. A specialist first visit pays about €17–19 and a follow-up about €7–8, so practices must run high volumes to cover costs. Some doctors are leaving panels, especially in small provinces.
- Evidence:
  - Adeslas: +3.5%, private policies only. The consultation fee went from €18 to €19, while surgery, diagnostic and emergency fees stayed frozen (per Unipromel). Asisa: +6.5% on all acts, backdated to 1 Jan 2025. Insurers refuse to negotiate with associations, citing Article 101 TFEU. Unipromel cites the European Commission's 29 Sep 2022 guidelines on collective agreements by solo self-employed people — [The Objective, 6 May 2025](https://theobjective.com/economia/2025-05-06/adeslas-asisa-suben-tarifa-medicos/). Corroborated by [OKDiario, 10 Jun 2025](https://okdiario.com/economia/admitida-demanda-medicos-contra-concurso-muface-considerarlo-nulo-discriminatorio-14913438), which reports Adeslas going from €18 to €19 over three years and Asisa raising first consultations by up to 12% and other acts by 6.5% for MUFACE/ISFAS/MUGEJU patients.
  - Asisa paid €17.06 for an ophthalmology consultation and €20.71 for rheumatology (2022 scales supplied by Unipromel). Per patient, insurers pay a public hospital about five times what they pay a private doctor (Unipromel claim) — [The Objective, 15 Feb 2025](https://theobjective.com/economia/2025-02-15/medicos-autonomos-ofensiva-judicial-muface/)
  - Older baseline: €7–15 per outpatient consultation and fee scales "30 años... congelados" (frozen for 30 years). Insurers account for 65% of private hospital groups' billing (ASPE) — [Vozpópuli, 10 Nov 2022](https://www.vozpopuli.com/espana/divorcio-sanidad-privada-hospitales-aseguradoras-bajas-primas-medicos.html). Per-act examples: minor surgery about €48, second consultation about €7–8, tear-duct wash €7.21 — [Vozpópuli, 3 Oct 2022](https://www.vozpopuli.com/espana/consulta-operar-fimosis-tarifas-denuncian-medicos-privada.html)
  - About 1,900 Sevilla doctors stopped seeing patients from Adeslas, DKV, Asisa and Mapfre from September 2023, and the protest spread to seven provinces — [The Objective, 24 Sep 2023](https://theobjective.com/sanidad/2023-09-24/siete-provincias-ofensiva-medicos-privados-sevilla-aseguradoras/) (search summary; page not fetched; single-source)
- Scale: The top five insurers hold 71% of health premiums: Adeslas 27.8%, Sanitas 15.7%, Asisa 13.9%, DKV 7.0%, Mapfre 6.1% — [Infocop, 13 Jun 2023](https://www.infocop.es/?p=50249). 24% of Spaniards had private health insurance in 2022 — [Vozpópuli, 10 Nov 2022](https://www.vozpopuli.com/espana/divorcio-sanidad-privada-hospitales-aseguradoras-bajas-primas-medicos.html). No count of affected self-employed doctors was found.
- Cost/impact: Unipromel says fees were cut 10% in 2008–09 and not raised substantially for about 15 years, and it asks for +150% ([The Objective, 6 May 2025](https://theobjective.com/economia/2025-05-06/adeslas-asisa-suben-tarifa-medicos/)). Litigation: an appeal at the Audiencia Nacional against the MUFACE 2025–2027 contract (April 2025), admitted for processing ([OKDiario, 10 Jun 2025](https://okdiario.com/economia/admitida-demanda-medicos-contra-concurso-muface-considerarlo-nulo-discriminatorio-14913438)). The new MUFACE contract raised premiums paid to insurers by 41% (budget €4,429M) while doctors were left out of the negotiation — [The Objective, 15 Feb 2025](https://theobjective.com/economia/2025-02-15/medicos-autonomos-ofensiva-judicial-muface/). Quote: "muchos médicos en provincias pequeñas directamente se van a ir del cuadro" ("many doctors in small provinces will simply leave the panel").
- Driver & status: A market force (insurer concentration and individual contracts) plus competition law, which blocks collective fee bargaining for the self-employed. No specific regulation applies. Status Oct 2026: unresolved. The 2025–2027 MUFACE contract is in force and Unipromel's lawsuit is pending; outcome not found. A CNMC report was requested in March 2026; its status is unknown.
- How it is solved today: Leaving panels, moving to private-pay patients, litigation via associations (Unipromel), and occasional small increases. Unipromel suggests patients pay up front and claim reimbursement, and proposes a "Muface Directo" model in which the state pays doctors directly. No software tools were documented.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Software cannot raise fees. It can support payer-mix profitability analysis, private-pay acquisition and reimbursement-claim ("reembolso") paperwork for patients.
- Confidence: High that the pain exists (several outlets, consistent figures). Most fee figures originate from Unipromel, an interested party.

### PRO-02: Allied health professionals (psychology, physiotherapy, podiatry, nutrition, speech therapy) paid €5–20 per insurer session on terms set by the insurer
- Who: Self-employed psychologists, physiotherapists, podiatrists, dietitian-nutritionists, speech therapists and dentists working for insurers. Mostly autónomos and micro clinics.
- Where: National. The 2026 move to the CNMC was led by Andalusian councils together with the national physiotherapy council.
- Day-to-day reality: The insurer fixes the tariff, caps the session length ("20 a 30 minutos por sesión, en el mejor de los casos": 20 to 30 minutes per session at best) and limits session numbers. The professional either accepts or loses the panel referrals.
- Evidence:
  - 88.76% of respondents said insurers impose conditions unilaterally ("dicha negociación no existe": there is no negotiation). ATA says insurers pay "desde 5 a 20 euros la sesión", the professional receives "menos de un tercio" of what the insurer allocates, and tariffs have not been updated in "más de 30 años" — [Infocop, 13 Jun 2023](https://www.infocop.es/?p=50249). A related Infocop item reports a sample of 782 responses and complaints about caps on session numbers — [Infocop](https://www.infocop.es/?p=71679) (search summary; page not fetched)
  - Physiotherapy: insurers paid about €5 per session regardless of condition or length (AEF, 2020) — [Redacción Médica, 13 Aug 2020](https://www.redaccionmedica.com/profesionales/20200813/los-fisioterapeutas-claman-por-las-bajas-tarifas-abonadas-mutuas/264642_0.html) (search summary; dated)
  - The CGCFE (physiotherapists' council) asked the CNMC for a report on self-employed professionals' capacity to negotiate with insurers — [Redacción Médica, 24 Mar 2026](https://www.redaccionmedica.com/profesionales/20260324/fisioterapia-eleva-competencia-las-demandas-del-colegiado-autonomo/260131_0.html)
- Scale: 43,200 self-employed psychologists as of 31 Mar 2021 (Ministry of Labour, cited by [Infocop, 13 Jun 2023](https://www.infocop.es/?p=50249)). No counts were found for physiotherapists or podiatrists.
- Cost/impact: Income "often does not cover the cost of running a practice" (ATA, via Infocop 2023). Not quantified in euros.
- Driver & status: The same insurer concentration as PRO-01. Status Oct 2026: unresolved, with the CNMC route open.
- How it is solved today: Accepting volume, leaving panels, private-pay or online practice. No pricing data was found for therapy marketplaces.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. The tariff is not fixable by software. Practice tools could help professionals move away from insurer dependence (booking, payments, online sessions).
- Confidence: Medium. The fee figures date from 2020–2023; the 2026 source confirms the issue is current but gives no new numbers.

### PRO-03: No-shows and front-desk appointment work in private clinics
- Who: Private clinics of all kinds (dental, physiotherapy, psychology, aesthetic, podiatry, nutrition). Micro and small.
- Where: National. No city-level data was found.
- Day-to-day reality: Reception staff phone or WhatsApp patients to confirm, empty slots go unfilled, and clinics hesitate to charge deposits.
- Evidence:
  - 12–19% of booked appointments are not attended, costing €2,500–7,500 per centre per month with consultations averaging €50–150. The cited "Elsevier" studies are not named. The Klinikare CEO, a vendor, extrapolates more than €300M a year in losses — [El Independiente, 29 Sep 2025](https://www.elindependiente.com/sociedad/2025/09/29/las-ausencias-a-citas-medicas-cuestan-hasta-7-500-euros-al-mes-a-las-clinicas-privadas/). The same wire story ran in [Vozpópuli](https://www.vozpopuli.com/sanitatem/las-ausencias-a-citas-medicas-cuestan-hasta-7500-euros-al-mes-a-las-clinicas-privadas.html) and [The Objective](https://theobjective.com/actualidad/2025-09-29/ausencias-citas-medicas-clinicas-privadas/), so these are not independent sources.
  - Spanish studies put no-shows at 10–20% — [Medesk blog (vendor), updated 19 Sep 2026](https://www.medesk.net/es/blog/penalizacion-a-pacientes-por-no-acudir-a-la-cita/)
  - Public benchmark: Castilla y León primary care lost 752,932 of 23,586,954 appointments in 2024 (about 3.2%) — [The Objective, 11 Aug 2025](https://theobjective.com/espana/castilla-y-leon/2025-08-11/absentismo-medico-castilla-y-leon-2024/) (search summary)
- Scale: Doctoralia alone is present in 5,000 private clinics — [descubre.vc/El Economista, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.descubre.vc/noticia/doctoralia-factura-20m-en-espa-a-y-consolida-su-expansi-n-tecnol-gica-en-latam-y-europa-2026-02-20). No census of private clinics was found.
- Cost/impact: €2,500–7,500 per month per centre (vendor-originated estimate).
- Driver & status: Patient behaviour, a market issue. No sector regulation was found. The legality of no-show fees or deposits under Spanish consumer law was not verified; the Medesk page covers Colombia and France only.
- How it is solved today: Clinni at €12.95/month + taxes, with reminders by email, SMS and Telegram and TicketBAI integration for the Basque Country; SMS pack prices are not published ([Clinni pricing, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://clinni.com/precios/)). Doctoralia's automated reminders "para reducir el ausentismo" (to reduce no-shows), price not published ([pro.doctoralia.es, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://pro.doctoralia.es/)). Klinikare's AI receptionist claims "hasta en un 50%" fewer no-shows (vendor claim, no price).
- Software-solvable?: Yes, through reminders, deposits, waitlist auto-fill and two-way WhatsApp. But the market is crowded and cheap (about €13/month entry price).
- Confidence: Medium. The problem is real, but most numbers come from vendors.

### PRO-04: Dependence on a dominant booking directory (Doctoralia/Docplanner) for patient acquisition, with opaque pricing
- Who: Self-employed specialists and micro/small clinics.
- Where: National.
- Day-to-day reality: Clinics pay a subscription for visibility and online booking. There is no public price list, and prices are set after a sales call ("plan personalizado").
- Evidence:
  - Doctoralia Spain's revenue was €20M in 2025 (+25%), and it is present in 5,000 private clinics — [descubre.vc citing El Economista, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.descubre.vc/noticia/doctoralia-factura-20m-en-espa-a-y-consolida-su-expansi-n-tecnol-gica-en-latam-y-europa-2026-02-20)
  - Docplanner group: ARR of US$300M at end-2025, EBITDA-positive, 13 countries. Products include booking, telemedicine, remote payments and "Noa" AI clinical notes — [Forbes Chile, 1 Jul 2026](https://forbes.cl/negocios/2026-07-01/una-polaca-y-una-espanola-el-tripadvisor-de-los-medicos-suma-3-millones-de-visitas-al-mes-en-chile-y-hub-clave-para-ia/)
  - The pro site shows no prices, only "Sesión gratuita" (free session) and "Plan personalizado" (custom plan) — [pro.doctoralia.es, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://pro.doctoralia.es/)
  - In user reviews, a Spanish endocrinologist called the product expensive and a long-time user cited unjustified price increases — [Capterra](https://www.capterra.com/p/253301/Doctoralia-Pro/reviews/) (search summary; HTTP 403 on fetch; single-source)
- Scale: 5,000 clinics, plus individual specialists in unknown numbers.
- Cost/impact: Unknown. Inference only: €20M ÷ 5,000 clinics ≈ €4,000 per clinic per year if all revenue came from those clinics, so this is not a real per-clinic price.
- Driver & status: A market force (network effects). Nothing regulatory.
- How it is solved today: Doctoralia subscription. Alternatives such as Top Doctors were not verified (no evidence found).
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Own-booking, Google profile and reputation tools exist, but the directory's two-sided network is the hard part.
- Confidence: Low–medium. Prices and complaints are thinly documented.

### PRO-05: Veterinary clinics — mandatory PRESVET antibiotic reporting and the RD 666/2023 sanctions regime
- Who: Companion-animal veterinary clinics (micro/small). About 16,460 clinicians are registered.
- Where: National, with reporting to the database of the autonomous community where the prescriber resides. Protests took place in Madrid, Sevilla, Pontevedra, A Coruña, Lugo, Valencia, Tarragona, Castilla y León (Valladolid) and Baleares (Palma).
- Day-to-day reality: Every antibiotic prescribed or administered must be reported (drug, species, quantity, days of treatment, % of package used) at least every 15 days. Antibiotics are grouped A–D with antibiogram (lab-test) prerequisites. Vets describe the platform as "difícil de usar" (hard to use), say it "a veces se cae" (sometimes goes down), and report it rejecting digital certificates.
- Evidence:
  - Mandatory for companion animals from 2 Jan 2025 (art. 39 RD 666/2023), at least fortnightly ("quincenal"). The MAPA report of May 2026 counts about 16,460 clinicians, about 3,220,000 prescriptions and 80% automated reporting via web services — [MAPA PRESVET](https://www.mapa.gob.es/es/ganaderia/temas/sanidad-animal-higiene-ganadera/Higiene-de-la-produccion-primaria-ganadera/medicamentos-veterinarios/Presvet.aspx) (search summary; HTTP 503 on fetch)
  - The Colegio journal confirms the "plazo de 15 días" (15-day deadline). The Castilla y León vets' council says a delay or omission "está tipificado como muy grave y la sanción mínima es de 60.000 euros" (is classed as very serious, with a €60,000 minimum fine). The OCV (vets' collegiate body) assembly of 14 Feb 2025 unanimously asked for a moratorium on PRESVET and repeal of the sanctions regime. The OCV says nearly 5,000 vets demonstrated at MAPA — [Colvet "Información Veterinaria", Mar 2025](https://colvet.es/files/reportajes/informacionveterinaria_marzo_2025.pdf)
  - Fines run from €600 (minor) to €1,200,000 (most serious). Quote: "Para mí es un exceso de burocracia" ("For me it is excessive bureaucracy") — [Hipertextual, 14 Feb 2025](https://hipertextual.com/2025/02/paren-veterinario-espana)
  - About 2,000 vets protested at MAPA on 5 Mar 2025. The government (DG Valentín Almansa) said repeal "no tendría sentido" (would make no sense) because the decree implements EU Regulation 2019/6 — [Público/EFE, 6 Mar 2025](https://www.publico.es/sociedad/claves-sobre-protestas-veterinarios-regulacion-medicamentos.html). Clinics closed nationwide from 10:00 to 12:00 on 19 Mar 2025, and about 400 vets gathered in Madrid on 26 Mar 2025 — [El Debate, 26 Mar 2025](https://www.eldebate.com/sociedad/20250326/veterinarios-siguen-lucha-contra-ley-medicamento-gobierno-injustificada-desproporcionada_282516_amp.html). More than 1,500 vets gathered at the Congress on 7 May 2025 — [Agronews CyL](https://www.agronewscastillayleon.com/veterinarios-protesta-diput) (search summary)
- Scale: About 16,460 companion-animal clinicians and about 3.22M prescriptions in roughly 16 months (MAPA, May 2026; search summary).
- Cost/impact: Fines from €600 up to €1.2M. A minimum of €60,000 for "muy grave" applies according to the CyL council; this reading was not checked against the Ley de Sanidad Animal. The time burden is not quantified in any source.
- Driver & status: RD 666/2023 of 18 July (distribution, prescription, dispensing and use of veterinary medicines), which implements Regulation (EU) 2019/6. In force, and the PRESVET duty applies. Amended by RD 767/2025 of 2 Sep (BOE 3 Sep 2025, in force 4 Sep 2025), which kept PRESVET — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2025/09/02/767); [Uría Menéndez](https://www.uria.com/es/publicaciones/9470-principales-novedades-en-materia-de-medicamentos-de-uso-veterinario). In March 2025 a change to the sanctions regime was reported as "en trámite en las Cortes Generales" (going through the Cortes) ([Colvet, Mar 2025](https://colvet.es/files/reportajes/informacionveterinaria_marzo_2025.pdf)). Its current status could not be verified.
- How it is solved today: 80% of clinics report automatically through practice-software web services (MAPA, search summary). The remaining 20% enter data manually on the web. Which vendors integrate (e.g., Qvet) and at what price could not be verified.
- Software-solvable?: Yes for reporting, though largely solved already by integrations. The residual opportunity is compliance checking (A–D categories, antibiogram evidence, audit trail ahead of inspections). The restriction itself cannot be fixed by software.
- Confidence: High.

### PRO-06: Veterinarians cannot dispense full treatments, so owners are sent to pharmacies (partly relieved by RD 767/2025)
- Who: Veterinary clinics (micro/small) and pet owners.
- Where: National.
- Day-to-day reality: The vet may supply only a partial dose and must prescribe the rest. Pharmacies sell packs of 20, 40 or 100 pills, leaving leftovers, and owners "recorrer farmacias" (go from pharmacy to pharmacy). Vets report delays in treatment.
- Evidence:
  - Partial-dose rule and pack-size leftovers — [Hipertextual, 14 Feb 2025](https://hipertextual.com/2025/02/paren-veterinario-espana)
  - Three vets said two animals had to be re-operated because dispensing was delayed (anecdotal) — [El Debate, 26 Mar 2025](https://www.eldebate.com/sociedad/20250326/veterinarios-siguen-lucha-contra-ley-medicamento-gobierno-injustificada-desproporcionada_282516_amp.html)
  - Vets say most of the medicines they need are not stocked by pharmacies — [Público, 6 Mar 2025](https://www.publico.es/sociedad/claves-sobre-protestas-veterinarios-regulacion-medicamentos.html)
  - RD 767/2025 widens "dispensación", allows dispensing of perfusion fluids, mineral supplements for perfusion, immunologicals and hormones (new Annex VI), lets the vet authorise a third party to collect exclusive-administration medicines, and requires establishments to document antibiotic-reduction measures — [Uría Menéndez](https://www.uria.com/es/publicaciones/9470-principales-novedades-en-materia-de-medicamentos-de-uso-veterinario); [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/spain/farmaceutico-sanitario/art/medicamentos-veterinarios-reforma-espana)
  - The Balearic vets' college (COVIB) called the changes positive but insufficient and still asks for less PRESVET bureaucracy — [Mallorca Diario](https://www.mallorcadiario.com/veterinarios-baleares-ven-avances-decreto-medicamento-exigen-mas-cambios) (article undated; may predate the final decree)
- Scale: All companion-animal clinics (see PRO-05).
- Cost/impact: Not quantified. Separately, vets complain that the 21% VAT on veterinary services, at the top rate since 2012, makes treatment more expensive ([Colvet, Mar 2025](https://colvet.es/files/reportajes/informacionveterinaria_marzo_2025.pdf)).
- Driver & status: RD 666/2023 together with RDL 1/2015 (Ley de garantías). The OCV demands an amendment to RDL 1/2015 so vets can retail all the medicines they need. RD 767/2025 is in force (partial relief) and the core restriction remains as of Oct 2026.
- How it is solved today: Paper or electronic prescriptions to pharmacies and partial supply from the clinic's own stock.
- Software-solvable?: Mostly no, because the barrier is regulatory. Software could help only with prescription routing and pharmacy stock lookup.
- Confidence: High on the rules, low on the cost of the impact.

### PRO-07: Community pharmacies spend hours a week managing medicine shortages
- Who: Community pharmacies (micro).
- Where: National.
- Day-to-day reality: Pharmacists locate alternatives, call distributors, contact prescribers about substitutions and handle frustrated patients.
- Evidence:
  - European pharmacies spent an average of 12 h/week on supply incidents in 2025, double the figure of five years earlier. More than 600 medicines are affected, and 70% of countries report no improvement on 2024 (PGEU) — [Farmacéuticos (CGCOF magazine), 23 Mar 2026](https://www.farmaceuticos.com/revista/internacional/escasez-medicamentos-europa-farmacias-incidencias/) (subscriber-only; partial read)
  - The CGCOF vice-president cites the PGEU figure of about 11 h/week. CisMED 2025: 207 medicines with incidents or alerts (285 in 2024, −27.37%), an average of 44 per week (60 in 2024), 6% non-substitutable, 101,364,392 shortage records reported (116,818,024 in 2024), and 13,483 pharmacies participating. FarmaHelp solves 7 in 10 cases for patients — [Corresponsables, updated 10 Feb 2026](https://www.corresponsables.com/actualidad/cismed-reduccion-27-medicamentos-con-incidencias-alertas-suministro-2025/)
- Scale: 13,483 pharmacies in CisMED.
- Cost/impact: About 11–12 h/week per pharmacy. This is an EU average; no Spain-specific hours figure was found.
- Driver & status: Market and supply chain (EU critical medicines). Improving in Spain in 2025.
- How it is solved today: CisMED (the pharmacy profession's own shortage-monitoring platform), FarmaHelp (patient-facing) and pharmacy management software (vendors not verified).
- Software-solvable?: Partly, and already well served by the profession's own tools.
- Confidence: High on the figures. Spain-specific time cost is a gap.

### PRO-08: Health-data protection exposure for small clinics and pharmacies (GDPR/LOPDGDD, clinical records)
- Who: Every clinic and pharmacy, especially autónomos running on spreadsheets and WhatsApp.
- Where: National (AEPD).
- Day-to-day reality: Patient data sits in Excel files or personal phones. Transparency notices and security are missing, and clinical records must be kept for at least five years after each care episode.
- Evidence:
  - The AEPD fined a Castilla y León pharmacy owner €16,000 for keeping clients' health data in "un documento Excel" without informing them and without adequate security — [Economist & Jurist, 8 May 2025](https://www.economistjurist.es/actualidad-juridica/jurisprudencia/multa-de-16-000-euros-a-una-farmaceutica-que-recopilaba-los-datos-personales-y-relativos-a-la-salud-de-los-pacientes-en-un-excel/)
  - AEPD 2025: 299 fines totalling about €40M (+14%), with no health-sector focus mentioned — [ECIJA, 27 Jan 2026](https://www.ecija.com/actualidad-insights/mas-sanciones-y-de-mayor-importe-la-aepd-sube-el-nivel-de-multas-en-2025/). This conflicts with €48.1M given in a summary of the AEPD annual report — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/es/knowledge-hub/memoria-anual-aepd-2025/) (search summary; not fetched)
  - Public health bodies receive warnings rather than fines (art. 77 LOPDGDD), so the monetary risk falls on private providers — [Legiscope blog, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.legiscope.com/blog/aepd-sanciones-sanidad-salud.html) (search summary of Legiscope pages; secondary; its sector fine statistics, e.g. an "average €18,000 fine", are unverified and not used)
  - Clinical records must be kept "como mínimo, cinco años contados desde la fecha del alta de cada proceso asistencial" (at least five years from discharge of each care episode) — [Ley 41/2002 art. 17.1, BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2002/11/14/41)
- Scale: Unknown.
- Cost/impact: €16,000 in the pharmacy case. No 2024–2026 fines against dental, physiotherapy, psychology or aesthetic clinics were found.
- Driver & status: GDPR and LOPDGDD 3/2018 (in force); Ley 41/2002 (in force). The EU European Health Data Space Regulation could not be checked in this session (EUR-Lex fetch failed).
- How it is solved today: Clinic EHR software (Clinni etc.) and data-protection consultancies. Prices were not collected.
- Software-solvable?: Yes, through compliant records, consent, access logs, retention and secure messaging.
- Confidence: Low–medium. The obligation is real, but there is little evidence of frequent enforcement against clinics.

### PRO-09: New duty for every health centre to keep verified staff-qualification records (RD 239/2026), due around January 2027
- Who: All authorised health centres and services, public and private, including dental, physiotherapy, psychology, aesthetic, podiatry and nutrition clinics.
- Where: National basic rule, enforced through the autonomous communities' authorisation and inspection regimes.
- Day-to-day reality: Each clinic must hold an up-to-date file for every health professional, whatever their employment type (employee, autónomo, partner). The file must show official qualification and specialty, and the clinic must inform new staff about safe practices.
- Evidence:
  - RD 239/2026 of 25 March (BOE no. 75, 26 Mar 2026, BOE-A-2026-6975), in force 1 Jul 2026, adds art. 7 to RD 1277/2003. Centres must guarantee care by staff with "la titulación oficial y las competencias y habilidades profesionales adecuadas" (the official qualification and appropriate professional skills) and keep updated information and a personal file for all health staff. Additional provision 1 gives six months to "disponer de la información actualizada de todo su personal sanitario" (have up-to-date information on all health staff). It also rewrites the unit definitions U.11 Nutrición y dietética and U.36 Tratamiento del dolor, and gives regions one year to adapt — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2026/03/25/239)
  - Base rule: prior sanitary authorisation by the autonomous community is compulsory before any centre opens (art. 3.2 RD 1277/2003) — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2003/10/10/1277)
- Scale: All private health centres. No national count was found.
- Cost/impact: Not documented. Exposure is in regional inspections.
- Driver & status: RD 239/2026, in force since 1 Jul 2026. Staff-records deadline about 1 Jan 2027 (my calculation: six months after entry into force). Regional adaptation of the annexes is due about July 2027.
- How it is solved today: No evidence was found. The likely current practice is HR folders and spreadsheets (inference).
- Software-solvable?: Yes. A credential registry with colegiación and specialty checks, expiry alerts and an inspection-ready export.
- Confidence: High on the obligation (official text). Low on how painful businesses find it, since no complaints were found because it is so new.

### Inferences
- The insurer fee squeeze (PRO-01/02) is the most widespread and severe clinic pain, cutting across doctors, psychologists, physiotherapists, podiatrists, nutritionists, dentists and speech therapists. Its root is market power, so SaaS can only help professionals reduce dependence on insurers (private-pay acquisition, reimbursement workflows, payer-mix analytics). It cannot fix fees.
- The veterinary regime is the clearest "regulation creates recurring data work" case. With 80% of reporting already automated through practice software, the reporting part of the pain is largely served. What remains is compliance logic and audit readiness, plus non-software issues (dispensing, 21% VAT).
- No-show tooling is cheap and abundant (about €13/month entry price), so on its own it is a weak wedge.
- RD 239/2026 is a fresh, little-noticed compliance deadline (about Jan 2027) that applies to every clinic. It could serve as a lead magnet for clinic-compliance software. This is an inference; no market evidence was found.

### Gaps
- No 2024–2026 evidence was gathered on dental-specific pains (insurer "planes de descuento" evidence dates from 2018–2019), aesthetic-medicine intrusion, optometry, podiatry or nutrition-specific issues.
- Sanitary authorisation timelines and costs by region (Madrid, Catalonia, Valencia, Andalusia), and the REGCESS registry: not researched.
- Telehealth rules: not researched; no evidence found.
- Clinic software prices for Gesden, Clinic Cloud (pricing URL returned 404) and Qvet: not obtained. Only Clinni (€12.95/month + taxes) was confirmed.
- Top Doctors pricing and model: no evidence found.
- Whether the Ley de Sanidad Animal sanctions reform reported "en trámite" in March 2025 was passed: unverified.

- Legality of no-show deposits or fees under Spanish consumer law: not verified.

## 2. Professional-services firms (gestorías/asesorías, small law firms, others)

### Takeaway
Gestorías face a talent shortage (7 in 10 struggle to hire, per an interested training provider) at the same time as invoicing rules keep moving. VeriFactu has been postponed again: it is legally due 1 Jan / 1 Jul 2027, and a move to October 2028 was announced on 5 Oct 2026 but not yet enacted. Meanwhile the B2B e-invoicing countdown has formally started, with turnover above €8M obliged from 6 Oct 2027 and everyone else from 6 Oct 2028. Small law firms have had a new pre-litigation step since 3 April 2025: the MASC requirement under LO 1/2025, with a documentary burden of proof, risk of inadmission and cost penalties. Courts apply it inconsistently. Court reorganisation into Tribunales de Instancia (2025) and LexNET's 365-day notifications add to deadline-management stress.

### Cited Findings
- "7 de cada 10 despachos tienen dificultades para encontrar personal cualificado" (7 in 10 firms struggle to find qualified staff). 98.4% of professionals are over 30 and most are over 45 — [Merca2/EDASE, 4 Aug 2025](https://www.merca2.es/2025/08/04/los-despachos-y-gestorias-se-quedan-sin-personal-crisis-en-el-sector-espanol-2219718/)
- B2B e-invoicing: Orden HAC/1028/2026 (BOE 5 Oct 2026) started the clock, with turnover above €8M obliged from 6 Oct 2027 and the rest from 6 Oct 2028 — [Garrigues, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.garrigues.com/es_ES/noticia/comienza-computo-plazos-aplicacion-factura-electronica-obligatoria-empresarios.md)
- VeriFactu: postponement to Oct 2028 announced but not yet in a norm with the rank of law — [CMS, 6 Oct 2026](https://cms.law/en/esp/publication/factura-electronica-b2b-arranca-la-cuenta-atras-y-veri-factu-se-aplaza-a-2028)
- MASC: a claim must attach proof of the prior negotiation attempt "so pena de inadmisión" (or be rejected) — [PwC, Jan 2025](https://periscopiofiscalylegal.pwc.es/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Retos-derivados-de-los-MASC-como-requisito-de-procedibilidad.pdf)
- Civil courts received 3,563,038 new cases in 2024, with 2,688,606 pending at year-end, both about 20% up on 2023 (CGPJ annual reports, cited in) — [InDret 2/2026](https://indret.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2006.pdf)
- Full detail is in PRO-10 to PRO-13 below.

### PRO-10: Gestorías/asesorías cannot hire or replace qualified staff
- Who: Gestorías, asesorías fiscales/laborales/contables and accounting firms. Micro and small.
- Where: National. No city breakdown was found.
- Day-to-day reality: Vacancies for tax, labour and accounting technicians stay open, owners are ageing, and fewer young people choose the career. Firms absorb more clients per technician during campaign peaks.
- Evidence:
  - 7 in 10 firms have difficulty finding qualified staff. 98.4% of active professionals are over 30, most over 45 (Barómetro del Sector del Despacho Profesional). Quote from EDASE's director: "No es un problema de empleo. Hay vacantes, hay necesidad, pero no hay quien las cubra" ("It isn't a jobs problem. There are vacancies and there is need, but no one to fill them") — [Merca2, 4 Aug 2025](https://www.merca2.es/2025/08/04/los-despachos-y-gestorias-se-quedan-sin-personal-crisis-en-el-sector-espanol-2219718/) (the source, EDASE, trains and recruits staff for the sector, so it is an interested party)
  - Hays Guía del Mercado Laboral 2026: 93% of Spanish companies struggle to find qualified talent, and finance/admin salaries are expected to rise 7% in 2026. INE ETIC Q1-2025: 8.7% of firms with 10–49 employees actively use AI, against 49.2% of large firms. An anecdote, without a source, of about 30 h/month spent typing invoices in a 4–8 person asesoría — [Copilot Gestoría blog (vendor), 23 Jul 2026, updated 22 Oct 2026](https://copilotgestoria.com/blog/no-encuentras-personal-asesoria-2026-automatizacion)
- Scale: More than 3 million SMEs and autónomos depend on these firms for tax and Social Security filings ([Merca2, 4 Aug 2025](https://www.merca2.es/2025/08/04/los-despachos-y-gestorias-se-quedan-sin-personal-crisis-en-el-sector-espanol-2219718/)). No count of gestorías was found.
- Cost/impact: Salary pressure (+7% expected for finance/admin, per Hays). Turnover and lost capacity are not quantified.
- Driver & status: A labour-market force plus growing regulatory complexity. Ongoing.
- How it is solved today: Recruiting and training schools (EDASE trained more than 13,000 people in 2024), plus automation tools (Copilot Gestoría, a3 by Wolters Kluwer, Sage, ContaPlus). No prices were found.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Document capture, client portals and automated bookkeeping reduce hours per client but do not create staff.
- Confidence: Medium (interested-party sources, but consistent).

### PRO-11: Gestorías must plan client migrations around invoicing rules that keep shifting (VeriFactu postponed again; B2B e-invoicing countdown started)
- Who: Gestorías/asesorías and their micro clients, including the clinics, law firms and academies in this inventory.
- Where: National, except that the Basque Country and Navarra have their own foral systems. Clinni's TicketBAI integration ([Clinni](https://clinni.com/precios/)) shows the Basque route; the scope of the foral exemption was not verified in this session.
- Day-to-day reality: Firms prepared clients for VeriFactu in 2025, then had to re-plan twice. They must now also plan B2B e-invoicing, which uses private exchange platforms plus a public solution, for every client. Clients keep asking "¿me afecta y cuándo?" ("does this affect me, and when?").
- Evidence:
  - RDL 15/2025 of 2 Dec (BOE 3 Dec 2025, validated 11 Dec) moved VeriFactu to 1 Jan 2027 for Corporate Income Tax payers and 1 Jul 2027 for everyone else — [Legal Today, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.legaltoday.com/actualidad-juridica/noticias-de-derecho/nueva-prorroga-verifactu-no-sera-obligatorio-hasta-2027-para-sociedades-y-otros-contribuyentes-2025-12-04/); [Maldita, 3 Dec 2025](https://maldita.es/malditobulo/20251203/hacienda-retrasa-a-2027-la-entrada-en-vigor-de-verifactu-el-sistema-de-verificacion-de-facturas-para-pymes-y-autonomos)
  - On 5 Oct 2026 the government announced it "se aplazarán hasta octubre de 2028" (will postpone until October 2028) the VeriFactu obligations not yet in force. No legal instrument has been identified yet — [CMS, 6 Oct 2026](https://cms.law/en/esp/publication/factura-electronica-b2b-arranca-la-cuenta-atras-y-veri-factu-se-aplaza-a-2028); [Emprendedores](https://emprendedores.es/gestion/aplazamiento-verifactu-2028/) (needs a norm with the rank of law); [TeamSystem, 7 Oct 2026](https://teamsystem.es/magazine/verifactu-se-aplaza-2028/) ("announced", with the RDL 15/2025 calendar still in force until a new norm appears). One headline calls it "el cuarto aplazamiento en tres años" (the fourth postponement in three years) — [wwwhatsnew, 6 Oct 2026](https://wwwhatsnew.com/2026/10/06/hacienda-verifactu-octubre-2028-aplazamiento-factura-electronica-2026)
  - B2B e-invoicing: RD 238/2026 of 25 Mar (BOE 31 Mar 2026; in force 20 Apr 2026 per the Consejo General de Gestores) — [AEAT news, 31 Mar 2026](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/eu_es/todas-noticias/2026/marzo/31/facturacion-electronica-obligatoria.html) (search summary); [Consejo General de Gestores, 30 Apr 2026](https://www.consejogestores.org/?p=14916). Orden HAC/1028/2026 of 2 Oct (BOE 5 Oct 2026, BOE-A-2026-20587, in force 6 Oct 2026) sets 6 Oct 2027 for turnover above €8M and 6 Oct 2028 for the rest — [Garrigues, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.garrigues.com/es_ES/noticia/comienza-computo-plazos-aplicacion-factura-electronica-obligatoria-empresarios.md); consistent with [CMS](https://cms.law/en/esp/publication/factura-electronica-b2b-arranca-la-cuenta-atras-y-veri-factu-se-aplaza-a-2028)
  - The draft-order calendar summarised by the Consejo General de Gestores puts the public solution (SPFE) online on 1 Aug 2027 and invoice-status reporting for firms with turnover of €8M or less on 1 Oct 2029 (draft dates; may differ in the final order) — [CGG, 30 Apr 2026](https://www.consejogestores.org/?p=14916)
- Scale: More than 3M SME and autónomo clients of advisory firms (Merca2, see PRO-10).
- Cost/impact: Not quantified; no gestoría survey of hours was found.
- Driver & status: RD 1007/2023 (VeriFactu) with RDL 15/2025 dates (in force: 1 Jan / 1 Jul 2027), and a postponement to Oct 2028 announced but not enacted. Ley 18/2022 (Crea y Crece), RD 238/2026 and Orden HAC/1028/2026 are in force, with deadlines of 6 Oct 2027 and 6 Oct 2028.
- How it is solved today: Invoicing and ERP vendors (Billin, Quipu, TeamSystem, Holded, a3, Sage). The tax/invoicing researcher covers prices.
- Software-solvable?: Yes. A gestoría-side "compliance cockpit" could track which clients are on compliant software and the deadline for each. This overlaps with the invoicing researcher's scope.
- Confidence: High on status (two law firms, the Consejo de Gestores and AEAT). Medium on gestoría workload (inferred).

### PRO-12: Small law firms — the MASC pre-litigation requirement (since 3 April 2025) adds a documented negotiation step, risk of inadmission and cost penalties
- Who: Small law firms and autónomo lawyers in civil and commercial work, plus landlords and property managers in eviction cases (administradores de fincas).
- Where: National, but criteria differ by court. Documented cases: the Las Palmas de Gran Canaria courts' unified criteria and the Collado Villalba (Madrid) criteria.
- Day-to-day reality: Before filing, the lawyer must send a negotiation invitation (burofax, certified email or mediation), prove that the counterparty received it at an address that belongs to them, record the outcome, and describe all of it in the claim. Otherwise the claim can be rejected. Refusing to negotiate can lead to adverse costs.
- Evidence:
  - LO 1/2025 of 2 January (BOE 3 Jan 2025) entered into force three months after publication. A MASC (medio adecuado de solución de controversias: an approved out-of-court dispute-resolution step) is required before civil and commercial claims, excluding labour, criminal and insolvency cases and cases with public-sector parties. Accepted methods include direct negotiation, mediation, conciliation, a neutral expert opinion, a confidential binding offer and collaborative law. Under art. 10, proof is a document from the neutral third party, a document signed by both parties, or any document proving the other party received the invitation, on what date, and had access to its content. Claims must include these documents "so pena de inadmisión". Cost rules: arts. 245, 394 and 395 LEC. Fines for "abuso del servicio público de justicia" (abuse of the public justice service): art. 247 — [PwC Tax & Legal, Jan 2025](https://periscopiofiscalylegal.pwc.es/wp-content/uploads/2025/01/Retos-derivados-de-los-MASC-como-requisito-de-procedibilidad.pdf)
  - Evictions: the requirement applies from 3 April 2025 — [CAF Madrid PDF, May 2025](https://cafmadrid.es/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/La-negociacion-previo-como-nuevo-requisito-de-procedibilidad-de-los-juicios-de-desahucio.pdf) (search summary)
  - Las Palmas courts: if the claim makes no reference to these points "la misma será inadmitida a trámite" (it will be rejected) — [Confilegal-hosted criteria, Apr 2025](https://confilegal.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/UNIFICACION-CRITERIOS-MASC-INSTANCIA-LPGC.pdf) (search summary). Collado Villalba excludes some procedures, such as cambiario (bills of exchange), summary possession and enforcement of judicial titles — [ICPM PDF](https://www.icpm.es/downDocs/2390_CRITERIOS%20MASC%20%20COLLADO%20VILLALBA_2.pdf) (search summary). Practice is therefore not uniform.
  - Case-law headline: "Los MASC no se cumplen con acreditar la entrega de un correo: hay que probar que la dirección pertenece al demandado" ("MASC is not satisfied by proving an email was delivered: you must prove the address belongs to the defendant") — [Economist & Jurist (related-article headline), page dated 13 Jul 2025](https://www.economistjurist.es/?p=231084)
  - Academic critique on access to justice — [Ferreiro Baamonde, InDret 2/2026](https://indret.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2006.pdf)
- Scale: Civil courts received 3,563,038 new cases in 2024 (about +20% on 2023); 2,688,606 were pending at the end of 2024 (CGPJ annual report 2025, cited by [InDret 2/2026](https://indret.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/2006.pdf)). Most civil claims now need a MASC step, subject to the exclusions. Court-referred mediation was negligible before the reform: in 2024, 647 non-family civil referrals produced only 70 agreements (same source).
- Cost/impact: Risk of inadmission, adverse costs and fines for abuse. Extra billable or non-billable hours are not quantified. No statistics on inadmissions or on the fall in filings after April 2025 were found.
- Driver & status: LO 1/2025 (in force; MASC required since 3 Apr 2025).
- How it is solved today: Burofax and certified-email services, mediators and lawyers' own templates. Practice software with LexNET integration includes MN Program ("desde 1 € al día", from €1 a day) and SuDespacho (from €35.95/month), both vendor prices reported by [Upliora blog, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.upliora.es/blog/ia-despachos-procuradores-abogados-lexnet-software-gestion-2026). Kmaleon, Aranzadi Fusión and Tirant do not publish prices.
- Software-solvable?: Yes. A MASC workflow could cover invitation templates, certified delivery with proof of the counterparty's address, timeline and evidence bundle, the interruption of limitation periods, and auto-drafting the claim section describing the negotiation.
- Confidence: High on the regulation. Medium on the size of the operational impact (no inadmission statistics).

### PRO-13: Court reorganisation and LexNET notification load overwhelm small firms' deadline control
- Who: Small law firms, autónomo lawyers and procuradores (court agents). Labour lawyers who litigate without a procurador are especially exposed.
- Where: National. The Madrid bar (ICAM) has raised the issue with the Ministry of Justice.
- Day-to-day reality: Notifications arrive through LexNET every day of the year and each one starts a deadline. After the court reform, filings go to new court inboxes. Deadlines must be calculated by hand, accounting for local holidays, August and grace days.
- Evidence:
  - LO 1/2025 rollout: 315 Tribunales de Instancia set up from 1 Jul 2025, 16 from 1 Oct 2025 and 100 from 31 Dec 2025, 431 in total. LexNET has no documented public API for third parties. Deadline calculation "must be validated by a person" — [Upliora blog (vendor), 21 Oct 2026](https://www.upliora.es/blog/ia-despachos-procuradores-abogados-lexnet-software-gestion-2026) (single-source)
  - LexNET notifies "los 365 días al año" (365 days a year), so labour lawyers without procuradores cannot take holidays, and the same applies during maternity or sick leave. The associations asked for a change in the law, and the Ministry agreed it should be regulated by law — [ICAM](https://web.icam.es/primera-reunion-en-el-colegio-con-el-ministerio-de-justicia-para-debatir-las-preocupaciones-de-la-abogacia-madrilena-en-el-uso-de-lexnet/) (page dated 2018/2020; old)
- Scale: Kmaleon Procuradores (Wolters Kluwer) claims more than 8,000 users (vendor claim, via Upliora).
- Cost/impact: Not quantified (missed deadlines lead to professional-liability claims; no data).
- Driver & status: LO 1/2025, with court phases completed by 31 Dec 2025, and the LexNET rules (in force). No 2025–2026 evidence of specific LexNET outages was found.
- How it is solved today: Practice-management software with LexNET integration (MN Program, SuDespacho, Kmaleon, New Procurador V5, Aranzadi Fusión, Tirant). Prices are mostly unpublished.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Notification triage and deadline computation are possible with a human check, but the lack of a public LexNET API limits automation.
- Confidence: Medium–low (vendor and old sources).

### Inferences
- For gestorías, the binding constraint is capacity (staff), while regulatory churn keeps raising per-client workload. Tools that cut hours per client (document intake, chasing clients for documents, client compliance status) fit the pain better than another accounting ledger. No direct evidence on document-chasing hours was found beyond a vendor anecdote.
- The MASC is a well-defined, nationwide, in-force workflow with a documentary burden of proof. That makes it a strong candidate for narrow SaaS for small firms, landlords and property managers. The lack of inadmission statistics means its revenue potential cannot yet be sized.
- The repeated VeriFactu postponements make a VeriFactu-only product risky. B2B e-invoicing (Oct 2027 / Oct 2028) is now the firmer deadline.

### Gaps
- Evidence on gestorías chasing client documents, campaign-period (renta) overload, or AEAT/Social Security appointment problems: none gathered.
- Prices of a3ASESOR, Sage Despachos, Lefebvre, Aranzadi and Kleos: not obtained (generally unpublished).
- Lawyers' professional-liability insurance prices; legal-aid (turno de oficio) pay; the mutualistas "pasarela RETA" (pension transfer) issue: not researched (search budget ran out).
- Statistics on MASC inadmissions and on the fall in civil filings since April 2025: not found.
- LexNET outages in 2025–2026 and CGAE outage certificates: unverified (search summary only, no identifiable page).
- Architects, engineers, translators, consultants and marketing agencies, including AI disruption: not researched.

## 3. Education and training providers

### Takeaway
Driving schools have the best-documented pain: a structural shortage of practical-exam capacity. The DGT rations exam slots through its CAPA allocation algorithm, there are 12 tests per examiner per day, and about 46% of candidates fail their first practical exam. Sector waiting-list claims (e.g., 82,000 in Andalusia, 63,000 in Barcelona) are disputed. When the DGT offered unlimited extra exams in pilot provinces, under 10% of the claimed students were put forward. Private 0–3 nurseries face a 2026 government proposal to cut child-to-staff ratios, which the employers' body ACADE says could close the 5,345 private centres. They also depend on regional free-place schemes paid in instalments. Other education sub-topics in the brief could not be researched.

### Cited Findings
- In the DGT's exam-reinforcement pilot (PRO), Navarra presented 275 students of the more than 6,000 claimed, Lleida 312 of 5,000, Almería 630 of 8,000 and Palma 407 of 8,000. Next in line: Barcelona 1,450 of 63,000 and Tenerife 575 of 11,000 — [DGT press note, 16 Jun 2026](https://www.dgt.es/export/sites/web-DGT/.galleries/downloads/nota_prensa/2026/20260616-nota-de-prensa.pdf)
- The sector estimates 82,000 students queuing in Andalusia (Sevilla more than 25,000, Málaga about 17,500), and protests were held on 17 Jul 2026 — [El Español, 17 Jul 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/invertia/20260717/autoescuelas-pie-guerra-alumnos-esperan-practico-andalucia-faltan-examinadores/1003744322825_0.amp.html)
- ACADE: 5,345 private early-years centres, more than 224,000 pupils (about 50% of the stage) and more than 60,000 jobs at risk from the ratio cut — [ACADE, 24 Jun 2026](https://educacionprivada.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NP_ACADE_Bajada_ratios_Infantil_amenaza_cierre_escuelas_privadas_24-06-2026.pdf)
- Full detail is in PRO-14 and PRO-15 below.

### PRO-14: Driving schools — practical-exam bottleneck, slot rationing and disputed waiting lists
- Who: Driving schools (autoescuelas), mostly micro and small, though some chains are large (Autoescuela Lara has 65 centres in Madrid).
- Where: National, with hotspots in Andalusia (Sevilla, Málaga, Almería, Cádiz), Catalonia (Barcelona, Lleida), Madrid, Murcia, Valencia and Alicante, Baleares (Palma), Navarra, Tenerife and Aragón.
- Day-to-day reality: Students who pass the theory test wait weeks or months for a practical slot. Schools keep giving paid lessons to hold students' skills, tie up cars and instructors, and lose students who give up. Slot allocation depends on each school's pass rate.
- Evidence:
  - The DGT says the "mal llamada 'lista de espera'" (the so-called waiting list) counts everyone who passed theory and has two years to sit the practical, and that fewer than 10% were presented when unlimited exams were offered. The DGT added 130 examiner posts to reach 21 examiners per million inhabitants. Pass rates: Lleida 46.15% (51.71% in Jan–Apr), Almería 70%, Baleares more than half failed — [DGT, 16 Jun 2026](https://www.dgt.es/export/sites/web-DGT/.galleries/downloads/nota_prensa/2026/20260616-nota-de-prensa.pdf)
  - DGT head Pere Navarro told the Congress "solo un 8 % de la bolsa de alumnos está en condiciones de examinarse" (only 8% of the pool is ready to sit the exam). The driving-schools confederation CNAE called this "inaceptable" (unacceptable) and the PRO plan "un parche" (a patch). Spain holds more than 1 million practical tests a year. Catalonia, Madrid, Murcia and Cádiz are the most saturated — [Motor.es, 19 May 2026](https://www.motor.es/noticias/pere-navarro-dgt-enfurece-autoescuelas-listas-espera-2026114254.html)
  - Valencia has about 19,000 students in the pool, up more than 3,000 in three years. Waits for a retest are "dos o tres meses" (two or three months). In Madrid, students wait from 1.5 to 5 months. The DGT says its staffing plan is 97% covered, with about 50 new examiners a year and 732 since 2017. Each examiner does 12 tests a day, and 46% fail their first practical — [Xataka, 5 Sep 2025](https://www.xataka.com/movilidad/se-frustran-dejan-autoescuelas-denuncian-que-lista-esperar-para-examen-conducir-interminable-dgt-niega)
  - Andalusia: 82,000 students (sector figure), up to 5 months for professional licences, and simultaneous protests at every DGT office in the region on 17 Jul 2026 (70–100 firms in Sevilla) — [El Español, 17 Jul 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/invertia/20260717/autoescuelas-pie-guerra-alumnos-esperan-practico-andalucia-faltan-examinadores/1003744322825_0.amp.html)
  - Baleares: more than 7,000 students waiting, with 2.5-month delays for cars — [Mallorca Diario](https://www.mallorcadiario.com/autoescuelas-alertan-colapso-obliga-muchos-alumnos-examinarse-practicas-fuera-mallorca) (search summary; HTTP 403 on fetch)
  - The CAPA algorithm splits each provincial office's exam capacity among driving schools according to their theory pass rates and number of candidates. Historic baseline from 2021: 704 operational examiners (almost 1,000 in 2006) and more than 2 million people waiting — [Vozpópuli, 25 Mar 2021](https://www.vozpopuli.com/economia/lista-espera-carne-conducir.html) (dated)
- Scale: More than 1M practical tests a year. Claimed pools of 63,000 in Barcelona, 82,000 in Andalusia and 19,000 in Valencia (all disputed).
- Cost/impact: Students pay extra lessons while waiting, and cars and instructors are tied up for weeks ([Motor.es](https://www.motor.es/noticias/pere-navarro-dgt-enfurece-autoescuelas-listas-espera-2026114254.html)). Students drop out ([Xataka](https://www.xataka.com/movilidad/se-frustran-dejan-autoescuelas-denuncian-que-lista-esperar-para-examen-conducir-interminable-dgt-niega)). No euro figures were found.
- Driver & status: Examiner capacity (DGT staffing plan, retirements, leave) and CAPA allocation. The DGT's Plan de Refuerzo Operativo (PRO) ran as a pilot in May–June 2026 and is being extended (Barcelona from 13 Jun 2026, Tenerife late June). 101 new examiners joined 46 provincial traffic offices in September 2025 (search summary; single-source). Status: ongoing as of Oct 2026.
- How it is solved today: Saturday PRO sessions, sending students to smaller exam centres, and protests. Driving-school management software and its prices were not researched (gap).
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Software can track readiness and pass probability (to win CAPA slots and send only ready students), schedule practical lessons and produce the verified student lists the DGT now asks for. It cannot create examiner capacity.
- Confidence: High that the pain exists. The magnitude is contested (sector versus DGT).

### PRO-15: Private 0–3 nurseries — proposed ratio cut and dependence on regional free-place schemes
- Who: Private early-years centres (escuelas infantiles 0–3), mostly micro and small.
- Where: National proposal. Regional schemes: Galicia (full free places in private centres), Madrid (cheque infantil), La Rioja (association opposing the cut).
- Day-to-day reality: Owners fear fewer children per classroom without compensation would cut income, while fees are capped by what families can pay or by subsidy rules. Where places are free (Galicia), centres join the scheme each year and are paid by the Xunta in instalments.
- Evidence:
  - ACADE (24 Jun 2026): if the ratios are applied "sin medidas compensatorias" (without compensating measures), the 5,345 private centres could close, leaving more than 224,000 pupils (about 50% of the stage) without places and putting more than 60,000 jobs at risk. Data source: "Las cifras de la educación en España. Curso 2023/2024 (edición 2026)" — [ACADE press release](https://educacionprivada.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/NP_ACADE_Bajada_ratios_Infantil_amenaza_cierre_escuelas_privadas_24-06-2026.pdf)
  - On 27 Jul 2026 ACADE met the Secretary of State for Education and rejected the "propuesta de reducción de ratios planteada por el Gobierno y consensuada con los sindicatos" (ratio-cut proposal put forward by the Government and agreed with the unions). ACADE stresses that non-concerted private centres "no reciben financiación" (receive no public funding) — [ACADE press release, 27 Jul 2026](https://educacionprivada.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/NdP_ACADE_Reunion_Secretario_Estado_Reduccion_Ratios.pdf)
  - The La Rioja association joined the opposition, citing more than 5,000 centres, about 60,000 jobs and about 224,000 pupils — [nuevecuatrouno, 3 Jul 2026](https://nuevecuatrouno.com/2026/07/03/escuelas-infantiles-rioja-alzan-contra-reduccion-ratios/) (search summary; HTTP 403 on fetch)
  - Galicia has paid for free places for 100% of 0–3 pupils in private and social-initiative centres since 2022/23: €45.7M for 2024/25. Payment runs as an advance of 100% of the September–December amount, then advances of 45% and 45%, with 10% at the end of the year. Centres have one month after the call is published in the DOG to join — [Xunta, 24 Jun 2024](https://www.xunta.gal/notas-de-prensa/-/nova/003039/xunta-consolida-gratuidad-las-escuelas-infantiles-0-3-iniciativa-social-privadas). For 2025/26: more than €65M and more than 32,000 families — [Xunta press releases](https://www.xunta.gal/es/notas-de-prensa/-/nova/014503/xunta-consolida-liderazgo-galicia-educacion-infantil-con-una-nueva-orden-ayudas) (search summary; exact page not fetched)
  - Madrid: a cheque infantil scheme for private centres (2020 support line) — [Comunidad de Madrid, 21 May 2020](https://www.comunidad.madrid/node/56851) (dated)
- Scale: 5,345 private centres, more than 224,000 pupils and more than 60,000 jobs (ACADE, using ministry statistics).
- Cost/impact: ACADE projects closures. This is an interested-party projection, not an observed outcome.
- Driver & status: A ministry proposal on 0–3 ratios, agreed with public-network unions (June–July 2026). Status: draft/proposal. No BOE text and no exact ratios were found as of 23 Oct 2026.
- How it is solved today: Lobbying through employers' associations. Software in use (enrolment, family billing, subsidy claims) was not researched.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Administering subsidies and free-place claims, waitlists and fees is software territory. The ratio rule is not.
- Confidence: Medium (interested-party projections; regulation still a draft).

### Inferences
- Driving schools' most actionable software angle is readiness and slot optimisation. The DGT now asks schools for verified lists of genuinely ready students, and CAPA rewards high pass rates. This is an inference, not documented demand.
- 0–3 nurseries combine regulatory uncertainty with subsidy administration (instalments, adhesion windows) that varies by region. A multi-region subsidy-admin tool would need per-region rules, so it is a fragmented market.

## 4. Care services (residences, home help, agencies) and households as employers

### Takeaway
Care providers are squeezed between public prices and new quality and staffing rules. The 2022 national accreditation agreement requires residences to reach about one professional per two residents by 2029. It also requires living units of no more than 15 residents and more single rooms, with existing centres given until December 2029. Meanwhile some regions paid concert prices of €54/day or less, against a sector estimate of at least €75. Home-help (SAD) companies work under municipal tenders with capped hourly prices that include travel time (€16.70 rising to €22/h in Vigo in 2025). Households employing the roughly 348,000–378,000 registered domestic workers took on a new risk-assessment duty under RD 893/2024, through the free Prevencion10 tool, due by November 2025. They also carry the 2022 reform's contribution duties.

### Cited Findings
- Madrid's residence framework agreement raised the price per place from €54.51 to €74.88/day (+37.4%) and the direct-care staffing ratio from 0.42 to 0.47 — [Comunidad de Madrid, 28 Jan 2021](https://www.comunidad.madrid/node/59061)
- Vigo's home-help (SAD) contract: €49.5M over 3 years, with the hourly price rising from €16.70 to €22 because of wage increases — [Vigo e, 17 Mar 2025](https://vigoe.es/vigo/local/vigo-pone-en-marcha-uno-de-sus-megacontratos-y-caballero-exige-que-lo-pague-la-xunta)
- The Prevencion10 tool launched 14 May 2025, giving employers six months to complete the assessment, with "más de 348.000 mujeres afiliadas" (more than 348,000 women registered with Social Security) — [INSST press release, 14 May 2025](https://www.insst.es/documents/d/portal-insst/yolanda-diaz-presenta-la-herramienta-para-evaluar-los-riesgos-laborales-de-las-trabajadoras-del-hogar-nota-de-prensa)
- Full detail is in PRO-16 to PRO-18 below.

### PRO-16: Elderly residences — 2022 accreditation model (staff ratios, living units, single rooms) due by 2029 against low public concert prices
- Who: Private and concerted elderly residences (small and medium; also large groups).
- Where: A national framework agreement, applied by each autonomous community. Madrid has a documented price and ratio framework. CEAPs and ACRA (Catalonia) are vocal.
- Day-to-day reality: Operators must hire more care staff (gerocultores) and restructure buildings into "home-like" units, while regional concert prices barely cover current costs. Staff rotas must meet ratio and contract-stability rules.
- Evidence:
  - Agreement of the Consejo Territorial (28 Jun 2022, approved 10–9). At least 80% of contracts must be indefinite and 66% full-time (50% in SAD). Living units of at most 15 residents. New centres capped at 75, 90 or 120 places depending on population density. New centres need 65% single rooms for public places. Regions had nine months to legislate, with a transition until 2030 — [Público, 29 Jun 2022](https://www.publico.es/sociedad/seran-partir-residencias-mayores.html)
  - Existing residences have until December 2029, and about 80% must create living units of fewer than 15 people. CEAPs: concert prices "tan bajos como 54 euros (o incluso menos)" (as low as €54 or even less) per place per day must rise to at least €75. Quote: "sin financiación suficiente" (without enough funding) the model cannot be applied — [Diari de Tarragona, 11 Jul 2022](https://www.diaridetarragona.com/sociedad/no-hay-dinero-para-aplicar-el-nuevo-modelo-de-residencias-OA11661096)
  - Ratio target of 0.51 professionals per resident by 2029; about 21,071 extra residence jobs, part of a total of more than 34,000 new jobs — [Valencia Plaza](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/el-gobierno-preve-que-el-acuerdo-del-modelo-de-residencias-suponga-la-creacion-de-mas-de-34000-empleos) (search summary)
  - Madrid: €54.51 to €74.88/day; direct care 0.42 to 0.47; gerocultores 0.33; cleaning, food and maintenance 0.15; direct-care staff cannot be subcontracted — [Comunidad de Madrid, 28 Jan 2021](https://www.comunidad.madrid/node/59061)
- Scale: No reliable count of residences or residents was found in this session. A search summary also attributed to CEAPs a figure of about 180,000 residents and 33,000 day-centre users paying out of pocket, but the source page could not be identified, so the figure is not used.
- Cost/impact: A gap of about €21 or more per place per day between low concert prices and the €75 CEAPs estimate (my calculation from the CEAPs figures).
- Driver & status: The 2022 Consejo Territorial accreditation agreement, with existing centres due by December 2029. Its transposition status in each region as of Oct 2026 was not verified, and the sources are from 2021–2022.
- How it is solved today: Re-tendered framework agreements at higher prices (Madrid). Rostering, family-communication and care-record software was not researched.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Rostering against ratios, compliance reporting to regions and family communication can be served by software. Funding and buildings cannot.
- Confidence: Medium (old sources; 2026 status unverified).

### PRO-17: Home-help (SAD) providers squeezed by capped hourly prices in municipal tenders that include travel time
- Who: SAD companies, from small regional operators to large groups, plus not-for-profits (e.g., Cruz Roja in Orihuela).
- Where: Municipal, funded by the region. Documented in Vigo (Galicia), El Puerto de Santa María (Cádiz), Orihuela (Alicante), Granada and Catalonia.
- Day-to-day reality: The company bids a price per hour of care. Travel between homes, coordination, supervision and training must be covered within that price, while collective-agreement wage rises keep pushing costs up.
- Evidence:
  - Vigo: €49.5M over 3 years (about €16M a year), hourly price rising from €16.70 to €22 "por el incremento de salarios" (because of wage increases). The mayor demands the Xunta pay it "íntegramente" (in full) — [Vigo e, 17 Mar 2025](https://vigoe.es/vigo/local/vigo-pone-en-marcha-uno-de-sus-megacontratos-y-caballero-exige-que-lo-pague-la-xunta)
  - A Catalan tender states the hourly price "incluye todos los costes organizativos como desplazamientos, coordinación, supervisión y formación" (includes all organisational costs such as travel, coordination, supervision and training) — [TED 2025 notices](https://ted.europa.eu/es/notice/92082-2025/pdf) (search summary; the exact notice was not confirmed)
  - Orihuela: cap of €17/h; €206,672 over 2 years — [Alicante Plaza, 15 Apr 2020](https://alicanteplaza.es/alicanteplaza/cuatro-empresas-optan-al-contrato-de-prestacion-del-servicio-municipal-de-ayuda-a-domicilio-en-orihuela) (dated)
  - El Puerto de Santa María: more than €5.6M over 2 years for about 400 users, funded mainly by the Junta de Andalucía. A worker agreement caps the working week at 35 hours — [Diario Bahía de Cádiz, 19 Jul 2023](https://diariobahiadecadiz.com/el-puerto/licitado-en-el-puerto-por-un-precio-historico-el-servicio-de-ayuda-a-domicilio)
  - The accreditation agreement requires at least 50% full-time contracts in SAD — [Público, 29 Jun 2022](https://www.publico.es/sociedad/seran-partir-residencias-mayores.html)
- Scale: Not found nationally.
- Cost/impact: Margins hinge on the gap between the price cap and wage rises. Loss figures were not found.
- Driver & status: Public procurement (Ley 9/2017 LCSP) and dependency-law funding. Ongoing.
- How it is solved today: Re-tendering at higher prices and lobbying regions. Scheduling and route software was not researched.
- Software-solvable?: Partly. Route optimisation to cut unpaid travel, visit verification, hours reporting to councils and rostering. The price cap itself is not fixable by software.
- Confidence: Medium.

### PRO-18: Households as employers of domestic workers — risk assessment (RD 893/2024) on top of the 2022 reform's duties
- Who: Households employing domestic and care workers (empleadas de hogar), plus the gestorías that handle their payroll.
- Where: National.
- Day-to-day reality: Each employing household must complete an online risk assessment of the home, implement measures, hand information material to the worker and keep the signed plan for labour inspectors. This comes on top of Social Security registration and contributions, including unemployment contributions since 2022.
- Evidence:
  - The free tool on Prevencion10.es was launched on 14 May 2025. Employers "dispondrán de un plazo de seis meses" (have six months) to complete the assessment, finishing "antes del próximo noviembre" (before next November). More than 348,000 women are registered with Social Security, about 38,000 of them live-in workers, with an average salary of about €1,014/month — [INSST press release, 14 May 2025](https://www.insst.es/documents/d/portal-insst/yolanda-diaz-presenta-la-herramienta-para-evaluar-los-riesgos-laborales-de-las-trabajadoras-del-hogar-nota-de-prensa)
  - Households' deadline 14 Nov 2025. Results are "no se envía a ningún lado, se guarda en la casa" (not sent anywhere, kept at home). Working-time records had not been implemented. At least a quarter of households with undocumented live-in workers will not do the assessment (claim by a workers' association) — [El Salto, 26 May 2025](https://www.elsaltodiario.com/trabajo-domestico/prevencion10-herramienta-evaluacion-riesgos-empleo-hogar)
  - More than 378,000 workers registered under the special Social Security scheme against about 540,000 counted in the EPA labour-force survey (implying informal work). The sector lost 53,000 workers in a year. The employers' association AESPD says the minimum wage has risen 61% since 2018. The platform collapsed, with the Ministry reporting almost 50M access attempts in 24 h and more than 16,000 suspicious IPs blocked. The technical guide, harassment protocol and training platform promised for early 2026 were still pending in May 2026. Unions UGT and CCOO asked the labour inspectorate not to be punitive at first — [El Español, 20 May 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/mujer/estilo-vida/20260520/asesora-financiera-nueva-ley-limpiadoras-hogar-unico-consigue-nadie-contrate/1003744249353_0.amp.html)
  - RD 893/2024 has been in force since 13 Sep 2024, with duties enforceable six months after the tool became available — [umivale activa report](https://umivaleactiva.es/dam/umivale-activa/novedades-normativas/2024/Informe-Externo-RD-893_2024.pdf) (search summary)
- Scale: About 348,000–378,000 registered workers (and roughly as many employing households). About 540,000 domestic workers in the EPA.
- Cost/impact: The tool is free. Fines were not quantified, as the press only mentions "amenaza de sanciones" (threat of sanctions). Opinion from an adviser: "Así lo único que conseguirán es que nadie las contrate" ("All they will achieve is that nobody hires them") (El Español, 20 May 2026).
- Driver & status: RD 893/2024 is in force and the household deadline (Nov 2025) has passed. The 2022 reform (RDL 16/2022, unemployment contributions) is in force (described in the INSST release and El Salto, which also cites ILO Convention 189, in force in Spain since 24 Feb 2024). The government guides were still pending as of May 2026.
- How it is solved today: Prevencion10 (free, risk assessment only), and gestorías or advisers for payroll (e.g., Dancausas Asesores is quoted). Household-payroll platforms and their prices were not researched.
- Software-solvable?: Yes. An all-in-one household-employer app could handle the contract, Social Security registration, monthly contributions and payslips, store the Prevencion10 output, record working time and remind employers of renewals. The free government tool covers only one step.
- Confidence: High (official INSST source plus two independent press sources).

### Inferences
- Care-sector pains are mostly about price and funding (public tenders and concerts), so SaaS value is in productivity (routing, rostering) and compliance reporting, not in fixing margins.
- Households as employers are a large, fragmented, compliance-heavy segment of roughly 350,000–380,000 registered employers. The government's free tool handles one duty while leaving payroll and contracts to households. This is a plausible B2C or B2B2C opening through gestorías (inference).

### Gaps
- Gerocultor shortages and turnover in 2024–2026; each region's progress transposing the 2022 accreditation agreement: not verified.
- Current concert prices by region in 2025–2026 beyond Madrid: not found.
- Childcare and cleaning agencies (agencias de colocación): not researched.
- Household-payroll software and agency prices: not researched.
- Size of fines for households that skip the risk assessment: not found.

## Top pains
Ranked by severity times breadth, with one-line justifications.
1. **PRO-01/PRO-02 Insurer fee squeeze on self-employed health professionals.** National, decades-old and cross-professional (doctors, psychologists, physiotherapists, podiatrists, nutritionists, dentists, speech therapists), with fees of €5–20 per session and €17–19 per specialist consultation. It reached the CNMC in March 2026. Software helps only indirectly.
2. **PRO-12 MASC requirement for civil claims.** In force since 3 Apr 2025 and affecting a civil system with 3.56M new cases a year. It brings a documentary burden of proof, inadmission risk and inconsistent court criteria, and fits software well.
3. **PRO-14 Driving-school exam bottleneck.** National, with hotspots in Andalusia, Catalonia, Madrid, Valencia and Baleares, protests in 2026 and examiners at 12 tests a day. Its size is disputed, but the scheduling and readiness problem is real, and software solves it only partly.
4. **PRO-10/PRO-11 Gestorías' staff shortage and invoicing rules that keep moving.** These firms serve more than 3M SMEs and autónomos. 7 in 10 struggle to hire, VeriFactu has been postponed again (announced for Oct 2028), and B2B e-invoicing is fixed for 6 Oct 2027 / 6 Oct 2028. A strong fit for software.
5. **PRO-18 Households as employers of domestic workers.** About 348,000–378,000 registered workers, a new risk-assessment duty (Nov 2025) on top of payroll, and a government platform that collapsed. A fit for software.
6. **PRO-05 Veterinary PRESVET reporting and sanctions.** About 16,460 clinicians, fortnightly reporting and fines up to €1.2M. The pain is high, but 80% of reporting is already automated, leaving compliance logic as the opening.
7. **PRO-16/PRO-17 Care providers (residences, SAD) squeezed between public prices and staffing rules.** Ratios are due by 2029 against concert prices of €54/day or less, and SAD hourly caps include travel. Software solves it only partly.
8. **PRO-03 Clinic no-shows.** Widespread (12–19%, €2,500–7,500/month per centre, vendor figures), but cheap tools already exist (about €13/month), so it is a weak standalone wedge.

## Noise and dead ends
- **Doctoralia/Top Doctors directory pricing (PRO-04):** prices are unpublished, complaints thin (Capterra blocked) and Top Doctors evidence absent. Weakly evidenced.
- **VeriFactu as an urgent October 2026 deadline:** postponed repeatedly. The legal dates are now 2027, and a move to Oct 2028 has been announced. It is a moving target, so standalone VeriFactu products are risky.
- **0–3 ratio cut (PRO-15):** only a proposal (no BOE text, no ratio figures found). Closure numbers are ACADE projections.
- **Driving-school "waiting list" totals:** the DGT showed fewer than 10% of claimed students were presented when unlimited exams were offered. The headline numbers (82,000, 63,000) overstate immediate demand.
- **Pharmacy shortages (PRO-07):** improving in Spain (−27% medicines affected in 2025) and already served by the profession's own free tools (CisMED, FarmaHelp). Partly solved.
- **Vet PRESVET reporting automation:** 80% already automated through practice software, so a reporting-only product would be late.
- **Vet dispensing limits and 21% VAT on veterinary services:** real complaints, but regulatory and tax issues, not solvable by software.
- **AEPD fines against clinics:** few documented 2024–2026 cases were found (the clearest is a €16,000 pharmacy case). Public bodies get warnings rather than fines. The enforcement risk to private clinics is weakly evidenced.
- **Psychologists' professional-liability insurance:** largely solved through colegio group policies. COP Madrid has a group policy with Markel, limit €6,000,000 per claim, 20 May 2025 – 19 May 2026 ([COP Madrid policy document](https://www.copmadrid.org/web//img_db/uploads/2025/06/img-683ec96b0b725.pdf)). Not a priority pain.
- **LexNET outages:** no 2025–2026 outage evidence was found. The documented LexNET pain (365-day notifications) comes from 2018–2020 sources.
- **Physiotherapy at €5/session and dental "discount plans":** the evidence is from 2018–2020, so treat it as dated and not current fact.
- **Telehealth rules:** no evidence of a specific pain was found (not researched in depth).
