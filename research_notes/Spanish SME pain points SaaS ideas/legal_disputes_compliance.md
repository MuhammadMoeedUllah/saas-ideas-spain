# Cross-sector legal exposure of Spanish SMEs, autónomos and startups: disputes, sanctions and horizontal compliance 2024–2027 (excluding labour and tax)

*Research date: 23 October 2026. Method: web search plus direct reading of primary sources (BOE, CGPJ statistical PDFs, EU Legislative Observatory, Moncloa, OEPM) and secondary legal and press sources. **Limitation:** the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. Everything after that point was checked only by fetching pages already found or official URLs. As a result, several dispute types and market-price questions have no evidence here and are listed under Gaps and "Noise and dead ends". Statuses are given as of 23 Oct 2026. Where a status could not be checked against an official source, that is stated.*

## 1. Litigation landscape: civil and commercial case volumes, delays by city, cost of litigating

### Takeaway
New civil cases fell in 2025: −7.7% nationally, −13.2% in first-instance courts, and −36% in Madrid's civil courts in Q3 2025. The drop coincides with the MASC pre-litigation requirement, but speed got worse. Estimated first-instance civil duration rose to 11.0 months and appeals to 9.6 months. The commercial courts (juzgados de lo mercantil) took 18.6% more cases with a resolution rate below 1. About 1.38 million enforcement files are pending in first-instance courts alone. Murcia, Madrid and Sevilla have the most congested civil courts; Zaragoza and Valladolid the least.

- Court fees (tasas judiciales) for companies: the **original** 2012 text of Ley 10/2012 set fixed civil fees (verbal €150; ordinario €300; monitorio €100; extrajudicial enforcement €200; involuntary insolvency €200; appeal €800; cassation €1,200) plus a variable 0.5% on the first €1M (variable part capped at €10,000) and 0.25% above that. ### Inferences
- The pendency rate is usually defined as pending ÷ resolved, so it roughly measures how many years of current output sit in the backlog. On that reading, an SME suing in Madrid faces about a year of queued civil work, Murcia about 17–18 months, and Zaragoza or Valladolid about 6 months. This is inference, not a CGPJ duration figure.
- Falling filings alongside rising pending stock in Madrid (+15.4%) supports the claim that MASC is creating a "funnel" (efecto embudo) rather than reducing disputes. See section 2.
- The +18.6% in commercial-court filings is probably driven partly by insolvency (concursos), which another researcher covers. It should not be read as pure growth in B2B, partner or IP disputes.
- A judgment is not the end of the problem. About 1.4M enforcement files are pending in first-instance courts, so collecting after winning is a separate pain (it overlaps with the debt-collection researcher).

- No case-type breakdown that isolates SME claimants: B2B contract claims, partner/shareholder actions (impugnación de acuerdos sociales), franchise, IP or unfair-competition cases. The CGPJ PxWeb tables exist ([PxWeb Mercantil](https://www6.poderjudicial.es/PXWeb-2025-v1/pxweb/es/06.-Juzgados%20de%20lo%20Mercantil/-/OUJMC001.px/)) but did not render.
- No commercial-court durations by city.
- No verified current court-fee amounts or exemptions for legal persons. No data on typical lawyer or procurador fees for a €5k–€50k B2B claim.

## 2. Ley Orgánica 1/2025 (justice-efficiency law): MASC pre-litigation requirement, tribunales de instancia, LexNET

### Takeaway
Since 3 April 2025, a documented good-faith attempt at negotiation (a MASC, *medio adecuado de solución de controversias*) has been a condition of admissibility (*requisito de procedibilidad*) for most civil and commercial claims. Eighteen months later there is still no official channel, registry or form. Courts apply different evidence standards city by city. About half of the Madrid lawyers surveyed report trouble proving the attempt, and a paid private market has emerged (certified notifications and online mediation at about €110–150 + VAT per file). The parallel conversion of about 3,800 single-judge courts into 431 *tribunales de instancia* brought misrouted filings and automatic LexNET returns.

### Cited Findings
- LO 1/2025 was published in the BOE on 3 Jan 2025 and took effect on 3 Apr 2025, making a MASC attempt compulsory before civil or commercial claims (3 Apr 2025) — [Economist & Jurist](https://www.economistjurist.es/?p=223479)
- Valid MASC methods: mediation, conciliation, independent expert opinion, confidential binding offer, and other negotiation recognised by law. The claim must attach proof and a short description of the negotiation. Refusing to take part counts against the refuser when costs are awarded. A party ordered to pay costs can ask for them to be reduced if it made an offer the other side rejected and the judgment is similar to that offer. The law also introduces the concept of "abuso del servicio público de justicia" (20 Jan 2025) — [Osborne Clarke](https://www.osborneclarke.com/es/insights/ley-organica-12025-cambios-en-la-jurisdiccion-civil-nueva-estructura-de-la-oficina)
- About 3,800 single-judge first-instance courts are being transformed into 431 tribunales de instancia in stages — [Osborne Clarke (20 Jan 2025)](https://www.osborneclarke.com/es/insights/ley-organica-12025-cambios-en-la-jurisdiccion-civil-nueva-estructura-de-la-oficina)
- Art. 10.2 requires proof of receipt, date and access to the full communication, but "la ley no define ni el soporte ni la autoridad que lo certifique" ("the law defines neither the medium nor the authority that certifies it"). No official channel exists: additional provision 3 (DA 3ª) foresees MASC services run by the Ministry and the regions, but there is no public registry, official form or certified channel, and the implementing rules are pending (6 Oct 2026) — [Moncloa.com](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/10/06/requisito-masc-lo-1-2025-juzgados-3443197)
- ICAM survey of 1,164 Madrid lawyers (December 2025): 49.3% had problems proving the MASC attempt in court, with inadmissions in many cases; 71.7% say courts interpret the requirement differently. **Single outlet reporting the survey** — [Moncloa.com (6 Oct 2026)](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/10/06/requisito-masc-lo-1-2025-juzgados-3443197)
- A private eIDAS-certified MASC platform (not named in the article) charges €110 + VAT to open a file (€150 + VAT for more than one invited party), €100 + VAT for the first mediation session and €120 + VAT per party for later sessions. It issues a certificate when the other side rejects or after 30 calendar days (art. 10.4). Its promoters argue a law firm cannot certify its own files. **Single source** — [Moncloa.com (6 Oct 2026)](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/10/06/requisito-masc-lo-1-2025-juzgados-3443197)
- Vendor data from Legal Pigeon: about 1,500 mediation files handled; 36% of sessions reached agreement in Q1 2026; MASC adds 30–90 days before a claim starts; the invited party has 30 days to decide; no official CGPJ MASC statistics exist. The company sells MASC mediation, so these figures are self-reported (7 May 2026) — [Legal Pigeon blog](https://www.legalpigeon.com/masc-un-ano-despues-mediacion-ley-1-2025/). Its service page promises a certificate "en menos de 30 días" from Ministry-registered mediators and publishes no prices (accessed 23 Oct 2026) — [Legal Pigeon MASC page](https://www.legalpigeon.com/masc-mediacion-conciliacion-masc-ley-2025/)
- Rush before the deadline: about 90,000 claims were pending distribution in Madrid and Barcelona (an estimate attributed to court offices), and a Madrid procurador saw nearly 80 filings a day. Free judicial conciliation before the court clerk (LAJ) "takes about six months" (3 Apr 2025) — [Economist & Jurist](https://www.economistjurist.es/?p=223479)
- Local judges' agreements (juntas de jueces) diverge by city:
  - Las Palmas de Gran Canaria (3 Apr 2025): MASC is required in monitorio (fast-track debt claims), and omitting it cannot be cured.
  - Valencia (31 Mar 2025): missing negotiation, or a missing description of it, is an incurable defect under art. 403.2 LEC.
  - Sevilla LAJ (1 Apr 2025): email counts only if the recipient consented and confirmed; WhatsApp, SMS and calls are excluded; the claim must be filed within 1 year.
  - Cádiz LAJ (26 Mar 2025): minimum waits of 30 days (no response) or 3 months (after a failed first meeting).
  - Zaragoza LAJ (28 Mar 2025): burofax, buromail and certified email are accepted, and plain email if it was the usual channel.
  - Barcelona: the proof can be supplied within 10 days.
  - Source (7 May 2025) — [Martínez Bernabéu Abogados](https://martinezbernabeu-abogados.com/acuerdos-de-jueces-y-laj-sobre-la-lo-1-2025-y-los-masc-2/)
- Appeal courts pushing back:
  - AP Alicante, Auto 48/2025 (18 Jul 2025), overturned an inadmission: "no se puede obligar al acreedor a renunciar, total o parcialmente, al crédito" ("a creditor cannot be forced to waive all or part of the debt").
  - AP Huelva (22 Sep 2025) raised "dudas de constitucionalidad muy severas" ("very serious constitutional doubts").
  - AP Navarra, Auto 335/2025, overturned an inadmission as "excesivamente rigorista".
  - Source — [Legal Pigeon blog (7 May 2026)](https://www.legalpigeon.com/masc-un-ano-despues-mediacion-ley-1-2025/)
- ICAM one-year review (6 Apr 2026): MASC are generally not free, so the requirement adds costs. Problems with the tribunales de instancia rollout include no clearly identified responsible staff, cases waiting to be reassigned, duplicate old and new inboxes, **automatic returns of filings in LexNET**, uncertainty over where to file, delays in case distribution and scheduling, and staff shortages. ICAM asks for the costs penalty to be removed and for MASC to stay voluntary — [ICAM](https://web.icam.es/reforma-ley-1-2025-balance-icam/)
- Effect on filings, family law example: divorce filings fell 11.7% to 84,424 in 2025, and contested divorces fell 24.7%. The family lawyers' association AEAFA blames MASC for an "efecto embudo" (funnel effect) that delays court access by months. The CGPJ does not attribute the fall to MASC (3 Apr 2026) — [Economist & Jurist](https://www.economistjurist.es/noticias-juridicas/el-numero-de-divorcios-en-2025-cae-se-registraron-un-117-menos-de-demandas-de-disolucion-matrimonial-que-en-el-ano-anterior/)

### Inferences
- For an SME creditor, landlord-tenant, or buyer or seller of goods and services, every claim now needs a negotiation step that adds 1–3 months. It costs from nothing (free LAJ conciliation, but about 6 months) to roughly €110–€400 + VAT for a private certified file plus mediation sessions. If the proof does not meet the local court's standard, the claim is inadmissible.
- SMEs on the receiving end of MASC invitations also have a new duty. Ignoring an invitation can count against them on costs, so they need to track and answer invitations within the 30-day window.
- Because the rules differ by city, a product that encodes local admissibility rules (Las Palmas vs Valencia vs Barcelona vs Zaragoza) and generates a court-ready evidence pack would solve a concrete, documented problem.

### Gaps
- No official numbers on MASC attempts, success rates or inadmissions (the absence of CGPJ statistics is itself reported).
- Exact rollout dates for the tribunales de instancia phases (commonly cited as July, October and December 2025) were **not verified** against the BOE in this pass.
- No LexNET outage statistics.
- No price data for burofax, buromail or notarial requirements used as MASC evidence.

## 3. Common disputes: commercial leases, squatting of premises, B2B and supplier contracts, partners, franchises, IP, unfair competition, online reviews

### Takeaway
This pass found solid quantitative evidence only for IP (OEPM filings and refusals) and for the new legal rules on online reviews and personalised offers. Commercial-lease disputes, squatting of business premises, B2B supplier disputes, partner/shareholder conflicts, franchise disputes, unfair-competition litigation and fake-review extortion **remain unquantified** because the search budget ran out.

### Cited Findings
- National trademark applications at the Spanish patent and trademark office (OEPM): 57,158 in 2025 (+11.5%), against 51,264 in 2024, 48,773 in 2023 and 45,227 in 2022. Total industrial-property applications reached 92,569 (the best in 10 years). Industrial designs: 16,032 (+14.8%). Published 17 Feb 2026; 2025 data provisional — [OEPM news](https://oepm.es/es/detalle-noticia/La-OEPM-cierra-2025-con-record-de-solicitudes-de-titulos-de-Propiedad-Industrial/); [OEPM Estadísticas 2025 (PDF)](https://www.oepm.es/export/sites/portal/comun/documentos_relacionados/PDF/Estadisticas-OEPM-2025.pdf)

- Unfair competition: a new art. 27.9 of Ley 3/1991 (unfair competition law) treats as misleading any individually targeted offer based on automated decisions that does not disclose the personalisation parameters — [BOE, Ley 10/2025](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2025/12/26/10); [KPMG (Jan 2026)](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/es/pdf/2026/01/legal-alert-regulacion-servicios-atencion-clientela-ley-10-2025.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)
- Partner, IP and unfair-competition cases are heard in the Juzgados de lo Mercantil, whose 2025 intake rose 18.6% to 119,564 with a 0.92 resolution rate — [CGPJ Anual 2025 PDF](https://www.poderjudicial.es/stfls/SALA%20DE%20PRENSA/DOCUMENTOS%20DE%20INTERES/Datos%20Nacionales%20Anual%202025.pdf)

### Inferences
- About 6,500 trademark and trade-name refusals a year, against a fast-rising number of filings, point to many naming conflicts that small businesses usually discover after launch. Refusals arise mainly from oppositions by earlier rights holders; this is inference, as the opposition counts were not extracted.

## 4. Consumer-facing obligations and claims: complaint volumes, official complaint forms, Ley 10/2025 on customer services, warranties, price display

### Takeaway
Ley 10/2025 on customer services (*Ley de Servicios de Atención a la Clientela*, in force 28 Dec 2025) has two layers:
- **A strict customer-service regime** for providers of basic services of any size and for large companies (≥250 staff, or >€50M turnover, or >€43M balance sheet). They must comply by **28 Dec 2026**.
- **Amendments to the general consumer law (TRLGDCU) that reach every B2C business**: answer complaints within 15 days with an ID key and receipt; show the full final price including fees; give 15 days' notice before automatic renewal; state the review-verification policy; and contracts made through unsolicited calls are void.

National complaint volumes (OMIC municipal consumer offices, consumer arbitration, FACUA, OCU) were not obtained.

### Cited Findings
- Ley 10/2025 was published in the BOE on 27 Dec 2025 and entered into force on 28 Dec 2025. In-scope companies have 12 months to adapt, i.e. until 28 Dec 2026 — [KPMG (Jan 2026)](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/es/pdf/2026/01/legal-alert-regulacion-servicios-atencion-clientela-ley-10-2025.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf); [Sepín (14 Jan 2026)](https://blog.sepin.es/blog/2026/01/14/novedades-ley-10-2025-servicios-atencion-clientela/); [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2025/12/26/10)
- Scope:
  - Art. 2.1: companies effectively providing water, gas or electricity; air, rail, sea or bus passenger transport; postal services; electronic communications; or financial services. **No size threshold.**
  - Art. 2.2: companies or groups selling mainly to consumers with ≥250 employees, or turnover >€50M, or balance sheet >€43M.
  - Cooperatives and non-profits with <250 workers in basic services get an adapted regime.
  - Financial services and telecoms are governed first by their sector rules.
  - Sources — [KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/es/pdf/2026/01/legal-alert-regulacion-servicios-atencion-clientela-ley-10-2025.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf); [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2025/12/26/10)
- Core obligations for in-scope firms:
  - Free and accessible service; automated answering cannot be the only channel; a human must be reachable from the main menu at any time.
  - 95% of requests for a human must be answered within 3 minutes on average; calls may not be cut because of long waits.
  - Every complaint gets a tracking key; at least 10 working days to complete an incomplete complaint.
  - Resolution within **15 working days** (2 hours for interruptions of basic services, 5 days for billing).
  - 24/7 incident line for continuous basic services.
  - An evaluation system audited **every year by ENAC-accredited auditors**.
  - Service in the official language the customer chooses in regions with a co-official language.
  - Sources — [KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/es/pdf/2026/01/legal-alert-regulacion-servicios-atencion-clientela-ley-10-2025.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf); [Sepín](https://blog.sepin.es/blog/2026/01/14/novedades-ley-10-2025-servicios-atencion-clientela/)
- Small energy and telecom retailers: until they reach 5% national market share (and if not large under art. 2.2), electricity, gas and telephone retailers are exempt from art. 14.2 (24/7 line) and arts. 16.2–16.4 (customer-service numbering and call blocking) — [BOE, transitional provision 4 (DT 4ª)](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2025/12/26/10); [KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/es/pdf/2026/01/legal-alert-regulacion-servicios-atencion-clientela-ley-10-2025.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)
- Sanctions: breaches are consumer-law infringements under the TRLGDCU and regional consumer laws — [KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/es/pdf/2026/01/legal-alert-regulacion-servicios-atencion-clientela-ley-10-2025.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf). A compliance vendor says fines can reach €100,000 per infringement. **Commercial source, seen only in a search snippet, not verified** — [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/es-es/servicios/sostenibilidad/soluciones-sostenibilidad-buen-gobierno/sostenibilidad-social/asesoramiento-cumplimiento-ley-servicio-atencion-cliente)
- TRLGDCU amendments by Ley 10/2025, final provision 3, which apply to traders (*empresarios*) generally:
  - Art. 20.1.c: full final price including taxes, discounts and extra charges, including management fees.
  - Art. 20.4: review verification statement.
  - Art. 21.2–21.3: complaints get an ID key and written receipt; phone lines cannot cost more than a standard call; complaints must be answered within "el plazo máximo de quince días" ("a maximum of fifteen days").
  - Art. 62.1: phone contracts concluded in breach of unsolicited-call rules are presumed to lack consent and are void; consent must be express and given within the two years before the call.
  - Art. 97.1.p: 15 days' notice before the deadline to opt out of a renewal.
  - Final provision 8 (DF 8ª): entry into force the day after publication.
  - Source — [BOE, Ley 10/2025](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2025/12/26/10)
  - KPMG summarises the renewal rule as 15 days' notice before automatic renewal of services or subscriptions contracted online, plus easy cancellation (Jan 2026) — [KPMG](https://assets.kpmg.com/content/dam/kpmgsites/es/pdf/2026/01/legal-alert-regulacion-servicios-atencion-clientela-ley-10-2025.pdf.coredownload.inline.pdf)
- Co-official languages: consumer groups' testimony in Congress (16 Oct 2025).
  - OCU: "al final ese coste se va a repercutir al consumidor" ("in the end the cost will be passed on to the consumer").
  - FACUA: many firms outsource customer service to Colombia or Morocco, where co-official languages are scarce.
  - The customer-service employers' association AEERC said the text "criminaliza" ("criminalises") companies.
  - Source — [Forbes España](https://forbes.es/?p=814900)
- Catalonia: under Codi de consum art. 126-9, consumers may request an official complaint form (hoja oficial de reclamación o denuncia), which must also be available online — [BOE, Llei 22/2010](https://www.boe.es/eli/es-ct/l/2010/07/20/22)
- Catalan consumer agency (Agència Catalana del Consum, ACC) total fines: €6.4M (2022) → €10.5M (2023, +62%). Inspections focused on energy, finance, transport and telecoms (14 Mar 2024) — [Vozpópuli](https://www.vozpopuli.com/espana/cataluna/generalitat-cuadruplica-ano-multas-castellano-ya-recauda-30-veces-mas-que-en-2021.html)

### Inferences
- The 15-day complaint deadline, the ID key and receipt, and the renewal notice affect every B2C SME (shops, gyms, academies, clinics, e-commerce, subscription startups) from 28 Dec 2025. Most handle complaints by email or WhatsApp with no logging, so they cannot prove they met the deadline. A lightweight "complaints and claims register" with SLA timers and receipts is a clear software fit.
- Ley 10/2025's full regime hits a few thousand SMEs at most: small internet providers, local water companies, small energy retailers and bus operators. These firms now face an annual ENAC-audited customer-service evaluation designed for large companies. The 5% exemption only removes the 24/7 line and numbering rules, not the 3-minute rule or the audit. The number of such firms is not quantified.
- It is unclear whether the 12-month adaptation period (transitional provision 1) also covers the TRLGDCU amendments (art. 21 etc.), which appear to be in force since 28 Dec 2025. A lawyer should confirm this.

### Gaps
- No national 2024–2025 complaint volumes: Ministerio de Consumo, OMIC offices, consumer arbitration boards (Juntas Arbitrales), FACUA and OCU annual figures.
- No fine levels by region for missing or unanswered hojas de reclamaciones (Madrid, Andalucía, C. Valenciana).
- Warranty and returns (3-year legal guarantee) and price-display rules are covered by the retail/e-commerce researcher and were not re-verified here.
- Previous TRLGDCU deadline for answering complaints (believed to be one month) not re-verified.

## 6. Horizontal obligations 2024–2027 and their real status (accessibility, AI Act, NIS2, ESG/VSME, LSSI and cookies, unsolicited calls)

### Takeaway
Status as of 23 Oct 2026:
- **In force:**
  - Accessibility (EAA / Ley 11/2023), since 28 Jun 2025; micro-enterprises providing services are exempt.
  - AI Act art. 4 (AI literacy, softened by the omnibus) and art. 50 transparency, since 2 Aug 2026.
  - **The 400 prefix for commercial calls, with operators blocking other calls since 17 Oct 2026.**
  - The Ley 10/2025 consumer-law changes, since 28 Dec 2025.
- **Upcoming:**
  - Ley 10/2025 customer-service adaptation: 28 Dec 2026.
  - AI watermarking grace period ends: 2 Dec 2026.
  - AI Act high-risk obligations: 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I).
  - Transposition of the CSRD omnibus: 19 Mar 2027.
- **Draft or stalled:**
  - Spain's AI organic law (bill in Congress).
  - Spain's NIS2 transposition (draft bill only; the Commission has referred Spain to the CJEU).
  - The EU Digital Omnibus amending GDPR and cookie rules (still in European Parliament committee).
- **Largely removed for SMEs:** CSRD reporting duties.

### Cited Findings
- **Accessibility:** Ley 11/2023 requirements have applied since 28 Jun 2025, with WCAG 2.1 AA and EN 301 549 as the technical references, including for e-commerce. Micro-enterprises providing services (<10 staff and ≤€2M turnover or balance sheet) are exempt; those making or selling products are not. Existing products and services can continue until 28 Jun 2030. Fines (via RDLeg 1/2013): minor up to €30,000; serious €30,001–€90,000; very serious up to €1M. Regions designate the enforcement authorities, and RD 143/2026 (25 Feb 2026) created a national technical coordination unit. **No enforcement actions reported.** Published 30 Jul 2026, updated 30 Sep 2026 — [RedInclusiva](https://redinclusiva.com/actualidad/ley-de-accesibilidad-de-productos-y-servicios-que-obliga-ya-a-webs-apps-y-comercios)
- Accessibility vendors (accessed 23 Oct 2026):
  - Accede Me: claims ">3.000 webs" use its widget; audits signed by a court-registered expert (perito judicial); claims "respaldo legal completo" ("full legal backing"), which is **unverified**; no public prices (7-day trial) — [Accede Me](https://accedeme.com/)
  - Lawwwing's WCAG module: €28/month — [Lawwwing pricing](https://lawwwing.com/precios/)
- **EU AI Act digital omnibus:** Regulation (EU) 2026/1744, in force 27 Jul 2026.
  - Annex III high-risk obligations moved to 2 Dec 2027; Annex I to 2 Aug 2028; high-risk systems used by public authorities to 2 Aug 2030.
  - Art. 4 (AI literacy) stays on providers and deployers but is softened to "take measures to support" literacy.
  - Providers whose systems were on the market before 2 Aug 2026 have until 2 Dec 2026 to comply with the art. 50(2) marking duty.
  - SMEs and small mid-caps get simplified technical documentation and quality management, and authorities must weigh SME economic viability when fining.
  - Source — [artificialintelligenceact.eu](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/ai-act-explorer/digital-omnibus/)
- Art. 50 transparency applies from 2 Aug 2026 and was not amended: users must be told they are talking to an AI; deepfakes must be disclosed; AI-generated public-interest text must be disclosed unless edited by a person. A new ban on AI-generated non-consensual intimate imagery and child sexual abuse material applies from 2 Dec 2026. Timeline: European Parliament approval 16 Jun 2026; Council adoption 29 Jun 2026; signed 8 Jul 2026 — [Usercentrics](https://usercentrics.com/knowledge-hub/eu-ai-act-high-risk-delay-article-50-transparency-consent/). Gibson Dunn (27 May 2026) confirms that art. 50 is not delayed and that art. 4 has applied since 2 Feb 2025 — [Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/). **Minor conflict:** political agreement dated 6 May (Gibson Dunn) or 7 May 2026 (Usercentrics).
- **Spain's AI law (Proyecto de Ley Orgánica para el buen uso y la gobernanza de la IA):** sent to Congress by the Council of Ministers on 26 May 2026; published in the parliamentary bulletin on 12 Jun 2026 (BOCG A-97-1); needs an absolute majority. Fines: minor up to €500k or 0.5% of turnover; very serious up to €35M or 7%. For SMEs and startups the lower of the two caps applies. AESIA, the AEPD, the CGPJ, Banco de España, CNMV and DGSFP act as market-surveillance authorities (16 Jun 2026) — [CMS](https://cms.law/es/esp/publication/claves-del-proyecto-de-ley-organica-de-inteligencia-artificial). AESIA runs a single window for reporting infringements and the mandatory sandbox (15 Jun 2026) — [Economist & Jurist](https://www.economistjurist.es/zbloque-1/ley-organica-de-ia-espana-aterriza-el-ai-act-con-aesia-sanciones-y-sandboxes/). - **NIS2:** Spain missed the 17 Oct 2024 transposition deadline. The draft Ley de Coordinación y Gobernanza de la Ciberseguridad was approved in first round on 14 Jan 2025, but it is not a bill in Congress and not in the BOE. The Commission issued a reasoned opinion on 7 May 2025 (INFR(2024)0270) and referred Spain to the CJEU on 8 Jul 2026, seeking a lump sum and daily penalties. The old NIS1 framework (RDL 12/2018, RD 43/2021) still applies. Directive maximum fines: €10M or 2% (essential entities) and €7M or 1.4% (important entities).   - A Ministry of Digital Transformation resolution requires all commercial calls to come from 9-digit numbers starting with **400**. Operators block calls that do not, from October 2026. ## Structured pain-point inventory

### LEG-01: Mandatory pre-litigation negotiation (MASC): proving the attempt, inadmission risk and delay
- Who: all sectors. Autónomos, micro, small and medium firms, and startups, both as claimants (unpaid invoices, supplier or customer contract claims, lease claims) and as recipients of MASC invitations.
- Where: national, with **city-specific admissibility criteria** (Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, Valencia, Sevilla, Cádiz, Zaragoza, Teruel, Barcelona, Madrid).
- Day-to-day reality: before suing, the business must send a provable negotiation request (burofax, certified email, mediator or conciliation), wait 30 days to 3 months, and attach proof plus a description to the claim. If the court considers the proof inadequate, the claim is inadmitted and the process restarts. Incoming invitations must be answered, or the business risks a costs penalty.
- Evidence:
  - In force since 3 Apr 2025 — [Economist & Jurist, 3 Apr 2025](https://www.economistjurist.es/?p=223479)
  - Refusing MASC counts against the refuser on costs — [Osborne Clarke, 20 Jan 2025](https://www.osborneclarke.com/es/insights/ley-organica-12025-cambios-en-la-jurisdiccion-civil-nueva-estructura-de-la-oficina)
  - ICAM survey (1,164 lawyers): 49.3% had problems proving the attempt; 71.7% see inconsistent interpretation. No official channel or registry; private solutions' validity "not guaranteed in advance" — [Moncloa.com, 6 Oct 2026](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/10/06/requisito-masc-lo-1-2025-juzgados-3443197) (single source for the survey)

Documented complaints: inconsistent acceptance by courts; ICAM calls the requirement disproportionate; no supplier can guarantee court acceptance.
- Software-solvable?: **yes.** Certified electronic delivery, timers for waiting periods, a city-aware admissibility checklist, a negotiation log and an automatic evidence pack are all workflow and document problems.
- Confidence: high

### LEG-11: Commercial phone calls: mandatory 400 prefix (blocking since 17 Oct 2026), consent, and void contracts

- Cost/impact: contact-centre staffing, annual audit fees (not quantified), consumer-law fines (up to €100,000 per infringement per a vendor, **unverified**) — [TÜV SÜD](https://www.tuvsud.com/es-es/servicios/sostenibilidad/soluciones-sostenibilidad-buen-gobierno/sostenibilidad-social/asesoramiento-cumplimiento-ley-servicio-atencion-cliente)

- How it is solved today: big-company contact-centre suites; advisory and audit firms (TÜV SÜD, KPMG). No SME pricing found.

## Top pains
1. **LEG-01 MASC pre-litigation requirement.** Every SME claim since April 2025 has to pass through it; there is no official channel; 49% of surveyed lawyers report proof problems; rules differ by city; inadmission is the penalty; and a paid market (€110+ VAT per file) has already formed. This is the most acute and software-shaped new legal pain.
2. **LEG-12 New consumer-law duties for all B2C businesses.** In force since 28 Dec 2025: 15-day complaint answers with ID keys and receipts, full-price display, renewal notices, review statements and void phone contracts. It affects millions of small B2C businesses and no SME tool exists yet.
3. **LEG-05 / LEG-06 / LEG-07 Complaint-driven data-protection exposure.** 30,931 complaints (+64%), video surveillance the #2 complaint area (4,050), commerce and hospitality fines up 1,653%. Fines are small for SMEs, but procedures are slow, appeals fail and everyday errors keep recurring.
4. **LEG-11 400 prefix and unsolicited-call rules.** A hard switch on 17 Oct 2026: non-compliant sales calls are blocked outright, contracts from unconsented calls are void, and LGTel fines reach €2M (vendor claim). It hits every phone-selling SME immediately.
5. **LEG-02 Slow civil justice and enforcement backlog.** Civil first instance takes 11 months (+6.4%), appeals 9.6 months, 1.38M enforcement files are pending, and Madrid and Murcia are worst. It is structural and widespread, but only partly software-solvable.
6. **LEG-15 Catalan-language compliance.** Regional only, but heavily evidenced: complaints up ninefold between 2021 and 2023, fines collected about 30 times higher, restaurants and bars about half of cases. The risk is mostly in fixed written material, which software can handle.
7. **LEG-13 Customer-service regime for basic-service SMEs.** A niche of small internet providers, energy retailers, transport operators and water utilities, facing a hard deadline of 28 Dec 2026, a 3-minute rule and annual ENAC audits designed for large firms.
8. **LEG-17 AI Act transparency and literacy.** In force since August 2026 for any SME using chatbots or AI content, but national penalties are still pending, so the urgency is moderate.

## Noise and dead ends
- **CSRD / ESG value-chain reporting for SMEs:** Omnibus I (Directive (EU) 2026/470) limits CSRD to firms with more than 1,000 employees and €450M turnover and shields under-1,000 firms from requests beyond voluntary standards. RD 214/2025 carbon footprints apply only to large reporting companies and the state sector. Weak as an SME *legal* pain; any remaining market pressure from banks and clients is not evidenced here.
- **NIS2 direct obligations for small firms:** not transposed in Spain (draft bill only; CJEU referral) and small firms are generally out of direct scope. The real pain is indirect client questionnaires (LEG-18). Do not build on a Spanish NIS2 deadline yet.
- **AI Act high-risk obligations:** postponed to 2 Dec 2027 (Annex III) and 2 Aug 2028 (Annex I). The Spanish AI law is still a bill.
- **EU Digital Omnibus cookie relief:** still at European Parliament committee stage (draft report June 2026), so do not assume banners become unnecessary. Conversely, cookie-banner compliance is **already cheaply solved** (€0–€12 a month).
- **Ley 10/2025's core customer-service regime for ordinary SMEs:** it does not apply below the 250-staff / €50M / €43M thresholds outside basic services. Only the consumer-law amendments (LEG-12) and the call-prefix rules (LEG-11) reach typical SMEs.
- **EAA for micro service businesses:** exempt. No enforcement reported anywhere in Spain yet. Overlay vendors' claims of "legal backing" are unverified.
- **AEPD "average fine €148k":** misleading for SMEs because it is skewed by 12 fines above €1M. Lawwwing's €33.9M total for 2025 conflicts with the €48.1M reported elsewhere.
- **Commercial-court growth (+18.6%):** probably driven partly by insolvency, not by B2B, partner or IP disputes. Do not use it as evidence of growth in partner disputes.
- **Court fees (tasas judiciales):** the current schedule for companies was not verified (2012 text partly annulled). Treat the cost figures as indicative only.
- **Not evidenced in this pass (search budget exhausted; follow-up needed before ranking):** commercial-lease rent rises and deposits; squatting of business premises; B2B supplier, partner/shareholder and franchise disputes; fake-review extortion; scam trademark-registry invoices; scammers posing as AEPD inspectors and "LOPD gratis" via FUNDAE; national consumer-complaint volumes (OMIC, arbitration, FACUA, OCU); municipal fines for terraces, signage and opening hours; prices of outsourced LOPD and DPO services.
