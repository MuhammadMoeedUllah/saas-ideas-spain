# Spanish construction, trades, real estate agencies, small landlords and property managers: SME pain points (2024–2026)

Prepared 23 October 2026. Pain-point entries are numbered CON-01 to CON-26 across the four key-question sections, then ranked in "Top pains". All facts carry an inline source and date. "Snippet-level" means the figure came from a search-result summary and the original page was not opened. Method caveat: the shared web-search budget ran out partway through. After that, facts were checked only by fetching specific primary or press URLs directly, mainly BOE consolidated texts, CGPJ, INSST and idealista/news articles. Some gaps below are a result of that.

---

## 1. Construction and trades (builders, renovation contractors, plumbers, electricians, HVAC and solar installers)

### Takeaway
The best-documented pains are:
- **Labour scarcity.** CNC puts the gap at about 700,000 workers, an advocacy estimate. The workforce is ageing: average age 45.1, only 10.8% under 30.
- **Compliance costs pushed down the chain by main contractors.** CAE document platforms charge each subcontractor roughly €100 to over €2,000 a year per platform. On top of that come Ley 32/2006 duties (REA registration, Libro de Subcontratación), with the contractor jointly liable for breaches.
- **A 21.5% rise in construction deaths in 2025**, the only sector where deaths went up.
- **87-day average payment terms in subcontracting.**
- **Permit and grant administration that runs to years.** One documented NextGenerationEU rehabilitation grant in Aragón took 30 months to approve, then sat unpaid for 10 more months.

Solar self-consumption installers lost about a third of their market in 2024. Software angles look strongest in four places: reusable CAE and subcontracting compliance documents, grant-file management, site-safety records, and simple quoting for micro firms.

### Cited Findings
- Construction employed 1,530,002 people in 2025 (+4.5%, 6.9% of Spanish employment). Of these, 25.3% were foreign, 9.9% women and 10.8% under 30 — [IFEMA / Fundación Laboral de la Construcción (FLC) Observatorio, 26 May 2026](https://www.ifema.es/construtec/noticias-sector/observatorio-construccion-informe-empleo-2025)
- National work deaths fell 9.6% in 2025, but construction rose by 29 deaths (+21.5%) — [INSST, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.insst.es/noticias-insst/cae-la-siniestralidad-laboral-y-se-reducen-un-9-6-por-ciento-los-accidentes-mortales)
- The average subcontracting payment term was 87 days in 2025 — [esDiario, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.esdiario.com/economia/260416/185240/85-grandes-empresas-incumple-plazos-legales-pago.html) (snippet-level)
- The structured entries below carry the detailed evidence.

### CON-01: Skilled-labour shortage and ageing site workforce
- **Who:** Construction firms of every size, but mainly micro (<10) and small (10–49) builders and renovation contractors. Also trades: bricklayers, formworkers, site foremen, crane operators.
- **Where:** National. The sources give no regional breakdown.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Firms cannot staff sites, especially with site foremen (*encargados*) and bricklayers (*albañiles*). The workforce is older and increasingly foreign, and few young people come in. The CNC presents this as a brake on housing delivery.
- **Evidence:**
  - The CNC says about 700,000 workers are missing to build the housing that PSOE and PP proposed. It blames an ageing workforce and lower qualifications among new entrants, and asks for vocational-training (FP) reform and for immigrants to be regularised or recruited — [merca2, 20 Jan 2025](https://www.merca2.es/2025/01/20/la-escasez-de-mano-de-obra-en-el-sector-de-la-construccion-agrava-la-crisis-de-la-vivienda-segun-la-cnc-2119541/).
    - The same figure was repeated in 2026: "carencia de 700.000 albañiles y operarios cualificados" ("a shortfall of 700,000 bricklayers and skilled operatives") — [El Español, 30 May 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/sociedad/20260530/juan-arquitecto-anos-experiencia-faltan-albaniles-espana-vez-mano-obra/1003744261899_0.html).
    - Caveat: the 700k figure is a CNC estimate that has stayed the same since 2021. It is a single, self-interested source.
  - In the CNC's first labour report, based on about 500 member firms, 65% found it "extremadamente difícil" ("extremely difficult") to hire site foremen. The hardest roles to fill were foremen, bricklayers, formworkers, concrete placers, carpenters, façade installers and crane operators — [Interempresas, 19 Apr 2022](https://www.interempresas.net/Construccion/386526-CNC-avisa-que-65-por-ciento-constructoras-ve-extremadamente-dificil-contratar-encargados.html). This predates 2024 and is context only.
  - FLC 2025 data:
    - 10.8% of workers are under 30, against 15.1% across all sectors.
    - 25.3% are foreign, against 15.7%.
    - 51.8% have lower-secondary education or less.
    - Source: [IFEMA/FLC, 26 May 2026](https://www.ifema.es/construtec/noticias-sector/observatorio-construccion-informe-empleo-2025).
  - Average age was 45.1 in 2025, up from 42.4 in 2015, and 22% of workers are 55 or older — [Alimarket on FLC "Radiografía del empleo en construcción 2025"](https://www.alimarket.es/construccion/noticia/426443/el-empleo-en-construccion-aumenta-un-4-5--en-la-comparativa-anual--siendo-el-sector-que-mas-crece); [IFEMA](https://www.ifema.es/construtec/noticias-sector/informe-fundacion-laboral-construccion) (snippet-level).
    - El Español reports that the 55+ group could retire within ten years, and that the under-30 share was 11.1% in Q1 2026 — [El Español, 1 Aug 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/ciencia/20260801/pascual-albanil-no-relevo-generacional-necesito-jovenes-quieran-aprender-no-encuentras/1003744335404_0.html) (snippet-level).
- **Scale:** 1.53 million construction workers ([FLC 2025](https://www.ifema.es/construtec/noticias-sector/observatorio-construccion-informe-empleo-2025)). I found no source for the number of firms affected.
- **Cost/impact:** No euro figure found. The CNC links the shortage to housing undersupply ([merca2, Jan 2025](https://www.merca2.es/2025/01/20/la-escasez-de-mano-de-obra-en-el-sector-de-la-construccion-agrava-la-crisis-de-la-vivienda-segun-la-cnc-2119541/)). In 2022 it warned the shortage could slow EU-funds execution ([Interempresas, 2022](https://www.interempresas.net/Construccion/386526-CNC-avisa-que-65-por-ciento-constructoras-ve-extremadamente-dificil-contratar-encargados.html)).
- **Driver & status:** A demographic and market force, not a regulation. The causes are baby-boomer retirements and young people's preference for non-manual jobs ([El Español, May 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/sociedad/20260530/juan-arquitecto-anos-experiencia-faltan-albaniles-espana-vez-mano-obra/1003744261899_0.html)). Ongoing. I did not verify the status of any immigration or FP measures.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - FLC training. The 2022 CNC report cited 80,000 students a year ([Interempresas, 2022](https://www.interempresas.net/Construccion/386526-CNC-avisa-que-65-por-ciento-constructoras-ve-extremadamente-dificil-contratar-encargados.html)).
  - Foreign hiring, which makes up 25.3% of the workforce.
  - Subcontracting.
  - I found no data on software spending.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Matching, crew scheduling and productivity tools can stretch scarce crews further, but they cannot create workers.
- **Confidence:** High that the shortage exists. Medium on the 700k figure.

### CON-02: CAE document platforms charge subcontractors once per client platform (RD 171/2004)
- **Who:** Subcontractors and autónomos in construction, installation and industrial maintenance, mostly autónomos, micro and small firms. Also the principal contractors that impose the platforms.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Each client or main contractor chooses its own CAE (*Coordinación de Actividades Empresariales*) platform. Subcontractors must register and pay on each one. They then upload the same prevention (PRL) paperwork, such as training, medical fitness, insurance and Social Security filings, and keep it current on every platform. Clients can tie site access or payment to the documents being validated.
- **Evidence:**
  - OCU public complaint, 11 Jul 2025. A subcontractor says Nalanda:
    - "tiene un precio elevadísimo" ("has an extremely high price");
    - "cambia sus tarifas cuando quiere, sin avisar" ("changes its rates whenever it wants, without warning");
    - and "si quieres trabajar con tus clientes estás OBLIGADO a pagarlo porque ellos son los que eligen a Nalanda" ("if you want to work with your clients you are OBLIGED to pay, because they are the ones who choose Nalanda").
    - No company reply is shown — [OCU reclamación pública, 11 Jul 2025](https://www.ocu.org/reclamar/lista-reclamaciones-publicas/mal-servicio-y-descuido-al-cli/882aec16fbadc4b4f3).
  - Nalanda has a TrustScore of 1.3/5 from 135 reviews, 97% of them one-star — [Trustpilot, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/www.nalandaglobal.com). These are unverified user reviews:
    - Oct 2026: fees went from "300 y pico" ("300-odd") to "más de 1400 euros" ("over 1,400 euros").
    - Oct 2026: "una cuota de 1400 euros para 100 trabajadores" ("a 1,400-euro fee for 100 workers").
    - 19 Oct 2026: "en una factura de 2000 euros anuales no puedo dar de alta más que a 15 trabajadores" ("for a 2,000-euro annual bill I can only register 15 workers").
    - Oct 2026: "50 eur por atención telefónica" ("50 euros for phone support").
    - 4 Dec 2025: automatic renewal "duplicando el precio" ("doubling the price").
  - Nalanda's own marketing tells clients they can condition "payments to your subcontractors to their correct documentary situation" — [Nalanda](https://nalandaglobal.com/en/?p=5276). Vendor source.
  - Published prices, from a competitor's comparison (prices checked 20 Oct 2026; vendor-authored, not neutral) — [DocuPRL, Jun 2026, updated Oct 2026](https://docuprl.es/blog/mejores-plataformas-cae-2026.html):
    - Dokify: from €490/yr for the principal; from €102.90/yr plus €15.44 per employee or machine for contractors (excl. VAT).
    - DocAcces: €32/month for up to 5 subcontractors, rising to €99 for up to 25.
    - UCAE: from €80/month.
    - PlayCAE: announced €199/month, with no cost to subcontractors.
    - Nalanda: price on request.
  - Legal basis: RD 171/2004 governs coordination when several companies share a workplace. The principal company answers for external workers' PRL compliance and Social Security registration — [Interempresas (Dokify-sponsored article)](https://www.interempresas.net/Logistica/612265-Gestion-de-subcontratas-como-garantizar-cumplimiento-normativo-y-evitar-riesgos-legales.html) (date not shown).
- **Scale:** Not quantified. I found no data on how many subcontractors are registered on these platforms.
- **Cost/impact:**
  - About €100 to over €2,000 a year per platform per subcontractor, from published prices and reviews.
  - Duplicated office hours, not quantified.
  - Risk of payment or site access being blocked.
- **Driver & status:** RD 171/2004 (CAE, implementing art. 24 of Ley 31/1995 PRL) is in force. For construction, Ley 32/2006 and RD 1109/2007 are also in force. No law requires any particular platform; the choice is the principals' market practice.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - Platforms: Nalanda, Dokify, plus Ctaima, e-coordina, Obralia and Metacontratas, whose prices and complaints I could not verify.
  - External prevention consultancies.
  - Office staff uploading documents by hand.
  - Documented complaints exist only for Nalanda, as above.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. An "upload once, sync to every client platform" document wallet with expiry alerts and connectors is pure software. A flat-fee model paid by the principal is another option.
- **Confidence:** High on the pain. Medium on price levels, which come from reviews and competitor sources.

### CON-03: Subcontracting-chain compliance under Ley 32/2006 (REA, Libro de Subcontratación) with joint liability
- **Who:** Main contractors and subcontractors, from micro to medium. Autónomos are barred from subcontracting.
- **Where:** National. REA registration is handled by the regional labour authority where the company has its registered office.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Before each subcontract, the contractor must:
  - check the sub's REA registration, its permanent-staff quota and its level in the chain;
  - record it in the on-site Libro de Subcontratación;
  - get approval for, and notify, any exceptional extra level.

  If any of this goes wrong, the contractor becomes jointly liable for the sub's wage and Social Security debts.
- **Evidence:**
  - Ley 32/2006 sets these rules — [BOE consolidated text, last updated 30 Dec 2021](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2006-18205):
    - Art. 4: subcontractors need their own organisation, PRL-trained staff and REA registration. Regular subcontractors need a minimum share of permanent staff: 10% in the first 18 months, 20% in months 19–36 and 30% from month 37.
    - Art. 5.2.d: "El tercer subcontratista no podrá subcontratar" ("the third-level subcontractor may not subcontract"). Autónomos cannot subcontract either.
    - Art. 8: the Libro must stay on site, listing every subcontract in date order.
    - Art. 7.2: breaches trigger "responsabilidad solidaria" (joint liability) for labour and Social Security obligations.
  - REA registration lasts three years and must be renewed in the six months before it expires — [Invoice Data Extraction blog](https://invoicedataextraction.com/blog/spain-rea-subcontratista-construccion) (commercial, undated; single source).
  - The Libro follows the model in Annex III of RD 1109/2007 and is validated (*habilitado*) by the labour authority — [BOJA 2007](https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/boja/2007/249/d2.pdf).
- **Scale:** Every construction site that uses subcontractors. No counts found.
- **Cost/impact:**
  - Joint liability for subcontractors' debts.
  - Fines from the Labour Inspectorate (ITSS) under LISOS, whose amounts I did not verify.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 32/2006 and RD 1109/2007 are in force. The consolidated text was last changed by RDL 32/2021, effective 31 Dec 2021 ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2006-18205)). I found no changes in 2024–26.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - CAE platforms (see CON-02).
  - The Libro itself.
  - Gestorías and prevention consultants.
  - No prices verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. A digital Libro, automated REA and quota checks, and chain-level alerts are all possible, though they depend on access to regional REA data.
- **Confidence:** High on the obligations. Low on enforcement intensity and cost.

### CON-04: Construction deaths rising while other sectors fall; safety paperwork and inspection exposure
- **Who:** Construction firms of every size. Inference: exposure is concentrated on micro and small sites.
- **Where:** National. Regional alarms have been raised in Granada (Andalucía) and Canarias.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Firms manage safety plans, coordinator instructions, training records and Labour Inspection visits. A serious accident brings an inspection and possible criminal or civil liability.
- **Evidence:**
  - Provisional 2025 figures: 584 deaths during working hours nationally (−9.6%). Construction was the exception: "con 29 muertos más, aumentó un 21,5%" ("with 29 more deaths, it rose 21.5%") — [INSST, 11 Feb 2026](https://www.insst.es/noticias-insst/cae-la-siniestralidad-laboral-y-se-reducen-un-9-6-por-ciento-los-accidentes-mortales).
  - El Español adds — [8 Jul 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/ciencia/20260708/oficial-muertes-accidentes-construccion-elevado-gobierno/1003744313511_0.html):
    - Construction's fatal-incidence index was 11.3 per 100,000 workers (+18%).
    - Total work deaths including commuting were 735, down from 796.
    - Accidents with sick leave totalled 620,386 (−1.3%).
    - Unions are calling for updated prevention rules and a stronger Labour Inspectorate.
    - Conflict: El Español writes "29 workers died", which contradicts INSST's "+29". INSST is the primary source.
  - Up to July 2025, construction had the most deaths of any sector: 103 including commuting, 21 more than a year earlier — [RTVC, 8 Oct 2025](https://rtvc.es/la-construccion-el-sector-que-concentra-el-mayor-numero-de-muertes-8-de-octubre-2025/) (snippet-level).
  - The Junta de Andalucía and builders met to discuss rising accidents in Granada as activity picks up — [Última Hora, 16 Oct 2025](https://www.ultimahora.es/noticias/comunidades/2025/10/16/2492169/junta-constructores-analizan-soluciones-para-reducir-siniestralidad-granada-ante-despegue-actividad.html).
- **Scale:** 1.53 million workers. Inference: an increase of 29 at +21.5% implies roughly 135 construction deaths during working hours in 2024 and about 164 in 2025.
- **Cost/impact:** Human cost and legal liability. I found no euro figures, for example for penalty surcharges on benefits (*recargo de prestaciones*) or fines.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 31/1995 PRL and RD 1627/1997 (construction sites) are in force. Unions want reform, but I found no draft.
- **How it is solved today:** External prevention services, health-and-safety coordinators and CAE platforms. No prices found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Digital site inspections, permits to work and training-record tracking cut paperwork, but controlling physical risk needs people on site.
- **Confidence:** High.

### CON-05: Late payment down the subcontracting and installer chain
- **Who:** Subcontractors, electrical, telecom and HVAC installers, and suppliers, mainly micro and small firms.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Large contractors pay beyond the legal limits, often through confirming (a bank-intermediated supplier payment scheme), and small firms end up financing the chain. Few claim the late-payment interest they are owed.
- **Evidence:**
  - PMcM "Estudio Plazos de Pago en España 2025" — [Forbes, 16 Apr 2026](https://forbes.es/economia/907227/el-85-de-las-grandes-empresas-incumple-los-plazos-legales-de-pago-segun-pmcm/):
    - Private sector paid in 67 days on average (legal maximum 60); the public sector in 70 (legal maximum 30). Both rose by 3 days.
    - 85% of large companies exceed the legal terms.
    - 89% of suppliers do not claim late-payment interest, and 94% do not claim the legal compensation.
  - Subcontracting averaged 87 days — [esDiario, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.esdiario.com/economia/260416/185240/85-grandes-empresas-incumple-plazos-legales-pago.html) (snippet-level).
  - FENIE, the electrical and telecom installers' federation — [Interempresas, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.interempresas.net/Electricidad/634041-Fenie-destaca-la-reduccion-de-la-morosidad-en-el-sector-instalador.html):
    - The installer default ratio fell to 3.5%, about half the previous year's.
    - Public administrations pay installers in 45 days (20 fewer).
    - Builders' and large firms' terms remain "alejados de los límites legales" ("far from the legal limits"), especially with confirming.
    - 90% want a sanctions regime.
  - Older or undated context:
    - The PMcM accused Ibex construction groups of average payment periods of 536 days (Ferrovial), 425 (Acciona) and 337 (Sacyr) — [Vozpópuli, date not shown](https://www.vozpopuli.com/economia/empresas/constructoras-pagos-morosidad-constructoras-pagos-morosidad_0_852814755.html).
    - Banco de España data put construction at 120 days and real estate at 140 — [idealista PMcM tag page, undated](https://www.idealista.com/news/etiquetas/pmcm).
- **Scale:** Not quantified.
- **Cost/impact:** Working-capital strain. No euro figure found.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 3/2004 on late payment is in force. A sanctions regime is being demanded but is not in force. Overlap: the cash-flow researcher covers this in depth.
- **How it is solved today:** Confirming, factoring, credit insurance and manual chasing. No prices verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Tracking progress certificates (*certificaciones*) and invoices, plus automated interest claims, would help, but the power imbalance remains.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.

### CON-06: Municipal works-licence bottlenecks
- **Who:** Developers, builders, renovation contractors and architects; homeowners commissioning work.
- **Where:** City level. The best data covers Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia and Málaga (a 2020 study). Valencia developers have complained.
- **Day-to-day reality:**
  - Major-works (*obra mayor*) licences take about a year instead of the legal three months.
  - First-occupation licences add months more.
  - Renovation firms must work out, municipality by municipality, whether they need a licence, a sworn statement (*declaración responsable*) or a prior notice (*comunicación previa*).
- **Evidence:**
  - ASPRIMA–EY — [EY press release, May 2020](https://www.ey.com/es_es/newsroom/2020/05/el-retraso-en-la-concesion-de-licencias-de-obra-nueva-tiene-un-sobrecoste-medio-para-el-comprador-de-casi-13-000-euros-por-vivienda). This predates 2024; I found no 2024–26 update.
    - New-build licences take about 12 months on average, against a guideline of 3 months for major works and 1 month for minor works.
    - First occupation takes about 3 months.
    - Delay and cost per home, by city:

      | City | Delay | Cost per home | Share of price |
      |---|---|---|---|
      | Madrid | 12 months | €28,606 | 6.56% |
      | Barcelona | 12 months | €26,145 | 5.76% |
      | Valencia | 12 months | €9,679 | 4.37% |
      | Málaga | 8 months | €7,272 | 3.11% |
  - The Ministry of Housing published a "Guía de Recomendaciones y Buenas Prácticas en materia de Licencias Urbanísticas en el Ámbito Residencial". It contains 20 recommendations, promotes digitising files and analysed 30 municipalities — [idealista/Europa Press, 29 Dec 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/12/29/878158-vivienda-publica-una-guia-para-ayudar-a-las-administraciones-a-agilizar-la).
  - A former Madrid councillor launched a consultancy claiming it can cut licence times by 40% — [idealista tag page, 25 Jan 2024](https://www.idealista.com/news/etiquetas/licencias-de-obra).
  - Valencia: "mantiene el embudo en las licencias de obra pese a los cambios en Urbanismo" ("the bottleneck in works licences persists despite changes in the planning department") — [Valencia Plaza, date not verified](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/embudo-pese-a-los-cambios-en-urbanismo).
- **Scale:** Not quantified.
- **Cost/impact:** €7,000 to €29,000 per new home (2020 figures).
- **Driver & status:** Municipal capacity and procedures. The state guide (Dec 2025) is not binding. I did not verify the regional declaración-responsable laws.
- **How it is solved today:** Planning consultancies and architects' permit services. No prices verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Applicant-side preparation and tracking tools help, but the bottleneck is municipal staffing.
- **Confidence:** Medium on the problem. Low on current figures.

### CON-07: NextGenerationEU rehabilitation grants: years of processing and pre-financing risk
- **Who:** Renovation contractors acting as rehabilitation agents or managers (*agentes/gestores de la rehabilitación*); owners' communities; homeowners; *administradores de fincas*.
- **Where:** Regional, since each autonomous community runs its own calls. Documented cases are in Aragón, Alicante (Comunitat Valenciana) and Euskadi.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Applicants wait years for approval and then for payment. When owners assign the grant to the contractor, the contractor carries the cash gap and the risk that the project collapses.
- **Evidence:**
  - Justicia de Aragón (regional ombudsman), case Q26/763/09, 15 Jun 2026 — [Justicia de Aragón](https://eljusticiadearagon.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Q25-753-08.pdf):
    - A grant under the PRTR (Spain's NextGenerationEU recovery plan), Programme 4, Orden VMV/1018/2022, took 30 months to be approved (29 May 2025).
    - It then went about 10 more months unpaid after the corrected documents were filed (11 Jun 2025): "superior a los 3 años desde la solicitud" ("more than 3 years since the application").
    - The regional government cited an "elevado volumen de solicitudes" ("high volume of applications") and the "complejidad de los procedimientos de gestión y fiscalización" ("complexity of the management and audit procedures"). It pays in batches (*por lotes*).
  - In Euskadi, beneficiaries can assign collection of the grant to the rehabilitation agent or manager, who must put the full amount into the works — [Gobierno Vasco](https://www.euskadi.eus/web01-a2next/es/contenidos/informacion/fnext_personas_benefici/es_def/index.shtml) (undated).
  - In Urbanova (Alicante), a €12m project that depended on about €9m of NextGen funds was abandoned. The contractor, appointed as rehabilitation agent, is claiming about €2m in lost profit — [Alicante Plaza, date not verified](https://alicanteplaza.es/laempresadelarehabilitaciondeurbanovaexigiraellucrocesantetrasabortarseelproyecto).
  - Administradores de fincas say the state housing plan's goals do not match what communities can manage, given bureaucracy and constant rule changes. Handling "ayudas, certificaciones y requisitos administrativos" ("grants, certifications and administrative requirements") is getting harder — [idealista/Conversia, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/06/17/901380-administradores-de-fincas-el-cambio-estructural-que-esta-redefiniendo-la-gestion-de).
- **Scale:** Not quantified. I found no national figures on applications or backlogs.
- **Cost/impact:** Cash gaps lasting several years, and one claim of about €2m.
- **Driver & status:** The PRTR residential-rehabilitation programmes funded by NextGenerationEU. I did not verify the final 2026 deadlines.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - Rehabilitation agents.
  - Rehabilitation offices at the colegios de arquitectos and aparejadores, which Aragón points applicants to ([Justicia de Aragón](https://eljusticiadearagon.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Q25-753-08.pdf)).
  - Tinsa's Agentia-R platform exists ([Tinsa](https://www.tinsa.es/sala-de-prensa/agentia-r/lanzamiento-agentiarplus/), not opened; price unknown).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. File and evidence management, payment tracking and cash-flow forecasting would help, but software cannot speed up the administration.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The evidence is one official case plus press reports.

### CON-08: Solar self-consumption installers: demand slump plus tariff and permitting friction
- **Who:** Solar PV installers and electricians, mostly micro and small.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Residential and small-business jobs are down from the 2022 peak, industrial sales cycles are longer, and the fixed part of electricity tariffs erodes customers' savings.
- **Evidence:**
  - UNEF figures for 2024 — [Energías Renovables, 23 Jan 2025](https://www.energias-renovables.com/autoconsumo/ya-hay-mas-de-8-000-megavatios-20250123); [Smartgridsinfo, 27 Jan 2025](https://smartgridsinfo.es/2025/01/27/autoconsumo-fotovoltaico-supera-umbral-8-gw-instalados-espana-segun-datos-unef):
    - 1,182 MW of self-consumption installed, −31% against 2023 and −53% against 2022.
    - Cumulative capacity of 8,137 MW.
    - Industrial installations of 674 MW (−34%).
  - UNEF figures for Q1 2025 — [Energías Renovables, 13 Jun 2025](https://www.energias-renovables.com/autoconsumo/el-mercado-del-autoconsumo-solar-cae-un-20250613):
    - The installation pace fell 17% against the 2024 quarterly average: residential −14%, commercial −17%, industrial −20%.
    - Reaching the PNIEC target of 19,000 MW by 2030 needs at least 1,810 MW a year.
    - UNEF blames the 75% fixed / 25% variable split of network tolls.
    - It asks for simplified processing up to 500 kW, a 5 km maximum distance, and a permit exemption under 15 kW (PV plus batteries). A new self-consumption decree was under review by MITECO (the energy ministry).
- **Scale:** Not quantified. I found no census of installers.
- **Cost/impact:** I found no data on closures or jobs.
- **Driver & status:** Electricity prices returning to normal and higher financing costs (UNEF). RD 244/2019 is in force. The new self-consumption decree was under review in June 2025; I did not verify its status as of October 2026.
- **How it is solved today:** Moving into batteries and industrial work. I found no prices for design or proposal software.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Faster proposals and permit filing would help, but the demand drop is macroeconomic.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The 2024–Q1 2025 data is solid; the later status is unknown.

### CON-09: Quoting, measurements and change orders run on heavy or costly tools
- **Who:** Micro and small builders, renovation contractors, trades and technical studios.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Budgets (measurements, BC3 price databases), progress certificates and change orders are prepared by technical staff in Presto or Arquímedes. Inference: micro firms use spreadsheets instead. The leading tools are seen as complex.
- **Evidence:**
  - Prices claimed by a competitor (OneEstimate, not verified) — [OneEstimate, 2 May 2026](https://oneestimate.ai/es/blog/mejores-software-presupuestos-construccion):
    - Presto: about €100/month, or a perpetual licence from about €2,000. Drawbacks listed: "steep learning curve", "dated interface", "costly for freelancers".
    - Arquímedes (CYPE): about €70/month; "requires advanced technical skills".
    - Procore: "typically $10,000+ USD/year".
  - A Presto 21 training course costs €471 (COIIM) — [Aprendemas](https://www.aprendemas.com/es/curso/curso-de-presto-21-1188673/) (undated).
  - Presto is the public-sector default: the Junta de Andalucía and the city of Zaragoza put out tenders for Presto licences and updates — [Junta de Andalucía](https://www.juntadeandalucia.es/haciendayadministracionpublica/apl/pdc_sirec_documentacion/rest/descargar/documento/337743); [Zaragoza](https://www.zaragoza.es/sede/servicio/contratacion-publica/anuncio/19595/document.pdf).
- **Scale:** Not quantified.
- **Cost/impact:** €70–€100 per month per seat, plus training. I found no data on hours lost or on disputes over change orders or unpaid private clients.
- **Driver & status:** A market force. E-invoicing and Verifactu add invoicing requirements, which the tax researcher covers.
- **How it is solved today:** Presto, Arquímedes, Excel and general invoicing apps. I could not verify field-service app prices: the vendor pages returned 404 or 403.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes.
- **Confidence:** Low. Prices come from a competitor and I found no user survey on the pain.

### CON-10: Energy performance certificate (CEE) rule changes ripple through every transaction
- **Who:** Technicians and certifiers (architects, engineers and now other qualified professionals), real estate agencies, landlords and sellers, and installers doing energy upgrades.
- **Where:** National, with regional registries.
- **Day-to-day reality:**
  - Agencies must display the CEE (*certificado de eficiencia energética*) when they rent or sell.
  - Since August 2025, a valid registered CEE is needed before a mortgage valuation.
  - The rules on who can certify are changing.
- **Evidence:**
  - RD 659/2025 of 22 Jul 2025 amends RD 390/2021 — [idealista, 23 Jul 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/construccion/2025/07/23/854394-ampliado-el-abanico-de-profesionales-que-pueden-emitir-certificados-energeticos):
    - More professionals can certify existing buildings after a two-module training course.
    - Accreditation is by sworn statement (*declaración responsable*) and is valid nationwide.
    - It creates a Registro Administrativo Centralizado de Técnicos Competentes (central register of qualified technicians), with 8 months to build the central system and 12 months for regions.
    - Real estate companies must display the CEE of properties they rent or sell.
  - Orden ECM/599/2025 of 10 Jun 2025 requires a valid registered CEE for mortgage valuations from 12 Aug 2025, and registry or cadastral certificates no more than three months old. A CEE has been required to sell or rent since 1 Jun 2013 — [idealista, 14 Aug 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/08/14/856865-un-notario-avisa-en-vigor-la-obligacion-de-contar-con-el-certificado-energetico-si).
  - Only about 10% of purchase demand targets A-rated homes. E-rated homes account for 39.7% and G-rated for 15.4% — [idealista, 26 Jun 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/06/26/850719-solo-el-10-de-la-demanda-en-compraventa-se-dirige-a-viviendas-con-etiqueta-a).
- **Scale:** Every sale and rental. No counts found.
- **Cost/impact:** I could not find CEE prices. Fines of up to €6,000 for certifying without a site visit come from a 2021 article and are outdated — [idealista CEE tag page](https://www.idealista.com/news/etiquetas/certificado-energetico).
- **Driver & status:** RD 390/2021, RD 659/2025 and Orden ECM/599/2025 are all in force. I could not verify whether Spain has transposed the recast EPBD, Directive (EU) 2024/1275.
- **How it is solved today:** Independent and online certifiers. No prices verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Ordering CEEs and tracking their expiry for agencies and landlords is software; the certificate itself needs a technician's visit.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### Inferences
- Several pains form one compliance chain: CAE platforms (CON-02), REA and the Libro (CON-03) and safety records (CON-04). Main contractors push costs onto subcontractors because the law makes them jointly liable. A neutral, subcontractor-owned compliance wallet that syncs to client platforms could attack the biggest grievance, paying once per platform.
- The fee anger in CON-02 is aimed at network-effect incumbents. Flat-fee challengers paid by the principal already exist (DocAcces, UCAE, PlayCAE), so switching costs and contractor lock-in are the main barriers, not technology.
- Grant processing (CON-07) and licences (CON-06) are bottlenecked on the administration's side. SaaS can only help applicants prepare and track their files, so the value lies in avoiding rejections and corrections (*subsanaciones*), not in speed.
- The labour shortage (CON-01) and the accident rise (CON-04) probably feed each other: more activity, less experienced and more foreign workers. That is inference, not demonstrated in the sources.

## 2. Real estate agencies

### Takeaway
Agencies face three pressures:
- **A rule change on fees.** Ley 12/2023 makes the landlord pay all rental management fees ("no podrán ser repercutidos al arrendatario, ni directa ni indirectamente": they may not be passed on to the tenant, directly or indirectly). The rule is now being enforced: the consumer ministry proposed a €3.6m fine on Alquiler Seguro in October 2025 for disguised tenant charges.
- **A collapsing long-term rental inventory.** Idealista's permanent-rental listings fell 44% from 2018 to 2025 and 10% year on year in Q3 2026, while seasonal rentals now make up 34% of the market. Lead volume is concentrated on very few homes: 135 contacts per listing in 10 days, 462 in Barcelona.
- **A regulatory patchwork.** Agent registries differ by region (mandatory in Catalonia, Valencia and Canarias; Madrid announced one in June 2026). Anti-money-laundering (AML) duties tighten under the EU AMLR from July 2027. Agencies must also display energy certificates.

I could not verify portal fee levels or CRM prices; both are quote-based and opaque.

### Cited Findings
- Agencies take part in about 70% of home purchase and rental transactions in Spain, according to FADEI, cited in [idealista, 29 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/node/904122).
- The structured entries below carry the detailed evidence.

### CON-11: Ley 12/2023 shifted rental agency fees to landlords; disguised tenant fees now face fines
- **Who:** Real estate agencies (micro and small), rental-management firms and rent-guarantee companies; landlords, who now bear the fee.
- **Where:** National. Enforcement comes from the Ministerio de Consumo, and in Madrid complaints were passed to the Comunidad de Madrid.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Agencies can no longer charge tenants a fee. Some rebranded the charge as a "solvency study", a "tenant service" or an "additional guarantee". Consumer authorities and tenant groups now pursue them.
- **Evidence:**
  - LAU art. 20.2, as currently consolidated: real estate management costs "no podrán ser repercutidos al arrendatario, ni directa ni indirectamente" ("may not be passed on to the tenant, directly or indirectly"). The housing law of May 2023 made the landlord pay these costs. Art. 20 was amended by Ley 12/2023 (DF 1.4, in force 26 May 2023) and rewritten again by RDL 29/2026 (art. 3.12, in force 8 Oct 2026) — [BOE consolidated LAU](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1994-26003); [Maldita, 20 Jun 2023](https://maldita.es/malditateexplica/20230620/inmobiliaria-vivienda-honorarios-trucos/).
  - Consumo proposed a €3,600,902 fine on Alquiler Seguro. The case was opened in March 2025 after a FACUA complaint (Nov 2023), and the fine is not final — [Noticias de Navarra, 27 Oct 2025](https://www.noticiasdenavarra.com/economia/2025/10/27/consumo-propone-multar-alquiler-seguro-10269149.amp.html).
  - El Salto's account of the same case — [El Salto, 30 Oct 2025](https://www.elsaltodiario.com/vivienda/multa-3-6-millones-alquiler-seguro-sacude-impunidad-inmobiliarias-saltarse-ley):
    - Six "very serious" infractions and one "serious". The fine breaks down as €1m for the "Servicio de Atención al Inquilino" clause, €1m for home insurance that was effectively compulsory, and €1.5m for three more infractions.
    - Disguised fee names included "garantía adicional" (additional guarantee), "estudio de solvencia" (solvency study) and "asesoramiento jurídico" (legal advice). One 82-year-old tenant paid a €1,400 "garantía adicional".
    - Tecnocasa and RedPiso were also accused, with no fine reported.
    - About ten Madrid intermediaries reported by the Sindicato de Inquilinos were referred to the Comunidad de Madrid.
  - Fines range from €10,001 to €100,000 and can reach four to six times the unlawful profit. The Madrid agents' federation AMADEI argues these charges are legal as "atypical contracts" — [idealista, undated](https://www.idealista.com/news/taxonomy/term/27931).
  - Earlier tactics included renaming fees as "estudio de viabilidad o solvencia económica" (viability or solvency study) — [Maldita, 20 Jun 2023](https://maldita.es/malditateexplica/20230620/inmobiliaria-vivienda-honorarios-trucos/).
- **Scale:** Agencies are involved in about 70% of transactions (FADEI, via [idealista, 29 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/node/904122)). I found no count of agencies.
- **Cost/impact:** Lost tenant-side income. Inference: the market norm for a rental was one month's rent, the minimum the MLS cartel enforced ([eldiario, 9 Dec 2021](https://www.eldiario.es/economia/cnmc-multa-idealista-remax-look-find-fijar-precios-operaciones-inmobiliarias_1_8562945.html)). Fines of up to €3.6m have been proposed.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 12/2023 is in force. RDL 29/2026 amended art. 20 again; it has been in force since 8 Oct 2026 and awaits a convalidation vote.
- **How it is solved today:** Landlord-paid fee models, rent-guarantee products and legal advice. No prices verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Compliant contract and fee templates and landlord-side billing are software, but the underlying problem is how fees are regulated.
- **Confidence:** High.

### CON-12: Long-term rental inventory is shrinking and shifting to seasonal leases
- **Who:** Agencies with rental portfolios, rental managers and landlords.
- **Where:** National. The fall is steepest in tense zones: San Sebastián, A Coruña, Bilbao, Pamplona, Vitoria, Tarragona, Lleida and Barcelona. Other cities: Alicante, Valencia, Málaga, Madrid, Palma. Las Palmas is up slightly.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Fewer long-term listings to manage. More seasonal and room contracts, which carry higher legal risk (see CON-19).
- **Evidence:**
  - Idealista's permanent-rental listings fell from 902,664 in 2018 to 510,091 in 2025 (about −44%). This counts adverts, not physical homes — [Artículo14, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.articulo14.es/economia/la-oferta-de-alquiler-se-desploma-un-44-desde-2018-casi-393-000-anuncios-menos-en-siete-anos-20261007.html) (snippet-level).
  - Observatorio del Alquiler (Fundación Alquiler Seguro), long-term stock at end-2025 — [Forbes, 28 Jan 2026](https://forbes.es/economia/864744/la-oferta-de-alquiler-pierde-33-400-viviendas-al-cierre-de-2025-y-se-ralentiza-el-ritmo-de-encarecimiento/):
    - Stock fell 4.7% (−33,418) to 683,920.
    - Barcelona accounts for 34.7% of the fall (−11,594). Sevilla fell 13%.
    - Madrid (+0.21%) and Málaga (+0.10%) were flat. Las Palmas fell because the tense-zone debate "ha retraído a los arrendadores" ("has made landlords hold back").
  - Idealista, Q3 2026 — [idealista, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/20/919563-la-oferta-de-alquiler-permanente-cae-un-10-en-un-ano-y-la-de-temporada-ya-supone-un):
    - Permanent supply −10% year on year; seasonal supply +7%; seasonal leases are 34% of the market.
    - Permanent supply by city: San Sebastián −52%, A Coruña −41%, Bilbao −41%, Pamplona −33%, Vitoria −32%, Tarragona −31%, Lleida −29%, Barcelona −22%, Alicante −22%, Valencia −12%, Málaga −11%, Madrid −6%, Palma −2%, Las Palmas +2%.
    - Seasonal share by city: San Sebastián 62%, Bilbao 59%, Barcelona 57%, Madrid 35%, Valencia 35%, Sevilla 33%, Alicante 31%, Palma 25%, Zaragoza 12%, Valladolid 11%.
    - Idealista spokesman Francisco Iñareta: "la intervención en el mercado sigue generando escasez allí donde se aplica" ("market intervention keeps creating scarcity wherever it is applied").
  - The ministry estimates 1.15 million people live in seasonal or room rentals, of whom about 422,000 would prefer a regular lease — [idealista, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/finanzas/economia/2026/09/07/912998-consumo-estima-que-mas-de-un-millon-de-personas-vive-en-alquiler-de-temporada-o-de) (snippet-level).
  - Tourist and seasonal surpluses account for only about 46% of the rental stock that has disappeared (idealista analysis with AirDNA and ministry data). This comes from a search snippet summarising idealista-based coverage; the exact article was not confirmed. Likely in [Artículo14, 7 Oct 2026](https://www.articulo14.es/economia/la-oferta-de-alquiler-se-desploma-un-44-desde-2018-casi-393-000-anuncios-menos-en-siete-anos-20261007.html) or [The Objective, 28 Jan 2026](https://theobjective.com/economia/2026-01-28/oferta-alquiler-larga-duracion-pierde-inmuebles-ano/). Low confidence.
- **Scale:** 683,920 long-term homes on the market at end-2025 (Observatorio). 1.15 million people in seasonal or room rentals.
- **Cost/impact:** A smaller rental-management revenue base for agencies. I found no euro figure.
- **Driver & status:** Rent controls in tense zones (CON-17), the seasonal-lease rules (CON-19) and the October 2026 decrees (CON-18). Idealista warns that Q3 data does not yet reflect the decrees' effect.
- **How it is solved today:** Agencies shift towards sales, seasonal or coliving products, and rental management charged at 6–10% of rent (per inmho; [idealista, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/empresas/2026/07/30/906830-50-empresas-compradas-al-ano-y-menos-del-3-de-cuota-asi-es-inmho-en-el-sector)).
- **Software-solvable?:** No for the supply shortage itself. Partly for helping agencies retain landlords with compliance and management tools.
- **Confidence:** High.

### CON-13: Lead overload and tenant screening in a supply-starved rental market
- **Who:** Agencies and individual landlords who advertise rentals.
- **Where:** National, with peaks in Barcelona, Girona, Baleares, Vizcaya, Las Palmas, Zaragoza, Tarragona, Valencia and Madrid.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Each rental listing draws more than a hundred enquiries within days. Agencies have to triage leads, check solvency and document their selection without charging the tenant for the work.
- **Evidence:**
  - The average home drew 135 interested people in 10 days in 2025, up from 124. By province: Barcelona 462 (a record), Girona 140, Baleares 137, Vizcaya 134, Las Palmas 121, Zaragoza 109, Tarragona 109, Valencia 104, Madrid 103 — [Forbes/Observatorio del Alquiler, 28 Jan 2026](https://forbes.es/economia/864744/la-oferta-de-alquiler-pierde-33-400-viviendas-al-cierre-de-2025-y-se-ralentiza-el-ritmo-de-encarecimiento/).
  - Tenants spend almost 40% of their net income on rent — same source.
  - Charging tenants for a "solvency study" is one of the disguised fees targeted in the Alquiler Seguro case — [El Salto, 30 Oct 2025](https://www.elsaltodiario.com/vivienda/multa-3-6-millones-alquiler-seguro-sacude-impunidad-inmobiliarias-saltarse-ley).
  - The industry warns that excessive guarantees demanded from tenants mean "solo acceden prácticamente los que no necesitan acceder" ("practically only those who don't need access get in") — [idealista/inmho, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/empresas/2026/07/30/906830-50-empresas-compradas-al-ano-y-menos-del-3-de-cuota-asi-es-inmho-en-el-sector).
- **Scale:** All rental listings. I found no count of agencies.
- **Cost/impact:** Agent hours per listing, not quantified. Risk of discrimination or data-protection problems in screening (inference).
- **Driver & status:** A market force plus Ley 12/2023's fee rule (in force). RDL 29/2026 caps guarantees (see CON-18).
- **How it is solved today:** Portal lead tools and CRMs (prices unverified, see CON-16) and manual screening.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Lead triage, standardised solvency verification paid by the landlord, and an audit trail of how tenants were selected.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high on lead volume. Low on how much time it costs.

### CON-14: Anti-money-laundering duties for agencies, tightening under the EU AMLR from July 2027
- **Who:** Real estate agencies (micro and small) and developers.
- **Where:** National, then EU-wide from July 2027.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Agencies that broker sales are obliged entities under Ley 10/2010. They must identify clients and beneficial owners, assess risk and report suspicious operations, and many small agencies are unaware they are covered.
- **Evidence:**
  - Ley 10/2010 art. 2.1.l covers "Los promotores inmobiliarios y quienes ejerzan profesionalmente actividades de agencia, comisión o intermediación en la compraventa de bienes inmuebles" ("property developers and anyone professionally brokering the sale of real estate") — [SEPBLAC list of obliged entities, Oct 2024](https://www.sepblac.es/wp-content/uploads/2024/10/sujetos_obligados_ley_10_2010.pdf).
  - The EU AML Regulation (EU) 2024/1624 "se aplicará desde julio de 2027" ("will apply from July 2027"). It brings stricter due diligence on all parties, beneficial-owner identification, an evidence-based risk self-assessment, sanctions and adverse-media screening, and a separate compliance officer — [finReg360 / El Confidencial, 23 Dec 2025](https://finreg360.com/el-negocio-inmobiliario-ante-las-novedades-en-la-prevencion-del-blanqueo-de-capitales/).
  - SEPBLAC has flagged deficiencies in the sector, such as missing whistleblowing channels — [idealista, undated](https://www.idealista.com/news/taxonomy/term/40917) (snippet-level).
  - A legal commentary argues many agencies are "el perímetro que no se sabe sujeto obligado" ("the perimeter that doesn't know it is an obliged entity") — [Economist & Jurist, undated](https://www.economistjurist.es/articulos-juridicos-destacados/inmobiliarias-joyerias-y-galerias-de-arte-el-perimetro-que-no-se-sabe-sujeto-obligado/).
- **Scale:** Every agency that brokers sales. No count found.
- **Cost/impact:** I found no sanction figures for agencies or compliance-cost data.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 10/2010 is in force. The EU AMLR applies from July 2027.
- **How it is solved today:** AML training courses and consultants. No prices verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Know-your-customer and beneficial-owner workflows, risk scoring, sanctions screening and record-keeping are standard regulatory-technology (regtech) features.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The obligations are verified; the pain and enforcement intensity are weakly evidenced.

### CON-15: Patchwork of regional agent registries and licensing requirements
- **Who:** Agencies and agents, especially those working across regions.
- **Where:**
  - Catalonia: mandatory registration (AICAT).
  - Comunitat Valenciana: mandatory registry.
  - Canarias: registry since 2022.
  - Madrid: voluntary, with a mandatory registry announced in June 2026.
  - Other regions not verified.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Training, insurance, guarantee and office requirements differ by region, so firms expanding across regions repeat the onboarding.
- **Evidence:**
  - Madrid's RAIN registry is voluntary and requires 200 hours of real estate training or 4 years' experience. On 29 Jun 2026 the Madrid regional government announced it will make registration mandatory. AMADEI and FADEI welcomed this, saying the differing rules create "ciudadanos de primera y de segunda" ("first- and second-class citizens") — [idealista, 29 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/node/904122).
  - Comunitat Valenciana: a mandatory registry with location, training and solvency requirements. The competition authority (CNMC) challenged it in court as disproportionate — [Valencia Plaza](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/vivienda-regula-un-registro-obligatorio-para-agentes-inmobiliarios-en-la-comunitat); [Valencia Plaza, CNMC appeal](https://valenciaplaza.com/competencia-recurre-el-registro-de-agentes-inmobiliarios-de-la-generalitat) (dates not verified).
  - Canarias: registry created by Decreto 11/2022 of 20 Jan 2022 — [Iberley](https://www.iberley.es/legislacion/decreto-11-2022-20-enero-crea-regula-registro-agentes-inmobiliarios-canarias-27033254).
  - Catalonia: registration, professional qualification, liability insurance and a guarantee are required — [JustAnswer, weak source](https://www.justanswer.es/legal-es/sugj0-para-poder-vender-pisos-en-catalu-a-es-totalmente.html). The idealista article above implies Catalonia's registry is mandatory.
- **Scale:** Not quantified.
- **Cost/impact:** Training (200 hours in Madrid), insurance and guarantees. No euro figures.
- **Driver & status:**
  - Regional decrees in force in Catalonia, Valencia and Canarias.
  - Madrid's mandatory registry announced, no date.
  - The CNMC's challenge in Valencia: outcome not verified.
- **How it is solved today:** Association training courses (API, COAPI, AICAT) and consultants. Prices not verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Tracking compliance credentials (training hours, insurance) is software, but registration itself is a legal formality.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### CON-16: Dependence on portals and CRM/MLS tools: opaque prices and competition-law exposure
- **Who:** Agencies (micro and small) and franchise networks.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Agencies depend on paid portal packages and on CRMs for publishing listings and sharing them through MLS networks. Prices are quote-based. In 2021 the competition authority found that MLS rules built into the software had been used to fix commissions.
- **Evidence:**
  - CNMC fine of 9 Dec 2021 — [eldiario, 9 Dec 2021](https://www.eldiario.es/economia/cnmc-multa-idealista-remax-look-find-fijar-precios-operaciones-inmobiliarias_1_8562945.html):
    - Fines by company:

      | Company | Fine |
      |---|---|
      | Idealista (via Idealista Tools) | €730,000 |
      | Remax | €375,720 |
      | Inmovilla | €83,149 |
      | Look & Find | €31,486 |
      | Witei | €18,925 (45% leniency reduction) |
      | Anaconda | €1,749 |
    - The MLS rules imposed minimum commissions of 4% on sales and one month's rent on rentals, split 50/50 between agencies.
    - The software vendors built filters and controls that enforced the rules.
    - Discrepancy: eldiario says the total was "more than €1.5m", but the listed amounts add up to about €1.24m. Another outlet reported €1.25m — [Economist & Jurist](https://www.economistjurist.es/economia/competencia-multa-a-siete-inmobiliarias-por-pactar-comisiones/).
  - Inmovilla claims more than 3,000 agencies and 5 million active properties, with prices "bajo cotización" (on quote). Its rating is 4.2/5 from 12 reviews. Complaints mention a steep learning curve, advanced features costing extra and slowness at peak hours (Sep 2024). One 2022 review says the user paid €79 + VAT on a 6-month contract but could not upload properties without paying more — [ComparaSoftware, accessed Oct 2026](https://www.comparasoftware.es/inmovilla).
- **Scale:** More than 3,000 agencies on Inmovilla alone.
- **Cost/impact:** Portal fees were not verified (see Gaps).
- **Driver & status:** A market force. The CNMC 2021 decision was appealable to the Audiencia Nacional; I did not verify the outcome.
- **How it is solved today:** Idealista (with Idealista Tools), Fotocasa, Habitaclia, Inmovilla, Witei and Mobilia. No verified prices.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes for the CRM and listing-syndication layer. No for portal market power.
- **Confidence:** Low. The key evidence is from 2021 and current fee data is missing.

### Inferences
- Rental agencies are losing on both sides: they can no longer bill tenants (CON-11), and stock is shrinking (CON-12). This should push them towards landlord-paid services that save landlords risk and time: compliance under the 2026 decrees, screening, rent collection and dispute workflows.
- Lead overload combined with the ban on charging tenants for screening (CON-13) creates demand for landlord-paid or agency-paid screening tools that keep an auditable record of selection.
- The 2021 CNMC case shows that CRM or MLS software that enforces commission rules carries competition-law risk. Any SaaS idea in this area should avoid price-coordination features.

### Gaps
- **Portals:** no verified 2024–26 data on Idealista, Fotocasa or Habitaclia fee levels or increases, and no evidence of new CNMC action against portals in 2025–26.
- **CRMs:** prices for Witei (the page is rendered in JavaScript and came back empty), Mobilia and Inmovilla (quote-only) were not obtained.
- **Agencies:** no count of agencies in Spain, no SEPBLAC sanction statistics for agencies, and no verified AML rental-intermediation threshold. The Spanish SEPBLAC list refers only to sales.
- **Registries and associations:** regional registry rules for Andalucía, Baleares, País Vasco, Navarra and Galicia, the outcome of the CNMC's appeal in Valencia, and API/COAPI positions were not verified.

## 3. Small landlords

### Takeaway
Small landlords face the most severe and fastest-changing pains in this area:
- **Unpaid rent.** Defaulting tenants owed €8,489 on average in 2025, about seven months' rent; €11,620 in Catalonia.
- **Evictions.** 18,317 rental-related evictions (*lanzamientos*) were carried out in 2025.
- **A mandatory negotiation step.** Since 3 April 2025, landlords must attempt a prior negotiation (MASC: *medios adecuados de solución de controversias*, alternative dispute resolution) before filing an eviction claim.
- **An unstable eviction moratorium.** The suspension of evictions for vulnerable households lapsed at the end of 2025 after Congress twice repealed its extension. RDL 29/2026 reinstated a broader suspension until 2030 from 8 October 2026, pending a convalidation vote.
- **Rent caps.** Caps apply in declared tense zones in Catalonia, País Vasco, Navarra, Galicia and Asturias. A 2% default cap on annual updates runs until the end of 2027.
- **A seasonal-rental crackdown.** The national registration number was annulled by the Supreme Court in May 2026, but Catalonia has regulated seasonal leases since January 2026 and RDL 29/2026 now requires a proven reason and a 12-month maximum.

Squatting filings are falling, but recovering a property through the civil courts can still take 18–24 months.

### Cited Findings
- Lawmaking is unusually fast. RDL 26/2026 (29 Sep) and RDL 27/2026 were repealed by Congress on 2 Oct 2026 and re-approved as RDL 28/2026 and RDL 29/2026 on 6 Oct. RDL 29/2026 (BOE-A-2026-20823) has applied since 8 Oct 2026; RDL 28/2026 is due to take effect on 15 Nov 2026 — [BOE consolidated LAU notes](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1994-26003); [idealista, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/13/918359-que-cambia-para-los-propietarios-con-los-decretos-prorrogas-tope-al-alquiler-del-2).
- Idealista reports that the Cortes have been dissolved for general elections on 29 Nov 2026, and that RDL 29/2026 must be convalidated by the Diputación Permanente (the standing committee that sits while Parliament is dissolved) — [idealista, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/13/918359-que-cambia-para-los-propietarios-con-los-decretos-prorrogas-tope-al-alquiler-del-2). Not independently verified.

### CON-17: Rent caps in declared tense zones and limits on annual updates
- **Who:** Individual landlords (autónomo-scale), large holders (*grandes tenedores*: 5 or more homes in Catalonia's zones, more than 10 nationally), agencies and property managers.
- **Where:**
  - **Catalonia:** 271 municipalities, plus 53 more declared by Resolución TER/3740/2026 (DOGC 19 Oct 2026), pending publication in the BOE. Examples include Martorell, Sant Andreu de la Barca, Tordera and Vila-seca.
  - **País Vasco:** 17–19 municipalities, depending on the source, including Bilbao, Vitoria-Gasteiz, Donostia, Errenteria and Irun. Pasaia, Zestoa and Arrasate were added from 28 Apr 2026.
  - **Navarra:** 21 municipalities since July 2025, including Pamplona, Tudela and Zizur Mayor.
  - **Galicia:** A Coruña since July 2025. Santiago from 29 Jul 2026 is reported but uncertain.
  - **Asturias:** Gijón, Avilés, Llanes, Gozón and Cabrales, from 30 Jul 2026 to 30 Jul 2029, in some cases for specific neighbourhoods only.
  - Sources: the regional lists come from secondary blog sources ([Arrenta](https://www.arrenta.es/blog/mercado-legislacion/listado-de-municipios-en-zonas-tensionadas-en-espana/); [Inmostats](https://inmostats.com/alquiler/zonas-tensionadas); [Vivara](https://www.vivara.io/blog/legal/zona-tensionada-alquiler/)). The official ministry page refused automated access with a 403 error ([MIVAU SERPAVI](https://www.mivau.gob.es/vivienda/alquila-bien-es-tu-derecho/serpavi/consultar-zonas-de-mercado-residencial-tensionado)).
  - Idealista's Q3 2026 report classifies San Sebastián, A Coruña, Bilbao, Pamplona, Vitoria, Tarragona, Lleida, Barcelona and Girona as declared-tense markets. It treats Madrid, Valencia, Málaga, Sevilla, Alicante and Palma as not tense — [idealista, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/20/919563-la-oferta-de-alquiler-permanente-cae-un-10-en-un-ano-y-la-de-temporada-ya-supone-un).
- **Day-to-day reality:** For each new contract, the landlord must find the last rent paid in the past five years and update it, or look up the state reference index. They must disclose both to the tenant, and they can only raise the rent by up to 10% in specific cases. Annual updates can no longer exceed the IRAV, a new official index for rent updates, and are capped at 2% unless the parties agree otherwise.
- **Evidence:**
  - Catalonia rules — [OCU, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.ocu.org/fincas-y-casas/gestion/alquiler/analisis/2026/10/nuevas-zonas-tensionadas-cataluna):
    - Homes rented in the last five years: the new rent is capped at the previous rent plus the legal update.
    - Homes not rented in five years: capped at the reference index.
    - Large holders (5 or more homes) are subject to whichever limit is stricter.
    - Up to +10% is allowed for qualifying renovation, energy or accessibility works, or contracts of 10 years or more.
    - Landlords must disclose the previous rent, the index and the applicable limits.
    - Declarations last three years.
  - LAU arts. 17.6–17.9 set the caps in tense zones. They were amended by RDL 29/2026 (art. 3.10), following Ley 12/2023. Art. 18 says updates "no podrá exceder del resultado de aplicar la variación porcentual" ("may not exceed the result of applying the percentage change") of the IRAV — [BOE consolidated LAU](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1994-26003).
  - RDL 29/2026 adds — [idealista, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/13/918359-que-cambia-para-los-propietarios-con-los-decretos-prorrogas-tope-al-alquiler-del-2):
    - For updates due before 31 Dec 2027, the increase is capped at 2% unless agreed otherwise.
    - If the rent already exceeds the reference index, "no procederá la aplicación de incremento alguno" ("no increase at all may be applied").
  - In tense zones, permanent rental supply is falling fastest. In the year to Q3 2026: San Sebastián −52%, A Coruña −41%, Bilbao −41%, Pamplona −33%, Barcelona −22% — [idealista, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/20/919563-la-oferta-de-alquiler-permanente-cae-un-10-en-un-ano-y-la-de-temporada-ya-supone-un).
- **Scale:** Catalonia alone has 271 municipalities, with 53 more pending. I found no count of landlords.
- **Cost/impact:** Rent foregone. Catalan sanctions for exceeding the caps were not verified.
- **Driver & status:**
  - Ley 12/2023 and the regional declarations: in force.
  - RDL 29/2026's 2% cap: in force since 8 Oct 2026, pending convalidation. The Basque nationalist party (PNV) has announced support, and the article says the decree has "más visos" ("better odds") of passing.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - Agencies and gestorías work out caps by hand.
  - SERPAVI, the state reference-price index.
  - Lawyers.
  - No prices.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. An address-level cap calculator, history of the last contract, IRAV and 2% update engine, and disclosure templates. The rules change often, which makes software more valuable, not less.
- **Confidence:** High on the rules. Medium on municipality counts.

### CON-18: Rapid-fire decree changes (RDL 26–29/2026) force landlords to rewrite contracts and processes
- **Who:** All landlords, whether individuals or companies; agencies; property managers; rent-guarantee and insurance firms.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Within three weeks, two decrees were published, repealed by Congress and re-issued. Landlords must now handle a series of new rules:
  - Tenant-requested extensions.
  - Small repairs that are now the landlord's job.
  - No passing property tax (IBI) on to tenants.
  - A tenant right to leave after six months without penalty.
  - Limits on guarantees.
  - A possible automatic 5/7-year renewal with compensation for refusing it.
- **Evidence:**
  - RDL 29/2026 (in force 8 Oct 2026) — [idealista, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/13/918359-que-cambia-para-los-propietarios-con-los-decretos-prorrogas-tope-al-alquiler-del-2):
    - An extraordinary extension of up to two years, in annual steps, for primary-residence leases ending before 31 Dec 2028, if the tenant is up to date with payments, including the previous eight months.
    - Deposit (*fianza*) of one month plus at most two months of additional guarantees. Rent-default insurance can no longer be demanded as a guarantee.
    - Small repairs from ordinary use move to the landlord.
    - IBI cannot be passed on to the tenant.
    - The tenant can leave after six months without compensation.
    - Luxury homes (over 300 m², or rent above 5.5 times the minimum wage, SMI) come under the general regime.
    - Purchases by large holders and entities at under 70% of market value are restricted until 31 Dec 2030.
    - Optional municipal property-tax surcharges on empty homes (up to 50%, and 100% or 150% in some cases).
  - The BOE consolidated text confirms the RDL 29/2026 changes: art. 36.5 (additional guarantee "no podrá exceder de dos mensualidades", may not exceed two months' rent) and a new art. 36.7 — [BOE consolidated LAU](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1994-26003).
  - RDL 28/2026 (due 15 Nov 2026) would add automatic renewal at the tenant's option: 5 years for individual landlords and 7 for companies. A landlord who refuses must pay up to 12 months' rent, or one month per year of contract if that is higher. One party needed for the vote has said it will vote against, and the interviewee in the article expects it to be "un brindis al sol" ("an empty gesture") — [idealista, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/13/918359-que-cambia-para-los-propietarios-con-los-decretos-prorrogas-tope-al-alquiler-del-2).
- **Scale:** Every residential lease. I found no landlord count.
- **Cost/impact:**
  - Re-papering contracts.
  - Lost flexibility.
  - Up to 12 months' rent in compensation if RDL 28/2026 enters into force.
  - Rent-default insurance can no longer be used as a guarantee.
- **Driver & status:**
  - RDL 29/2026: in force, pending convalidation.
  - RDL 28/2026: scheduled for 15 Nov 2026, likely to fail.
  - RDL 26/2026 and 27/2026: repealed on 2 Oct 2026.
  - General elections on 29 Nov 2026, as reported by idealista.
- **How it is solved today:** Lawyers, agencies and owners' associations. No prices found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. A contract-clause engine, alerts for extension requests and deadlines, and versioned templates tied to the law in force on each date.
- **Confidence:** High on content, from the BOE and idealista. Status is volatile.

### CON-19: Seasonal-rental crackdown and registration whiplash
- **Who:** Small landlords letting seasonally or by the room; seasonal and coliving operators; agencies. Tourist rentals are covered by the hospitality researcher.
- **Where:** National, plus Catalonia (regional law in force since 2 Jan 2026) and Navarra (bill registered May 2026).
- **Day-to-day reality:**
  - From July 2025, owners needed a registration number for seasonal and tourist rentals.
  - In May 2026 the Supreme Court annulled it, and owners are now chasing refunds.
  - Since 8 Oct 2026, a seasonal lease must state and prove its reason and last no more than 12 months. Otherwise it counts as a primary-residence lease, with long minimum terms and rent caps.
- **Evidence:**
  - The registration system (Registro Único, RD 1312/2024) applied from 1 Jul 2025. By February 2026, about 420,000 registration numbers had been issued and more than 80,000 refused, some of them social-housing (VPO) homes — [idealista, 22 Feb 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/02/22/884998-registradores-detecta-vpo-entre-los-pisos-turisticos-que-han-solicitado-el-codigo).
  - Supreme Court judgment 620/2026 (reported 21 May 2026) annulled the registry and the registration number as an invasion of regional powers. The appeal was brought by the Generalitat Valenciana. The court kept the Ventanilla Única Digital (single digital data window) and the duty for platforms to send data — [idealista, 21 May 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/05/21/898563-el-supremo-tumba-el-registro-unico-de-alquiler-de-corta-duracion-por-invadir).
  - After the ruling, more than 100,000 tourist licences were reactivated. The tourist-flat association Fevitur estimates compensation claims above €160m, and owners are seeking refunds of the fees they paid — [idealista tag page (22 May and 5 Jun 2026 items)](https://www.idealista.com/news/etiquetas/alquiler-de-temporada).
  - Catalonia's regulation of seasonal and room rentals took effect on 2 Jan 2026. It caps rents for all residential leases regardless of length and requires seasonal contracts to state and prove their purpose. Experts say it has already cut supply. Seasonal supply fell 63% in Girona, 58% in Tarragona and 37% in Barcelona in the year to Q3 2026 — [idealista tag page](https://www.idealista.com/news/etiquetas/alquiler-de-temporada); [idealista, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/20/919563-la-oferta-de-alquiler-permanente-cae-un-10-en-un-ano-y-la-de-temporada-ya-supone-un).
  - RDL 29/2026 rules for seasonal and room leases — [idealista, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/13/918359-que-cambia-para-los-propietarios-con-los-decretos-prorrogas-tope-al-alquiler-del-2):
    - Seasonal leases become housing leases under Title II of the LAU.
    - They need a proven reason for being temporary, last more than 31 days and generally no more than 12.
    - More than 12 months without justification, or more than two consecutive contracts with the same parties, turns the lease into a primary-residence lease from signature.
    - For room rentals, the rents for all rooms together cannot exceed the rent for the whole home. In tense zones and for large holders, the reference index applies.
  - A Coruña appeal court overturned an eviction because the purpose of the lease, not its length, defines a seasonal rental — [idealista tag page, 6 Jul 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/etiquetas/alquiler-de-temporada).
- **Scale:** About 420,000 registration numbers issued (tourist and seasonal combined). 1.15 million people live in seasonal or room rentals ([idealista, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/finanzas/economia/2026/09/07/912998-consumo-estima-que-mas-de-un-millon-de-personas-vive-en-alquiler-de-temporada-o-de)).
- **Cost/impact:** Registration fees, amount not verified. Risk of a lease being reclassified as a 5/7-year primary-residence lease under capped rent.
- **Driver & status:**
  - RD 1312/2024 registry: annulled by the Supreme Court in May 2026.
  - EU Regulation 2024/1028 on platform data sharing: still relevant.
  - Catalan law: in force since 2 Jan 2026; its number was not verified.
  - RDL 29/2026: in force, pending convalidation.
  - Navarra bill: in parliament.
- **How it is solved today:** Lawyers, gestorías and specialised seasonal agencies. No prices.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Recording the reason for the lease, tracking duration and consecutive contracts, and checking caps for room rentals.
- **Confidence:** High.

### CON-20: Unpaid rent, slow evictions, the new negotiation step and the on-off eviction moratorium
- **Who:** Small individual landlords, agencies managing rentals, rent-guarantee firms.
- **Where:** National. By region:
  - Catalonia: 6,814 evictions (about 26.6% of the national total).
  - Andalucía: 3,782.
  - Comunitat Valenciana: 2,992.
  - Madrid: 2,267 (−17.7%).
  - País Vasco: 974 (+60.2%).
- **Day-to-day reality:** After about seven months of unpaid rent, the landlord must first attempt a documented negotiation (MASC), then sue. They may then face a vulnerability-based suspension. During all of this they keep paying the mortgage, IBI and community fees.
- **Evidence:**
  - CGPJ (judiciary council) data for 2025 — [CGPJ, 20 Mar 2026](https://www.poderjudicial.es/cgpj/es/Poder-Judicial/Consejo-General-del-Poder-Judicial/Oficina-de-Comunicacion/Notas-de-prensa/Las-ejecuciones-hipotecarias-y-los-concursos-registrados-en-los-organos-judiciales-crecieron-en-2025-un-30---respecto-al-ano-anterior); [Forbes](https://forbes.es/economia/893652/los-desahucios-caen-un-11-en-2025-hasta-los-25-540-segun-cgpj/):
    - 25,540 evictions carried out (−11%). Of these, 18,317 (71.7%) arose from rental-law (LAU) cases (−10.9%) and 4,346 (17%) from mortgage foreclosures.
    - Data note: the "other causes" figure as extracted (1,877) makes the parts add up to 24,540. The percentages fit 25,540, which suggests "other" is really 2,877.
    - Q4 2025: courts received 2,732 requests to suspend an eviction for vulnerability. They granted 472 suspensions: 221 under art. 1 of RDL 11/2020, 84 under art. 1 bis, and 167 under art. 441.5 of the civil procedure law (LEC).
  - País Vasco: 974 evictions (+60.2%), with rental-arrears cases up 43.8% — [CGPJ TSJ País Vasco](https://www.poderjudicial.es/cgpj/es/Poder-Judicial/Tribunales-Superiores-de-Justicia/TSJ-Pais-Vasco/En-Portada/Los-lanzamientos-practicados-en-el-Pais-Vasco-en-2025-crecieron-un-60-2--respecto-al-ano-anterior).
  - Observatorio del Alquiler (Fundación Alquiler Seguro), tenant arrears in 2025 — [idealista, 5 Mar 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/03/05/887280-los-inquilinos-morosos-ya-deben-siete-meses-de-renta-una-media-de-8-490-euros-en):
    - Average debt per defaulting tenant: €8,489.3, about seven months' rent.
    - By region: Catalonia €11,619.9; Madrid €10,420.4; Baleares €10,354.4; Barcelona province €14,036.5. In Murcia, defaulters owe more than nine months' rent.
    - Inconsistency: idealista reports +16.5% year on year. Against the 2024 average of €7,957.6 ([idealista, 21 Feb 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/02/21/832887-por-que-cataluna-es-la-region-de-espana-con-mas-inquilinos-morosos)), the rise is about 6.7%.
  - Prior negotiation (MASC): Ley Orgánica 1/2025 took effect on 3 Apr 2025. Art. 5.2 makes prior negotiation a precondition for filing, exempting only the matters it lists — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2025-76):
    - The exemptions include "la tutela sumaria de la tenencia o de la posesión" (summary protection of possession), the remedy used against squatters, and the juicio cambiario (bill-of-exchange claims).
    - Eviction for non-payment is not exempt.
    - The lawyer José Ramón Zurdo (ANA) says "los procesos se han complicado todavía más" ("the processes have become even more complicated") — [idealista, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/04/15/886094-ley-antiokupas-ha-mejorado-algo-todo-lo-que-tienes-que-saber).
  - Moratorium status — [BOE consolidated RDL 11/2020](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2020-4208):
    - Art. 1 of RDL 11/2020 ran "hasta el 31 de diciembre de 2025" ("until 31 December 2025").
    - The extension in RDL 16/2025 (23 Dec 2025) was repealed by a Congress resolution of 27 Jan 2026.
    - A second attempt, RDL 2/2026 (3 Feb 2026), was repealed on 26 Feb 2026.
  - RDL 29/2026 art. 2 creates a new suspension regime until 31 Dec 2030 — [idealista, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/13/918359-que-cambia-para-los-propietarios-con-los-decretos-prorrogas-tope-al-alquiler-del-2):
    - Economically vulnerable tenants evicted for non-payment: the administration must offer an alternative within two months or pay the debt, otherwise it becomes the debtor.
    - Other claimants: suspensions of up to three years, reviewed every 12 months. Where the landlord is an individual, the administration covers unpaid rent, utilities and court costs, but not lost profit.
    - Funds that bought homes below market value: suspension without compensation.
- **Scale:** 18,317 rental-arrears evictions in 2025. I found no count of defaulted contracts.
- **Cost/impact:**
  - About €8,500 average debt, plus legal costs.
  - Lawyers describe 18–24 months or more for the slow route, cited for squatters and holdover tenants (*inquiokupas*) — [idealista, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/04/15/886094-ley-antiokupas-ha-mejorado-algo-todo-lo-que-tienes-que-saber).
  - I found no CGPJ average duration for rental-arrears evictions.
- **Driver & status:**
  - LO 1/2025 MASC: in force.
  - RDL 11/2020 suspension: lapsed after 31 Dec 2025, apart from transitional cases.
  - RDL 29/2026 suspension to 2030: in force since 8 Oct 2026, pending convalidation.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - Rent-guarantee firms such as Alquiler Seguro.
  - Rent-default insurance, prices not verified, which can no longer be required as a tenant guarantee under RDL 29/2026.
  - Lawyers, tenant-arrears registers and agencies.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Screening, early-arrears alerts, generating and logging the MASC offer (for example a *burofax*, a certified letter), and case files ready for court. Court speed is outside software's reach.
- **Confidence:** High.

### CON-21: Squatting (okupación) risk and slow recovery
- **Who:** Small landlords with empty or second homes; sellers; agencies, since squatted homes are hard to sell.
- **Where:** National. Most civil filings come from Andalucía, ahead of Catalonia, and Barcelona is the most affected province. Squatted listings are highest in Girona (8.9% of homes for sale), then Barcelona (3.7%) and Madrid (2.4%).
- **Day-to-day reality:** Fast removal is possible only for a home that is someone's dwelling (*allanamiento de morada*) and only with quick action. Otherwise recovery runs through civil or criminal courts for 18–24 months or more.
- **Evidence:**
  - Civil squatting filings (CGPJ data):
    - 1,401 in the first nine months of 2025 (−21%), which coincided with the new prior-negotiation rule — [idealista, 16 Dec 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/12/16/876563-los-juzgados-reciben-mas-de-1-400-demandas-por-okupacion-de-viviendas-este-ano).
    - 487 in Q2 2025 (−24%), with Andalucía overtaking Catalonia — [idealista, 17 Oct 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/10/17/867671-andalucia-adelanta-a-cataluna-como-la-region-con-mas-demandas-por-okupacion-de).
    - 460 in Q1 2026 (−22.3%), the lowest first quarter in the series. Experts note criminal cases are excluded and negotiations delay filings — [idealista, 15 Jul 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/07/15/906009-las-demandas-por-okupacion-de-viviendas-se-desploman-pero-el-problema-persiste-los).
  - 23,010 squatted homes were listed for sale in Q3 2025, about 3% of homes for sale — [idealista, 21 Oct 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/10/21/868295-el-numero-de-viviendas-okupadas-a-la-venta-supera-las-23-000-hasta-septiembre-donde).

- **Scale:** Inference: about 1,800–1,900 civil filings a year, based on 1,401 in nine months, plus criminal cases. 23,010 squatted listings.
- **Cost/impact:** 18–24 or more months of lost use and legal costs. Squatted homes sell at a discount (inference).
- **Driver & status:**

  - The "ley antiokupas" (2025): I did not verify its exact legal number.
  - Pending express-eviction bills: inference, they likely lapsed with the dissolution of the Cortes.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - Cheap insurance (€39.85 a year).
  - Lawyers.
  - Private eviction-mediation firms (*desokupa*), not verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Mostly no. Vacancy monitoring and legal-workflow tools help at the edges; the bottleneck is the courts.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.

### Inferences
- For a small landlord, the regulatory load in 2025–26 is no longer one law but a moving target: caps, the 2% limit, extensions, seasonal-lease rules, the negotiation step and the moratorium. Four decree-laws in about three weeks of autumn 2026, with elections pending, make an up-to-date compliance engine a plausible product. Volatility is also a risk for anyone building one.

- Squatting is highly visible in the media, but filings are falling and €40-a-year insurance exists, so it is weaker as a SaaS opportunity.

## 4. Property managers (administradores de fincas) and owners' communities (comunidades de propietarios)

### Takeaway
About 1 million owners' communities are run by roughly 16,000 administradores de fincas, most of whom handle 50–60 communities each. The profession is fragmented and ageing, and consolidation is under way: inmho, the largest operator, manages 15,000 communities and bought 50 firms last year. The main pains are:
- New legal workflows: a three-fifths vote on tourist flats since 3 April 2025, and the prior negotiation step that seems to apply before suing defaulters.
- Recovering arrears.
- A heavy load of documentation and compliance (coordination with suppliers, data protection, accessibility, energy-renovation grants) scattered across email, paper and "múltiples plataformas independientes" (many separate platforms).
- No specific legal cover for online meetings. A PP bill was admitted in May 2026, but by inference it lapsed when Parliament was dissolved in October 2026.

### Cited Findings
- Spain has about 1 million owners' communities and about 16,000 administradores de fincas, who typically manage 50–60 communities each — [idealista, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/empresas/2026/07/30/906830-50-empresas-compradas-al-ano-y-menos-del-3-de-cuota-asi-es-inmho-en-el-sector)
- The structured entries below carry the detailed evidence.

### CON-22: Tourist-flat approvals under the new three-fifths rule (Ley de Propiedad Horizontal)
- **Who:** Owners' communities and administradores de fincas (micro firms); small landlords who want to let for tourist use.
- **Where:** National law (the LPH). Barcelona funds communities that vote to ban tourist flats. Tourist hotspots are presumably most affected (inference; not verified).
- **Day-to-day reality:** Any owner who wants to start tourist letting now needs the community's express prior approval. Communities call meetings to approve, condition or ban it by a three-fifths majority, and they may set special fees.
- **Evidence:**
  - The Ley de Propiedad Horizontal (LPH), as amended by Ley Orgánica 1/2025 (final provisions 1 and 4.2), effective 3 Apr 2025 — [BOE consolidated LPH](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1960-10906):
    - Art. 7.3: the owner "deberá obtener previamente la aprobación expresa de la comunidad de propietarios" ("must first obtain the express approval of the owners' community").
    - Art. 17.12: such resolutions need "el voto favorable de las tres quintas partes del total de los propietarios" ("the favourable vote of three fifths of all owners"), who must also hold three fifths of the ownership shares.
    - The same majority can set special fees or raise the owner's share of costs by up to 20%.
    - "Estos acuerdos no tendrán efectos retroactivos" ("these resolutions shall not have retroactive effect").
  - Barcelona raised its budget for grants to communities that ban tourist flats from €56,000 to €256,000, up to €2,500 per community, available until 31 December — [idealista, 18 Aug 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/08/18/910490-barcelona-quintuplica-las-ayudas-a-las-comunidades-de-propietarios-para-prohibir).
  - More than 80,000 national registration numbers were refused (tourist and seasonal combined; reasons not broken down) — [idealista, 22 Feb 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/02/22/884998-registradores-detecta-vpo-entre-los-pisos-turisticos-que-han-solicitado-el-codigo).
- **Scale:** Every community with a tourist flat or an application for one. Not counted.
- **Cost/impact:** Meetings, legal advice and disputes. Barcelona pays up to €2,500 per community to cover the cost of such votes.
- **Driver & status:** LO 1/2025, in force since 3 Apr 2025.
- **How it is solved today:** Administradores and lawyers. Barcelona's grants pay part of the cost.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Calculating majorities by owners and shares, logging votes, generating minutes and certificates. Disputes stay legal.
- **Confidence:** High on the rule. Medium on scale. Overlap: the hospitality researcher covers tourist flats in depth.

### CON-23: Community arrears and the harder recovery process (special payment claim plus negotiation step)
- **Who:** Communities and administradores; also owners who don't pay.
- **Where:** National. The example ruling comes from Cantabria.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Administradores chase unpaid fees and one-off levies (*derramas*), issue debt certificates and file a special fast-track payment claim (*monitorio*). Since April 2025 they probably must also try a documented negotiation first.
- **Evidence:**
  - LPH art. 21 gives communities a special *monitorio* claim for unpaid fees. It needs a debt certificate signed by the secretary with the president's approval. If the owner opposes the claim, the community can obtain a preventive attachment of assets without posting a bond. The reserve fund must be at least 10% of the last ordinary budget (art. 9.1.f, Ley 10/2022) — [BOE consolidated LPH](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1960-10906).
  - LO 1/2025 art. 5.2 lists the claims exempt from prior negotiation. The domestic *monitorio* is not among them; only the European payment order is exempt, in art. 5.3 — [BOE LO 1/2025](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2025-76). Interpretation: community debt claims appear to need a negotiation attempt first. I did not verify how courts apply this.
  - The Audiencia Provincial de Cantabria upheld a 20% surcharge on a defaulting owner: €2,775 of debt plus €555 surcharge, with interest and costs — [idealista, 2 Sep 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/09/02/911748-un-tribunal-avala-cobrar-un-recargo-del-20-a-un-vecino-moroso).
  - Unpaid *derramas* are a recurring theme in consumer coverage — [idealista, 21 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/06/21/901209-que-pasa-si-un-vecino-no-paga-una-derrama-lo-que-puedes-hacer).
- **Scale:** I found no national total for community arrears.
- **Cost/impact:** Not quantified nationally.
- **Driver & status:** LPH art. 21 and LO 1/2025, both in force.
- **How it is solved today:** Administradores, lawyers and community management software (prices not verified).
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Arrears dashboards, automated reminders, negotiation-offer generation and logging, and debt certificates.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### CON-24: Administradores' documentary and compliance burden in a fragmented, ageing profession
- **Who:** Administradores de fincas, typically micro offices with 50–60 communities each, and the consolidators buying them.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Each building creates obligations: coordination with suppliers (CAE), data protection, accessibility works, energy-renovation grants, technical certifications, maintenance deadlines, and the relationship with each supplier. All of it is tracked by hand across email, paper and separate platforms, and appointments are renewed annually by vote rather than by contract.
- **Evidence:**
  - Scale and consolidation — [idealista, 30 Jul 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/empresas/2026/07/30/906830-50-empresas-compradas-al-ano-y-menos-del-3-de-cuota-asi-es-inmho-en-el-sector):
    - About 1 million communities and about 16,000 administradores, most managing 50–60 communities.
    - inmho manages 15,000 communities, under 3% of the market, and the second operator about half that. It bought 50 firms last year.
    - Firms with more than 500–600 communities used to be "muy raro" ("very rare").
    - Many administradores have managed the same buildings for decades "sin un plan de sucesión claro" ("without a clear succession plan").
    - An administrador handles in cash terms about 20 times what they bill.
    - Rental management commissions run 6–10% of rent.
  - Burdens named by Conversia, a compliance services company, through its administradores-de-fincas director — [idealista, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/06/17/901380-administradores-de-fincas-el-cambio-estructural-que-esta-redefiniendo-la-gestion-de):
    - "Coordinación de actividades empresariales" (CAE with suppliers), "protección de datos", accessibility work, energy-renovation grants, certifications, "seguimiento de vencimientos" (deadline tracking), "control de cumplimiento" (compliance control) and supplier coordination.
    - Information is scattered across "correos electrónicos, documentación física, múltiples plataformas independientes" ("emails, paper documents, many separate platforms").
    - Any incident can become "jurídico o reputacional" (a legal or reputational matter).
- **Scale:** About 16,000 administradores and 1 million communities.
- **Cost/impact:** Not quantified: no hours or euro figures found.
- **Driver & status:** A build-up of obligations across the LPH, PRL/CAE, data protection, accessibility rules and grant rules, all in force. Consolidation is a market force.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - Sector software: Gesfincas, Fincas Pro and Comunidad Feliz were named in the brief, but I could not verify their prices or complaints.
  - Compliance consultancies such as Conversia (prices not found).
  - Consolidators.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. A single compliance and maintenance calendar per building, onboarding suppliers' CAE documents, and portals for owners. Consolidators need standard tooling.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Scale is solid; the burden is described qualitatively by a vendor.

### CON-25: Meetings, minutes and notices: no specific legal cover for online meetings
- **Who:** Administradores and owners' communities. Owners who live elsewhere, have reduced mobility or have been affected by emergencies such as the DANA floods are most affected.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Meetings are still held mainly in person, and online attendance has no explicit legal basis. Governance disputes reach the courts: for example, the Audiencia Provincial de Bizkaia annulled a resolution that left a community without a president.
- **Evidence:**
  - The LPH (arts. 16–17) does not expressly regulate online meetings. It refers only to communications by "cualquier medio que permita tener constancia de su recepción" ("any means that leaves proof of receipt") — [BOE consolidated LPH](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1960-10906).
  - Congress agreed on 26 May 2026 to consider a PP bill, already passed by the Senate. It would allow online or hybrid meetings, an electronic address for notices and a digital minutes book (*Libro de Actas Digital*). The administradores' general council (CGCAFE) says the practice "carece de una cobertura legal específica" ("lacks specific legal cover") and calls the change a "demanda histórica" ("long-standing demand") of the profession. The bill was at the amendment stage — [idealista, 27 May 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/finanzas/economia/2026/05/27/899214-el-congreso-tramita-una-ley-del-pp-para-permitir-juntas-de-vecinos-telematicas-con-el).
  - A community cannot be left without a president for lack of agreement (Audiencia Provincial de Bizkaia) — [idealista, 3 Aug 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/08/03/907818-una-comunidad-de-propietarios-no-puede-quedarse-sin-presidente-por-falta-de-acuerdo).
- **Scale:** About 1 million communities hold at least an annual meeting. Inference: the LPH requires one per year, a requirement not re-checked here.
- **Cost/impact:** Not quantified.
- **Driver & status:** The bill was admitted in May 2026. Inference: it likely lapsed when the Cortes were dissolved for the 29 Nov 2026 elections ([idealista, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/13/918359-que-cambia-para-los-propietarios-con-los-decretos-prorrogas-tope-al-alquiler-del-2)).
- **How it is solved today:** Hybrid meetings held informally, paper minutes books, and software from administradores' vendors (prices not verified).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Meeting, voting and minutes tools exist, but legal certainty depends on the law changing.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high on the gap. The bill's status is inferred.

### CON-26: Coordinating works and maintenance (lifts, accessibility, energy renovation) in communities
- **Who:** Administradores, community presidents, and contractors and installers working for communities.
- **Where:** National. Grants are regional and municipal.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Communities have to approve, finance and supervise large jobs such as lifts or energy upgrades. That means running grant applications, coordinating suppliers' safety documentation, and handling neighbours who don't pay their share (see CON-23).
- **Evidence:**
  - Installing a lift costs €30,000–60,000 on average and takes 2–4 months, with grants of up to 75% in some cases. It needs a simple majority — [idealista, 14 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/14/913707-como-instalar-un-ascensor-en-tu-edificio-paso-a-paso).
  - Administradores report that energy-renovation and accessibility work is getting harder to manage because of bureaucracy and changing rules — [idealista/Conversia, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/06/17/901380-administradores-de-fincas-el-cambio-estructural-que-esta-redefiniendo-la-gestion-de).
  - Grant payment delays affect communities and agents (see CON-07) — [Justicia de Aragón, 15 Jun 2026](https://eljusticiadearagon.es/wp-content/uploads/2026/07/Q25-753-08.pdf).
- **Scale:** Not quantified.
- **Cost/impact:** €30,000–60,000 per lift, plus weeks of coordination.
- **Driver & status:** Accessibility and energy policies and NextGenerationEU programmes, in force. I did not verify the new lift technical rule (RD 355/2024) or any mandatory inspection or adaptation deadlines.
- **How it is solved today:** Administradores, engineering firms, rehabilitation agents and the colegios' offices.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Comparing quotes, tracking works and grants, and collecting suppliers' CAE documents.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low.

### Inferences
- The administración de fincas market is huge (about 1 million communities), fragmented (about 16,000 firms) and consolidating. Consolidators buying about 50 firms a year need standard operating software, which is a distribution channel as well as a customer.
- Three 2025–26 legal changes add documented procedures: the three-fifths tourist vote, MASC before arrears claims, and CAE with suppliers. Each is a template-plus-audit-trail workflow that software can own.

### Gaps
- **Arrears:** I found no national figure for community arrears (CGCAFE data not found).

- **Rules and fees:**
  - Administradores' fees per community or per home were not found; only the 6–10% rental-management commission was.

## Top pains

Ranked by severity times breadth, with software-solvability as a tie-breaker.

1. **CON-20 Unpaid rent, slow evictions, the negotiation step and the moratorium (small landlords, national).** Defaulting tenants owe about €8,500 on average, and 18,317 rental-arrears evictions were carried out in 2025. A new pre-filing step exists, and the suspension regime has been switched off and on again until 2030. This is the costliest and most widespread landlord pain, and parts of it (screening, arrears alerts, the negotiation file) are software-solvable.
2. **CON-17 and CON-18 Rent caps and the October 2026 decree changes (landlords, agencies, property managers; national plus tense zones in Catalonia, País Vasco, Navarra, Galicia and Asturias).** Every lease is affected by the 2% cap, extensions, the limits on guarantees and contract changes. Four decree-laws in three weeks create continuous demand for compliance tools. Their volatility is also a risk for anyone building them.
3. **CON-01 Skilled-labour shortage (construction, national).** The shortage is structural and affects all firms: the workforce averages 45.1 years old, only 10.8% are under 30, and the CNC says about 700,000 workers are missing. It drives delays and wage pressure, and is only partly software-solvable.
4. **CON-02 CAE document platforms (subcontractors and autónomos, national).** This is a recurring cost of €100–€2,000+ per year per platform, with documented anger (Trustpilot 1.3/5, an OCU complaint) and lock-in imposed by principals. It is clearly software-solvable, through reusable compliance wallets.
5. **CON-12 and CON-13 Collapsing long-term rental stock and lead overload (agencies, national, worst in tense cities).** Permanent supply fell 10% in a year, seasonal leases make up 34% of the market, and each listing draws 135 contacts (462 in Barcelona). The pressure is on agencies' business model and workload, and screening and lead tools are solvable.
6. **CON-11 Ban on tenant fees, now enforced (rental agencies, national).** A proposed €3.6m fine shows enforcement is real. Agencies' revenue now has to come from landlords, which favours landlord-paid service software.
7. **CON-24 Administradores' compliance burden in a fragmented, consolidating market (national).** About 1 million communities are managed by about 16,000 firms, with no single source of truth for obligations. Consolidators need standard tools.
8. **CON-05 Late payment through the subcontracting chain (construction and installers, national).** The average subcontracting term is 87 days, and 85% of large firms pay late. The pain is widespread but only partly solvable, and it overlaps with the finance researcher.

Honourable mentions: CON-04 (construction deaths +21.5%; severe but mostly not a software problem) and CON-19 (seasonal-rental crackdown; severe but concentrated in Catalonia and the tense cities).

## Noise and dead ends

- **National rental registration number (Registro Único, RD 1312/2024):** a real compliance pain from July 2025 to May 2026, but the Supreme Court annulled it ([idealista, 21 May 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/05/21/898563-el-supremo-tumba-el-registro-unico-de-alquiler-de-corta-duracion-por-invadir)). What remains are refund and compensation claims and platform data-sharing, not an ongoing workflow.
- **RDL 26/2026 and RDL 27/2026:** repealed within days ([BOE LAU notes](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1994-26003)).
- **RDL 28/2026 (automatic 5/7-year renewal with up to 12 months' compensation):** scheduled for 15 Nov 2026 but expected to fail ([idealista, 13 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/10/13/918359-que-cambia-para-los-propietarios-con-los-decretos-prorrogas-tope-al-alquiler-del-2)). Do not build products on it.
- **Eviction moratorium as a fixed assumption:** it lapsed in early 2026 after RDL 16/2025 and RDL 2/2026 were repealed, and returned on 8 Oct 2026 through RDL 29/2026, pending convalidation, with elections on 29 Nov ([BOE RDL 11/2020](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2020-4208)). Its status is too volatile to be a stable product premise. Build for change, not for a particular rule.
- **Bills on online meetings and express eviction of squatters:** both likely lapsed with the dissolution of Parliament (inference). Online-meeting tools already exist; the gap is legal, not technical.
- **Squatting as a SaaS opportunity:** civil filings are falling (−21% to −24% year on year), insurance already costs about €40 a year ([idealista, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/07/13/904588-seguro-antiokupas-cuanto-cuesta-que-cubre-y-cuanto-protege)), and the bottleneck is the courts. The problem's visibility exceeds the addressable software need.
- **Materials price volatility:** I found no 2024–26 evidence. The 2022 price spike (CNC, [Interempresas, 2022](https://www.interempresas.net/Construccion/386526-CNC-avisa-que-65-por-ciento-constructoras-ve-extremadamente-dificil-contratar-encargados.html)) is old. Weakly evidenced now.
- **HVAC F-gas phase-down:** the EU Regulation 2024/573 has applied since 11 Mar 2024, with HFCs phased out by 2050 ([European Commission](https://climate.ec.europa.eu/eu-action/fluorinated-greenhouse-gases/f-gas-legislation_en)). I found no Spain-specific evidence of installer pain (prices, certification backlog).
- **Solar demand slump:** real (−31% in 2024), but macroeconomic: electricity prices and interest rates. Not software-solvable beyond faster proposals and permits.
- **Works-licence delays:** real, but the bottleneck is municipal and the latest hard numbers are from 2020. Applicant-side software has limited leverage.
- **AML for agencies:** the obligations are clear, but I found little evidence of enforcement against small agencies, and the tougher EU regime starts only in July 2027. It is a future pain, not yet an acute one.
- **Portal fee increases and CRM pricing:** could not be verified (quote-based). The CNMC MLS cartel case is from 2021. Treat claims of portal-fee pain as unproven until sourced.
- **The CNC's "700,000 missing workers":** an advocacy estimate unchanged since 2021. Use the FLC demographic data instead.
- **Data errors to avoid repeating:**
  - El Español's "29 construction deaths in 2025" misreads INSST's "+29".
  - The CGPJ eviction total is internally inconsistent (25,540 vs 24,540); use 25,540.
  - Idealista's "+16.5%" growth in tenant debt conflicts with its own 2024 figure (about +6.7%).
  - The 2021 CNMC fine total is reported as both "more than €1.5m" and €1.25m; the listed amounts add up to about €1.24m.
