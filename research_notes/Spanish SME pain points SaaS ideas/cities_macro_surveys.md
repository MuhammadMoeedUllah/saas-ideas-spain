*Research date: 23 October 2026. Method note for the report writer: the shared web-search budget for this run was exhausted part-way through, and several regional newspaper domains (Levante-EMV, Las Provincias, Heraldo, Diario Sur, La Vanguardia, Europa Press) could not be fetched. For that reason, city-level hard data comes mainly from INE microtables pulled directly through INE's own data service (DIRCE, Coyuntura Demográfica de Empresas, Indicadores Urbanos). Survey data comes from primary PDFs and APIs (Banco de España, ECB SAFE API, INE, European Commission, CEPYME/CEOE), and city shocks come from official pages and from elDiario.es, Motor16, RACE and Última Hora. ## Q1. How do SME and autónomo conditions and pains differ across Spain's major cities?

### Takeaway
The business base is overwhelmingly micro everywhere: more than 51% of companies in every province studied have no employees. Retail and food-and-beverage are the top two employer sectors in every city. The real differences come from a few local factors:
- **Local shocks.** Valencia has the long tail of the October 2024 DANA plus a new flood ("dana Adam", 18–19 Oct 2026) in Safor and Marina Alta. The Balearic and Canary Islands face insularity and fuel and freight surcharges. Bizkaia has industrial distress.
- **Growth gaps.** Málaga and Alicante have the fastest-growing business bases. Bizkaia is the only province studied with a shrinking company count. Valladolid, Sevilla, Murcia, Las Palmas and Vigo showed negative employer-unit net growth in at least one quarter of 2026.
- **Extreme seasonality in the Balearics.**
- **Divergent municipal rules.** These are covered in Q2.

### Inferences
- Most of what differs between cities comes from **shocks and rules**, not from a different kind of company. The sector mix is remarkably similar: retail plus F&B make up 24–34% of employer units everywhere. Retail is 15–21% of autónomos. A city-agnostic micro-business tool can therefore serve every metro, while localisation needs to cover municipal rules (ZBE, fees) and region-specific shocks.
- The tourist-heavy provinces have the highest F&B shares (Málaga, Alicante, the Balearics, the Canaries, all 16–17%) together with low incomes and high unemployment. That points to thin margins and to staffing that depends on housing: tight labour markets inside tourist hotspots alongside high local unemployment. This is an inference that combines INE tables with the housing headlines.
- In the DANA zone, the mixed net-growth figures suggest recovery is **heterogeneous**: some heavily hit towns such as Paiporta are growing again while others are still shrinking. Small bases make the quarterly rates volatile, so this should not be read as a definitive measure of damage.
- Repeat floods within two years (October 2024, October 2026) make business-continuity and claims-documentation needs **recurring** for the Valencia region, not a one-off.

## Q2. Cross-city municipal costs and rules: ZBE, loading, IAE/IBI and the new waste fee

### Takeaway
- **Low-emission zones (ZBE).** Only 68 of the 149 municipalities obliged to have one were active in September 2026. The rules diverge widely:
  - Madrid fines non-resident unlabelled vehicles on a large scale.
  - Barcelona bans unlabelled vans on weekdays but rations 24 day-passes.
  - Valencia exempts economic-activity vehicles.
  - Málaga's label-B step on 30 Nov 2026 is in legal doubt.
  - Zaragoza will not be operational before 2030.
  - Santa Cruz starts an 18-month information-only phase.
- **The new waste fee required by Ley 7/2022** (art. 11.3, due by April 2025) has produced litigation and refunds: the TSJM annulled Madrid's 2025 fee, the TSJC annulled the Las Palmas ordinance with refunds from October 2026, and the Vélez-Málaga mayor resigned. Business-specific amounts were not found.
- **Loading and unloading rules, IAE and IBI:** not researched successfully (gaps).

### Inferences
- For a business operating vans across several municipalities, the pain is less any single ZBE than the **patchwork**: different calendars, domicile tests (one of which, Málaga's, was struck down), day-pass quotas, exemptions and stop-start suspensions. The pass-through to commercial premises is an inference; the evidence found covers residential tenants.

## Q3. What do national surveys say are SMEs' biggest obstacles, how has that shifted from 2023 to 2026, and how well do firms survive?

### Takeaway
- **2026 is dominated by an energy and input-cost shock** linked to the Iran war. In the Banco de España's EBAE survey, firms citing energy as a limiting factor rose from 42% in 2026Q1 to 63.2% in 2026Q3.
- **Policy uncertainty** peaked at 62% in Q2 and stood at 57.6% in Q3.
- **Labour shortage** is a persistent structural constraint at about 43–48%, and 58–60% in construction and hospitality.
- **Financing constraints are rising from a low base:** access to finance 18.2% and borrowing costs 26.8% in Q3 2026, and SMEs feel interest costs more.
- **ECB SAFE (Spain):** skilled-staff availability is the top "most pressing problem" (25.0% in 2025-H2). "Regulation" has risen steadily, from 10.9% in 2023-H1 to 14.9%, now above the euro area.
- **ATA, Q1 2026:** 81.1% of autónomos saw costs rise and 38.3% suffer late payment.
- **Survival:** young micro-employers are fragile. Only 83.4% of employer units with 1–5 employees and up to 5 years old survived two quarters. Under-30 autónomos fare worst (85.5%).

### Inferences
- **How the obstacles shifted from 2023 to 2026:**
  - 2023–2024: cost and labour concerns (SAFE "costs" about 21–22%).
  - 2025: costs eased and skills and regulation stayed high.
  - 2026: a renewed **energy-cost shock** plus **policy uncertainty**, with financing beginning to bite.
  - "Regulation" is the slowest-moving but steadiest riser, and is now above the euro-area average.
- The gap between SMEs and large firms widened at the end of 2025 (SMEs contracting investment and expecting job cuts) and is visible again in the effect of interest costs (Q3 2026). Micro employers and individual entrepreneurs are shrinking in number according to the EPA, even as employment grows.
- Most top-ranked obstacles are macro (energy, uncertainty, labour supply) and **not directly software-solvable**. Software can address the operational spillovers: repricing and pass-through, cost monitoring, late-payment collection, admin time, hiring and scheduling.

## Q4. Digitalisation: what does INE's latest ICT and e-commerce survey show by company size and region?

### Takeaway
The 2025–2026 edition, published 22 Oct 2026, shows a sharp rise in AI use: 33.6% of firms with 10+ employees and 21.4% of micro firms, against 21.1% and 13.4% a year earlier. It also shows a deep gap for micro firms:
- Only 36.2% of micro firms have a website and 11.8% sell online.
- Among 10+ firms, 82.9% have a website and 29.0% sell online.
- ERP use is 60.9% and CRM 33.9% among 10+ firms.

Madrid and Cataluña lead. Castilla-La Mancha, Murcia, the Canaries and the Balearics lag on CRM and ERP. The figures are not strictly comparable with earlier years because of the switch to CNAE-2025 and the inclusion of food-and-beverage services.

### Cited Findings
- **Firms with 10+ employees, Q1 2026:**
  - Connectivity and web: 98.3% have internet; of those, 82.9% have a website and 65.4% use social media.
  - Technology adoption: AI 33.6%; paid cloud 39.4%; in-house data analytics 34.3%.
  - Business software: ERP 60.9%; CRM 33.9%.
  - Staff: 15.4% employ ICT specialists; 37.7% allow telework (77.3% among firms with 250+ employees).
  - E-commerce sales in 2025: 29.0% of firms, for €419,706M. By sector: construction only 7.0% and services 35.1%.
  - Source: [INE ETICCE 2025–Q1 2026, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/ETICCE20251T2026.htm).
- **Firms with fewer than 10 employees, Q1 2026:** 81.2% have internet; of those, 36.2% have a website, 32.1% use social media, 21.4% use AI and 25.1% allow telework; 11.8% sold online in 2025 ([INE, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/ETICCE20251T2026.htm)).
- **Prior edition (Q1 2025, firms with 10+ employees):** AI 21.1%, paid cloud 44.3%, ERP 63.6%, CRM 34.5%, website 84.5%, social media 69.7%; e-commerce sales 26.6% of firms in 2024 for €399,368M. Micro firms: AI 13.4%, website 37.0%, social media 37.8%, e-commerce sales 8.6% ([INE, 22 Oct 2025](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/ETICCE20241T2025.htm)).
- **Methodology break:** from reference year 2025 the survey uses CNAE-2025 and includes division 56 (food and beverage services) and group 95.1, so "los datos de este año no sean estrictamente comparables" ("this year's data are not strictly comparable") ([INE, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/ETICCE20251T2026.htm)).
- **By region** (10+ firms, Q1 2026, % of firms with internet): web / telework / social media / AI / cloud / data analytics / ERP / CRM ([INE, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/es/ETICCE20251T2026.pdf)):

  - Madrid: 85.4 / 52.5 / 65.6 / 40.1 / 48.0 / 37.9 / 63.7 / **41.4**
  - Cataluña: 88.2 / 45.0 / 73.4 / **40.5** / **48.7** / 40.0 / 68.2 / 41.0
  - Comunitat Valenciana: 83.9 / 36.3 / 65.8 / 34.1 / 39.3 / 34.8 / 63.1 / 33.7
  - Andalucía: 77.8 / 29.6 / 63.6 / 31.8 / 32.6 / 31.2 / **53.5** / 29.5
  - Aragón: 79.9 / 32.2 / 62.1 / 29.3 / 32.5 / 32.4 / 58.7 / 27.2
  - País Vasco: 83.7 / 38.4 / 58.9 / 28.7 / 38.7 / 37.1 / 66.9 / 33.8
  - Illes Balears: 79.6 / 26.7 / 75.8 / 30.0 / 35.7 / 28.4 / **47.3** / 28.1
  - Canarias: 75.3 / 28.2 / 62.2 / 30.3 / 33.1 / 34.1 / **49.4** / 27.8
  - Región de Murcia: 75.6 / 28.5 / 58.8 / 24.6 / 33.8 / 31.6 / 56.5 / 27.4

  - INE: Madrid, Cataluña and Navarra have the highest ICT intensity; Castilla-La Mancha, Ceuta and Melilla the lowest.
- **GEM 2025–26:** e-commerce (46%), data analytics (40%) and cloud (40%) lead digital adoption among entrepreneurs; 29% of early-stage entrepreneurs integrate AI fully, against 19% of established businesses ([Emprendedores, 4 Jun 2026](https://emprendedores.es/actualidad/informe-gem-2025-2026/)).

### Inferences
- The apparent year-on-year falls among 10+ firms (cloud 44.3% to 39.4%, ERP 63.6% to 60.9%, social media 69.7% to 65.4%) are likely **artefacts of the scope change**: adding about 56 (bars and restaurants), which are less digitised. They should not be read as de-digitisation. The AI jump (21.1% to 33.6%) is large enough to be real despite the break.
- The island economies have high social-media use (the Balearics 75.8%) but low ERP use (47.3–49.4%). That points to front-office digital marketing without back-office systems, which fits seasonal, hospitality-heavy firms.
- About two-thirds of micro firms still have no website and almost nine in ten do not sell online. The headroom for simple, cheap tools is large, but these are also the firms with the least capacity to adopt.

### Gaps
- ERP, CRM and cloud breakdowns for 10–49, 50–249 and 250+ firms, and for micro firms, were not in the press release. They are in INEbase tables I did not download.
- Reasons for not using AI or cloud (barriers) were not retrieved.
- No city-level digitalisation data; INE publishes only by region.

### CITY-22: The micro-firm digital gap: 36% have a website, 12% sell online, 21% use AI
- Who: micro firms and autónomos with fewer than 10 employees, especially retail, hospitality and personal services.
- Where: national; the regional gap is in CITY-23.
- Day-to-day reality: most micro firms run without a website, online sales or back-office software, and depend on social media and walk-in trade.
- Evidence: website 36.2%, social media 32.1%, AI 21.4%, e-commerce sales 11.8% ([INE, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/ETICCE20251T2026.htm)). The prior year was 37.0%, 37.8%, 13.4% and 8.6% ([INE, 22 Oct 2025](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/ETICCE20241T2025.htm)). Two INE editions.
- Scale: about 3.15 million companies have fewer than 10 employees: 1,800,443 with none plus 1,348,909 with 1–9 ([INE 73022](https://www.ine.es/jaxiT3/Tabla.htm?t=73022)).
- Cost/impact: lost online sales not quantified.
- Driver & status: market force (capacity, cost, skills). Public programmes such as Kit Digital belong to other researchers' scope.
- How it is solved today: see the software-market researcher; no prices captured here.
- Software-solvable?: yes (simple web, booking, e-commerce and AI-assistant bundles), with adoption being the bottleneck.
- Confidence: high.

### CITY-23: Regional laggards on CRM and ERP (Castilla-La Mancha, Murcia, Canarias, Baleares, Andalucía)
- Who: firms with 10+ employees in lagging regions.
- Where: Castilla-La Mancha (CRM 25.0%), Murcia (CRM 27.4%, AI 24.6%), Canarias (ERP 49.4%), Baleares (ERP 47.3%), Andalucía (ERP 53.5%), against Madrid (CRM 41.4%) and Cataluña (ERP 68.2%).
- Day-to-day reality: back-office processes such as stock, invoicing and customer follow-up stay manual or in spreadsheets more often outside Madrid and Cataluña.
- Evidence: regional table above ([INE, 22 Oct 2026](https://www.ine.es/dyngs/Prensa/es/ETICCE20251T2026.pdf)). Single official source.
- Scale: not quantified per region in counts.
- Cost/impact: not quantified.
- Driver & status: market force.
- How it is solved today: see the software-market researcher.
- Software-solvable?: yes (sector-specific, low-cost ERP and CRM localised for island and seasonal businesses).
- Confidence: high for the gap; medium for its business impact.

## Top pains
1. **Energy and input-cost shock of 2026 (CITY-14, CITY-07).** The most widespread obstacle now: 63.2% of firms in EBAE Q3 2026, 81.1% of autónomos with cost rises, and freight surcharges of 20–25% on the islands.
2. **Labour and skills shortage, tied to housing in tourist cities and the islands (CITY-16, CITY-05).** Structural: 43% of all firms, about 60% in construction and hospitality, and the top SAFE problem for Spanish SMEs at 25%.
3. **Policy and regulatory uncertainty with a rising regulatory burden (CITY-15, CITY-19).** 57.6–62% of firms in 2026. SAFE "regulation" has climbed steadily to above the euro-area level, and the rules keep shifting before the 29 Nov 2026 elections.
4. **Patchwork ZBE rules for vans and trades (CITY-12, CITY-03, CITY-04).** A software-addressable cross-city pain with hard enforcement evidence (261,115 Madrid fines in six months) and constantly changing calendars and court rulings.
5. **Climate-disaster exposure in the Valencia region (CITY-01, CITY-02).** Two major floods in two years; 27,466 aid recipients and 251,940 insurance claims from 2024, and 6,528 new claims in three days in October 2026. Recovery is uneven.
6. **New, litigated municipal waste fee (CITY-13).** Every municipality is obliged by Ley 7/2022. Annulments in Madrid and Las Palmas create refund and appeal workloads, but business-specific amounts are unknown.
7. **Fragility of young micro-employers and the shrinking count of employers (CITY-21).** Two-quarter survival of 83.4% for young firms with 1–5 employees; employers −2.9% and individual entrepreneurs −4.0% y/y.
8. **The micro-firm digital gap (CITY-22).** Widespread (about 3.15 million firms) and clearly software-solvable, though it is an opportunity more than a felt "pain".

- **Santa Cruz de Tenerife's ZBE:** 18-month information-only phase from September 2026 ([elDiario.es, 24 Aug 2026](https://www.eldiario.es/canariasahora/tenerifeahora/politica/bop-regulacion-zona-bajas-emisiones-santa-cruz-tenerife_1_13464117.html)). No fines before about 2028.
