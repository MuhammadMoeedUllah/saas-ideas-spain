# Startups and tech-ecosystem pain points in Spain's innovation hubs (2024–2026)

Compiled 23 Oct 2026 for the data-capture stage of the "Spanish SME pain points → SaaS ideas" project. Scope: new startups and their founders in Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Málaga, Bilbao, Sevilla, Zaragoza and the Canary Islands. Other researchers cover tax/invoicing, labour/payroll, procurement and subsidies, cash flow, legal disputes and macro surveys, so overlaps are flagged briefly only.

**How this was researched (read before using):** the web-search budget shared by all agents ran out after my first ~16 searches. After that I fetched known URLs directly: BOE, ENISA and ONE pages, reports and press articles. I also pulled the text out of three PDFs: Dealroom/SpainCap 2025, ACCIÓ 2026 and GEM España 2025-26. I could not collect Reddit, X or LinkedIn founder posts or podcasts. Every fact below carries an inline source and date. "Inference" marks my own reasoning. "Single source" marks claims I could not corroborate.

---

## 1. Ley de Startups (Ley 28/2022): uptake, ENISA certification, tax benefits, stock options, digital-nomad permit and Beckham regime

### Takeaway
The law is still in force. The BOE consolidated text shows it unchanged since 22 Dec 2022.
- **Uptake is far below expectations.** About 2,527–2,529 companies had been certified by Oct 2026. ENISA's forecast for 2023 alone was 5,000–7,000.
- **About one certified firm in five has already lost the status.**
- **Founders' main complaints:**
  - the definition is narrow: a 5/7-year age cap, a €10M turnover cap and a rule that 60% of staff have Spanish contracts;
  - the tax perks only help companies that are already profitable;
  - day-one costs were left untouched: the autónomo quota, VAT from the first euro and quarterly filings.
- **Implementation was slow.** Certification only started in mid-2023. The Foro Nacional de Empresas Emergentes was due by mid-2023 but was only set up around mid-2025, and the main startup association was left out of its plenary.

### Cited Findings
**Uptake and certification**
- ENISA's certification page showed **2,527 "empresas certificadas"** (accessed 23 Oct 2026) — [ENISA](https://www.enisa.es/servicios/certificacion/).
- Status as of 11 Oct 2026 — [Economía 3, 15 Oct 2026](https://economia3.com/2026/10/15/762915-certificado-de-empresa-emergente-2-529-startups-lo-tienen-pero-no-garantiza-el-visado/):
  - **2,529** companies had been recognised as empresas emergentes;
  - **2,046** still held the status;
  - **483 had lost it**, "casi una de cada cinco" (almost one in five);
  - ENISA counted **1,929** in January 2026, so about 600 were added in eight months.
- ENISA CEO Carolina Rodríguez, interviewed 21 Oct 2026 — [El Español/Invertia, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/invertia/disruptores/ecosistema-startup/startups/20261021/carolina-rodriguez-enisa-certificado-startups-espana-valencia/1003744429744_0.html):
  - "más de 2.500 startups" certified nationally and **275 in Valencia**;
  - the same interview also gives "en torno a 200" for Valencia, and a search listing showed a headline saying "3.000", so the numbers are internally inconsistent;
  - "Los inversores cada vez lo piden más" ("investors increasingly ask for it");
  - the certificate is a requirement in some public calls.
- More than 2,000 were certified by 20 Feb 2026 — [El Diario de Madrid, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.eldiariodemadrid.es/articulo/emprendedores/enisa-2000-startups-certificadas-madrid-barcelona-ecosistema-emprendedor/20260220112754121816.html):
  - **Madrid province 509, Barcelona 466**, Zamora 1, Teruel 2;
  - the first 1,000 were reached in July 2024;
  - the certificate is a scoring criterion in calls by the Xunta de Galicia, Castilla-La Mancha, Ceuta and the councils of **Madrid, Valencia, Málaga**, Móstoles and La Rioja, and in ICEX and Cámara de Comercio programmes.
- Status in May 2024 — [Autónomos y Emprendedor, 16 May 2024](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/autonomos/37-startups-logra-certificacion-enisa-beneficiarse-ayudas-ventajas-fiscales/20240514155913036328.html):
  - ENISA had certified more than 1,000 of about 1,700 applications (about 1,600 resolved), so roughly **63% certified and 37% not**;
  - 91% of certified firms would recommend the process;
  - a consultant put average processing at 1–1.5 months;
  - validation could not start until mid-summer 2023 because the ministerial order defining "empresa emergente" was still pending.
- In March 2023 ENISA (José Bayón) expected **5,000–7,000 certified companies in 2023**, "en torno a la mitad del total nacional" (about half of all startups in Spain), with 1,300 already pre-registered — [Innovaspain, 10 Mar 2023](https://www.innovaspain.com/ley-de-startups-emprendedores-enisa-carlos-mateo-jose-bayon-pons-ip-samaipata/).
- **What the law says** — [BOE consolidated Ley 28/2022](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739) (accessed 23 Oct 2026; it lists only the initial text of 22/12/2022, with no later amending laws):
  - **Definition (art. 3):**
    - at most 5 years since registration (7 for biotech, energy, industrial and some other sectors);
    - not the result of a merger, spin-off or transformation of non-startups;
    - no dividends paid;
    - not listed;
    - seat or permanent establishment in Spain;
    - "al 60 % de la plantilla con un contrato laboral en España" (60% of staff on a Spanish employment contract);
    - an innovative project with a scalable business model.
  - **Certification (art. 4.2):** ENISA must decide within three months. Silence counts as approval: "entenderla estimada por silencio administrativo positivo".
  - **Conflict:** Legalitas states that a non-answer means rejection ("se entenderá desestimada la solicitud") — [Legalitas, 22 Apr 2025](https://www.legalitas.com/actualidad/certificado-enisa-para-startups). The BOE text prevails.
- **€10M turnover cap and other limits** (cited consistently, but the BOE extract above did not show the turnover clause):
  - €10M turnover cap and the 5/7-year age limit — [Economía 3, 15 Oct 2026](https://economia3.com/2026/10/15/762915-certificado-de-empresa-emergente-2-529-startups-lo-tienen-pero-no-garantiza-el-visado/);
  - €10M cap — [Premoney, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.premoney.es/blog/certificado-empresa-emergente-enisa);
  - €10M cap — [Tetuan Valley, 28 Jul 2026](https://tetuanvalley.com/blog-financiacion-publica-para-startups/).
- Applying is free. Premoney, an advisory firm, says it obtained 29 approvals out of the 30+ files it handled; this is a self-selected sample, not an ENISA rate — [Premoney, 7 Sep 2026](https://www.premoney.es/blog/certificado-empresa-emergente-enisa).

**Tax and company-law perks** (all from the [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739) unless noted)
- **Corporate tax at 15%** in the first tax period with a positive taxable base and the three following, while the status is kept.
- **Tax deferral:** tax due for the first two profitable periods can be deferred, without guarantees, by 12 and 6 months respectively. No instalment payments (pagos fraccionados) are required in those periods.
- **No forced dissolution:** losses do not force the company to dissolve for three years — [Economía 3, 15 Oct 2026](https://economia3.com/2026/10/15/762915-certificado-de-empresa-emergente-2-529-startups-lo-tienen-pero-no-garantiza-el-visado/).
- **Social Security relief for some founders:** "una bonificación del cien por cien de la cuota correspondiente a la base mínima" (a 100% rebate on the minimum-base contribution) for up to three years. It only covers controlling founders who are also employees elsewhere (new art. 38 quinquies, Ley 20/2007).
- **Cheaper incorporation:** using the standard bylaws (estatutos tipo) through CIRCE with share capital under €3,100, notary and registry fees are "60 y 40 euros respectivamente". Publication in the official business gazette (BORME) is free. Registration must happen within six business hours.
- **Foreign investors (art. 9):** non-resident investors get a tax number (NIF) without needing a foreigner number (NIE). The tax agency (AEAT) has 10 business days. The NIF can be revoked if the investment is not documented within six months.
- **Business angels (art. 68.1 LIRPF, from 1 Jan 2023):**
  - a 50% income-tax deduction on a maximum base of **€100,000 a year**;
  - shares must be bought at incorporation or in a capital increase within 5 years (7 for empresas emergentes);
  - shares must be held for more than 3 and less than 12 years;
  - "será necesario obtener una certificación expedida por la entidad" (the company must issue a certificate).
  - **Conflict:** Venza claims a €250,000 limit — [Venza, 16 Jun 2025](https://venza.legal/ley-startups-2025-fiscalidad-inversion/). The BOE prevails.

**Stock options**
- **The rule** (arts. 42.3.f and 14.2.m LIRPF) — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739):
  - the yearly tax exemption is **€50,000** for certified startups, against €12,000 for other companies;
  - startups do not have to offer equal terms to all staff, but the plan must sit "dentro de la política retributiva general de la empresa" (within the company's general pay policy);
  - anything above the exemption is taxed at listing, at sale, or "transcurrido el plazo de diez años a contar desde la entrega" (ten years after the shares are delivered).
- **Valuation:** shares are valued at the price an independent expert set in the last capital increase within the prior year, otherwise at market value — [GM Tax Consultancy, undated, © 2026](https://gmtaxconsultancy.com/es/impuestos/tributacion-stock-options-ley-startups/) (single source).
- **Tax rates:** the gain on exercise is taxed as employment income at 19–47%; the gain on sale is taxed as savings income at 19–30% — [Iurit Corp, 25 Aug 2026](https://iuritcorp.com/stock-options-en-espana-y-fiscalidad-en-startups/).

**Digital-nomad permit and entrepreneur residence**
- **Durations set by Ley 28/2022** (amending Ley 14/2013) — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739):
  - international telework visa: up to 1 year;
  - telework residence: up to 3 years, renewable for 2-year periods;
  - entrepreneur residence: 3 years, renewable for 2, and it needs "un informe favorable emitido por ENISA" (a favourable report from ENISA).
- **Digital-nomad permit in 2026** — [Anchorless, updated 21 Oct 2026](https://anchorless.io/es/blog/sp-mudarse/espana-visa-nomada-digital) (commercial guide, single source on how the immigration office behaves):
  - single-applicant income threshold **€34,188/year (€2,849/month = 200% of the minimum wage, SMI)**;
  - the SMI is €1,221 × 14 payments under RD 126/2026 (BOE 19 Feb 2026);
  - the immigration office for skilled profiles (UGE) has 20 working days and silence counts as approval;
  - in H1 2026 the UGE reorganised, applies requirements more strictly and now often rejects outright instead of asking for documents;
  - fee 790-038: €70.40 initial, €75.60 renewal;
  - no official count of permits granted.
- **Entrepreneur residence route** — [Economía 3, 15 Oct 2026](https://economia3.com/2026/10/15/762915-certificado-de-empresa-emergente-2-529-startups-lo-tienen-pero-no-garantiza-el-visado/):
  - no minimum investment or jobs;
  - ENISA has 10 business days to issue its report; the UGE has 20 business days (silence counts as approval); the consulate has 10 business days;
  - "Sin un informe favorable no hay autorización" ("without a favourable report there is no authorisation");
  - the investor "golden visa" could no longer be requested from April 2025, but the entrepreneur residence remains;
  - headline: the startup certificate "no garantiza el visado" (does not guarantee the visa).

**Beckham regime (special tax regime for people moving to Spain)**
- **What Ley 28/2022 changed in art. 93 LIRPF** — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739):
  - entrepreneurs carrying out an "actividad emprendedora" now qualify;
  - so do highly qualified professionals serving startups, if that work is more than 40% of their income;
  - the required non-residence period before moving fell to five years;
  - the regime covers the year of the move plus five more;
  - employment income is taxed at 24% up to €600,000 and 47% above.
- **How it is claimed:** by filing Modelo 149 within six months of registering with Social Security. The tax regime is a separate application from the immigration permit — [Anchorless, 21 Oct 2026](https://anchorless.io/es/blog/sp-mudarse/espana-visa-nomada-digital).
- No 2025–2026 amendment was found in the sources consulted (see Gaps).

**Implementation and governance**
- **Foro Nacional de Empresas Emergentes (the official forum to monitor the law):**
  - the law ordered a royal decree regulating it within six months (final provision 11) — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739);
  - in June 2024 the Asociación Española de Startups (AES) said the law was not fully applied because the Foro had not been formed, and called it "una medida urgente" — [Telemadrid, 6 Jun 2024](https://www.telemadrid.es/programas/madrid-trabaja/La-Asociacion-Espanola-de-Startups-se-muestra-partidaria-de-ampliar-la-ley-que-regula-estas-empresas-9-2676722318--20240606044057.html);
  - by December 2025 the Foro had existed for "about six months". AES was excluded from the plenary while Barcelona Tech City was included, and AES was only added to working groups in August — [Computerworld, 23 Dec 2025](https://www.computerworld.es/article/4111037/carlos-mateo-la-inmensa-mayoria-de-startups-que-hay-en-espana-son-tecnologicas.html);
  - startup associations formally protested the exclusion on 27 May 2025 — [AES news list](https://asociacionstartups.es/actualidad.html).
- **AES president Carlos Mateo on the law:**
  - "una especie de producto mínimo viable" ("a kind of minimum viable product");
  - the definition "se queda corta" (falls short);
  - he opposes the 60% rule, the age limits and the €10M cap — [Computerworld, 23 Dec 2025](https://www.computerworld.es/article/4111037/carlos-mateo-la-inmensa-mayoria-de-startups-que-hay-en-espana-son-tecnologicas.html);
  - "No es tan ambiciosa como nos habría gustado" ("not as ambitious as we would have liked") — [El Diario de Madrid, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.eldiariodemadrid.es/articulo/entrevistas/carlos-mateo-asociacion-startups-emprendimiento-innovacion-espana/20251209125147115994.html).
- Investors and industry groups (Kfund, Adigital, EsTech) propose a "Scaleup certification under Startup Law" and support for startup listings ("IBEX Tech") — [Dealroom/SpainCap report, June 2025](https://www.spaincap.org/storage/documents/2025/06/the-spanish-tech-ecosystem.pdf).

### Inferences
- Cumulative certifications (2,529) are far below the number of startups. Catalonia alone counts 2,403 startups (ACCIÓ) and the national estimate is ">5,000 active" (Computerworld). So most Spanish startups are not certified at any given time, and the base currently certified is only about 2,046.
- The 15% corporate tax rate only starts in the first profitable year, and only while the company is still certified (at most 5–7 years from incorporation). Many VC-backed startups that stay loss-making for years may never use it. I found no usage statistics for this perk.
- Because silence counts as approval, ENISA's speed is not the bottleneck. The real problems are eligibility and keeping the status, shown by the 483 certifications already lost.
- The 10-year backstop on deferred stock-option tax creates a risk of tax being due without any sale to pay it, given how few exits happen (see STA-18).
- Online legal guidance contradicts itself: Legalitas on silence, Venza on the €250k angel limit. Founders cannot easily get authoritative, up-to-date rules, which points to demand for compliance and eligibility tools.

### Gaps
- ENISA publishes no official statistics on denial rates, 2025–26 resolution times or reasons for losing the status.
- I found no official count of digital-nomad permits or entrepreneur residence permits. A search snippet cited "about 28,000" nomad visas from a commercial site; it is unverified and excluded.
- Beckham regime: I found no 2025–2026 amendment, but I could not check AEAT or DGT (tax directorate) rulings. The Basque and Navarre provincial (foral) income-tax regimes, which matter for Bilbao, were not checked.
- I found no 2026 bill to reform the Ley de Startups or create a scale-up certification.

## 2. Ecosystem data per hub (startups, funding 2024–2026, sector mix, bottlenecks)

### Takeaway
- **National:** 2025 raised about €3.1B, but sources disagree on growth. H1 2026 grew 5% to €2,059M only because of 11 megarounds, which took 58% of the capital. Activity outside megarounds fell 9%.
- **Madrid and Barcelona** take more than two thirds of VC. Madrid overtook Barcelona in H1 2026 (€738M against €717M).
- **Valencia** is the clear third hub: 1,650+ startups and an average round of €4.7M in 2025.
- **Bilbao, Málaga, Sevilla, Zaragoza and the Canary Islands** have thin, poorly measured startup data. Regional GEM entrepreneurship surveys rate financing, the domestic market and bureaucracy as the weakest local conditions.

### Cited Findings
**National**
- **Dealroom 2026 report** (12 May 2026; partners include Enisa, Kfund, Wayra, SpainCap, Endeavor) — [Dealroom](https://dealroom.co/reports/the-spanish-tech-ecosystem-report-2026/):
  - ecosystem value €125B, 8th in Europe;
  - €3.1B of VC in 2025 against €1.9B in 2024;
  - Madrid €1.2B and Barcelona €1.1B, together more than two thirds of the total;
  - AI startups are 18.4% of startups founded since 2021, against 7.7% of those founded in 2010–2020;

  - megarounds: 15 deals worth €1,354M (44%);
  - cities: Barcelona €1,374M in 140 deals, Madrid above €800M;
  - sectors: software €516M (+493%);

  - without megarounds: €862M (−9%);

  - ">5,000 active startups", per the "Informe Nacional de Empresas Tech 2025" cited by Mateo — [Computerworld, 23 Dec 2025](https://www.computerworld.es/article/4111037/carlos-mateo-la-inmensa-mayoria-de-startups-que-hay-en-espana-son-tecnologicas.html);
  - about 200 Spanish startups raise their first VC round each year; about 3,000 early-stage VC-backed, 209 at breakout stage and 39 at late stage; "No new unicorn since 2023" — [Dealroom/SpainCap, June 2025](https://www.spaincap.org/storage/documents/2025/06/the-spanish-tech-ecosystem.pdf);
  - an aggregator cites StartupBlink (Sept 2026) at 6,152 startups and ICEX at 12,000+. These are weakly sourced — [mean.ceo blog, 21 Oct 2026](https://blog.mean.ceo/startups-spain-news-october-2026/).

  - early-stage entrepreneurial activity (TEA) 7.8% in 2025, up from 7.2% in 2024;
  - experts rate "Políticas gubernamentales que reducen la burocracia y los impuestos a nuevas empresas" (government policies that cut red tape and taxes for new firms) at **3.7–3.8 out of 10**;
  - they rate ease of access to financing at 3.8–4.0 and R&D transfer at 3.7–4.2 (two most recent editions).

## 3. Founder pains (fundraising, bureaucracy, hiring, foreign founders, public funding, R&D credits, sales, internationalisation, failure, AI Act)

### Takeaway
The best-evidenced founder pains are the following (each is detailed in the STA entries below):
- **Capital:** a thin step from seed to Series A, with capital concentrated in megarounds led by foreign or mixed syndicates.
- **Public funding:** a slow, conditional stack of ENISA loans, CDTI grants and loans, and EU programmes, which pushes founders to pay advisers.
- **Day-one fixed costs:** autónomo registration and quota, VAT from the first euro and quarterly filings.
- **Talent:** rising AI and data salaries, and candidates who overwhelmingly want fully remote work.
- **Paperwork for every round:** notary and commercial-registry formalities, plus onboarding foreign investors.
- **Immigration:** for non-EU founders and hires.
- **Public-sector sales:** a market dominated by single-bidder contracts.
- **AI Act compliance:** new obligations for AI startups.

Data on failure reasons only exists for general entrepreneurs (GEM). It shows that sale opportunities and lack of profitability dominate. Taxes and bureaucracy are cited in 5.6% of closures and disputes with partners in only 0.7%.

### Cited Findings
- **Why businesses close (all entrepreneurs, not startups)** — [GEM España 2025-26 PDF](https://observatoriodelemprendimiento.es/descargas/informe-gem-espana-2025-2026/?wpdmdl=6241&ind=17802413629191&refresh=79350689&filename=Informe-GEM-Espana-2025-2026.pdf):

Details in STA-13.

- Many pains cluster around the company's legal and financial core: the cap table, capital increases, investor tax certificates, the ESOP clock and keeping certification. Today these are handled by notaries, lawyers and gestorías working off spreadsheets. This looks like the most software-shaped cluster for startups.

## Pain-point inventory (structured STA entries)

### STA-01: Narrow, expiring startup certification gatekeeps all Ley de Startups benefits
- **Who:** Spanish-registered tech startups from pre-seed to Series A, typically 1–50 staff. Excluded are:
  - firms older than 5 years (7 for biotech, energy or industrial);
  - firms with turnover above €10M;
  - remote-first or foreign-team firms with less than 60% of staff on Spanish contracts;
  - spin-outs of non-startups;
  - any firm that has paid dividends.
- **Where:** national (ENISA). Highest counts: Madrid province 509 and Barcelona 466 (Feb 2026); Valencia 275 (Oct 2026). The certificate scores points in calls by the Madrid, Valencia and Málaga councils, among others.
- **Day-to-day reality:** founders send ENISA their deeds, tax number, business plan and evidence of innovation, then must keep meeting every requirement. Once they cross the age or turnover line, or restructure, they lose the 15% tax rate, the 50% angel deduction, the €50k stock-option exemption and any residence routes tied to the status. Investors and public calls increasingly ask for the certificate.
- **Evidence:**
  - As of 11 Oct 2026, 2,529 recognised, 2,046 still certified and 483 lost (about 19%) — [Economía 3, 15 Oct 2026](https://economia3.com/2026/10/15/762915-certificado-de-empresa-emergente-2-529-startups-lo-tienen-pero-no-garantiza-el-visado/); ENISA's page shows 2,527 — [ENISA](https://www.enisa.es/servicios/certificacion/) (accessed 23 Oct 2026).
  - ENISA forecast 5,000–7,000 certifications in 2023 — [Innovaspain, 10 Mar 2023](https://www.innovaspain.com/ley-de-startups-emprendedores-enisa-carlos-mateo-jose-bayon-pons-ip-samaipata/). The first 1,000 only arrived in July 2024 — [El Diario de Madrid, 20 Feb 2026](https://www.eldiariodemadrid.es/articulo/emprendedores/enisa-2000-startups-certificadas-madrid-barcelona-ecosistema-emprendedor/20260220112754121816.html).
  - 37% of applications were not certified by May 2024 — [Autónomos y Emprendedor, 16 May 2024](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/autonomos/37-startups-logra-certificacion-enisa-beneficiarse-ayudas-ventajas-fiscales/20240514155913036328.html).
  - AES says the definition "se queda corta" (falls short) and opposes the 60% rule, the age limits and the €10M cap — [Computerworld, 23 Dec 2025](https://www.computerworld.es/article/4111037/carlos-mateo-la-inmensa-mayoria-de-startups-que-hay-en-espana-son-tecnologicas.html).
  - "Los inversores cada vez lo piden más" (investors increasingly ask for it) — ENISA CEO, [El Español, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/invertia/disruptores/ecosistema-startup/startups/20261021/carolina-rodriguez-enisa-certificado-startups-espana-valencia/1003744429744_0.html).
- **Scale:** about 2,046 currently certified, against more than 5,000 active startups nationally ([Computerworld](https://www.computerworld.es/article/4111037/carlos-mateo-la-inmensa-mayoria-de-startups-que-hay-en-espana-son-tecnologicas.html)) and 2,403 in Catalonia alone ([ACCIÓ](https://www.accio.gencat.cat/web/.content/bancconeixement/documents/pindoles/ACCIO-analisi-ecosistema-startup-catalunya-2026-pindola-en.pdf)). Inference: most startups are uncertified at any given time.
- **Cost/impact:** without the status, a startup has:
  - no 15% corporate tax rate (25% otherwise, per [Tetuan Valley](https://tetuanvalley.com/blog-financiacion-publica-para-startups/));
  - no 50% angel deduction on up to €100k;
  - only the €12k stock-option exemption instead of €50k;
  - no autónomo rebate for founders who are also employed elsewhere;
  - no three-year protection from dissolution because of losses — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739).
  - It also loses points in public calls that score the certificate.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 28/2022 arts. 3–4 and Orden PCM/825/2023. **In force.** The BOE consolidated text shows no amendment since 22 Dec 2022 (checked 23 Oct 2026). AES is lobbying to widen the definition; no reform bill found.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - founders apply themselves, for free, through the ENISA portal using its "Guía de Certificación 2026";
  - or they use consultants and gestorías such as Premoney and Managora, which publish no fees;
  - complaint: online legal guidance contradicts itself. Legalitas says ENISA's silence means rejection; the BOE says it means approval.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. An eligibility checker, continuous monitoring of the age, turnover and payroll thresholds, and automatic generation of the application pack are automatable. The thresholds themselves are policy.
- **Confidence:** High.

### STA-02: Stock-option tax hits illiquid equity, and the €50k regime only covers certified startups
- **Who:** early employees and key hires (CTO packages of 0.3–3% equity) at certified startups from seed to Series B; founders designing employee share plans (ESOPs).
- **Where:** national (state income tax). The provincial (foral) regimes in Bizkaia, Gipuzkoa, Álava and Navarra were not checked.
- **Day-to-day reality:** companies must:
  - fit the plan within the "política retributiva general" (general pay policy);
  - document valuations (an independent expert at the last capital increase, or market value);
  - track every grant and exercise.
  Employees pay 19–47% income tax on any exercise gain above €50k. That tax can only be deferred until a sale, a listing or 10 years after delivery, whichever comes first. A decade with no exit ends in a tax bill with no cash from a sale to pay it.
- **Evidence:**
  - €50k exemption only for startups (€12k for other companies); deferral until listing, sale or 10 years — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739).
  - Valuation rule — [GM Tax Consultancy, © 2026](https://gmtaxconsultancy.com/es/impuestos/tributacion-stock-options-ley-startups/) (single source).
  - Exercise taxed at 19–47%; sale at 19–30% — [Iurit Corp, 25 Aug 2026](https://iuritcorp.com/stock-options-en-espana-y-fiscalidad-en-startups/).
  - Pay bands for a CTO or Head of Engineering include "+ 0.5/3% equity" (5–10 years' experience) and "0.3/1.5%" (more than 10 years) — [Manfred, 12 Jan 2026](https://www.getmanfred.com/blog/guia-salarial-2026-salarios-en-tecnologia-espana-manfred).
  - Few exits: 19 in H1 2026 (−32%), none with a disclosed value — [Bankinter H1 2026](https://www.fundacionbankinter.org/informe-observatorio/h1-2026-informe-de-inversion-en-startups/).
- **Scale:** employees of the roughly 2,046 certified startups can use the €50k regime; everyone else falls back to €12k with an equal-terms requirement. Catalan startups alone employ 30,537 people ([ACCIÓ](https://www.accio.gencat.cat/web/.content/bancconeixement/documents/pindoles/ACCIO-analisi-ecosistema-startup-catalunya-2026-pindola-en.pdf)).
- **Cost/impact:** tax at marginal rates up to 47% on paper gains. Inference: a tax event lands after 10 years whether or not there is liquidity, and plans need manual tracking over that decade.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 28/2022 final provision 3, amending arts. 14.2.m and 42.3.f LIRPF. **In force** since 1 Jan 2023; no 2025–26 change found.
- **How it is solved today:** tax advisers and startup lawyers; global equity-management SaaS such as Ledgy or Carta (Spanish pricing and usage not verified); phantom-share plans (prevalence not verified in this session).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. ESOP administration, vesting, valuation evidence, a 10-year tax-event clock and employee tax simulators are automatable. Corporate formalities remain: transfers of SL shares need a public deed, which I did not verify in this session.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The law is verified; documented complaints are scarce.

### STA-03: The step from seed to Series A, with capital concentrated in megarounds and foreign or mixed syndicates
- **Who:** VC-backed startups raising seed (€1–5M) or Series A (€5–10M), using Bankinter's definitions; typically 5–50 staff, B2B SaaS and deeptech.
- **Where:** national; hardest outside Madrid and Barcelona (see STA-17).
- **Day-to-day reality:** founders who closed pre-seed or seed struggle to find a Series A lead. Rounds with only domestic investors are small. Large cheques come from mixed or foreign syndicates, so fundraising becomes an international process.
- **Evidence:**
  - In 2025, seed fell to 89 deals (−10%, third annual decline), "el dato más preocupante del informe" (the most worrying figure in the report) — [Bankinter, 22 Jan 2026](https://www.fundacionbankinter.org/noticias/startups-2025-observatorio/).
  - H1 2026 — [Bankinter H1 2026](https://www.fundacionbankinter.org/informe-observatorio/h1-2026-informe-de-inversion-en-startups/):
    - "la Serie A se queda plana" (Series A stays flat): 15 deals;
    - Series B −24%, Series C −29%, pre-seed −25%;
    - megarounds take 58% of capital;
    - domestic-only rounds are 46% of deals but €214M, 10% of capital (−26%).
  - Graduation from seed to Series A is below the European average; only 32% of 2020–24 VC went to late stage — [Dealroom/SpainCap, June 2025](https://www.spaincap.org/storage/documents/2025/06/the-spanish-tech-ecosystem.pdf).
  - 66% of the 77 funds in Catalonia's 20 largest rounds were international — [ACCIÓ, Feb 2026](https://www.accio.gencat.cat/web/.content/bancconeixement/documents/pindoles/ACCIO-analisi-ecosistema-startup-catalunya-2026-pindola-en.pdf).
  - The La Razón headline "El escalón de la Serie A en financiación de startups" (31 Aug 2025), tracked by AES (headline only) — [AES news](https://asociacionstartups.es/actualidad.html).
  - In Valencia, supporting firms beyond the early stages is a historic challenge — [Valencia Plaza, 9 Jun 2026](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/plaza-innovacion/la-ronda-media-de-las-startups-valencianas-se-dispara-hasta-los-47-millones-en-2025).
- **Scale:** about 200 first VC rounds a year. About 3,000 early-stage VC-backed startups, against 209 at breakout stage and 39 at late stage ([Dealroom 2025](https://www.spaincap.org/storage/documents/2025/06/the-spanish-tech-ecosystem.pdf)). 376 rounds in 2025 ([Bankinter](https://www.fundacionbankinter.org/noticias/startups-2025-observatorio/)).
- **Cost/impact:** 11 rounds took 58% of H1 2026 capital; the median round is €2.0M; no new unicorn since 2023 ([Dealroom 2025](https://www.spaincap.org/storage/documents/2025/06/the-spanish-tech-ecosystem.pdf)).
- **Driver & status:** a market force (supply of capital and of fund investors). Policy proposals (scale-up certification, support for startup listings, a "TIBI" model for institutional capital) are **not enacted** ([Dealroom/Kfund, June 2025](https://www.spaincap.org/storage/documents/2025/06/the-spanish-tech-ecosystem.pdf)).
- **How it is solved today:**
  - ENISA participative loans as a bridge (€25k–€1.5M, with the own-funds matching rule);
  - venture debt and other non-equity funding (€2.3B in 2024);
  - corporate VC (in about 1 in 5 rounds) — [Dealroom 2025](https://www.spaincap.org/storage/documents/2025/06/the-spanish-tech-ecosystem.pdf);
  - accelerators;
  - a fractional CFO to prepare (€750–€2,000 a month, [Premoney](https://www.premoney.es/blog/cfo-externo)).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Investor-readiness tools (data rooms, KPI reporting, investor CRM and matching) help. The shortage of capital is structural.
- **Confidence:** High.

### STA-04: Public funding (ENISA, CDTI Neotec/PID/LIC, EIC) is slow, conditional and paperwork-heavy
- **Who:** pre-seed to Series A tech startups and university spin-offs, especially R&D-heavy deeptech; Neotec is for companies under 3 years old.
- **Where:** national (ENISA, CDTI), plus regional agencies such as ACCIÓ, IVACE, SPRI, ICF, AvalMadrid, SODERCAN and IDEPA, listed by [Tetuan Valley](https://tetuanvalley.com/blog-financiacion-publica-para-startups/).
- **Day-to-day reality:** founders spend weeks on business plans, technical reports and financial projections.
  - ENISA requires own funds at least equal to the loan, and filed or audited accounts.
  - Neotec requires at least €20k of share capital and a budget of at least €175k.
  - Money arrives 3–9 months later.
  - Credit lines and funds keep changing: the old ENISA lines were replaced, and the FEPYME fund is drawn down as loans are approved.
- **Evidence:**
  - **ENISA terms** — [Tetuan Valley, 28 Jul 2026](https://tetuanvalley.com/blog-financiacion-publica-para-startups/) (single source for timings):
    - €25k–€1.5M;
    - interest "alrededor de Euríbor +4%" plus a variable part capped at 6.5%;
    - 0.5% opening fee;
    - 3–6 months until the money arrives; 45–60 days of analysis;
    - "tus fondos propios tienen que ser al menos iguales al préstamo que pides" (own funds must at least equal the loan), the number one reason for refusal.
  - **ENISA requirements in 2026** — [Premoney, revised 7 Sep 2026](https://www.premoney.es/blog/requisitos-solicitar-enisa):
    - accumulated losses reduce own funds;
    - audited accounts above €300k;
    - the old lines (Jóvenes Emprendedores, Emprendedores, Crecimiento) "ya no estructuran la oferta actual" (no longer make up the current offer);
    - five lines exist now.
  - **FEPYME fund:** €303M, active until 31 Aug 2026, used up as operations are approved; competition is getting tougher — [The Startup Oasis, 12 May 2026](https://startupsoasis.substack.com/p/35-status-del-fondo-de-emprendimiento).
  - **Neotec and EIC** — [Tetuan Valley](https://tetuanvalley.com/blog-financiacion-publica-para-startups/) (single source):
    - Neotec: up to €250k (€325k if a PhD is hired), covering 70–85% of the plan;
    - one competitive call a year, around May–June, and a company can only win once;
    - 6–9 months until the money arrives;
    - EIC Accelerator: €634M budget in 2026, about 12 months of process, "la tasa de aprobación es baja" (the approval rate is low).
  - **CDTI loans:** PID and LIC are open all year (PID up to 85% of the budget, LIC 75% or 85% with EU regional funds). Applications are often rejected because the technical report does not explain the technology's value — [The Startup Oasis, 11 Aug 2026](https://startupsoasis.substack.com/p/41-las-ayudas-del-cdti-que-puedes).
  - **ENISA volume:**
    - nearly 9,000 loans to 7,800 companies, over €1,400M, by end-2024 — [Dealroom/SpainCap, June 2025](https://www.spaincap.org/storage/documents/2025/06/the-spanish-tech-ecosystem.pdf);
    - Valencia: more than 800 companies financed, €120M — [El Español, 21 Oct 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/invertia/disruptores/ecosistema-startup/startups/20261021/carolina-rodriguez-enisa-certificado-startups-espana-valencia/1003744429744_0.html).
- **Scale:** thousands of firms (7,800 ENISA borrowers cumulatively). A single public pot of €303M (FEPYME).
- **Cost/impact:** 3–9 months of delay. Adviser fees are not published. Many firms pay a fractional CFO (€750–€2,000 a month) to build the financials.
- **Driver & status:**
  - ENISA 2026 lines: **in force, restructured**;
  - FEPYME window: **deadline passed on 31 Aug 2026**; status afterwards not verified;
  - CDTI Neotec: annual call; **2026 call details not verified**;
  - EIC: EU programme, **in force**.
- **How it is solved today:** specialist consultancies (for example the Premoney and Tetuan Valley partnership; fees not public), accelerators, fractional CFOs, ENISA's client portal.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Eligibility pre-checks (own-funds rule, age, capital), document assembly, financial-model templates, a calendar of calls and tracking of post-award reporting are automatable. Approval stays discretionary.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high. Several sources agree, but timing data comes mostly from advisers.

### STA-05: R&D tax credits, cash refunds, binding R&D certificates and researcher Social Security rebates are underused
- **Who:** R&D-intensive startups (deeptech, biotech, AI), often still making losses, from seed to Series B.
- **Where:** national (tax agency AEAT and Ministry of Science). The evidence base is strongest for Catalonia.
- **Day-to-day reality:** to claim, startups must document R&D projects and staff time, ideally obtain a binding R&D certificate (Informe Motivado Vinculante), and decide whether to cash in the credit at a 20% discount. Many skip it because they are making losses. The innovative-SME label (Sello Pyme Innovadora), valid for 3 years, is needed to combine credits with Social Security rebates for researchers.
- **Evidence:**
  - Deductions — [Tetuan Valley, 28 Jul 2026](https://tetuanvalley.com/blog-financiacion-publica-para-startups/) (single source):
    - 25–42% for R&D and 12% for technological innovation, plus 17% for researcher staff (up to 59% in total);
    - paid in cash with a 20% discount if no tax is due;
    - a binding R&D certificate is recommended;
    - common mistake: "descartar las deducciones por estar en pérdidas" (dropping the credits because the company is loss-making);
    - the innovative-SME label lasts 3 years.
  - 89% of Catalan startups invest in R&D; 58% spend more than 25% of revenue on it and 39% more than 50% — [ACCIÓ, Feb 2026](https://www.accio.gencat.cat/web/.content/bancconeixement/documents/pindoles/ACCIO-analisi-ecosistema-startup-catalunya-2026-pindola-en.pdf).
  - The credits can be cashed in and do not require competing in a call — [The Startup Oasis, 9 Jun 2026](https://startupsoasis.substack.com/p/37-las-mejores-convocatorias-de-financiacion).
- **Scale:** inference: about 2,100 of the 2,403 Catalan startups invest in R&D (89%).
- **Cost/impact:** a 20% discount on credits taken as cash. Consultant fees were not found. Credits are lost if not documented (inference).
- **Driver & status:** the R&D deduction and cash-refund rules of the corporate-tax law (Ley 27/2014), the binding-certificate rules (RD 1432/2003) and the researcher rebate (RD 475/2007). **Presumed in force; not re-verified in the BOE in this session.** Overlaps with the tax researcher.
- **How it is solved today:** R&D tax consultancies (fees not verified), binding certificates through the ministry's process, in-house timesheets.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes or partly. R&D time-tracking, project documentation and claim packs suit software. The certificate remains a public step.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### STA-06: Day-one fixed costs and filings: autónomo registration and quota, VAT from the first euro, quarterly returns
- **Who:** every founder-director who controls a new SL. Hardest for pre-revenue, bootstrapped and pre-seed founders.
- **Where:** national; the complaint is voiced by AES in Madrid.
- **Day-to-day reality:** on incorporating, a founder who controls the company must register as self-employed (autónomo) and pay the quota. They must charge and declare VAT "desde el primer euro" (from the first euro) and file quarterly returns "desde el minuto uno" (from minute one). In practice they hire a gestoría before they have any revenue.
- **Evidence:**
  - Mateo: "Hemos normalizado un atraco a mano armada a los emprendedores desde el día uno"; "la burocracia es enorme"; Spain does not apply the €85,000 VAT exemption (his claim) — [El Diario de Madrid, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.eldiariodemadrid.es/articulo/entrevistas/carlos-mateo-asociacion-startups-emprendimiento-innovacion-espana/20251209125147115994.html).
  - "la obligación de que una persona se dé de alta como autónomo en el momento en el que crea una sociedad" (having to register as autónomo the moment you create a company) is a major barrier — [Computerworld, 23 Dec 2025](https://www.computerworld.es/article/4111037/carlos-mateo-la-inmensa-mayoria-de-startups-que-hay-en-espana-son-tecnologicas.html).
  - AES asks to eliminate the autónomo quota and exempt the first €85,000 from VAT — [AES manifesto](https://asociacionstartups.es/); [Telemadrid, 16 Apr 2026](https://www.telemadrid.es/programas/madrid-trabaja/Madrid-se-consolida-como-referente-de-emprendimiento-y-el-sector-exige-menos-burocracia-9-2880401945--20260416044908.html).
  - The law only relieves founders who also hold a job elsewhere: a 100% rebate on the minimum-base quota for up to 3 years — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739).
  - Among all entrepreneurs, 5.6% of closures are attributed to "Políticas fiscales (impuestos, burocracia)", and experts rate the government's cutting of red tape and taxes at 3.7–3.8 out of 10 — [GEM España 2025-26](https://observatoriodelemprendimiento.es/gem-spain/).
- **Scale:** about 9,200 registered startup founders in 2023 ([ClosingGap via Autónomos y Emprendedor](https://www.autonomosyemprendedor.es/articulo/emprendedores/negocios-dirigidos-solo-mujeres-reciben-apenas-3-total-financiacion-disponible/20240927183406038686.html)), plus all new SL founders who control their company.
- **Cost/impact:** the monthly autónomo quota and gestoría fees. Amounts were not verified here; they are covered by the labour and tax researchers.
- **Driver & status:** the Social Security rules for controlling shareholders (autónomo societario) and the VAT law (Ley 37/1992). **In force.** Spain's non-use of the EU small-business VAT exemption (Directive 2020/285) is AES's claim and was **not verified**.
- **How it is solved today:** gestorías; accounting SaaS such as Holded (€15–€199 a month excluding VAT, [Holded](https://www.holded.com/es/precios)); the Ley 28/2022 rebate for founders who are also employed.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Automated bookkeeping and quarterly filings cut gestoría time. The quota and VAT burden are policy.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The voice is strong but comes from advocacy; overlaps with tax and labour.

### STA-07: Notary and commercial-registry formalities for incorporation and every capital increase
- **Who:** every SL startup, especially those raising several rounds or convertible notes.
- **Where:** national.
- **Day-to-day reality:** incorporation is quick only on the standard route:
  - CIRCE with standard bylaws takes about 24 hours, and fees are €60 (notary) and €40 (registry) if capital is under €3,100;
  - it must start at a business-support point (PAE);
  - it needs a certificate that the name is free, a certificate that capital has been paid in, and a notary appointment.
  Every funding round after that needs a public deed and registration, notary fees under RD 1426/1989, and capital fully paid in.
- **Evidence:**
  - Fees of €60 and €40; free gazette publication; registration in 6 business hours — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739).
  - Official steps — [ONE](https://one.gob.es/en/procedures/start-your-business) (accessed 23 Oct 2026):
    - CIRCE takes "around 24 hours";
    - minimum capital is €1 and must be fully paid "including any later increases";
    - the name certificate costs €18.19;
    - the standard application (DUE) must start at a PAE;
    - notary fees follow RD 1426/1989.
  - Packaged incorporation through Qonto and Finutive costs €130–€610 and promises "tu negocio listo en 4 días" (your business ready in 4 days) — [Qonto](https://qonto.com/es/pricing/creation).
  - Founders interviewed in GEM complain about "la abundancia de normativa, los plazos administrativos, las tasas y la falta de información clara" (too many rules, administrative deadlines, fees and unclear information) — [GEM España 2025-26](https://observatoriodelemprendimiento.es/gem-spain/).
- **Scale:** 376 rounds in 2025 ([Bankinter](https://www.fundacionbankinter.org/noticias/startups-2025-observatorio/)); inference: each one needs company-law formalities. Every new SL goes through incorporation.
- **Cost/impact:** notary and registry fees for capital increases and legal fees per round were **not quantified** (gap).
- **Driver & status:** company law (Ley de Sociedades de Capital), Ley 28/2022 and the €1 minimum capital shown on ONE. **In force.**
- **How it is solved today:** startup lawyers, notaries, packaged incorporations (Qonto/Finutive) and gestorías.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Round-closing workflows, generation of resolutions and deeds, the cap table and a digital share register can be automated. Notarisation remains.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The process is verified; costs and delays per round are not.

### STA-08: Onboarding foreign investors and co-founders (tax numbers, apostilles, representatives)
- **Who:** startups raising from non-resident angels or funds, or with foreign co-founders, from seed to growth.
- **Where:** national (tax agency AEAT and consulates).
- **Day-to-day reality:** each foreign investor needs a Spanish tax number (NIF) before the deed. Since Ley 28/2022, a foreigner number (NIE) is no longer needed. Investing companies apply with Form 036, apostilled registry certificates and powers of attorney. An investing company cannot get a NIF if its own representatives lack Spanish NIFs.
- **Evidence:**
  - NIF without NIE; AEAT has 10 business days; the NIF can be revoked if the investment is not documented within 6 months — [BOE art. 9](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739).
  - Consular route; Forms 030/036; apostille or legalisation plus translation; a NIF "cannot be assigned to the legal entity if the legal representatives" lack a Spanish NIF; 10 days for companies — [ONE](https://one.gob.es/en/procedures/application-tax-identification-number-nif-international-investors).
  - Mixed local and international rounds carry 55% of H1 2026 capital; domestic-only rounds 10% — [Bankinter H1 2026](https://www.fundacionbankinter.org/informe-observatorio/h1-2026-informe-de-inversion-en-startups/).
- **Scale:** inference from Bankinter: about 90% of H1 2026 capital involved non-domestic investors.
- **Cost/impact:** delays to closings were not quantified (gap).
- **Driver & status:** Ley 28/2022 art. 9, **in force**. Foreign-investment screening (RD 571/2023) was **not verified**.
- **How it is solved today:** lawyers, notaries and gestorías, plus the investor's own counsel.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Checklists for identity checks, tax numbers and powers of attorney, and closing trackers shared with investors and the notary.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### STA-09: Non-EU founders need an ENISA favourable report plus UGE approval to reside, and the golden visa is gone
- **Who:** non-EU founders relocating to start or grow a company (pre-seed to seed). Common in Barcelona and Madrid; also in Valencia and Málaga (inference).
- **Where:** national (ENISA, the UGE immigration office and consulates).
- **Day-to-day reality:** the founder must get ENISA's favourable report on the business plan, then UGE authorisation and a consular visa. A foreigner number (NIE) and a residence and work permit are needed before using CIRCE. Holding the startup certificate does not guarantee the visa.
- **Evidence:**
  - Route and timings — [Economía 3, 15 Oct 2026](https://economia3.com/2026/10/15/762915-certificado-de-empresa-emergente-2-529-startups-lo-tienen-pero-no-garantiza-el-visado/):
    - 3-year residence, renewable for 2;
    - ENISA has 10 business days; the UGE has 20 business days (silence counts as approval); the consulate has 10;
    - "Sin un informe favorable no hay autorización" (without a favourable report there is no authorisation);
    - the golden visa ended in April 2025.
  - Entrepreneur residence needs "un informe favorable emitido por ENISA" — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739).
  - Non-EU citizens need an NIE and a residence and work permit for self-employment — [ONE](https://one.gob.es/en/procedures/start-your-business).
  - 28% of Catalan startups have a foreign founder; 21% of founders are foreign; Barcelona is the top EU hub for international founders — [ACCIÓ, Feb 2026](https://www.accio.gencat.cat/web/.content/bancconeixement/documents/pindoles/ACCIO-analisi-ecosistema-startup-catalunya-2026-pindola-en.pdf).
  - ENISA publishes a "Guía de Visados 2026" — [ENISA](https://www.enisa.es/servicios/certificacion/).
- **Scale:** inference: about 550 Catalan startups with a foreign founder (28% of the 1,978 with nationality data). National numbers were not found.
- **Cost/impact:** weeks to months of process. Lawyer fees were not found.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 14/2013 as amended by Ley 28/2022, **in force**. The golden visa was **repealed in April 2025** (per Economía 3).
- **How it is solved today:** immigration lawyers, gestorías (Economía 3 cites Managora) and ENISA's guide.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Dossier builders and status tracking help; decisions stay administrative.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.

### STA-10: Remote hires and digital-nomad permits: higher income thresholds, a stricter UGE, Social Security certificates and residence-card queues
- **Who:** startups hiring non-EU remote staff or relocating staff, foreign startups employing people in Spain, and founders on nomad permits.
- **Where:** national. Delays for residence-card (TIE) appointments are reported in Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia.
- **Day-to-day reality:** applicants must show at least €2,849 a month (2026), with apostilled and sworn-translated documents and a valid Social Security coverage certificate. The UGE now rejects outright more often instead of asking for documents. A foreign employer without a valid certificate may have to register and pay Spanish Social Security. After approval, TIE appointments are hard to get.
- **Evidence:**
  - From [Anchorless, 21 Oct 2026](https://anchorless.io/es/blog/sp-mudarse/espana-visa-nomada-digital) (commercial, single source on practice):
    - the threshold and how the UGE behaves;
    - refusal reasons: health insurance with co-payments, freelancers billing more than 20% to Spanish clients, missing apostilles, and an unresolved dispute over US Social Security certificates;
    - TIE delays in Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia.
  - Durations under the law: visa up to 1 year; residence up to 3 years, renewable for 2 — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739).
  - About 70% of tech candidates want fully remote work — [Manfred Tech Career Report 2025](https://www.getmanfred.com/developer-career-report).
- **Scale:** no official count of permits (gap).
- **Cost/impact:** refusals and document requests add weeks. Using an employer of record costs $599 per employee per month ([Deel](https://www.deel.com/pricing); [Remote](https://remote.com/pricing)).
- **Driver & status:** Ley 14/2013 (as amended by Ley 28/2022), **in force**. The 2026 minimum wage comes from RD 126/2026 (as reported by Anchorless). The stricter practice is administrative and reported, not a rule change.
- **How it is solved today:** immigration lawyers, relocation services and employer-of-record providers.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Pre-checks of eligibility and documents, guidance on Social Security certificates, and appointment and renewal tracking.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### STA-11: Beckham regime for founders and key hires: eligibility tied to startup criteria and a six-month election window
- **Who:** founders relocating to Spain and highly qualified hires of startups.
- **Where:** national state income tax. The provincial (foral) regimes in Bizkaia (Bilbao), Gipuzkoa, Álava and Navarra were not checked.
- **Day-to-day reality:** founders must qualify either through an "actividad emprendedora" backed by ENISA, or as highly qualified professionals earning more than 40% of their income from startups. They must file Modelo 149 within 6 months of registering with Social Security. The immigration permit does not give them the tax regime.
- **Evidence:**
  - Rules — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739):
    - 5-year non-residence look-back;
    - entrepreneurs qualify;
    - the over-40% condition for professionals serving startups;
    - 24% tax up to €600k and 47% above;
    - year of the move plus 5 years.
  - Modelo 149 timing; tax and immigration handled by separate authorities; ordinary freelancers not eligible — [Anchorless, 21 Oct 2026](https://anchorless.io/es/blog/sp-mudarse/espana-visa-nomada-digital) (single commercial source).
- **Scale:** not found.
- **Cost/impact:** inference from the BOE rates: missing the window means paying progressive rates up to 47% instead of a flat 24% on income up to €600k.
- **Driver & status:** art. 93 LIRPF as amended by Ley 28/2022, **in force**. No 2025–26 amendment was found, but this was not exhaustively verified.
- **How it is solved today:** tax advisers.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. An eligibility and deadline tracker linked to the immigration and Social Security dates.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low.

### STA-12: Tech-talent cost, AI and data pay premiums, and competition from remote and foreign employers
- **Who:** seed to Series B startups hiring engineers and data or AI staff, mainly in Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia and Málaga.
- **Where:** national; Catalan data available.
- **Day-to-day reality:** startups compete for senior engineers with foreign companies hiring remotely. About 70% of candidates want fully remote work, AI and data roles command the top pay bands, and on-site roles cost more. Founders use equity to close the gap, which brings in the problems of STA-02.
- **Evidence:**
  - Pay bands and trends — [Manfred Guía Salarial 2026, 12 Jan 2026](https://www.getmanfred.com/blog/guia-salarial-2026-salarios-en-tecnologia-espana-manfred) (based on more than 120,000 profiles):
    - AI Engineer €51–70k (5–10 years' experience) and €71–90k (more than 10 years);
    - Data Scientist €71–100k (more than 10 years);
    - Backend €41–50k (5–10 years);
    - data, infrastructure and AI pay is rising while backend is flat and frontend is falling;
    - on-site hiring may cost at least 20% more;
    - international companies are seeking experienced Staff-level profiles in Spain.
  - About 70% prefer fully remote and fewer than 10% fully on-site; remote is about 30–35% of job offers — [Manfred Tech Career Report 2025](https://www.getmanfred.com/developer-career-report) (data as of 10 Nov 2025; 112,660 profiles).
  - 59% of Catalan startups have international employees; startup employment grew 34% to 30,537 — [ACCIÓ, Feb 2026](https://www.accio.gencat.cat/web/.content/bancconeixement/documents/pindoles/ACCIO-analisi-ecosistema-startup-catalunya-2026-pindola-en.pdf).
  - Mateo: "España seguirá perdiendo talento y oportunidades" (Spain will keep losing talent and opportunities) — [El Diario de Madrid, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.eldiariodemadrid.es/articulo/entrevistas/carlos-mateo-asociacion-startups-emprendimiento-innovacion-espana/20251209125147115994.html).
- **Scale:** 30,537 employees in Catalan startups alone.
- **Cost/impact:** the pay bands above; at least 20% extra for on-site roles; CTO equity of 0.3–3%.
- **Driver & status:** a market force (remote demand, AI boom). No regulatory driver.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - HR add-ons in Holded (from €1.50 per employee per month) or Factorial (Factorial's pricing page returned 404);
  - employers of record (Deel and Remote, $599 per month);
  - employee share plans;
  - recruiter fees were not found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Pay benchmarking, equity-pay tools and remote-employment compliance help; the talent supply is structural.
- **Confidence:** Medium-high.

### STA-13: Selling to the public sector: single-bidder contracts, solvency and experience barriers, complex innovation procurement
- **Who:** startups selling to government (GovTech, cybersecurity, AI and data), from seed to Series B.
- **Where:** national central government (Administración General del Estado). Regional and local procurement was not measured.
- **Day-to-day reality:** tenders often have a single bidder and are won by large integrators. Requirements for financial solvency and prior experience shut out young firms. Innovation procurement (compra pública innovadora) is "muy complicada" (very complicated), and some contracts are declared secret.
- **Evidence:**
  - In 2025, 45.8% of central-government contracts went to the only bidder (€6,307M), more than double the European Commission's 20% risk threshold — [AES, 26 Jun 2026](https://asociacionstartups.es/casi-la-mitad-de-los-contratos-publicos-se-adjudican-sin-competencia-aes-reclama-abrir-la-contratacion-tecnologica-y-reformar-la-ley-de-contratos/):
    - about 100 firms (0.2% of winners) took 53% of the value;
    - SMEs won about a quarter;
    - solvency and experience requirements act as barriers;
    - the data source is cited only as "los últimos datos oficiales" (the latest official data).
  - Mateo: "la compra pública innovadora en la Administración pública es muy complicada" (innovation procurement in public administration is very complicated) — [Computerworld, 23 Dec 2025](https://www.computerworld.es/article/4111037/carlos-mateo-la-inmensa-mayoria-de-startups-que-hay-en-espana-son-tecnologicas.html).
  - AES appealed to the procurement-appeals tribunal (TACRC) against a €17M Interior Ministry contract declared secret (The Objective, 24 Sep 2026; headline only) — [AES news](https://asociacionstartups.es/actualidad.html).
- **Scale:** all central-government procurement. The startup share was not measured.
- **Cost/impact:** startups are shut out of a large buyer. Bid costs were not quantified.
- **Driver & status:** the public-contracts law (Ley 9/2017, LCSP), **in force**. AES asks for reform; no bill found. Overlaps with the procurement researcher.
- **How it is solved today:** subcontracting through integrators and consortia (inference); tender alert services (not verified).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Tender discovery, matching with consortium partners and packs of solvency documents. The structural barriers need legal reform.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The data comes from an advocacy group; the official dataset was not identified.

### STA-14: A small domestic market forces early internationalisation, and corporate demand looks weak

- **Where:** national. Data for Catalonia, Andalusia and the Basque Country.
- **Day-to-day reality:** startups must sell abroad early, which means handling foreign currency invoicing, foreign VAT and contracts. Meanwhile experts rate the domestic market's dynamism poorly.

- **Scale:** inference: about 1,465 exporting Catalan startups (61% of 2,403).
- **Cost/impact:** not quantified.
- **Driver & status:** a market force.

### STA-15: AI Act and AI-governance compliance for AI startups
- **Who:** AI-native startups (18.4% of startups founded since 2021) and startups that use AI (29.7% of Catalan startups work with AI and big data), from seed to Series B.
- **Where:** EU-wide. Spain's supervisor is AESIA, based in A Coruña.
- **Day-to-day reality:** teams must classify their systems and meet the transparency rules of Art. 50, which apply from 2 Aug 2026 (2 Dec 2026 for generative systems already on the market). They must also prepare for high-risk obligations whose dates have moved, while the national rules remain unclear.
- **Evidence:**
  - AI startups are 18.4% of those founded since 2021 — [Dealroom, 12 May 2026](https://dealroom.co/reports/the-spanish-tech-ecosystem-report-2026/).
  - 713 Catalan startups (29.7%) work with AI and big data — [ACCIÓ](https://www.accio.gencat.cat/web/.content/bancconeixement/documents/pindoles/ACCIO-analisi-ecosistema-startup-catalunya-2026-pindola-en.pdf).
  - 79.8% of Basque new ventures see AI as important — [GEM CAPV](https://observatoriodelemprendimiento.es/presentado-informe-gem-pais-vasco-2025-26/). 27% of Valencian ones include AI in their strategy — [GEM CV](https://observatoriodelemprendimiento.es/presentado-informe-gem-de-la-comunidad-valenciana/).
  - Current timeline — [AI Act Explorer timeline](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/implementation-timeline/) (updated 31 Aug 2026):
    - Art. 50 applies from 2 Aug 2026;
    - generative systems already on the market have until 2 Dec 2026;
    - Annex III high-risk obligations apply from **2 Dec 2027**;
    - Annex I high-risk obligations apply from **2 Aug 2028**.
  - The original law applied most rules after 24 months and some high-risk duties after 36 months from 1 Aug 2024 — [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artificial_Intelligence_Act). By my arithmetic that meant Aug 2026 and Aug 2027, so the timeline above reflects a postponement.
  - AESIA is advising Poland and Lithuania on applying the Regulation ([30 Sep 2026](https://aesia.digital.gob.es/es/actualidad/20260930-aesia-recibe-en-a-coruna-a-delegaciones-de-polonia-y-lituania)) and meeting business groups ([20 Oct 2026](https://aesia.digital.gob.es/es/actualidad/20261020-la-aesia-con-las-empresas-para-convertir-la-ia-en-un-motor-de-competitividad-e-innovacion-responsable)).
  - Mateo: "Regular en exceso hace que nuestras startups no puedan competir en igualdad de condiciones" (over-regulating means our startups can't compete on equal terms) — [El Diario de Madrid, 9 Dec 2025](https://www.eldiariodemadrid.es/articulo/entrevistas/carlos-mateo-asociacion-startups-emprendimiento-innovacion-espana/20251209125147115994.html).
- **Scale:** more than 250 Spanish AI startups ([Dealroom 2025](https://www.spaincap.org/storage/documents/2025/06/the-spanish-tech-ecosystem.pdf)); 713 in Catalonia work with AI ([ACCIÓ](https://www.accio.gencat.cat/web/.content/bancconeixement/documents/pindoles/ACCIO-analisi-ecosistema-startup-catalunya-2026-pindola-en.pdf)).
- **Cost/impact:** not quantified.
- **Driver & status:**
  - Regulation (EU) 2024/1689: **in force**; Art. 50 **applies since 2 Aug 2026**;
  - high-risk obligations: **postponed** to Dec 2027 and Aug 2028 according to the AI Act Explorer. The amending act (the "Digital Omnibus") was **not verified** in an official source, and the same site's homepage still mentions national AI sandboxes by 2 Aug 2026;
  - Spain's own AI law (draft from 2025): **status not verified**.
- **How it is solved today:** law firms and consultancies (prices not found); compliance SaaS (not verified).
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. AI inventories, risk classification, technical documentation and content labelling are naturally software.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The deadline status is only partly verified.

### STA-16: Anti-piracy IP blocking by internet providers takes legitimate startup sites and shops offline
- **Who:** startups hosted on shared content-delivery networks such as Cloudflare: SaaS, e-commerce and media, at any stage.
- **Where:** national (Movistar, O2, MásOrange, Vodafone, Digi and smaller providers). The court orders come from commercial courts in Barcelona.
- **Day-to-day reality:** during live sports broadcasts, internet providers block IP ranges that pirate streams share with legitimate sites. Company websites become unreachable and online shops stop selling at peak hours. Startups lack the evidence they would need to claim compensation.
- **Evidence:**
  - From [Qué!, 17 Apr 2026](https://www.que.es/2026/04/17/bloqueos-futbol-operadoras/):
    - dynamic blocking for LaLiga, together with Telefónica, since Feb 2025;
    - an order from Juzgado Mercantil nº 9 de Barcelona extends blocking to other sports and to films and series;
    - providers must apply the block lists within 30 minutes;
    - reported impact on corporate sites and online shops;
    - the government has acknowledged the collateral damage;
    - Mateo warns of threats to net neutrality and to effective judicial protection.
  - AES-tracked headline: "Las startups españolas se hartan de los bloqueos de IP y piden recabar evidencias" (BandaAncha, 26 Aug 2026; the article returned 403) — [AES news](https://asociacionstartups.es/actualidad.html).
- **Scale:** no count of affected sites (gap).
- **Cost/impact:** lost sales and downtime during broadcasts, not quantified.
- **Driver & status:** court-ordered blocking, **in force and ongoing** as of Apr–Aug 2026. No appeals reported.
- **How it is solved today:** changing IPs or CDN settings, and complaints. No tools were verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Detecting blocks from Spanish internet providers, automatic evidence logs for claims, and failover across several CDNs.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

- **Who:** seed to Series A startups without a CFO, typically 5–50 staff.
- **Where:** national.
- **Day-to-day reality:** founders rebuild their numbers again and again for investors, ENISA and CDTI applications, and the board. Cash forecasts are unreliable. ENISA asks for filed annual accounts, and audited ones for loans above €300k.

### STA-20: Paperwork behind the angel tax deduction (company certificates, holding windows, misinformation)
- **Who:** pre-seed and seed startups raising from Spanish business angels, and the angels themselves.
- **Where:** national state income tax. Regional top-up deductions and the provincial (foral) regimes were not checked.
- **Day-to-day reality:** for each angel, the company must issue a certificate confirming the requirements are met. The shares must be bought at incorporation or in a capital increase within 5 years (7 for certified startups) and held for more than 3 and less than 12 years. Contradictory information circulates online, for example a €250k limit instead of €100k.
- **Evidence:**
  - 50% deduction, €100k base, the holding window, and "certificación expedida por la entidad" (a certificate issued by the company) — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-21739).

- **Scale:** not quantified. Inference: most early rounds with local angels.
- **Cost/impact:** administrative work, plus a risk that the tax agency withdraws the benefit if requirements lapse (Venza).
- **Driver & status:** Ley 28/2022 final provision 3 (art. 68.1 LIRPF), **in force** since 1 Jan 2023.
- **How it is solved today:** lawyers and gestorías draft the certificates (no price data).
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes or partly. Certificates and investor tax packs generated from the cap table, with tracking of the holding windows.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low. The pain is inferred from the legal requirement; I found no complaint data.

## Top pains
Ranked by severity times breadth, with software fit noted.
1. **STA-03 Seed to Series A step.** Seed fell three years in a row to 2025; Series A is flat at 15 deals in H1 2026; megarounds take 58% of capital. This is the most consequential pain, but software only helps with investor readiness.
2. **STA-04 Public funding stack (ENISA, CDTI, EIC).** It reaches thousands of firms (7,800 ENISA borrowers). Money takes 3–9 months, the own-funds rule is the top reason for refusal and the lines keep changing. Founders pay advisers, and software can automate checks and paperwork.
3. **STA-06 Day-one fixed costs and filings.** This affects every new founder and is the loudest community complaint ("atraco a mano armada"). Software only reduces the hours; the money is policy, and it overlaps with tax and labour.
4. **STA-01 Narrow, expiring certification.** Only about 2,046 firms are currently certified against 5,000+ startups, and 19% of certifications are already lost. Investors and public calls increasingly ask for it. Eligibility and compliance monitoring is a clear software niche.
5. **STA-12 Talent cost and remote competition.** AI and data salaries are rising, about 70% of candidates want fully remote work and on-site roles cost at least 20% more. It touches every scaling startup.
6. **STA-07 and STA-08 Formalities for every round and foreign investors.** 376 rounds in 2025 each need a notary, the registry and often foreign tax numbers. The process is verified but its costs are not, and the work is largely software-shaped around the cap table.
7. **STA-09 and STA-10 Immigration for founders and hires.** 28% of Barcelona-area startups have a foreign founder, the UGE is stricter in 2026 and residence-card queues are reported in Madrid, Barcelona and Valencia.
8. **STA-15 AI Act compliance.** Up to 30% of startups work with AI, transparency rules apply from Aug 2026 and high-risk duties from Dec 2027 or Aug 2028. It is clearly software-solvable, though the deadline status is only partly verified.

## Noise and dead ends
- **"ENISA certification delays."** Weak evidence. Average processing was 1–1.5 months in 2024, 91% of users would recommend the process, and silence counts as approval after 3 months (BOE art. 4.2). The real pain is eligibility and losing the status, not speed. The claim that silence means rejection (Legalitas, 22 Apr 2025) contradicts the BOE.
- **"15% corporate tax for four years" as a headline benefit.** Misleading. It only applies from the first profitable year while the firm is still certified, so it rarely helps loss-making startups (inference; no usage data). It is not a pain a product can fix.
- **Angel deduction "€250k limit"** (Venza, Jun 2025). Wrong: the BOE says €100k a year. Treat it as noise, though it shows how much misinformation founders face (STA-20).
- **Incorporation time and cost.** Largely solved cheaply: CIRCE takes about 24 hours, fees are €60 and €40 on the standard route, and packaged services cost €130–€610 and promise 4 days. The remaining pain is the recurring corporate acts of each funding round (STA-07), not incorporation itself.
- **Golden visa.** Repealed in April 2025 (Economía 3), so it is a dead end for founders; the entrepreneur residence route remains (STA-09).
- **Disputes between co-founders as a cause of failure.** Weak evidence. In GEM, "incidente con socios" explains only 0.7% of closures among all entrepreneurs. No startup-specific data was found.
- **Fear of failure.** High in GEM (Basque Country 54.2%, Valencia 52.8%, Andalusia 45.6%), but it is cultural and not software-solvable.
- **Gender funding gap (STA-22).** Well evidenced but not software-solvable.
