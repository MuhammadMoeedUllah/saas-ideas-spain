# Pain points of Spanish hospitality, food-service, tourism and short-term-rental SMEs and autónomos (2024–2026)

*Data-capture notes compiled 23 Oct 2026. Method: web searches until the shared per-turn search budget ran out part-way through, then direct fetches of official texts (BOE, BOPV), ministry and association press releases (Consumo, CEHAT, Exceltur), trade and regional press, and vendor pricing pages. Citations marked "(search summary)" mean only the search engine's summary of the page was seen, not the page itself. Vendor blogs (Chekin, net2rent, Qamarero, Fooderise) have a commercial interest and are flagged where they are the only source. Lobby estimates (Fevitur, Apartur, Aptur CV, CEHAT) are labelled as such. "Single source" means the claim was seen in only one outlet. Other researchers cover labour/payroll, tax/invoicing, licensing, finance and cross-sector compliance, so those topics are only mentioned briefly here.*

## Key question 1 — Restaurants, bars and cafés: staffing, costs, no-shows, delivery, reservations/POS, terraces, noise, reviews, allergens, APPCC and the food-waste law

### Takeaway
The most severe and widespread restaurant pains are structural: chronic staff shortages with 40–60% annual turnover and rising absenteeism, and a margin squeeze from energy, supply, rent and financing costs that is now showing up as falling turnover and forecast closures in Mallorca. Third-party channels take a large cut: delivery commissions run 15–35% (up to 35–42% all-in), and no-show protection is paywalled. On top of this sits a growing compliance layer: APPCC records, allergen information, food-waste duties applying from April 2026, the Madrid terrace ordinance in force from January 2026, and a terrace smoking ban that is in Congress but not yet in force. Many of the tool categories (reservations, POS, delivery integration) are already crowded; the gaps are in compliance evidence, cost control and staffing.

### Cited Findings
- Sector size: more than 300,000 companies, 1.85 million jobs and €166,211M turnover (6.7% of GDP in value added); restaurants, bars, cafés and pubs alone employ 1.39 million and turn over about €116,200M — [Hostelería de España website, undated figures, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.hosteleriadeespana.es/)
- Q3 2026 cost increases for tourism firms: oil-linked costs +7.3%, energy +6.9%, other supplies +6.2%, labour costs "cercano al 5,0%, con el absentismo", and interest rates up 25%. 39.1% of tourism firms report worse results and 30.6% better (balance −8.5) — [Exceltur, Perspectivas Turísticas nº98, Oct 2026](https://www.exceltur.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/EXCELTUR-Informe-Perspectivas-N98-Balance-del-IIIT26-y-previsiones-para-IVT26.pdf)

### HOS-01: Staff shortages, 40–60% turnover and rising absenteeism
- **Who:** Bars, restaurants, cafés and hotels of all sizes, from autónomo to medium. Micro and small firms are least able to pay premiums to attract staff.
- **Where:** National. Most acute in highly seasonal coastal and island destinations (Baleares, Canarias, Costa del Sol). A staffing startup is piloting in A Coruña.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Owners cannot fill camarero de sala, cocinero, ayudante de cocina, barra, jefe de cocina and encargado roles. With 40–60% annual rotation they are always recruiting and training, owners often cover shifts themselves, and absenteeism leaves shifts uncovered at short notice.
- **Evidence:**
  - Hostelería accounts for 13.37% of all hard-to-fill vacancies in Spain (SEPE Observatorio de las Ocupaciones), "sólo por detrás de algunos sectores administrativos y sanitarios". The six hardest profiles are those listed above — [El Debate, 26 Jul 2026](https://www.eldebate.com/espana/galicia/20260726/empresa-gallega-encuentra-solucion-cubrir-vacantes-hosteleria-forma-rapida_443319_amp.html) (single source for the 13.37% figure).
  - Annual rotation is 40–60% in traditional establishments and above 70% in very seasonal businesses. The source notes "No existe una estadística oficial única" — [El Debate, 26 Jul 2026](https://www.eldebate.com/espana/galicia/20260726/empresa-gallega-encuentra-solucion-cubrir-vacantes-hosteleria-forma-rapida_443319_amp.html) (sector estimates, single source).
  - Absenteeism in tourism firms rose to 7.7% of agreed hours in summer 2026, up from 6.6% in June. Its total cost equals 3.8% of turnover. Exceltur lists "escasez de talento y aumento del absentismo" among the adverse factors of summer 2026 — [Exceltur nº98, Oct 2026](https://www.exceltur.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/EXCELTUR-Informe-Perspectivas-N98-Balance-del-IIIT26-y-previsiones-para-IVT26.pdf)
  - Tourism employment rose by 105,160 Social Security affiliates year on year. Wages rose 3.5% in hostelería against 3.0% in the rest of the economy, and 85.6% of new tourism contracts in Sep 2026 were permanent (indefinidos) — [Exceltur nº98, Oct 2026](https://www.exceltur.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/EXCELTUR-Informe-Perspectivas-N98-Balance-del-IIIT26-y-previsiones-para-IVT26.pdf)
  - In 2023, Hostelería de España president José Luis Yzuel said "Faltan unos 100.000 trabajadores" ("about 100,000 workers are missing") — [Valencia Plaza (search summary; 2023 quote)](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/restauracion-faltan-100000-empleados-por-que-ocurre-esto-espana)
  - Mallorca restaurateurs cite "high staff turnover" alongside soaring rents and no-shows — [Majorca Daily Bulletin, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.majorcadailybulletin.com/news/local/2026/08/17/145039/mallorca-bars-and-restaurants-suffer-per-cent-drop-turnover-during-the-first-half-august.html)
- **Scale:** 1.85 million jobs and more than 300,000 companies — [Hostelería de España](https://www.hosteleriadeespana.es/)
- **Cost/impact:** Absenteeism alone costs about 3.8% of turnover (Exceltur). I found no source for the cost per departure (recruiting, training, lost productivity).
- **Driver & status:** A tight labour market, unsocial hours and seasonality. On collective bargaining, unions and employers signed a modification of the VI ALEH (state labour agreement for hospitality) on 13 Apr 2026, covering LGTBI protocols, climate-risk prevention and a hearing before disciplinary dismissal — [CEHAT, 14 Apr 2026](https://cehat.com/notaprensa/sindicatos-y-patronales-dan-un-paso-decisivo-para-la-hosteleria-con-la-firma-del-nuevo-acuerdo-estatal-aleh/). Status: in force. Convenio wages and time recording belong to the labour researcher.
- **How it is solved today:** Job portals, temp agencies (ETTs) and word of mouth. New on-demand matching apps are appearing, such as Rapitalento (A Coruña, launched mid-2026; requests by web or WhatsApp voice note, AI matching, free for workers, establishments pay a monthly or quarterly subscription whose price is not disclosed) — [El Debate, 26 Jul 2026](https://www.eldebate.com/espana/galicia/20260726/empresa-gallega-encuentra-solucion-cubrir-vacantes-hosteleria-forma-rapida_443319_amp.html). I verified no prices for scheduling or absence-management tools.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Staffing marketplaces, shift planning and absence prediction reduce friction, but pay, hours and housing (HOS-26) drive the shortage.
- **Confidence:** High that the pain is severe and widespread; medium on the exact magnitudes.

### HOS-02: No-shows and the cost of guaranteeing reservations
- **Who:** Reservation-driven restaurants, mostly micro and small, in cities and tourist areas.
- **Where:** National. 2025 no-show rates: Alicante 3.1%, Valencia 3.2%, Madrid 3.3%, Málaga 3.3%, Barcelona 3.5%, Sevilla 3.7%. Mallorca restaurateurs list no-shows among their pressures.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Tables are held for parties who never arrive, especially at peak services. To protect themselves, restaurants increasingly ask for a card guarantee or prepayment, which creates friction with customers and pushes them onto paid software tiers.
- **Evidence:**
  - TheFork data: the national no-show rate was 3.3% in 2025, down from 3.6% in 2024. 21% of restaurants use a card guarantee (huella bancaria), double the 2023 share, and 7% use card prepayment, triple the share of two years earlier. TheFork removes users with four no-shows in 12 months; 2% of users were removed in 2025 — [Majorca Daily Bulletin, 28 Feb 2026](https://www.majorcadailybulletin.com/news/local/2026/02/28/140559/spanish-restaurants-take-stand-against-shows-balearic-sector-struggled-last-year.html); same figures in [Vinetur, 24 Feb 2026 (search summary)](https://www.vinetur.com/2026022496663/la-restauracion-espanola-reduce-la-tasa-de-no-show-al-33-en-2025-segun-thefork.html). Both reports rest on the same TheFork data, so they are not independent.
  - In a 2025 TheFork survey of diners who missed a booking without cancelling, 55% said they forgot, 38% had a last-minute problem and 7% were embarrassed to call — [Vinetur (search summary)](https://www.vinetur.com/2026022496663/la-restauracion-espanola-reduce-la-tasa-de-no-show-al-33-en-2025-segun-thefork.html)
  - With an average spend of €27 per diner and 2.9 diners per booking, each no-show table loses about €78.30 of potential revenue. A restaurant taking 500 bookings a month would lose more than €15,500 a year (revenue, not profit) — [ESdiario (search summary; date unclear)](https://www.esdiario.com/economia/260708/192740/coste-reservas-fantasma-restaurantes.html)
  - A claim that no-shows cost affected establishments 5–20% gives no methodology — [Vinetur, 12 Aug 2025 (search summary)](https://www.vinetur.com/2025081290379/las-reservas-fantasma-afectan-a-los-restaurantes-espanoles-pese-a-la-reduccion-de-no-shows-este-verano.html) (low reliability).
- **Scale:** The number of restaurants that take bookings in Spain was not found.
- **Cost/impact:** About €78 of potential revenue per no-show table, and at least €15,500 a year for a restaurant taking 500 bookings a month (ESdiario estimate).
- **Driver & status:** A market and behavioural problem. I found no Spanish rule specifically governing no-show penalties (gap).
- **How it is solved today:** Prices from a comparison written by a competing vendor (Qamarero), updated 22 Oct 2026 — [Qamarero](https://qamarero.com/blog/mejores-software-reservas-restaurantes/):
  - TheFork Manager: price on quote; the "garantías" features are only in the Performance plan.
  - CoverManager: per-venue price not published; three months free for new restaurants signing by 31 Oct 2026.
  - Restoo: Mini €39, Pro €79, Premium €149, Gold €249 per month excl. VAT; prepayments only in Premium.
  - resmio: €0, €69.90 or €129.90 per month plus VAT, with a 12-month lock-in or €10/month extra for monthly cancellation.
  - OpenTable: Core €149 or Pro €299 per month, plus €1.50 per seated diner who comes through its network.
  - Last.app "The Book": a basic version is included with its POS plans.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes, through deposits, card pre-authorisation, reminders and risk scoring. But the market is crowded and the rate is already falling, so the opportunity is mainly cheaper or bundled protection.
- **Confidence:** High on the rates; low to medium on the losses.

### HOS-03: Delivery-platform commissions (Glovo, Uber Eats, Just Eat)
- **Who:** Restaurants, pizzerias, fast-casual outlets and dark kitchens, mostly micro and small.
- **Where:** National, concentrated in cities (Madrid, Barcelona, Valencia, Sevilla, Málaga, Zaragoza, Bilbao).
- **Day-to-day reality:** The platform takes 15–35% of each order, plus monthly fees, marketing fees and shared promotions, and pays out every two weeks or monthly. Restaurants either raise menu prices on the apps or absorb the margin loss.
- **Evidence:**
  - Uber Eats charges 30% + VAT on its Básico plan, 25% with a 6-month lock-in (Plus) and 15–20% with a 12-month lock-in (Premium). Glovo charges 15–35% + VAT depending on zone and demand, plus €39/month for the tablet and support and 2–5% for marketing. Just Eat charges 13% + VAT if the restaurant delivers itself, or 25–35% + VAT plus €0.30 per order if Just Eat delivers — [Qamarero, 30 Sep 2025](https://qamarero.com/blog/comisiones-delivery-cuanto-cobran-realmente-las-plataformas/). This is a vendor blog that cites no sources.
  - Glovo plan tiers: Marketplace 15–18%, Standard 25–28%, Premium 28–30%, Top 30–35%. Estimated all-in cost is 35–42% of revenue once VAT on the commission, about 1.5% service charges, shared promotions (for example 25% flash discounts) and refunds or disputes (1–2% of turnover) are included. Payouts are monthly — [Fooderise, 10 May 2026](https://www.fooderise.com/en/blog/integrer-glovo-restaurant-fonctionnement-commission-2026). This source is generic and multi-country, not Spain-specific.
  - Payout timing conflicts: another guide says Glovo settles every 14 days — [Qamarero Glovo guide (search summary)](https://qamarero.com/blog/cuanto-cobra-glovo-restaurantes/)
- **Scale:** One claim says 78% of Spanish restaurants see commissions as their main delivery challenge — [Qamarero, 30 Sep 2025](https://qamarero.com/blog/comisiones-delivery-cuanto-cobran-realmente-las-plataformas/). It is unsourced and has low reliability.
- **Cost/impact:** 15–35% commission per order, or 35–42% of delivery revenue all-in (Fooderise estimate).
- **Driver & status:** A concentrated market. I found no Spanish or CNMC regulation of commission levels in 2025–2026 (gap). The Ley Rider employment model belongs to the labour researcher.
- **How it is solved today:**
  - Own online ordering plus aggregator integrators. Last.app plans include a delivery integrator covering Glovo, Uber Eats and Just Eat, with 100, 550 or unlimited orders included and extra orders at €0.25 or €0.19 each. Its online store costs up to €40/month plus 4% and €0.20 per sale, and QR ordering €25/month — [Last.app pricing, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.last.app/precios)
  - Negotiated rates for high-volume chains, according to older press (search summary of a Xataka article, undated).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Direct ordering, CRM and loyalty tools can move repeat customers off the platforms, but the platforms keep control of demand aggregation.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The figures come from vendors and I found no independent 2025–26 data.

### HOS-04: Margin squeeze from energy, supplies, rent and financing, with falling turnover and forecast closures in Mallorca
- **Who:** Bars, cafés and restaurants (micro and small) and leisure firms. Hotels are faring better.
- **Where:** National. Most acute in Mallorca and the Baleares in 2025–2026.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Costs keep rising and prices cannot follow them fully. In island destinations tourists are spending less on eating out: owners call them "sandwich tourists", and all-inclusive hotels capture their meals.
- **Evidence:**
  - Tourism firms' costs in Q3 2026: oil-linked +7.3%, energy +6.9%, supplies +6.2%, labour about +5% and interest rates +25%. 39.1% of firms report worse results and 30.6% better. Hotels did best (53.1% improved; balance +29.3), while leisure firms had a balance of −6.5 — [Exceltur nº98, Oct 2026](https://www.exceltur.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/EXCELTUR-Informe-Perspectivas-N98-Balance-del-IIIT26-y-previsiones-para-IVT26.pdf)
  - Mallorca bars and restaurants saw turnover fall about 14% in the first half of August 2026, according to CAEB Restauración's Juanmi Ferrer. Causes cited include economic weakness in source markets such as Germany, all-inclusive hotels, heat, soaring rents, staff turnover and no-shows — [Majorca Daily Bulletin, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.majorcadailybulletin.com/news/local/2026/08/17/145039/mallorca-bars-and-restaurants-suffer-per-cent-drop-turnover-during-the-first-half-august.html)
  - Balearic restaurants lost 4.2% of their custom in summer 2025, the largest drop of any region, according to a Delectatech report — [Majorca Daily Bulletin, 28 Feb 2026](https://www.majorcadailybulletin.com/news/local/2026/02/28/140559/spanish-restaurants-take-stand-against-shows-balearic-sector-struggled-last-year.html)
  - Early-2026 reports predicted 500–600 restaurant closures in Mallorca, and said one in ten bars and cafés had closed over the last decade — [Majorca Daily Bulletin, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.majorcadailybulletin.com/news/local/2026/08/17/145039/mallorca-bars-and-restaurants-suffer-per-cent-drop-turnover-during-the-first-half-august.html) (single source; the original reports were not traced).
  - A Palma restaurant owner said they would advise against opening a restaurant this year (paraphrased) — [Majorca Daily Bulletin, 28 Feb 2026](https://www.majorcadailybulletin.com/news/local/2026/02/28/140559/spanish-restaurants-take-stand-against-shows-balearic-sector-struggled-last-year.html)
- **Scale:** The whole sector (more than 300,000 companies, per Hostelería de España).
- **Cost/impact:** Costs rising 6–7% a year against tourism sales growth of 4.1% in Q3 2026 (Exceltur). Mallorca turnover −14% in early August 2026, and 500–600 closures forecast.
- **Driver & status:** Market forces: energy and fuel prices (linked to the Middle East conflict), inflation, interest rates, rents and shifting tourist spending.
- **How it is solved today:** Recipe costing (escandallo), stock control and menu engineering inside POS systems, for example stock control in Last.app's Growth plan at €95/month (Last.app pricing). Energy brokers and group purchasing exist but their prices were not verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Cost control, procurement, dynamic menus and demand forecasting help, but software cannot change input prices.
- **Confidence:** Medium. I found no 2025–26 data on food-cost inflation specific to restaurants (gap).

### HOS-05: Terraces — restrictive municipal ordinances, permit risk and conflict with residents over noise
- **Who:** Bars, cafés and restaurants with terraces (micro and small).
- **Where:** Madrid (new ordinance in force since 1 Jan 2026) and Valencia (2022 terrace cut in Honduras and Blasco Ibáñez). Search results also show disputes in Cádiz, Alicante (La Explanada) and Zaragoza, but these were undated or older.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Terraces are a core revenue source, but ordinances cap their size by sidewalk width and limit noise and enclosures, sanctions are getting tougher, and residents' associations push for earlier closing. Disputes over terrace cuts end in protests and court cases.
- **Evidence:**
  - Madrid's new terraces and kiosks ordinance applies from 1 Jan 2026. The 2022 ordinance had been annulled by the TSJ de Madrid for formal defects. The new text limits space by sidewalk width, toughens sanctions, requires low-noise materials, does not allow activity above 45 dB from 23:00 (according to FRAVM's reading), and does not cut opening hours. Madrid has more than 6,600 authorised terraces, up 20.9% since 2019; Chamberí went from about 200 in 2013 to about 700 — [El Salto, 23 Nov 2025](https://www.elsaltodiario.com/comunidad-madrid/ordenanza-terrazas-almeida-un-traje-medida-hosteleria-segun-asociaciones-vecinales)
  - The ordinance is numbered "Ordenanza 7/2025" according to a search summary of an [Aparejadores Madrid document](https://www.aparejadoresmadrid.es/documents/d/aparejadores/gt884). The number was not checked in the BOCM.
  - Hostelería Madrid called the initial draft "claramente perjudicial para el sector, especialmente para las pequeñas y medianas empresas" ("clearly harmful to the sector, especially small and medium businesses"). The residents' federation FRAVM submitted 43 allegations and none were accepted; only 7 of 106 allegations were incorporated overall. A 2018 CERMI report found 56% of Madrid's terraces obstructed pedestrians — [El Salto, 23 Nov 2025](https://www.elsaltodiario.com/comunidad-madrid/ordenanza-terrazas-almeida-un-traje-medida-hosteleria-segun-asociaciones-vecinales)
  - Valencia (older context): a 2022 terrace cut affected 72 venues, which estimated losses of almost €200 a day, called the measure "injusta y arbitraria" and announced legal action. Citing council data, they said "apenas un 3% de las denuncias por ruido van vinculadas a la actividad de las terrazas" ("barely 3% of noise complaints are linked to terraces") — [Valencia Plaza, 2 Nov 2022](https://valenciaplaza.com/hosteleros-honduras-decretan-jornada-cierre-terrazas). This falls outside the 2024–26 window.
- **Scale:** More than 6,600 authorised terraces in Madrid alone. National figure not found.
- **Cost/impact:** About €200 a day per venue in lost revenue (Valencia 2022 claim). Fine amounts under the new Madrid ordinance were not reported.
- **Driver & status:** Municipal terrace and noise ordinances. Madrid's is in force since 1 Jan 2026. Acoustic protection zones (ZPAE/ZAS) in Madrid, Valencia (Ruzafa), Sevilla and San Sebastián were not verified (gap).
- **How it is solved today:** Architects and gestorías prepare terrace licence plans; costs were not found. I identified no specialised SaaS (gap).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Permit and renewal tracking, layout compliance and noise-monitoring evidence can be software, but the underlying decisions are political.
- **Confidence:** Medium for Madrid; low for other cities.

### HOS-06: Smoking and vaping ban on hospitality terraces (bill in Congress)
- **Who:** Bars, cafés, restaurants, beach clubs and hotels with terraces or pools; all sizes.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** If the bill passes, staff will have to stop customers smoking or vaping on terraces and owners must change their signage. The sector fears losing customers at terrace times.
- **Evidence:**
  - The Council of Ministers approved the bill amending Ley 28/2005 on 21 Jul 2026 and sent it to Congress, where parliamentary groups can table amendments; until it is passed the current rules apply — [INEAF](https://www.ineaf.es/tribuna/ley-antitabaco/) and [Nuevecuatrouno, 21 Jul 2026](https://nuevecuatrouno.com/2026/07/21/la-prohibicion-de-fumar-en-las-terrazas-supera-su-primer-gran-tramite/) (search summaries); confirmed by [Que.es, 25 Jul 2026](https://www.que.es/2026/07/25/ley-antitabaco-terrazas-playas/) ("el pasado martes").
  - The bill bans smoking and vaping on bar and restaurant terraces, beaches, pools, bus shelters and campuses, sets 15-metre buffers around schools and health centres, and treats e-cigarettes, nicotine pouches and shisha like tobacco. Fines for businesses range from €200 to €600,000, and businesses get two months to update signage after entry into force — [Que.es, 25 Jul 2026](https://www.que.es/2026/07/25/ley-antitabaco-terrazas-playas/)
  - The hospitality employers argue that a terrace ban would be an "excepción en Europa" ("an exception in Europe") — [Onda Vasca (search summary)](https://www.ondavasca.com/el-gobierno-amplia-la-prohibicion-de-fumar-a-terrazas-playas-y-piscinas/)
- **Scale:** Every establishment with a terrace (number not found).
- **Cost/impact:** Not quantified (gap). Fines of €200–€600,000 if the bill passes.
- **Driver & status:** Bill amending Ley 28/2005, approved by the Council of Ministers on 21 Jul 2026 and now in Congress. **Draft / not in force** as of Oct 2026.
- **How it is solved today:** Not applicable yet.
- **Software-solvable?:** No. This is a behavioural and regulatory change.
- **Confidence:** High on the status; low on the impact.

### HOS-07: Fake-review attacks and reputation extortion
- **Who:** Bars, restaurants and cafés, mostly micro and small, heavily dependent on Google and Tripadvisor ratings.
- **Where:** National. Courts in Madrid and Barcelona are cited.
- **Day-to-day reality:** A venue suddenly receives a burst of one-star reviews from faceless profiles, or an offer to buy positive reviews. Its rating and its visibility in Google's ranking drop before any complaint is reviewed, and the owner has little recourse.
- **Evidence:**
  - One bar received 20 negative reviews from faceless profiles in under an hour and its 4.8 rating "se desploma en tiempo real". Smear campaigns cost "apenas 200 euros" and "packs de odio" are sold by click farms using VPNs. Courts in Madrid and Barcelona have awarded up to €20,000 in compensation (no cases or dates given) — [Merca2, 28 Apr 2026](https://www.merca2.es/2026/04/28/resenas-bar-google-2373862/). This is an opinion-style piece with no primary sources.
  - A journalist quoted by Ara: restaurateurs have all received offers like "Tú me pagas 200 euros al mes y yo te hago veinte reseñas positivas en Tripadvisor" ("you pay me €200 a month and I write you twenty positive reviews") — [Ara (search summary; undated)](https://es.ara.cat/comer/lee-resenas-restaurante-alerta-hay-falsas_1_5373668.html)
  - Since the Omnibus Directive was transposed in May 2022, fake reviews count as a serious infraction, with fines of up to €100,000 — [Merca2, 28 Apr 2026](https://www.merca2.es/2026/04/28/resenas-bar-google-2373862/); also [Puromarketing (search summary)](https://www.puromarketing.com/141/215522/ciberextorsion-falsas-resenas-amenaza-creciente-para-reputacion-imagen-negocios-empresas). Not checked against the BOE.
  - In April 2025, PSOE and Sumar proposed letting companies request the removal of fake reviews; whether it passed is unknown — [Marketing4ecommerce (search summary)](https://marketing4ecommerce.net/empresas-eliminar-resenas-falsas-de-internet/)
- **Scale:** Not quantified (gap).
- **Cost/impact:** Attacks cost the attacker about €200; compensation of up to €20,000 has been awarded in court (single source).
- **Driver & status:** Platform ranking algorithms and click-farm markets. Unfair-practice rules (Omnibus transposition) in force since 2022. The 2025 removal proposal's status is unknown.
- **How it is solved today:** Google's "flag as inappropriate" option and its owner review-management tool, reports to the Policía Nacional or Guardia Civil cybercrime units, unfair-competition and right-to-honour lawsuits — [Merca2](https://www.merca2.es/2026/04/28/resenas-bar-google-2373862/). Reputation-management SaaS exists, but its prices were not verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Monitoring and anomaly alerts, certified evidence capture and takedown or legal-claim workflows are software, but removal depends on Google.
- **Confidence:** Low to medium; the evidence is mostly anecdotal.

### HOS-08: Food-waste law (Ley 1/2025) — leftovers, prevention plans and donation duties
- **Who:**
  - All restaurants and bars: free leftovers.
  - Chains and larger venues: prevention plans and donation agreements.
  - Micro firms (fewer than 10 staff and no more than €2M) and establishments of 1,300 m² or less are exempt from the plan and donation duties.
- **Where:** National. Sanctions are enforced by the autonomous communities, which may set higher thresholds.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Restaurants must tell diners they can take leftovers home free, and supply food-grade reusable or easily recyclable containers at their own cost. Larger operators must document a prevention plan, apply the priority hierarchy (donation first) and sign donation agreements.
- **Evidence:**
  - From the law itself — [BOE, Ley 1/2025 (published 2 Apr 2025)](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2025/04/01/1):
    - Art. 8: hospitality businesses must let customers take uneaten food at no extra cost (buffet libre excepted), say so clearly and visibly, preferably on the menu, and use food-grade, reusable or easily recyclable containers.
    - Art. 6.4: a prevention plan and donation agreements are required.
    - Art. 6.4(c): hospitality establishments of 1,300 m² or less are exempt, unless several premises under the same tax ID add up to more.
    - Art. 6.6: micro firms are excluded from all art. 6 duties.
  - Sanctions (art. 21): minor infractions up to €2,000 (or a warning), serious €2,001–€60,000, very serious €60,001–€500,000. Serious infractions include having no plan when one is required and deliberately destroying edible food (art. 20); very serious means repeating a serious infraction within two years — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2025/04/01/1); matching ranges in [Andersen alert, Apr 2026](https://es.andersen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Andersen-Ley-de-desperdicio-alimentario_alerta.pdf)
  - Timing: final provision 20 reads "La presente ley entrará en vigor el 2 de enero de 2025" ("this law enters into force on 2 January 2025"), a drafting oddity since the law was published in April. The mandatory art. 6 measures apply "transcurrido el plazo de un año desde la publicación" ("one year after publication"), so from about 3 Apr 2026 — [BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2025/04/01/1); [Andersen](https://es.andersen.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/04/Andersen-Ley-de-desperdicio-alimentario_alerta.pdf)
  - Press framing: "desde el 3 de abril de 2026" restaurants "deberán ofrecer las sobras de la comida en tápers gratis"; serious fines start at €2,001 — [El Español, 15 Apr 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/sociedad/20260415/entrado-vigor-multas-restaurantes-no-ofrezcan-tapers-gratis-sobras/1003744204930_0.html)
  - **Discrepancy:**
    - The one-year deferral in final provision 20 covers only art. 6. The leftovers duty in art. 8 has no deferral of its own, so it arguably applied earlier than April 2026 (my reading).
    - The list of infractions in art. 20 contains no specific infraction for failing to offer a container. Press stories about fines for "no tupper" appear overstated unless regional regimes add one.
- **Scale:** Every restaurant for the leftovers duty. The plan and donation duties hit mainly chains and large venues.
- **Cost/impact:** Container costs, which are not quantified. Fines of €2,001–€60,000 for a missing plan.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 1/2025. **In force**; art. 6 obligations apply from April 2026.
- **How it is solved today:** Hostelería de España runs a compliance help service at hosteleriacircular.es ([HdE website](https://www.hosteleriadeespana.es/)). Consultants and law firms advise larger operators. Surplus-food apps exist but were not verified.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Waste measurement, plan documentation and donation-traceability logs suit chains. For small restaurants the burden is mostly packaging cost.
- **Confidence:** High (official text).

### HOS-09: Food-safety self-control records (APPCC) and allergen information
- **Who:** Every bar, restaurant, café, hotel kitchen and caterer.
- **Where:** National. Inspections are run by regional health authorities.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Kitchens must keep temperature, cleaning, pest-control, traceability and allergen records up to date for health inspections. Allergen information for each dish must be available in writing, or given orally if it is backed by a written or electronic record. Every menu change means updating the allergen matrix.
- **Evidence:**
  - The legal basis for APPCC is Reglamento (CE) 852/2004 art. 5 ("Sí: necesitas crear, aplicar y mantener procedimientos basados en APPCC"), RD 1021/2022 art. 20 for bars and restaurants (which repealed RD 3484/2000), and RD 1086/2020 art. 30 on temperatures for prepared meals. Required records cover temperatures, cleaning, pests, maintenance, traceability (Reglamento 178/2002 arts. 18–19), allergen sheets, and frozen-food temperatures kept for at least one year (Reglamento 37/2005) — [Qamarero, updated 6 Oct 2026](https://qamarero.com/blog/appcc-hosteleria/) (vendor blog; the norms cited are real but were not each re-checked).
  - Allergens for non-prepacked food: RD 126/2015 art. 4.1(b) requires allergen information. Art. 6.2 requires it in writing on labels or signs. Art. 6.5(a) allows oral information if it is "registrada de forma escrita o electrónica" (recorded in writing or electronically) and easily accessible to staff, inspectors and customers. Art. 6.5(b) requires signs telling customers where the information is — [BOE, RD 126/2015](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2015/02/27/126)
  - Food-safety sanctions under Ley 17/2011 art. 52: minor up to €5,000, serious €5,001–€20,000, very serious €20,001–€600,000. Very serious cases can also lead to "cierre temporal del establecimiento... por un plazo máximo de cinco años" (temporary closure for up to five years) — [BOE, Ley 17/2011](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2011/07/05/17); the same range is quoted by a vendor blog — [Qamarero, Jul 2025](https://qamarero.com/blog/como-hacer-una-carta-con-alergenos-para-un-restaurante-o-bar/)
- **Scale:** Every food-service establishment (more than 300,000 hospitality companies).
- **Cost/impact:** Fine exposure of up to €600,000 plus closure. Actual inspection frequency and the number of fines imposed were not found (gap).
- **Driver & status:** Reglamento (CE) 852/2004, RD 1021/2022, RD 126/2015 and Ley 17/2011, all **in force**.
- **How it is solved today:** APPCC consultancies, which draft the plan and train staff (prices not verified); paper logbooks; digital APPCC apps and temperature sensors (prices not verified); allergen-menu builders inside POS and menu tools.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Digital logs with sensors, recipe-based allergen matrices and inspection-ready exports.
- **Confidence:** High on the legal duty; low on how often it is enforced.

### HOS-10: A costly, fragmented tech stack (POS, reservations, delivery integration) and moving fiscal-software deadlines
- **Who:** Bars, restaurants and cafés, from autónomo to small chain.
- **Where:** National. Basque Country (TicketBAI) and Navarra have their own fiscal-software regimes; that topic belongs to the tax researcher.
- **Day-to-day reality:** A typical venue pays separately for a POS, a reservation tool, a delivery integrator, a kitchen display and an online store, each priced per location, with setup fees and add-ons. Repeated postponements of Verifactu leave owners unsure whether and when to change their POS.
- **Evidence:**
  - Last.app charges €50 (Starter), €95 (Growth) or €175 (Unlimited) per location per month on annual billing, plus €500 setup. A kitchen display costs €35/month and extra delivery orders €0.19–0.25 each — [Last.app pricing, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.last.app/precios)
  - Reservation tools cost €39–€299/month, plus per-diner fees in some cases (see HOS-02) — [Qamarero, 22 Oct 2026](https://qamarero.com/blog/mejores-software-reservas-restaurantes/)
  - Verifactu: the previous deadlines were 1 Jan 2027 for companies and 1 Jul 2027 for autónomos. A postponement to October 2028 has been announced but the change was not yet published as of 5 Oct 2026, and "the obligation remains" — [Qamarero, 5 Oct 2026](https://qamarero.com/blog/verifactu-retraso-octubre-2028/) (vendor, single source; to be verified by the tax researcher).
- **Scale:** Whole sector.
- **Cost/impact:** From about €600 to more than €2,100 a year per location for the POS alone, before reservations, payment fees and hardware.
- **Driver & status:** A market problem, plus Verifactu (RD 1007/2023): postponement to Oct 2028 announced, but its formal publication was not verified.
- **How it is solved today:** All-in-one POS suites (Last.app, Qamarero, Revo, Ágora, Glop, Cuiner). Revo, Ágora and Glop pricing pages could not be retrieved (gap).
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. This is a software-cost and integration problem, but the market is crowded.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### HOS-11: Municipal waste fee (tasa de residuos) and the free tap-water duty (Ley 7/2022)
- **Who:** Bars and restaurants, which are heavy waste generators.
- **Where:** Set by each municipality.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Municipalities had to introduce a waste fee covering the full real cost of the service by 2025, so food-service premises face new or higher bills. Separately, restaurants must offer free tap water.
- **Evidence:** Ley 7/2022 art. 11.3 requires local entities to establish, "en el plazo de tres años", a "tasa... específica, diferenciada y no deficitaria" (a specific, separate fee that covers its cost) that enables pay-as-you-throw. Art. 18.3 obliges hostelería and restauración to offer "la posibilidad de consumo de agua no envasada de manera gratuita" (free non-bottled water). The law entered into force on 10 Apr 2022, so the fee deadline fell around Apr 2025 (my calculation) — [BOE, Ley 7/2022](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/l/2022/04/08/7)
- **Scale/Cost:** Increases in specific cities for hospitality premises were **not found** (gap).
- **Driver & status:** Ley 7/2022, **in force**; the fee deadline has passed.
- **How it is solved today:** Gestorías handle appeals against fee assessments (not verified).
- **Software-solvable?:** Mostly no. Waste measurement could reduce pay-as-you-throw bills.
- **Confidence:** Low on impact; high on the legal duty.

### Inferences
- Restaurant pain clusters into three layers: (1) structural labour and cost problems, which software can only mitigate; (2) channel tolls (delivery, reservations and reviews), which are crowded with tools; and (3) a growing compliance-evidence layer (APPCC, allergens, food waste, terraces and noise, and soon tobacco signage), where documentation and audit trails are software-friendly and fragmented.
- Restaurant losses are concentrated where tourism shifted toward all-inclusive hotels and low spending (Mallorca), which suggests destination-specific pain rather than a national collapse. National tourism sales still grew 4.1% in Q3 2026.
- No-show rates are falling (3.6% → 3.3%) as guarantees spread, so no-show tooling is maturing and probably not a greenfield opportunity.

- Inspection and fine counts for APPCC and allergens; prices of APPCC consultants and apps.

- TheFork's per-diner commission, CoverManager prices and Revo/Ágora/Glop prices were not published or could not be retrieved.
- Whether regional food-waste sanction regimes add a specific "no free container" infraction.
- Status of the 2025 PSOE–Sumar fake-review removal proposal.

## Key question 2 — Hotels and tourist accommodation: SES.Hospedajes, Registro Único, tourist taxes, OTA commissions and parity, PMS and channel-manager costs

### Takeaway
Every lodging business, from a single tourist flat (VUT) to a hotel, now has to file per-guest data within 24 hours under SES.Hospedajes, with up to about 43 fields including payment data. The decree is unamended, Brussels opened an infringement case against Spain in June 2026, and fines run up to €30,000. The national short-term-rental registry (NRUA) took about 467,000 applications at €27.05 each and revoked about 20% of them before the Supreme Court annulled its procedure in May 2026, leaving operators in a regional-code patchwork under the EU STR Regulation and arguing over refunds. Tourist taxes are multiplying: Catalonia doubled its rates in April 2026, the Basque territories start in January 2027, and Galician cities have added their own. Booking.com's dominance (70–90% share; a €413M CNMC fine; more than 1,700 hotels in civil claims) and Airbnb's move to a 15.5% host-only fee keep distribution costs high.

### Cited Findings
- Booking says it works with "casi 200.000 hoteles y alojamientos en España" (almost 200,000 hotels and accommodations in Spain) — [Forbes, 19 Sep 2024](https://forbes.es/turismo/502788/la-cnmc-ve-un-impacto-positivo-de-la-multa-historica-de-413-millones-a-booking-para-el-tejido-empresarial/)
- 341,001 tourist homes were listed on platforms in May 2026, 40,836 (10.7%) fewer than a year earlier, with 1.71 million bed places (INE experimental statistics) — [idealista, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/06/24/903471-estos-son-los-municipios-con-mas-viviendas-turisticas-en-espana)
- Hotels are doing comparatively well: RevPAR +6% in April–May 2026, occupancy 69%, and direct-channel revenue +19% year on year for summer — [CEHAT/PwC, 1 Jul 2026](https://cehat.com/notaprensa/el-sector-hotelero-mantiene-unas-perspectivas-favorables-para-verano-y-otono-impulsado-por-la-fortaleza-de-la-demanda-turistica/)

### HOS-12: SES.Hospedajes traveller-data reporting (RD 933/2021)
- **Who:** Hotels, hostales, hostels, campsites, tourist apartments, VUT owners and property managers. Non-professional hosts must still report even though they are exempt from keeping the register. Micro and small businesses dominate.
- **Where:** National, except Catalonia (Mossos d'Esquadra) and the Basque Country (Ertzaintza), which run their own reporting systems.
- **Day-to-day reality:** For every booking, cancellation and check-in, the operator must send a report (parte) within 24 hours. Each one carries guest identity, contact and address details, kinship for minors, contract data and payment data. Guests over 14 must sign. Records must be kept for 3 years. A platform outage does not extend the deadline, and small errors such as a missing document support number get the report rejected.
- **Evidence:**
  - From the decree — [BOE, RD 933/2021](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2021/10/26/933):
    - Entry into force 27 Apr 2022.
    - Art. 6.3: data must be sent "de manera inmediata, y en todo caso en un plazo no superior a 24 horas" (immediately, and within 24 hours at most).
    - Art. 5.3: retention for "tres años".
    - Art. 4.2: every guest "mayor de catorce años" (over 14) must sign.
    - Annex I.A (professional lodging): about 43 data fields, including 5 payment fields (type, payment-method identification, holder, card expiry date, payment date). This is my tool-assisted count.
  - Use of the SES.Hospedajes platform has been mandatory since 2 Dec 2024. Other operational rules — [net2rent guide, published 11 Dec 2025, updated 18 Sep 2026](https://net2rent.com/guia-completa-ses-hospedajes/) (PMS vendor):
    - The 24-hour clock is triggered by the booking, its cancellation and the check-in, and "Una caída de la plataforma no amplía el plazo" (an outage does not extend the deadline).
    - Common errors: the document support number (número de soporte) is missing, or the operator did not tick "web service" at enrolment, which forces re-registration establishment by establishment.
    - Old Hospederías/WebPol credentials no longer work, and acknowledgement receipts must be kept for inspections.
  - The number of data points rose "de nueve a 42" (from nine to 42). The sector called it "inaceptable" and studied legal action. Fines of €30,000 are mentioned — [Economist & Jurist, 2 Dec 2024](https://www.economistjurist.es/actualidad-juridica/entra-en-vigor-el-registro-de-viajeros-que-incrementa-de-nueve-a-42-los-datos-que-la-industria-hotelera-recurrira-en-los-tribunales/)
  - Fines under LO 4/2015 (citizen-security law): minor €100–€600 per report (incorrect, incomplete or late data); serious €601–€30,000 (failing to register as a provider or to communicate, or giving false data). Inspections are "cada vez más habituales" (increasingly common), a claim with no source given — [net2rent](https://net2rent.com/guia-completa-ses-hospedajes/); same ranges in [Chekin (search summary)](https://chekin.com/blog/registro-de-viajeros-ses-hospedajes-2025/)
  - **Conflict on payment data:** one vendor FAQ says the card number, IBAN and expiry date are not mandatory and only the payment method must be given — [Chekin, 12 Mar 2025](https://chekin.com/blog/preguntas-frecuentes-sobre-ses-hospedajes/). But Annex I.A lists the card expiry date ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/rd/2021/10/26/933)), and net2rent says the data-protection authority (AEPD) reportedly considers the last four card digits proportionate. Operators face ambiguous guidance.
  - Data-minimisation conflict: the AEPD fined €2,000 for scanning guests' ID cards — [Cuatrecasas, Nov 2023 (search summary)](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/spain/propiedad-intelectual/art/registro-datos-personales-establecimientos-hoteleros)
  - The European Commission announced infringement procedure INFR(2026)4005 against Spain on 4 Jun 2026, citing proportionality, data minimisation and the three-year retention ("desproporcionado"). CEHAT is demanding that RD 933/2021 be repealed and a dialogue table set up — [CEHAT, 5 Jun 2026](https://cehat.com/notaprensa/cehat-recoge-el-aval-de-bruselas-el-expediente-sancionador-a-espana-por-el-registro-de-viajeros-confirma-que-la-norma-era-inaplicable-y-vulneraba-el-derecho-europeo/). The stage of the procedure was not stated; this is a single source and an interested party.
  - The BOE consolidated text shows no amendments or annulments (latest version 27 Oct 2021) — [BOE consolidated text](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2021-17461)
  - A Zaragoza council motion (filed by Vox) says the sector rejected the rule unanimously, that the sanctions are disproportionate, and that 95% of those affected are pymes and micropymes — [Ayuntamiento de Zaragoza, VOX motion (search summary; undated; partisan)](https://www.zaragoza.es/cont/paginas/organizacion/plenos/mociones/VOX-10519.pdf)
- **Scale:** About 341,000 listed tourist homes (INE, May 2026) plus hotels, hostels and campsites; Booking alone lists about 200,000 hotels and accommodations in Spain.
- **Cost/impact:** €100–€600 per faulty report and up to €30,000 for serious breaches. Staff minutes per check-in and data-security liability were not quantified (gap).
- **Driver & status:** RD 933/2021 and LO 4/2015. **In force and unamended** as of Oct 2026. EU infringement INFR(2026)4005 opened in June 2026; its outcome is pending. I could not verify a Supreme Court ruling on the industry's challenge (gap).
- **How it is solved today:**
  - Manual web form, signed in with a digital certificate, DNIe or Cl@ve.
  - PMS web-service integrations (net2rent; Smoobu includes online check-in in plans from €29 to €55 a month for the first unit, plus €9.60 per additional unit).
  - Dedicated check-in and compliance tools such as Chekin, from €3.95/€5.95/€7.95 per property per month. Annual billing saves up to 20%, minimum unit counts are stated inconsistently (3, 10 or 20), and deposits cost 1.5% + €0.30 — [Chekin pricing, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://chekin.com/en/pricing/)
  - Documented complaints: outages, the 24-hour clock, lost credentials, re-enrolment and email-only support (net2rent).
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Data capture, validation, transmission, retention and audit trail can all be automated, but the space is already crowded (Chekin and PMS vendors), so differentiation would be price, reliability and multi-system coverage (SES, Mossos, Ertzaintza).
- **Confidence:** High.

### HOS-13: The short-term-rental registry (Registro Único de Arrendamientos, NRUA, RD 1312/2024) — fees, revocations, delistings, annulment and refund claims
- **Who:** VUT owners (autónomos and private owners), property managers, owners of seasonal and room rentals, and platforms.
- **Where:** National. Revocations were concentrated in Andalucía, Canarias, Cataluña and the Comunitat Valenciana. Cities with the most revoked applications: Sevilla, Marbella, Barcelona, Málaga, Madrid, Benalmádena, Adeje, Valencia, Torrevieja, Fuengirola.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Since 1 Jul 2025 owners have needed a €27 code per unit and per rental type to advertise. The application portal (registradores.org) failed at launch, about one in five applications was revoked (missing licence, no owners'-community approval, protected housing), and platforms delisted units. After the Supreme Court annulled the procedure in May 2026, owners are back to regional codes and arguing over refunds and damages.
- **Evidence:**
  - Launch week: StayAlliance reported the portal "experimentó fallos persistentes que impidieron a numerosas empresas completar el procedimiento" (suffered persistent failures that stopped many businesses completing the procedure) and estimated that fewer than 50% of units had registered. Each code cost €27, so a unit used for both tourist and seasonal rental could need three codes (€81). Platforms had 48 hours to remove a listing once notified — [idealista, 4 Jul 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/07/04/851896-de-las-incidencias-a-las-denuncias-asi-ha-sido-el-estreno-del-registro-de)
  - Main reasons for revocation: no tourist licence, no 3/5 owners'-community approval, or the property is protected housing. As of 4 Jul 2025 there were 255,172 applications and 22,368 revocations — [idealista, 7 Jul 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/07/07/852282-los-motivos-que-provocan-mas-revocaciones-del-codigo-obligatorio-para-los)
  - In July–August 2025, 34,529 of 68,594 applications were revoked. Over the first eight months of 2025 there were 260,595 applications and 51,692 revocations — [Europa Press via Bolsamania, 2 Sep 2025](https://www.bolsamania.com/noticias/empresas/economia--el-nuevo-registro-de-alquileres-turisticos-obtuvo-68500-solicitudes-en-verano-y-la-mitad-fueron-rechazadas--20818271.html)
  - 53,876 revoked tourist-rental applications were notified to platforms for delisting. By city: Sevilla 2,289, Marbella 1,802, Barcelona 1,564, Málaga 1,471, Madrid 1,257. By region: Andalucía 16,740, Canarias 8,698, Cataluña 7,729, Comunitat Valenciana 7,499 — [idealista/Europa Press, 15 Sep 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/09/15/859916-el-gobierno-notifica-mas-de-53-500-pisos-turisticos-ilegales-para-que-se-retiren)
  - As of 8 Jan 2026: 400,362 applications, 299,754 approved and 84,250 revoked (about 21%). Tourist rentals were 72% of applications and temporary rentals 14.7%. Definitive codes by province: Málaga more than 36,000 (10,327 revoked), Alicante 35,916 (8,309 revoked), Barcelona 30,092, Las Palmas 20,962, Madrid 18,899, Santa Cruz de Tenerife 17,693, Baleares 16,672 — [idealista, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/01/20/879570-una-de-cada-cinco-viviendas-de-alquiler-de-corta-estancia-se-quedan-sin-el-codigo)
  - Final totals: more than 467,000 applications, about 373,600 approved and about 20% rejected. The base fee was €27.05 per application — [idealista, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/06/17/902074-se-avecinan-demandas-contra-el-registro-unico-de-alquileres-los-registradores-no)
  - **The annulment:**
    - Supreme Court ruling 620/2026 of 19 May 2026, on an appeal by the Generalitat Valenciana, annulled "los preceptos del Real Decreto impugnado referidos al procedimiento de registro único de arrendamientos" (the provisions on the single registration procedure) for lack of state competence. It was published in the BOE on 8 Jun 2026 — [Notarios y Registradores, 21 May 2026](https://www.notariosyregistradores.com/web/participa/noticias/el-ts-anula-registro-arrendamientos-corta-duracion/)
    - Ruling 629/2026 of 21 May 2026, on an appeal by Apartur and ATA (corrected by an order of 11 Jun 2026), annulled arts. 2(f) and (i), 5, 8, 9, 10 and 12(b), and the references to the procedure and the registration number in arts. 1, 2(j) and 6 — [BOE-A-2026-13893, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.boe.es/boe/dias/2026/06/26/pdfs/BOE-A-2026-13893.pdf)
  - Aftermath:
    - About 111,000 rejected homes were unblocked. Fevitur puts the average loss per affected owner at about €33,000 and possible collective claims above €160M (lobby estimate). The Ministry of Housing (MIVAU) asked the regions to "refuercen los controles" (tighten controls), and Andalucía asked for an analysis of compensation — [idealista, 22 May 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/05/22/898792-el-fin-del-registro-de-viviendas-de-corta-estancia-reabre-100-000-licencias)
    - Lawyers argue the nullity "sí tiene efectos retroactivos" (is retroactive), so fees should be refunded. The Colegio de Registradores says it "no tiene efectos retroactivos" and that existing codes "son válidos" (remain valid) — [idealista, 17 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/06/17/902074-se-avecinan-demandas-contra-el-registro-unico-de-alquileres-los-registradores-no)
    - Fee refunds can be claimed for unjust enrichment within one year of the ruling becoming final — [Chekin, 2 Jun 2026](https://chekin.com/blog/real-decreto-1312-2024/) (vendor).
  - **What remains:**
    - The Ventanilla Única Digital (the state data-exchange window) still operates. Platforms must still share monthly data under EU Regulation 2024/1028, applicable since 20 May 2026; platforms with fewer than 4,250 listings may report quarterly.
    - Listings now display regional codes (RTA, HUT, RTV, VUT, REAT, ETV, VV).
    - The annual informative return (Orden VAU 1560/2025) remains.
    - Spain has not adopted the state penalty regime the EU regulation requires — [Chekin, 2 Jun 2026](https://chekin.com/blog/real-decreto-1312-2024/) and [Chekin, 3 Jun 2026](https://chekin.com/blog/reglamento-ue-2024-1028/) (vendor; single source).
    - Exceltur says the annulment "urge un sistema alternativo eficaz y jurídicamente sólido de control de la oferta ilegal de VUT" (makes an effective, legally sound alternative system for controlling illegal tourist-flat supply urgent) — [Exceltur nº98, Oct 2026](https://www.exceltur.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/EXCELTUR-Informe-Perspectivas-N98-Balance-del-IIIT26-y-previsiones-para-IVT26.pdf)
- **Scale:** About 467,000 applications nationally; about 111,000 rejected homes.
- **Cost/impact:** €27.05 per code (×1–3 per unit); lost bookings during blocking (Fevitur: about €33,000 per affected owner, a lobby figure); possible €160M in claims.
- **Driver & status:** RD 1312/2024 is **partially annulled** (rulings of 19 and 21 May 2026). EU Regulation 2024/1028 has been **in force since 20 May 2026**, but the national penalty regime is pending. I found no replacement decree as of Oct 2026.
- **How it is solved today:** Gestorías and real-estate lawyers, whose fees were not found; PMS and channel tools that store and display the registration number (Smoobu, Chekin, net2rent); now also refund-claim lawyers.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Tracking regional codes against each platform listing, packaging EU 2024/1028 data, and automating refund claims are software tasks, but the regime itself is legal and political.
- **Confidence:** High.

### HOS-14: OTA dominance — Booking.com parity and commissions, the CNMC fine, hotel damages claims, and Airbnb's host-only fee
- **Who:** Independent and small or medium hotels, hostels, tourist apartments and VUT managers.
- **Where:** National. The CNMC complaint originated with the Madrid hotel association, per a search summary.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Hotels depend on Booking for a large share of their bookings, pay higher commissions for visibility programmes (Preferente, Genius), and until 2024 faced price-parity constraints. Contracts are English-only under Dutch law with Amsterdam courts. VUT managers using a PMS are being moved onto Airbnb's 15.5% host-only fee.
- **Evidence:**
  - On 30 Jul 2024 the CNMC fined Booking €413.24M (two fines of €206.62M) for abuse of dominance since 1 Jan 2019. The conduct sanctioned:
    - parity clauses;
    - unilateral price cuts;
    - opaque Preferente, Preferente Plus and Genius programmes;
    - English-only contracts under Dutch law with Amsterdam courts;
    - ranking by total bookings, which pushes hotels to concentrate sales on Booking.
    - Source: [Genbeta, 1 Aug 2024](https://www.genbeta.com/actualidad/booking-com-culpable-abuso-posicion-dominante-cnmc-impone-multa-record-413-millones-euros)
  - Booking's share of hotel OTA intermediation in Spain was 70–90% in 2019–2022, and Booking is appealing to the Audiencia Nacional — [Vozpópuli, 31 Jul 2024](https://www.vozpopuli.com/economia/empresas/multa-booking-reservas.html); the CNMC said the share was "cercana al 90% en algunos años" (close to 90% in some years) — [Forbes, 19 Sep 2024](https://forbes.es/turismo/502788/la-cnmc-ve-un-impacto-positivo-de-la-multa-historica-de-413-millones-a-booking-para-el-tejido-empresarial/)
  - Civil damages claims:
    - More than 1,700 hotels have joined an initiative run by the litigation firm Eskariam with the Asociación Española de Directores de Hoteles, and about 1,200 have supplied documents.
    - Total claim about €1,000M; the first lawsuit alone is above €300M. The claimed damage is 1.65% to just over 2% of turnover over the 20 years affected.
    - Parity clauses applied in Spain from 2004 until June 2024, when Booking dropped them after its DMA gatekeeper designation.
    - Filings were due in H1 2026, with first rulings expected between late 2027 and early 2028.
    - CEHAT signed a separate agreement with CSS Abogados in Nov 2024.
    - Source: [Vozpópuli, updated 30 Dec 2025](https://www.vozpopuli.com/legal/un-millar-de-hoteles-espanoles-buscara-recuperar-1000-millones-de-booking-en-pleitos-desde-2026.html)
  - Airbnb: under the split-fee model hosts pay 3% and guests 14.1–16.5%. The host-only fee is 15.5% for most hosts and is required for hotels and for hosts using property-management software, and Airbnb is moving all home hosts to it — [Airbnb Help Center, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.airbnb.com/help/article/1857)
  - Counter-trend: hotels' direct-channel revenue was up 19% year on year in summer 2026 — [CEHAT/PwC, 1 Jul 2026](https://cehat.com/notaprensa/el-sector-hotelero-mantiene-unas-perspectivas-favorables-para-verano-y-otono-impulsado-por-la-fortaleza-de-la-demanda-turistica/)
- **Scale:** Booking has about 200,000 hotel and accommodation partners in Spain; more than 1,700 hotels are in the damages claims.
- **Cost/impact:** Claimed overcharge of 1.65–2% of turnover (claimants' expert estimate). Typical 2025–26 Booking and Expedia commission percentages could not be verified (gap).
- **Driver & status:** The CNMC decision is **under appeal at the Audiencia Nacional**. A reported suspension of the fine appeared in a search summary that I could not trace to a dated source (gap). The DMA has applied to Booking since 2024. Civil claims are being filed in 2026.
- **How it is solved today:** Booking engines and metasearch (Cloudbeds says it adds no commission on its booking engine; it charges metasearch commission only after the stay), revenue-management tools, and litigation funders such as Eskariam.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Direct-booking stacks, CRM and loyalty tools reduce dependence; assembling evidence for claims (historical rate-parity and commission data) is also a software task.
- **Confidence:** High on the fine and the claims; medium on commission levels.

### HOS-15: Multiplying tourist taxes and the burden of collecting them (Catalonia doubled in 2026; Basque Country from 2027; Galician cities)
- **Who:** Hotels, hostels, campsites, VUT owners and managers. The establishment collects and pays the tax as "sustituto del contribuyente".
- **Where:**
  - Catalonia: Barcelona plus a regional rate elsewhere.
  - Basque Country: Gipuzkoa including San Sebastián, Bizkaia including Bilbao, and Álava.
  - Galicia: Santiago, A Coruña and Vigo.
  - Baleares: since 2016.
  - None in Madrid, Andalucía or Canarias. The Comunitat Valenciana's tax was repealed in Nov 2023.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Operators must charge each guest a per-night amount that varies by establishment type, municipality and season, apply exemptions (minors, long stays) and night caps, keep per-property records and file returns, quarterly in Gipuzkoa. Managers with portfolios across several regions face a different regime in each.
- **Evidence:**
  - Catalonia — Ley 2/2026 de 6 de marzo:
    - The Parlament approved the bill on 25 Feb 2026 with PSC, ERC and Comuns, effective 1 Apr 2026. The maximum Barcelona municipal surcharge rises from €4 to €8, and other councils can add up to €4 — [Forbes, 25 Feb 2026](https://forbes.es/economia/879865/el-parlament-aprueba-la-ley-que-incrementara-la-tasa-turistica-a-partir-del-1-de-abril/)
    - The Barcelona rate is roughly doubled, youth hostels get a new tariff, the rate in force at booking applies if the tax is paid then, the surcharge also applies to cruise stays of 12 hours or less, and 25% of revenue goes to housing policy — [Cuatrecasas, 2 Apr 2026](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/global/fiscalidad/art/cataluna-impuesto-estancias-establecimientos-turisticos)
    - Rates in the rest of Catalonia rise 50% from Apr 2026 and 100% from Apr 2027 — [net2rent (search summary)](https://net2rent.com/tasa-turistica-en-cataluna-que-es-cuanto-se-paga-y-como-gestionarla/)
    - Combined Barcelona rates: 5-star €11, 4-star €7.40, tourist flat €8.50, other establishments €6 — [DonPorqué, 30 Jul 2026](https://donporque.com/tasa-turistica-espana-2026/) (secondary source)
    - **Conflict:** Forbes gives both a Barcelona maximum of €4.50 until March 2027 and €6 after, and "7 € for the top category"; the exact figures must be read from the DOGC tables (images, not retrieved).
  - Gipuzkoa — Norma Foral 3/2026, de 12 de junio (BOPV nº119, 25 Jun 2026) — [BOPV](https://www.euskadi.eus/bopv2/datos/2026/06/2602836a.pdf):
    - Minimum–maximum rates per night: hotels 5-star €4.50–6.50, 4-star €3.50–5.50, 3-star €1.50–3.50, 2-star €1.00–2.50, 1-star €0.75–1.75; tourist apartments €1.50–3.50; campsites, rural houses and hostels €0.75–1.75; agroturismos €0.50–1.25; VUT, whole or by rooms, €4.50–6.50; cruises €6.00–7.00.
    - Charged for at most 6 nights per stay.
    - Municipal surcharge of up to 50% where a municipality has 750 or more bed places, or 5,000 or more residents with tourist intensity of 10% or more. A 100% rebate applies where there are 25 places or fewer.
    - The establishment files a quarterly self-assessment (autoliquidación).
    - **Takes effect 1 Jan 2027**; bookings made before 5 Feb 2026 are exempt.
  - Basque Country as a whole: all three territories start on 1 Jan 2027, after a summer-2026 start was dropped; municipalities have six months to adapt their ordinances — [Chekin, 10 Jun 2026](https://chekin.com/blog/tasa-turistica-pais-vasco/)
  - **Contradicted claim:** a report that San Sebastián already charged "hasta 7,50 euros por noche" in summer 2026 — [Moncloa.com, 10 Jun 2026 (search summary)](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/06/10/san-sebastian-donostia-tasa-3383636/) — is contradicted by the official text, which takes effect on 1 Jan 2027.
  - Galicia: Santiago and A Coruña have charged a voluntary municipal tax of €1–€2.50 since late 2025 — [DonPorqué, 30 Jul 2026](https://donporque.com/tasa-turistica-espana-2026/). Vigo starts in October; the year was not confirmed — [Xataka (search summary)](https://www.xataka.com/magnet/vigo-empieza-a-cobrar-tasa-turistica-octubre-importante-no-precio-sino-intencion)
  - Baleares (Impuesto de Turismo Sostenible), high season 1 May–31 Oct: 5-star €4, 4-star €3, 3-star and apartments €2 — [DonPorqué, 30 Jul 2026](https://donporque.com/tasa-turistica-espana-2026/) (single secondary source)
- **Scale:** Every lodging establishment in Catalonia, the Baleares, the Basque Country (from 2027) and the participating Galician municipalities. Catalonia had 51,300 listed tourist homes and the Baleares 21,304 in May 2026 (INE via [idealista](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/06/24/903471-estos-son-los-municipios-con-mas-viviendas-turisticas-en-espana)).
- **Cost/impact:** Rates of €1–€11 per person per night make price competition harder, and collection and filing add administrative work every quarter.
- **Driver & status:** Catalonia: Ley 2/2026, **in force from 1 Apr 2026**, with a second step on 1 Apr 2027. Gipuzkoa: Norma Foral 3/2026, **upcoming on 1 Jan 2027**; Bizkaia and Álava are aligned to the same date, but their norm numbers were not verified. Galicia: municipal ordinances, in force.
- **How it is solved today:** Chekin includes tourist-tax handling in all plans; PMS modules; gestorías file the returns.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. A multi-jurisdiction engine covering rates, exemptions, night caps, surcharges, collection at booking and return preparation.
- **Confidence:** High for Catalonia's status and Gipuzkoa; medium for exact Barcelona amounts.

### HOS-16: Costly, fragmented PMS, channel-manager and compliance subscriptions for small hotels and VUT managers
- **Who:** Small hotels, hostels and VUT managers with roughly 1–200 units.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Managers combine a PMS, a channel manager, online check-in and police reporting, tourist-tax handling, deposits, dynamic pricing and messaging. Most are priced per unit with add-ons and minimum commitments, and the larger PMS vendors publish no prices.
- **Evidence:**
  - Smoobu: the listed prices are per month and scale "por número de unidades seleccionadas" (by number of units selected): Flex €29 plus 0.9% per booking, Prepago €35 (€31.50 billed annually), Pro+ €55 (€49.50 annually). Each additional unit costs €9.60/month and extra write-access accounts €12/month. The FAQ quotes €28 for the first unit, which conflicts with the price table. Police reporting is not mentioned — [Smoobu pricing, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.smoobu.com/es/precios/)
  - Chekin: from €3.95/€5.95/€7.95 per property per month, with inconsistently stated minimums and transaction fees (1.5% + €0.30 on deposits; damage protection $2.50–$4.00) — [Chekin pricing](https://chekin.com/en/pricing/)
  - Mews is priced "on a per-room basis" on custom quotes, with payments, POS and add-ons extra — [Mews pricing](https://www.mews.com/en/pricing). Cloudbeds' Flex, One, Experience and Enterprise plans are all quote-only — [Cloudbeds pricing](https://www.cloudbeds.com/pricing/)
  - Lodgify's pricing page returned 403 and could not be read. Avantio, Hostaway and Avirato pricing was not retrieved (gap).
- **Scale:** About 341,000 listed tourist homes and about 200,000 Booking partners, most of them small operators.
- **Cost/impact:** Indicatively, a 10-unit manager on Smoobu Prepago pays about €35 + 9 × €9.60 ≈ €121/month, plus Chekin at €3.95–€7.95 per property (≈ €40–€80/month). That is roughly €1,950–€2,400 a year (my calculation; the pricing pages are ambiguous), before payment fees, dynamic-pricing tools or a hotel channel manager.
- **Driver & status:** A market problem, plus regulatory add-ons (SES, tourist taxes, registration numbers, EU data sharing).
- **How it is solved today:** Bundled PMSs with built-in compliance (net2rent, Smoobu) and point tools (Chekin).
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. This is pure software consolidation and pricing, but the market is crowded.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### HOS-17: Andalusia's new mandatory electronic complaints system (Hoj@)
- **Who:** Every business selling in Andalusia, including hotels, apartments, VUT and property managers. Its application to restaurants was not stated in the source.
- **Where:** Andalucía (Málaga, Sevilla, Granada, Cádiz, Costa del Sol).
- **Day-to-day reality:** Businesses must register on the Hoj@ platform, designate authorised people, display a QR poster, keep paper complaint forms and answer electronic complaints within 10 working days.
- **Evidence:** An Order of 24 Apr 2026 (BOJA nº82, 30 Apr 2026), implementing Decreto 82/2022, entered into force on 20 May 2026. Registration is mandatory by 20 Nov 2026 for large firms (more than 250 staff, turnover above €50M or more than 20 establishments in Andalucía) and by 20 May 2027 for everyone else. A complaint counts as received when opened or after 10 calendar days, and the business has 10 working days to reply. Failing to reply is an infraction under art. 71.8.3ª of Ley 13/2003 — [Chekin, 9 Jun 2026](https://chekin.com/blog/boja-andalucia-nuevo-sistema-hoj-de-quejas-y-reclamaciones/) (vendor, single source; the BOJA text was not checked).
- **Scale:** All Andalusian accommodation businesses (90,649 listed tourist homes in Andalucía in May 2026, INE via [idealista](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/06/24/903471-estos-son-los-municipios-con-mas-viviendas-turisticas-en-espana)).
- **Cost/impact:** Fine amounts not stated.
- **Driver & status:** Andalusian Order of 24 Apr 2026. **Upcoming deadlines** on 20 Nov 2026 and 20 May 2027. This overlaps with cross-sector consumer compliance.
- **How it is solved today:** Not yet solved; there is no evidence of tools.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Complaint inbox, deadline alerts and response templates, ideally integrated into a PMS or helpdesk.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low until checked against the BOJA.

### Inferences
- SES.Hospedajes and the NRUA show a recurring pattern: national data-reporting mandates arrive with poor portals and ambiguous guidance and are then challenged by courts or Brussels. Operators carry the operational risk throughout, which favours compliance tools that absorb regulatory change across several regimes (SES, Mossos, Ertzaintza, regional registries, EU 2024/1028, tourist taxes).
- With the NRUA annulled and no state penalty regime for EU 2024/1028, enforcement is moving to the regions and to the platforms themselves (Airbnb and Consell de Mallorca agreements). Managers will be policed by platform delisting algorithms as much as by inspectors.
- Hotels are not the most distressed segment (53% improved results; direct revenue +19%). The distress is concentrated in VUT owners and managers and in restaurants.

- Counts of SES.Hospedajes reports submitted, rejected and sanctioned.
- 2025–26 commission percentages for Booking and Expedia, and whether the Audiencia Nacional suspended the CNMC fine. One search summary said it did, but I could not trace it to a dated source.
- Exact Barcelona and Catalonia rates by establishment type (the DOGC tables were not retrieved), the Bizkaia and Álava norm numbers, and the start year of Vigo's tax.
- Lodgify, Avantio, Hostaway, Avirato and Cloudbeds prices.

## Key question 3 — Tourist-rental regulation by city (Barcelona, Madrid, Málaga, Sevilla, Valencia, Alicante, Palma/Baleares, Canarias) and knock-on effects

### Takeaway
City rules are tightening everywhere, but in different ways:
- Barcelona: all 10,101 HUT licences lapse in Nov 2028, backed by the Constitutional Court in 2025 and the Supreme Court in Mar 2025.
- Madrid: Plan Reside bans dispersed VUT in the historic centre, with escalating fines of €30,001, €60,001 and €100,001.
- Málaga: no new VUT in 43 barrios (approved 2024).
- Valencia and Alicante: moratoria.
- Palma: announced ban on new tourist rentals in any form.
- Canarias: a law capping vacation homes at 10% of residential buildable area.
- Nationwide: since April 2025 owners' communities can veto new VUT with a 3/5 majority.

Enforcement now also runs through the platforms: Airbnb was fined €64M and delisted 65,000 listings, and the Consell de Mallorca has its own delisting deal with Airbnb. Listed supply fell 10.7% year on year by May 2026. Knock-on effects on managers and cleaning firms are claimed by lobbies but weakly evidenced.

### Cited Findings
- Listed tourist homes in May 2026 (INE):
  - By municipality: Madrid 10,836; Málaga 8,288; Barcelona 8,231; Marbella 6,987; Sevilla 6,937; Valencia 5,393; Mijas 4,465; La Oliva 3,979; Arona 3,967.
  - By region: Andalucía 90,649 (−5,527); Cataluña 51,300 (−5,546); Comunitat Valenciana 51,268 (about −12,000, the largest fall); Canarias 48,356 (−2,330); Baleares 21,304 (−3,057).
  - Source: [idealista, 24 Jun 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/06/24/903471-estos-son-los-municipios-con-mas-viviendas-turisticas-en-espana)
- Policy pipeline (headlines only):
  - Podemos proposed a five-year moratorium on new tourist licences in cities over 200,000 people (9 Mar 2026).
  - Brussels' draft "Affordable Housing Act" would let municipalities limit tourist rentals (9 Sep 2026).
  - The EU housing commissioner warned of "hoteles en la sombra" (shadow hotels) (7 Oct 2026).
  - Source: [idealista tag page, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/etiquetas/normativa-alquiler-turistico)

### HOS-18: Barcelona — all 10,101 tourist-flat licences (HUT) lapse in November 2028
- **Who:** HUT owners (many autónomos), property managers, and cleaning, laundry and maintenance suppliers.
- **Where:** Barcelona city.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Licensed operators have a fixed end date. No new licences are issued, so they must plan exits, conversions or moves to other uses while paying a doubled tourist tax (HOS-15).
- **Evidence:**
  - Mayor Collboni announced in July 2024 that the licences of "los 10.101 apartamentos actualmente aprobados" would end by November 2028. The Supreme Court rejected an owners' appeal around 13 Mar 2025 — [Genbeta, 14 Mar 2025](https://www.genbeta.com/actualidad/barcelona-va-ganando-airbnb-booking-tribunal-supremo-apoya-que-prohiba-pisos-turisticos-hay-fecha-para-ello)
  - The Constitutional Court upheld the Catalan decree-law in 2025 (exact date not given). Collboni: "10.000 pisos turísticos se convertirán en viviendas residenciales" ("10,000 tourist flats will become homes"). Apartur says tourist flats are "apenas el 0,77% del parque residencial" (barely 0.77% of the housing stock) and that eliminating them would cost about 40% of the city's tourist accommodation capacity (lobby figure) — [Moncloa.com, 12 Jun 2026](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/06/12/barcelona-collboni-pisos-3384249/)
  - Mechanism: the Catalan decree-law requires municipalities to approve specific planning before 2028 for tourist flats to continue, and Barcelona will not do so — [Hosteltur (search summary)](https://www.hosteltur.com/165598_apartur-barcelona-prohibe-pisos-turisticos-pero-permitira-mas-hoteles.html)
- **Scale:** 10,101 licences; 8,231 listings in May 2026 (INE).
- **Cost/impact:** Loss of the business in 2028. Effects on cleaning and maintenance jobs were not quantified (gap).
- **Driver & status:** Catalan Decreto-ley 3/2023 and Barcelona planning. **In force; deadline November 2028.** Upheld by the Constitutional Court (2025, per press) and the Supreme Court (March 2025).
- **How it is solved today:** Lawyers and associations (Apartur). Some owners are moving to seasonal or medium-term rentals, but that route is also being regulated (see Gaps).
- **Software-solvable?:** No for the ban itself. Partly for the transition: portfolio planning and compliance with seasonal-rental rules.
- **Confidence:** High on the policy; low on the knock-on effects.

### HOS-19: Madrid — Plan Reside, the licence freeze and escalating fines
- **Who:** VUT owners and managers in the historic centre and in upper floors anywhere in the city.
- **Where:** Madrid city. Historic centre (APE 00.01, written "AP-01" in the source) plus city-wide rules.
- **Day-to-day reality:** No new dispersed VUT are allowed in residential buildings in the centre. Elsewhere only ground or first floors (and basements, per idealista) with independent access qualify. Inspections and coercive fines push illegal flats out, and appeals are pending.
- **Evidence:**
  - The City approved the plan at an extraordinary plenary on 21 May 2025. It ends dispersed VUT in the historic centre, requires independent access city-wide, limits VUT to basement, ground or first floor, sets fines of up to €190,000 per home, and keeps the licence suspension in place since Apr 2024 — [idealista, 21 May 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/05/21/845876-aprobado-el-plan-reside-de-madrid-prohibe-viviendas-turisticas-dispersas-en-el)
  - The Comunidad de Madrid approved it on 27 Aug 2025. Listings fell from 16,959 (Jul 2025) to 14,297 (7 Nov 2025), down 15.7% (Inside Airbnb data cited by the council). Fines escalate to €30,001, €60,001 and €100,001 for repeated non-compliance under art. 204 of the regional land law. 1,423 flats stopped tourist use under an agreement with the Colegio de Administradores de Fincas — [Forbes, 11 Nov 2025](https://forbes.es/economia/825920/los-pisos-turisticos-en-la-ciudad-de-madrid-caen-un-157-desde-la-aprobacion-del-plan-reside/)
  - The council claims a 40% fall in tourist flats between Aug 2024 and May 2026 (INE), against 15% for Spain — [Madrid City Council press release (title and search summary only; page returned 403)](https://diario.madrid.es/blog/notas-de-prensa/madrid-lidera-a-nivel-nacional-la-reduccion-de-los-pisos-turisticos-caen-un-40-en-la-capital-frente-al-15-del-conjunto-de-espana/)
  - Litigation:
    - The TSJ de Madrid admitted the PSOE's appeal and asked for the administrative file. The PSOE claims the plan could let up to 73% of central buildings become tourist accommodation (party estimate) — [Brains RE News, 20 Oct 2025](https://brainsre.news/justicia-revisa-plan-reside-madrid-tras-recurso-psoe/)
    - The earlier Plan Especial de Hospedaje faces 14 appeals (search summary).
    - A Madrid court annulled a €30,001 fine on a tourist flat for wrong legal classification (3 Feb 2026, not final; headline only) — [idealista tag page](https://www.idealista.com/news/etiquetas/normativa-alquiler-turistico)
- **Scale:** 10,836 listings in May 2026 (INE); 14,297 in Nov 2025 (Inside Airbnb).
- **Cost/impact:** Fines of €30,001–€190,000 per home; loss of the business for non-compliant flats.
- **Driver & status:** Plan Reside (a PGOU modification). **In force since Aug–Sep 2025, appealed** at the TSJ de Madrid with no ruling found.
- **How it is solved today:** Urban-planning consultants and lawyers; moving to whole-building tourist use, which the plan allows.
- **Software-solvable?:** Mostly no. Partly through compliance checks on licences against listings and inspection-risk alerts.
- **Confidence:** High.

### HOS-20: Andalusia — Málaga's 43-barrio ban, mass revocations in Sevilla and the Costa del Sol
- **Who:** VUT owners and managers (many autónomos), cleaning and laundry firms.
- **Where:** Málaga city, Sevilla, Marbella, Benalmádena, Fuengirola, Mijas; Granada and Cádiz not verified.
- **Day-to-day reality:** In saturated barrios new registrations are blocked. Elsewhere units need independent access and services. Thousands of applications were revoked and delisted in 2025, and Málaga province carries the country's largest VUT stock.
- **Evidence:**
  - Málaga's PGOU amendment, based on Andalusian Decreto 31/2024 (which lets councils limit VUT), sets three bands: no new VUT in 43 barrios where VUT exceed 8% of housing; new VUT capped at 8% in 32 barrios; and new VUT allowed in 296 others only with independent access and services. The study counted 11,559 registered VUT, 7,035 of them on platforms. Initial approval was given in October 2024, pending plenary approval and publication in the BOP — [idealista, 24 Oct 2024](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2024/10/24/820750-malaga-prohibe-nuevas-viviendas-turisticas-en-43-barrios-de-la-ciudad). Final approval and 2026 status were not verified (gap).
  - Revoked registry applications notified for delisting (Sep 2025): Sevilla 2,289 (the most of any city), Marbella 1,802, Málaga 1,471, Benalmádena 926, Fuengirola 686; Andalucía 16,740 in total — [idealista, 15 Sep 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/09/15/859916-el-gobierno-notifica-mas-de-53-500-pisos-turisticos-ilegales-para-que-se-retiren)
  - In Andalucía, more than 90,000 registry applications produced 66,643 approvals and 21,294 rejections. Málaga province had more than 36,000 definitive codes and 10,327 revocations — [idealista, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/01/20/879570-una-de-cada-cinco-viviendas-de-alquiler-de-corta-estancia-se-quedan-sin-el-codigo)
  - Andalucía appealed the national registry (May 2025, headline) and after the annulment asked for an analysis of compensation for affected operators — [idealista, 22 May 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/05/22/898792-el-fin-del-registro-de-viviendas-de-corta-estancia-reabre-100-000-licencias)
- **Scale:** Málaga province 45,176 listings; Sevilla city 6,937 (INE, May 2026).
- **Cost/impact:** Lost registrations and bookings; Hoj@ duties come on top (HOS-17).
- **Driver & status:** Decreto 31/2024 (Junta de Andalucía), **in force**. Málaga's PGOU amendment had initial approval in 2024; final status not verified. Sevilla's own caps were not verified (gap).
- **How it is solved today:** Gestorías handle registration with the Registro de Turismo de Andalucía (RTA); PMSs display the RTA code.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Checking zone and cap eligibility before buying or listing, and tracking registrations.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### HOS-21: Comunitat Valenciana — Valencia and Alicante moratoria and the largest supply drop
- **Who:** VUT owners and managers, and investors in whole-building apartments.
- **Where:** Valencia city (Cabanyal-Canyamelar), Alicante city, Torrevieja; Benidorm not verified.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Licence suspensions spread barrio by barrio and to whole buildings. The Comunitat Valenciana lost the most listings, and one in three of the listings sanctioned in Airbnb's €64M fine were located there.
- **Evidence:**
  - Valencia extended its May 2024 moratorium in Cabanyal-Canyamelar, suspending building permits for new tourist apartments after licence applications rose more than 220%. Compromís asked for a one-year extension — [idealista, 28 Jan 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/01/28/829610-valencia-suspende-las-licencias-para-nuevos-pisos-turisticos-en-el-barrio-del)
  - Alicante (22 Jul 2025) extended its moratorium to whole blocks, pensions and tourist hostels for two years. Aptur CV is suing, claiming the council's own report found no excess of tourist flats, and citing €220M of economic impact and 2,500 jobs in 2024 (lobby figures) — [idealista, 22 Jul 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/07/22/854283-alicante-aprueba-ampliar-la-moratoria-a-licencias-de-pisos-turisticos-en-bloques)
  - Registry revocations: Valencia city 731, Torrevieja 700, Comunitat Valenciana 7,499 — [idealista, 15 Sep 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/09/15/859916-el-gobierno-notifica-mas-de-53-500-pisos-turisticos-ilegales-para-que-se-retiren). Alicante province had 35,916 definitive codes and 8,309 revocations — [idealista, 20 Jan 2026](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2026/01/20/879570-una-de-cada-cinco-viviendas-de-alquiler-de-corta-estancia-se-quedan-sin-el-codigo)
  - One in three of the 65,122 listings in Airbnb's fine were in the Comunitat Valenciana, followed by Canarias and Galicia — [RTVC/Europa Press, Dec 2025 (search summary)](https://rtvc.es/consumo-sanciona-64-millones-airbnb-anunciar-viviendas-sin-licencia/)
  - It was the Generalitat Valenciana's appeal that won Supreme Court ruling 620/2026 against the national registry — [Notarios y Registradores](https://www.notariosyregistradores.com/web/participa/noticias/el-ts-anula-registro-arrendamientos-corta-duracion/)
- **Scale:** Comunitat Valenciana 51,268 listings (about −12,000 year on year); Alicante province 32,148; Valencia city 5,393 (INE, May 2026).
- **Cost/impact:** Moratoria freeze new supply; existing operators face delisting risk.
- **Driver & status:** Municipal moratoria, **in force** (Alicante for two years from Jul 2025). Regional Decreto-ley 9/2024 and Valencia's city-wide 2024–25 rules were not verified (gap).
- **How it is solved today:** Lawyers (Aptur CV litigation); registration with the regional tourism register (RTV).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly, through eligibility and zoning lookup and compliance monitoring.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### HOS-22: Baleares (Palma/Mallorca) and Canarias — bans, caps and island-level delisting deals
- **Who:** VUT owners and managers, hostels and vacation-home (vivienda vacacional) owners.
- **Where:** Palma and Mallorca; Canarias (Las Palmas de Gran Canaria, Santa Cruz de Tenerife, Adeje, Arona, La Oliva); Ibiza not verified.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Palma announced a ban on any new tourist rental and on new hostels. Island authorities are getting Airbnb to delist units without the island registration number. Canarias is legislating a hard cap on vacation homes.
- **Evidence:**
  - Palma's mayor announced on 14 Oct 2025 a ban on new tourist rentals "en cualquiera de sus modalidades" (in any form) across the municipality, to be approved in plenary, with three months' retroactive effect. The city's 639 legal tourist homes would be kept, and new hostels would also be banned — [idealista, 14 Oct 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/10/14/867487-palma-prohibira-nuevos-alquileres-turisticos-en-cualquiera-de-sus-modalidades). Final approval was not verified (gap).
  - Airbnb removed more than 2,300 Mallorca listings that lacked the Consell de Mallorca number, the second phase of an agreement with the Consell; a councillor said "no habrá tregua" (there will be no truce) — [idealista, 27 Oct 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/10/27/869388-airbnb-elimina-mas-de-2-300-anuncios-en-mallorca-que-no-cuentan-con-numero-de)
  - The Canarias draft law (March 2025) would:
    - cap vacation homes at 10% of residential buildable area per settlement (20% in La Palma, La Gomera and El Hierro);
    - require a minimum of 35 m² and 10 years of age;
    - allow no new vacation homes until municipal planning permits them, for five years.
    - The CNMC warned it could wipe out supply — [idealista, 19 Mar 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/03/19/837355-cnmc-avisa-a-canarias-de-que-su-ley-para-regular-el-alquiler-turistico-puede-acabar)
    - The final law's number, date and transition terms were not verified (gap).
  - Airbnb removed unregistered Canarias listings under an April 2025 agreement with the regional government; registered listings rose more than 45% between 1 Jan and 31 Aug 2025 — [idealista, 30 Sep 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/09/30/862069-airbnb-elimina-anuncios-de-alquileres-a-corto-plazo-en-canarias-que-no-completaron)
  - In some parts of Canarias, obsolete tourist accommodation is being converted into substandard housing (infravivienda) — [Exceltur SolyTUR 2024, Apr 2025](https://www.exceltur.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Exceltur_Solytur-2024_230425.pdf)
- **Scale:** Baleares 21,304 listings; Canarias 48,356; Las Palmas province 26,998; Tenerife 21,358 (INE, May 2026). Canarias had 8,698 registry revocations in Sep 2025.
- **Cost/impact:** Frozen new supply; delisting of non-compliant units.
- **Driver & status:**
  - Palma: ban **announced** (Oct 2025); adoption not verified.
  - Canarias: vacation-rental law, draft in March 2025; the final law was not verified.
  - Balearic "contención turística" decree-laws were not verified.
- **How it is solved today:** Registration with the island or regional registers (ETV, VV codes); lawyers.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly, through compliance monitoring and eligibility checks.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low because of the status gaps.

### HOS-23: Owners' communities can veto new tourist flats by a 3/5 majority (reform of the Ley de Propiedad Horizontal)
- **Who:** Prospective VUT owners and managers in apartment buildings.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Since April 2025, opening a new VUT in an apartment building requires explicit approval by three-fifths of the owners, representing three-fifths of the participation quotas. Many units fail to get it, and missing approval was one of the main reasons registry codes were revoked.
- **Evidence:**
  - From 3 Apr 2025 new tourist flats need "el visto bueno expreso" (express approval) of three-fifths of owners and quotas, without amending the title deed or statutes — [idealista, 2 Apr 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/finanzas/economia/2025/04/02/839265-las-comunidades-de-vecinos-tendran-la-ultima-palabra-sobre-los-pisos-turisticos)
  - Ley Orgánica 1/2025 has applied since 3 Apr 2025, and licences granted before that date keep their acquired rights — [Moncloa.com, 12 Jun 2026](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/06/12/barcelona-collboni-pisos-3384249/)
  - Missing 3/5 community approval was one of the three main reasons for revoking registry codes — [idealista, 7 Jul 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/07/07/852282-los-motivos-que-provocan-mas-revocaciones-del-codigo-obligatorio-para-los)
- **Scale:** Every new VUT in a multi-unit building nationwide.
- **Cost/impact:** Blocked openings and community disputes. I could not verify the reported option for communities to raise fees by up to 20% (gap).
- **Driver & status:** LO 1/2025 (justice-efficiency law) amending Ley 49/1960. **In force since 3 Apr 2025.** The exact article text was not retrieved because the BOE page was too long.
- **How it is solved today:** Community administrators (administradores de fincas) and lawyers.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Community voting and minutes and evidence of acquired rights are digitisable, but the veto itself is legal.
- **Confidence:** High.

### HOS-24: Enforcement through platforms and consumer law — exposure for managers and knock-on effects on the service ecosystem
- **Who:** Property managers (gestoras), hosts, cleaning and maintenance suppliers, and platforms.
- **Where:** National. Most sanctioned listings were in the Comunitat Valenciana, Canarias and Galicia.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Listings vanish when platforms act on government orders or regional agreements. Large managers are investigated for presenting themselves as private hosts or giving misleading information. Lobbies warn of large losses for owners and service firms.
- **Evidence:**
  - Consumo fined Airbnb €64,055,311 (signed in Dec 2025) over 65,122 listings, mainly for advertising unlicensed properties or showing registration numbers that did not match official records. The TSJ de Madrid denied Airbnb's request for interim suspension in March 2026. In July 2025 Airbnb had removed 65,000 listings — [Ministerio de Derechos Sociales, Consumo y Agenda 2030, 22–23 Mar 2026](https://www.dsca.gob.es/es/comunicacion/notas-prensa/justicia-obliga-airbnb-pagar-64-millones-euros-previstos-multa-que-le); also [RTVC (search summary)](https://rtvc.es/consumo-sanciona-64-millones-airbnb-anunciar-viviendas-sin-licencia/)
  - Consumo opened proceedings against large tourist-flat managers (names not disclosed) for misleading information (art. 5 of the unfair-competition law) and for presenting a company as a private individual (art. 27). Fines can reach €100,000, or four to six times the illicit profit — [idealista, 20 Feb 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/02/20/833191-consumo-abre-expedientes-a-grandes-gestoras-de-pisos-turisticos-por-posibles)
  - The consumer association CECU complained that Airbnb still allowed listings without a number and that Booking blocked new ones but had not removed existing listings — [idealista, 4 Jul 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/07/04/851896-de-las-incidencias-a-las-denuncias-asi-ha-sido-el-estreno-del-registro-de)
  - Lobby estimates, which were not borne out (see Noise): Fevitur predicted the registry would remove 70% of supply and cost €13,737M between July and December 2025 — [idealista, 16 Jun 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/06/16/849309-fevitur-avisa-el-registro-unico-de-alquileres-de-corta-duracion-eliminara-el-70-de). In practice listed supply fell 10.7% year on year by May 2026 (INE).
- **Scale:** 65,122 sanctioned listings; 341,001 listed units nationally.
- **Cost/impact:** Delisted units lose all platform revenue. Managers face fines of up to €100,000 or 4–6× profit. Cleaning and maintenance revenue losses were not quantified (gap).
- **Driver & status:** TRLGDCU consumer law and unfair-competition law, enforced by Consumo. The Airbnb fine is final in the administrative route and under appeal at the TSJ de Madrid, with interim relief denied. The proceedings against managers had no outcome found.
- **How it is solved today:** Lawyers; platform-compliance staff inside large managers.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Continuous listing audits (does the code match the official register, is the host's trader status disclosed), cross-platform compliance dashboards and automated disclosure texts.
- **Confidence:** High on enforcement; low on knock-on effects.

### Inferences
- The regulatory centre of gravity is now regional and municipal, through zoning caps, moratoria and island registers, plus building-level community vetoes. A manager operating in Madrid, Málaga, Valencia and Palma faces four different eligibility rule sets, plus EU data-sharing that is enforced through the platforms.
- Because supply only fell about 10% year on year despite the registry turmoil, the realised pain is concentrated in compliance work, uncertainty and city-specific freezes, not mass extinction. Barcelona's 2028 deadline is the exception.
- Knock-on effects on cleaning and maintenance firms are plausible (fewer units, especially in Barcelona after 2028), but I found no independent evidence. The only figures are lobby claims (Fevitur €33,000 per owner, Aptur CV 2,500 jobs in Alicante, Apartur 40% of capacity).

### Gaps
- Sevilla's barrio caps, Valencia's city-wide 2024–25 rules, Benidorm, San Sebastián's VUT restrictions, Ibiza, Granada and Cádiz were not verified because the search budget was exhausted.
- Final approval of Málaga's 43-barrio ban, Palma's ban and the Canarias law (number, date, transition period for existing vacation homes).
- An October 2026 decree (reported as Real Decreto-ley 29/2026) reportedly ends "alquiler temporal sin causa" (seasonal rentals without justified cause), which would close the medium-term-rental escape route for former tourist flats. Only seen as an idealista headline; not verified.
- No independent data on employment in cleaning, laundry and maintenance firms tied to VUT.

## Key question 4 — Tour and activity operators and guides: licensing, booking tools, seasonality

### Takeaway
Evidence for this segment is thin: the search budget ran out before guide licensing and free-tour rules could be researched. What was verified: leisure and activity firms are the weakest tourism subsector in 2026, with flat sales (+0.3% in Q3) and more firms reporting worse results than better, as tourists cut spending on activities. Booking software costs $49–$499 a month plus 1–1.5% per booking.

### Cited Findings
- Leisure providers' sales were +0.3% in Q3 2026, against +4.1% for tourism overall. Exceltur reports "mayor contención del gasto en destino" (more restrained spending at destination) and "mayor sensibilidad en el gasto de actividades, experiencias y otros consumos complementarios" (more price sensitivity on activities and experiences). Leisure results: 19.0% of firms improved, 55.5% stayed the same and 25.5% worsened (balance −6.5). Leisure employment grew 6.6% — [Exceltur nº98, Oct 2026](https://www.exceltur.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/EXCELTUR-Informe-Perspectivas-N98-Balance-del-IIIT26-y-previsiones-para-IVT26.pdf)

### HOS-25: Activity and tour operators — weak spending at destination, OTA dependence and booking-software fees
- **Who:** Tour, activity and experience operators and guides (autónomos and micro firms).
- **Where:** National, especially city and coastal destinations. City-specific guide rules were not verified.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Operators depend on marketplaces (Viator, GetYourGuide, Civitatis) and pay booking-software fees per transaction, while tourists trim spending on extras. Seasonality compresses income into a few months.
- **Evidence:**
  - Bókun's plans: FREE (1 user); START $49/month plus a 1.5% booking fee; PLUS $149/month plus 1.25% (the plan card says 1%); PREMIUM $499/month plus 1%. There is no Bókun fee on Viator bookings, and channel-manager pricing is on request — [Bókun pricing, accessed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.bokun.io/pricing)
  - FareHarbor and TuriTop pricing pages did not load (gap). Viator, GetYourGuide and Civitatis commission rates were not verified (gap).
  - Weak 2026 demand for leisure: see Cited Findings above (Exceltur).
- **Scale:** Not quantified (gap).
- **Cost/impact:** Software fees of 1–1.5% per booking plus a monthly fee; marketplace commissions unknown.
- **Driver & status:** Market forces (spending restraint, OTA concentration). Regional licensing of tour guides and municipal free-tour rules (Madrid, Barcelona, Sevilla, San Sebastián, Granada) were not verified (gap).
- **How it is solved today:** Bókun, FareHarbor, TuriTop, Regiondo and direct websites. Complaints were not researched.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Direct booking, channel management and dynamic pricing help, but demand and licensing are external.
- **Confidence:** Low.

### Inferences
- Exceltur's data suggest activity operators are the weakest tourism subsector in 2026. Without data on licensing burdens and commissions, though, this segment cannot yet be ranked against restaurants or tourist rentals.

### Gaps
- Regional tour-guide licensing (habilitación de guías), free-tour regulations and group-size limits by city, turismo activo registration and insurance requirements, and OTA commission levels were not researched because the shared search budget was exhausted. This is the highest-priority follow-up if the user continues the search.

## Key question 5 — Seasonality and worker housing in tourist areas (Baleares, Ibiza, Canarias, Málaga)

### Takeaway
Seasonality shows up as turnover above 70% in very seasonal businesses. Housing pressure is documented at destination level: tourist-flat beds now outnumber hotel beds on the coast and drive 69.5% of the increase in tourist pressure since 2019, and 61% of 131 surveyed municipalities link tourist-flat growth to housing-access problems. Mallorca restaurateurs cite soaring rents. However, I found no verified, dated evidence on housing specifically for tourism workers (caravans in Ibiza, hotel staff housing) before the search budget ran out.

### Cited Findings
- In the 728 sun-and-beach municipalities:
  - Tourist-flat (VUT) beds grew 20.5% between 2022 and 2024 (+206,949) against +3.0% for hotels (+35,333). VUT reached 1,213,512 beds, more than the 1,202,243 hotel beds.
  - VUT overnight stays rose 45.6% between 2019 and 2024 against +0.9% for hotels, and 69.5% of the increase in tourist pressure comes from VUT.
  - Source: [Exceltur SolyTUR 2024, 23 Apr 2025](https://www.exceltur.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Exceltur_Solytur-2024_230425.pdf)
- 61% of 131 surveyed tourist municipalities see a direct link between tourist-flat growth and housing-access problems; 14% have specific rules, 26% are studying them and 35% have none — [idealista/Europa Press, 26 Jun 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/06/26/850838-un-60-de-destinos-de-sol-y-playa-detecta-conflictos-entre-acceso-a-la-vivienda-y) (Auren, Redintur and Iatur observatory)

### HOS-26: Seasonal staffing and worker housing in island and coastal destinations
- **Who:** Hotels, restaurants and beach clubs in seasonal destinations (micro to medium), and their seasonal workers.
- **Where:** Baleares (Mallorca, Ibiza), Canarias, Costa del Sol (Málaga), Costa Blanca (Benidorm).
- **Day-to-day reality:** Businesses must rehire much of their staff every season. Workers struggle to find affordable rentals where tourist flats and second homes absorb housing, so recruitment fails and turnover rises.
- **Evidence:**
  - Turnover above 70% a year in very seasonal businesses — [El Debate, 26 Jul 2026](https://www.eldebate.com/espana/galicia/20260726/empresa-gallega-encuentra-solucion-cubrir-vacantes-hosteleria-forma-rapida_443319_amp.html)
  - Mallorca restaurateurs cite soaring rents and high staff turnover — [Majorca Daily Bulletin, 17 Aug 2026](https://www.majorcadailybulletin.com/news/local/2026/08/17/145039/mallorca-bars-and-restaurants-suffer-per-cent-drop-turnover-during-the-first-half-august.html)
  - Destination-level housing pressure from VUT growth and conversions of old tourist accommodation into substandard housing in parts of Canarias — [Exceltur SolyTUR 2024](https://www.exceltur.org/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/Exceltur_Solytur-2024_230425.pdf)
  - Exceltur names "escasez de talento" (talent shortage) as a persistent adverse factor in summer 2026 — [Exceltur nº98](https://www.exceltur.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/EXCELTUR-Informe-Perspectivas-N98-Balance-del-IIIT26-y-previsiones-para-IVT26.pdf)
- **Scale:** Not quantified for workers specifically (gap).
- **Cost/impact:** Not quantified: no data on rents paid by seasonal workers, employer-provided housing costs or unfilled posts.
- **Driver & status:** Housing scarcity and the concentration of activity in one season. Balearic and Canarian measures on worker housing were not verified (gap).
- **How it is solved today:** Not verified. Employer-leased staff housing and housing allowances are reported anecdotally but were not sourced here.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Staff-housing marketplaces, pre-season rehiring pipelines and planning for fixed-discontinuous (fijo discontinuo) staff could help, but the root cause is housing supply.
- **Confidence:** Low to medium. The pain is very likely real, but worker-specific evidence was not captured.

### Inferences
- Worker housing is plausibly a top-five pain in the Baleares and Canarias, but it ranks lower here because of the evidence gap, not because it is less severe.

### Gaps
- Ibiza and Mallorca worker-housing data (caravan settlements, rents, employer housing), Canarias hotel-staff housing, and Balearic decrees allowing employer-provided housing were not researched because the search budget was exhausted.

## Top pains
Ranked by severity times breadth, with weight on hard evidence:
1. **HOS-12 SES.Hospedajes traveller reporting.** It hits every lodging business (about 341,000 listed VUT plus about 200,000 Booking partners) on every booking. It requires up to about 43 fields within 24 hours, carries fines of up to €30,000, remains unamended and is under an EU infringement case (INFR(2026)4005). Tools exist, but the burden and the legal uncertainty persist.
2. **HOS-13 and HOS-18–24 STR regulatory whiplash.** The NRUA took about 467,000 applications at €27.05 each and revoked about 20% before it was annulled in May 2026. Operators now face regional codes, EU 2024/1028 with no Spanish penalty regime, city bans and moratoria (Barcelona 2028, Madrid Plan Reside, Málaga, Alicante, Palma), community vetoes and platform delistings: the most volatile compliance environment in the sector.
3. **HOS-01 Staff shortage, turnover and absenteeism.** Hostelería accounts for 13.37% of hard-to-fill vacancies, turnover runs 40–60% (above 70% when seasonal), and absenteeism takes 7.7% of hours at a cost of 3.8% of turnover. It affects all of the sector's 1.85 million jobs.
4. **HOS-04 Margin squeeze in restaurants and bars.** Energy is up 6.9%, supplies 6.2%, labour about 5% and interest rates 25%. 39.1% of tourism firms report worse results; Mallorca restaurant turnover fell 14% in August 2026, with 500–600 closures forecast.
5. **HOS-14 and HOS-03 Platform tolls.** Booking holds a 70–90% share, was fined €413M and faces about €1bn in claims from more than 1,700 hotels. Airbnb's host-only fee is 15.5%. Delivery apps take 15–35% (35–42% all-in): a structural transfer of margin that small operators cannot negotiate away.
6. **HOS-15 Tourist-tax multiplication.** Catalonia doubled its rates from April 2026, Gipuzkoa, Bizkaia and Álava start in January 2027 with quarterly returns, and Galician cities have added their own. Rates of €1–€11 per night, each with different exemptions and caps per jurisdiction.
7. **HOS-08 and HOS-09 Food-compliance evidence (APPCC, allergens, food waste).** Universal, recurring documentation, with fines of up to €600,000 and closure under Ley 17/2011, plus new Ley 1/2025 duties from April 2026. Very software-friendly, though actual enforcement is unmeasured.
8. **HOS-02 No-shows.** A 3.3% rate costs about €78 per lost table. The pain is real but declining and already crowded with tools, so it ranks low for severity.

## Noise and dead ends
- **"NRUA compliance" as a product category is a dead end.** The state registration procedure and number were annulled (rulings 620/2026 and 629/2026, May 2026). What survives is EU 2024/1028 data sharing via the Ventanilla Única and regional codes.
- **Fevitur's forecast that the registry would wipe out 70% of supply and cost €13.7bn** ([idealista, 16 Jun 2025](https://www.idealista.com/news/inmobiliario/vivienda/2025/06/16/849309-fevitur-avisa-el-registro-unico-de-alquileres-de-corta-duracion-eliminara-el-70-de)) **was not borne out.** INE shows −10.7% year on year by May 2026. Treat lobby loss figures (Fevitur €33,000 per owner and €160M; Aptur CV 2,500 jobs; Apartur 40% of capacity) as advocacy.
- **Fines for not giving "free tuppers" under Ley 1/2025 appear overstated in the press.** The law's list of infractions (art. 20) has no specific one for this. The plan and donation duties exempt micro firms and establishments of 1,300 m² or less, so most small restaurants face only the leftovers and container duty.
- **The terrace smoking ban is not in force.** It is a bill sent to Congress on 21 Jul 2026; timing and content may change.
- **Verifactu is postponed again** (announced for October 2028, not yet formalised as of 5 Oct 2026). Point-of-sale urgency built on 2026–27 deadlines is overstated; this belongs to the tax researcher.
- **The claim that San Sebastián already charges a Basque tourist tax in summer 2026 is false per the official text** (Norma Foral 3/2026 takes effect on 1 Jan 2027).
- **Emergency hotel price caps (Real Decreto-ley 4/2026)** were a one-off: one week in 14 municipalities of Andalucía and Extremadura after storm Leonardo in February 2026 ([CEHAT, 18 Feb 2026](https://cehat.com/notaprensa/cehat-defiende-la-trayectoria-solidaria-del-sector-hotelero-ante-emergencias-y-cuestiona-la-limitacion-general-de-precios-aprobada-por-el-gobierno/)). Not a recurring pain.
- **No-show tools and online check-in/police-reporting tools are already cheaply solved.** No-shows are declining (3.6% → 3.3%), with many tools at €0–€299/month. SES/check-in tools cost €3.95–€7.95 per property per month (Chekin) or come built into PMSs. Room remains only for bundling or price competition.
- **Fake-review extortion** is real but supported only by anecdotes and opinion pieces with no hard incidence data. Removal depends on Google, so it is only partly software-solvable.
- **The 2022 Valencia terrace dispute** (Honduras and Blasco Ibáñez) is outdated for the 2024–26 window. Use Madrid's 2026 ordinance as the current evidence.
- **The municipal waste fee (Ley 7/2022)** is a legal certainty, but I found no evidence of its impact on hospitality, and it is not software-solvable.
- **Hotels as a segment are not distressed:** 53.1% improved their results in Q3 2026 and direct-channel revenue grew 19%. Pain ideas aimed at large hotels compete with mature PMS and revenue-management vendors (Mews and Cloudbeds, quote-based).
