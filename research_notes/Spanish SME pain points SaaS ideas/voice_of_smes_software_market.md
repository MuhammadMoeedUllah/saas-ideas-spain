# Voice of Spanish SME owners and autónomos online, and the SME software market they buy from (2024–2026)

Scope: cross-sector owner complaints, beyond the standard tax, labour and finance categories, plus the market for cross-sector SME software. Research date: 23 Oct 2026. "Observed 23 Oct 2026" means I fetched the vendor page that day. Method limits, also repeated in the Gaps sections:
- reddit.com is blocked to the research crawler, so no Reddit threads could be read or verified.
- The shared WebSearch quota ran out partway through. After that, research went on by fetching known URLs directly (vendor pricing pages, Trustpilot pages, Forocoches threads, official pages).
- X, LinkedIn, Burbuja.info and emprendedores.es comments could not be covered.
- Trustpilot states that it does not verify review content. All review quotes are self-reported.

## Q1. What do Spanish SME owners and autónomos complain about online, especially outside the standard categories? (scams, telecom and energy contracts, insurance, banks, customer acquisition)

### Takeaway
The places I could reach (Forocoches, Trustpilot, OCU Reclama, the press) were dominated by four things:
- Kit Digital frustration (see Q3).
- Fake reviews: extortion, and a grey market of paid reviews.
- Cyber-fraud: fraud incidents grew 25.6% in 2025.
- B2B customers falling outside consumer protection in telecom and energy disputes.

Classic "business scams" (fake data-protection inspectors, fake directories) appear in Spanish sources mostly from 2018–2020. I found no fresh 2024–2026 evidence for them (see Noise).

### Cited Findings
- INCIBE-CERT handled 122,223 incidents in 2025 (+25.6% year on year) and detected 237,028 vulnerable systems (+28.9%). The 017 helpline received 142,767 queries (+44%). — [INCIBE press release, 24 Sep 2026](https://www.incibe.es/incibe/sala-de-prensa/gran-canaria-impulsa-la-prevencion-en-ciberseguridad-entre-las-empresas-ante)
- The AEPD received 3,027 notifications of personal-data breaches in Jan–Jun 2026, against 1,271 a year earlier (+138%). Jan–Aug 2026: 4,556 against 1,728 (+163%). Hotels and their booking-software processors drove much of the rise. — [AEPD, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.aepd.es/prensa-y-comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/la-aepd-recibe-mas-de-3.000-notificaciones-de-brechas-de)
- An extortion offer to Catalan restaurants, quoted in an opinion column: "Tú me pagas 200 euros al mes y yo te hago veinte reseñas positivas en Tripadvisor" ("You pay me 200 € a month and I'll write you twenty positive reviews on Tripadvisor"). The writer says every restaurant he spoke with had received such proposals. — [Ara, Xavier Bosch, 3 Dec 2024](https://es.ara.cat/opinion/extorsion-restaurantes_129_5218159.html)
- Infoautónomos says the consumer administration handles only final consumers, so it cannot process an autónomo's complaint about a supplier linked to their business. Companies (personas jurídicas) are also excluded from the telecom ministry's complaint route. — [Infoautónomos, updated 26 Feb 2025](https://www.infoautonomos.com/blog/internet/como-reclamar-un-servicio-de-telefonia-o-internet-siendo-autonomo)
- Asufin puts the average current-account maintenance fee at 160 € a year in 2025, up from 148.82 € two years earlier. Five banks charge up to 240 €. These figures are for consumer accounts, not business accounts. — [Diario de León / Asufin, 27 May 2025](https://www.diariodeleon.es/economia/250527/2049229/bancos-disparan-comision-mantener-cuenta-corriente-160-euros.html)
- On Forocoches "Empleo / Emprendimiento", the busiest owner-facing threads on 23 Oct 2026 included:
  - a lawyer offering services "a precios forococheros" (1,068 replies);
  - a thread recruiting commission-based recommenders for "seguros / luz / telefonía" (45 replies);
  - a TripAdvisor review-swap thread.
  This shows the commission-driven sales channel for insurance, energy and telecom, and the demand for cheap legal help and reviews. — [Forocoches subforum f=23, observed 23 Oct 2026](https://forocoches.com/foro/forumdisplay.php?f=23)

### VOX-01: Fake-review extortion and review bombing of local businesses
- Who: restaurants and bars most visibly; also local service businesses. Autónomo / micro <10.
- Where: national. Documented in Catalonia (Barcelona, Girona, Empordà, Delta de l'Ebre). Court awards are reported (unverified) in Madrid and Barcelona.
- Day-to-day reality: A bar sees about 20 suspicious profiles post one-star reviews in under an hour, alleging cockroaches, rotten food and an insulting waiter, and its 4.8 rating "se desploma en tiempo real" ("collapses in real time"). Extortionists offer to stop or remove the reviews for money: "O me pagas tanto o te escribiré comentarios negativos en Tripadvisor" ("Either you pay me this much or I'll write negative comments on Tripadvisor").
- Evidence:
  - Xavier Bosch, opinion column: the threat quoted above; "Es una práctica casi mafiosa" ("It's an almost mafia-like practice"); all the restaurants he spoke with had received offers. — [Ara, 3 Dec 2024](https://es.ara.cat/opinion/extorsion-restaurantes_129_5218159.html)
  - About 20 profiles in under an hour hit an unnamed bar. A "pack de odio" (hate pack) against a competitor costs "apenas 200 euros". Fines for fake reviews reach 100,000 €. Courts in Madrid and Barcelona reportedly award up to 20,000 € in compensation, but no cases are named, so this is unverified. — [Merca2, 28 Apr 2026](https://www.merca2.es/2026/04/28/resenas-bar-google-2373862/)
  - Spanish law firm Álvarez Villamediana says Google's reporting system "rara vez funciona" ("rarely works"), because it comes down to the owner's word against the reviewer's. The extortion cases in this article are from the US and elsewhere, not Spain. — [Xataka Móvil, 14 Sep 2025](https://www.xatakamovil.com/movil-y-sociedad/pagas-te-frio-a-resenas-negativas-google-maps-asi-estafa-que-esta-destrozando-reputacion-pequenos-negocios/amp)
  - "Cada vez son más los negocios que denuncian una práctica alarmante" ("More and more businesses report an alarming practice"). No figures. — [Puro Marketing, 14 May 2025](https://www.puromarketing.com/141/215522/ciberextorsion-falsas-resenas-amenaza-creciente-para-reputacion-imagen-negocios-empresas)
  - Chef Alberto Chicote's restaurant was offered five-star reviews and told it could get negative ones if it did not pay. — [Genbeta, 14 Aug 2023](https://www.genbeta.com/actualidad/estan-vendiendo-resenas-falsas-cinco-estrellas-a-bares-restaurantes-no-baratas-asi-negocio-para-dar-reputacion-a-local)
- Scale: no Spanish count found. Global proxy: in 2024 Google removed more than 240M policy-violating reviews and more than 12M fake business profiles, and restricted more than 900,000 accounts. — [Ara, 9 May 2025](https://es.ara.cat/comer/lee-resenas-restaurante-alerta-hay-falsas_1_5373668.html)
- Cost/impact:
  - Lost rating and visibility. Merca2 says Google's algorithm may demote the business before it verifies the complaints.
  - Up to 20,000 € in court compensation (unverified).
  - In non-Spanish cases, extortion payments of 75–150 USD. — [Puro Marketing, 14 Jul 2022](https://www.puromarketing.com/141/36684/ultimo-timo-online-pesadilla-marketing-pagas-hunden-reputacion-online-restaurante); [Xataka Móvil, 14 Sep 2025](https://www.xatakamovil.com/movil-y-sociedad/pagas-te-frio-a-resenas-negativas-google-maps-asi-estafa-que-esta-destrozando-reputacion-pequenos-negocios/amp)
- Driver & status: local demand runs through Google Maps and Tripadvisor rankings. Merca2 and Puro Marketing say fake reviews have been illegal in Spain since May 2022, under the EU "Omnibus" rules. Status (Oct 2026): still being reported (Merca2, Apr 2026). I found no Spain-specific enforcement data.
- How it is solved today:
  - Free: Google's reporting tool and its "Herramienta de Gestión de Reseñas" for owners (criticised as "rara vez funciona").
  - Police: the Policía Nacional Unidad de Delitos Telemáticos or the Guardia Civil.
  - Lawyers: unfair-competition law, and the right to honour (which also protects companies).
  - Workarounds: preserving certified screenshots; removing the phone number from the listing to stop WhatsApp approaches (Xataka).
  - Reputation agencies exist (for example OnbrandinG, cited by Puro Marketing), but I captured no prices.
- Software-solvable?: partly. Monitoring and alerting on review spikes, certified evidence capture and escalation workflows can be automated. Removal still depends on Google's decision.
- Confidence: medium. There are several press sources, but few direct Spanish owner quotes and no scale data.

### VOX-02: Pressure to accumulate reviews and the paid-review grey market
- Who: restaurants, bars and local services. Autónomo / micro <10.
- Where: national. Catalonia in the press; forum posters' locations are not stated.
- Day-to-day reality: Owners are pitched paid reviews and some swap or buy them. Legitimate owners push for reviews in person (QR codes, a free sip of wine, a prize-wheel spin). On whether people pay for reviews: "Nadie te reconocerá que ha pagado" ("Nobody will admit they've paid").
- Evidence:
  - An offer of 200 €/month for 20 Tripadvisor reviews. — [Ara, 3 Dec 2024](https://es.ara.cat/opinion/extorsion-restaurantes_129_5218159.html)
  - Review-selling firms: 5 Google reviews for 95 USD, 10 for 185 USD (Reviews Company); 50 reviews for 635.25 USD (Social Blade). Owners also use QR codes, wine and prize wheels to collect reviews. — [Ara, Rosa Rodon, 9 May 2025](https://es.ara.cat/comer/lee-resenas-restaurante-alerta-hay-falsas_1_5373668.html)
  - Fake five-star reviews at 2.5 € each, sold as "el más barato del mercado" ("the cheapest on the market"). — [Genbeta, 14 Aug 2023](https://www.genbeta.com/actualidad/estan-vendiendo-resenas-falsas-cinco-estrellas-a-bares-restaurantes-no-baratas-asi-negocio-para-dar-reputacion-a-local)
  - A TripAdvisor review-swap thread: "si alguien está interesado puedo pagarle 1.5 euros" ("if anyone's interested I can pay them 1.5 euros"). Forum post, unverified. — [Forocoches, opened 10 Oct 2026](https://forocoches.com/foro/showthread.php?t=10832787)
  - On the supply side, a local-SEO consultant pitches clinics, workshops, renovation firms, installers and lawyers ("Te ayudo a conseguir más clientes desde Google"), with no prices given. — [Forocoches, 12 Oct 2026](https://forocoches.com/foro/showthread.php?t=10834580)
- Scale: not quantified for Spain.
- Cost/impact:
  - 1.5–2.5 € per fake review; 200 €/month packages.
  - Restaurateurs in Ara warn that Google can suspend accounts caught buying reviews.
  - Fines of up to 100,000 € (Merca2, 28 Apr 2026).
- Driver & status: ranking depends on review volume. Ara (May 2025) reports that Google's warning alerts for removed suspicious five-star reviews launched in the US, UK and India, with global rollout planned from May 2025. Spain status: not verified.
- How it is solved today: manual QR or table cards; illegal paid reviews; legitimate review-request tools (I captured no Spanish prices).
- Software-solvable?: yes, for legitimate, policy-compliant post-visit review requests (WhatsApp, SMS, email). Incentivised or fake reviews remain illegal and violate platform rules.
- Confidence: medium.

### VOX-03: Phishing, vishing and CEO/BEC fraud aimed at small businesses
- Who: all sectors. Autónomo / micro <10 / small 10–49.
- Where: national. Regional breakdowns are available for Canarias and Las Palmas.
- Day-to-day reality: Owners get spoofed calls, SMS and emails impersonating banks, public bodies or suppliers. In BEC ("business email compromise") and CEO fraud, a fake executive or supplier orders an urgent transfer or a change of bank account. A historic AEAT-impersonation example used the subject line "Denuncia de facturas no declaradas" ("Report of undeclared invoices").
- Evidence:
  - INCIBE, 2025: 122,223 incidents (+25.6%); 142,767 queries to 017 (+44% on 2024). In Canarias, companies made 7% of 017 queries. Top themes: vishing 14.5%, fraudulent purchases 10.8%, smishing 9.2%, identity impersonation 6.7%. The fetch flagged an internal inconsistency in the regional percentages. — [INCIBE, 24 Sep 2026](https://www.incibe.es/incibe/sala-de-prensa/gran-canaria-impulsa-la-prevencion-en-ciberseguridad-entre-las-empresas-ante)
  - More than 470,000 fraud and cybercrime offences in 2024 (Interior Ministry). CEO/BEC fraud averages more than 15,000 € per incident, attributed to INCIBE. SMEs are preferred targets because they have fewer verification protocols. Secondary source: a finance blog aggregating official data. — [El blog del Euríbor, 21 Jul 2026](https://www.euribor.com.es/2026/07/21/los-10-tipos-de-estafa-mas-denunciados-en-espana-en-2025/?amp=1)
  - The Guardia Civil (EDITE Navarra) investigated AEAT-impersonating emails demanding payment of "undeclared invoices". Old example. — [Escudo Digital, 29 Mar 2020](https://www.escudodigital.com/interior/estafas-suplantan-a-la-agencia-tributaria-para-reclamar-facturas-falsas-a-empresas.html)
- Scale: 122,223 incidents in 2025, across all victim types and not only firms (INCIBE).
- Cost/impact: more than 15,000 € per BEC incident (secondary, single source). Recovery after the transfer is "very low" (same source).
- Driver & status: fraud is growing fast. INCIBE's counts are up 25.6% in 2025, and 017 queries up 44%. Status (Oct 2026): rising.
- How it is solved today: the free INCIBE 017 helpline; bank fraud alerts; antivirus and security suites (no SME prices captured); Kit Digital's subsidised "ciberseguridad" category (prices not captured).
- Software-solvable?: partly. Payment-change verification workflows, email authentication checks and micro-training can be productised. Social engineering cannot be fully eliminated.
- Confidence: high for the trend (official data); medium for SME-specific losses (secondary source).

### VOX-04: Data breaches at SaaS vendors (e.g., booking software) put notification duties on their SME customers
- Who: hotels mainly (hospitality, covered in depth by another researcher), but the issue reaches any SME that relies on third-party SaaS processors. Micro to medium.
- Where: national.
- Day-to-day reality: One incident at a single booking-software provider in March 2026 caused breaches at several hotels at once. Unauthorised third parties accessed guest and booking data that could be used for fraud attempts. Each hotel must notify the AEPD and, where the risk is high, its guests. The AEPD stresses that notifying the Agency "es tan importante como informar a las personas afectadas" ("is as important as informing the people affected").
- Evidence: 3,027 notifications in H1 2026 against 1,271 (+138%); 4,556 in Jan–Aug against 1,728 (+163%); more than 71 million communications to data subjects. Single source (official). — [AEPD, 20 Oct 2026](https://www.aepd.es/prensa-y-comunicacion/notas-de-prensa/la-aepd-recibe-mas-de-3.000-notificaciones-de-brechas-de)
- Scale: 4,556 breach notifications in Jan–Aug 2026. No SME share given.
- Cost/impact: not quantified. GDPR exposure, and follow-on fraud against guests.
- Driver & status: more attacks on SaaS supply chains. Status (Oct 2026): H1 2026 alone exceeded all of 2025.
- How it is solved today: data-protection consultancies and DPO services (no prices captured); breach-response handled ad hoc (my inference).
- Software-solvable?: partly. Breach-notification workflows and vendor-risk registers can be productised, but the root cause lies with the vendors.
- Confidence: high for the trend; low for how owners themselves describe the pain (no owner quotes).

### VOX-05: Telecom contracts: business lines fall outside consumer protection
- Who: autónomos, micro <10 and small companies (S.L., S.A.).
- Where: national.
- Day-to-day reality: When an autónomo or an S.L. disputes a bill or a permanencia (minimum-term) penalty, consumer arbitration often isn't available. For companies, "la única vía posible será la vía judicial" ("the only possible route will be the courts").
- Evidence:
  - The consumer administration handles only final consumers. An autónomo can use consumer arbitration only if the phone is not part of their professional activity (the contract type is a clue, not proof). The telecom ministry route is open to natural persons, including autónomos, but excludes companies, associations and comunidades de bienes. Otherwise, the courts. — [Infoautónomos, updated 26 Feb 2025](https://www.infoautonomos.com/blog/internet/como-reclamar-un-servicio-de-telefonia-o-internet-siendo-autonomo)
  - Since 1 Apr 2012, lines held by an S.L. or S.A. can only go to court. The Secretaría's decisions are non-binding: "no tiene este carácter ejecutivo" ("does not have that enforceable character"). Old article, but consistent with Infoautónomos 2025. — [Legálitas, 23 Apr 2014](https://www.legalitas.com/actualidad/solucion-de-conflictos-entre-operadores-de-telefonia-y-sus-clientes)
  - The rule that a permanencia penalty must be proportional to the unserved period (art. 62.5 TRLGDCU) is consumer law. The article does not cover business lines. — [Economist & Jurist, 15 Jan 2022](https://www.economistjurist.es/articulos-juridicos-destacados/como-reclamar-la-penalizacion-por-incumplir-la-permanencia-de-companias-telefonicas)
  - In a Comunidad de Madrid arbitration file, the operator invoked art. 3 RDL 1/2007 to challenge the claimant's consumer status. Seen in a search snippet only. — [Comunidad de Madrid arbitration document](https://www.comunidad.madrid/info/docs/2023-10/03423.5.2020.pdf)
- Scale: I found no data on business telecom disputes.
- Cost/impact: not quantified. Penalties and disputed bills must be fought in court for small sums.
- Driver & status: the legal definition of a consumer (art. 3 TRLGDCU). Status: unchanged as of the Feb 2025 Infoautónomos update. I found no newer reform.
- How it is solved today:
  - In-house complaint, then the free ministry route (non-binding), then court.
  - Legal subscriptions (Legálitas promo plans 16.92–45.50 €/month, but at least one plan excludes business matters; see VOX-20).
- Software-solvable?: partly. Contract and permanencia tracking, renewal alerts and complaint-letter generation are software. The missing legal remedy is regulatory.
- Confidence: medium. The legal structure is solid; owner voice is thin in the sources I could reach.

### VOX-06: Energy mis-selling by phone and exit penalties
- Who: autónomos / micro <10 (shops, bars, offices). The complainant's business status is not always stated.
- Where: national. Example from Murcia.
- Day-to-day reality: A caller posing as the incumbent supplier (Endesa) promises "un importante descuento" ("a significant discount") and instead switches the customer to another retailer at a higher kWh price. The customer exercised the right of withdrawal and was charged a penalty: "Me engañan y no se trata de Endesa, se trata de la comercializadora IRIS ENERGÍA" ("They deceive me, and it isn't Endesa, it's the retailer IRIS ENERGÍA").
- Evidence:
  - The OCU Reclama complaint above, with a 40 € penalty. — [OCU Reclama, 1 Aug 2023](https://www.ocu.org/reclamar/lista-reclamaciones-publicas/penalizaci-C3-B3n-tras-ser-enga-C3-B1ado/CPTES01844975-35)
  - CNMC data for 2017: 793,080 SMEs on the free market against 13,945 on the regulated tariff; 964,067 complaints (3.4 per 100 supply points). Some retailers raised contract prices 20–30%, even in the first bill. Door-to-door selling was banned from Oct 2018 (RDL 15/2018). — [Público, 15 Mar 2019](https://www.publico.es/economia/asi-te-engana-compania-luz.html)
  - I found no 2024–2026 business-specific dataset.
- Scale: about 793k SMEs on the free market (2017 data, old).
- Cost/impact: penalties (40 € in the example) and price rises (20–30% in 2017–18).
- Driver & status: a liberalised market sold through commission-based channels. The Forocoches thread recruiting commission recommenders for "seguros / luz / telefonía" (Oct 2026) shows the channel is alive. Current CNMC enforcement status: not verified.
- How it is solved today: OCU complaints, consumer authorities (where the customer qualifies as a consumer), brokers.
- Software-solvable?: partly. Bill auditing and contract or renewal tracking, yes. The selling practice itself is a market and enforcement issue.
- Confidence: low (old data; little 2024–2026 business-specific evidence).

### VOX-07: Banking and card-acceptance costs, and funds held by payment fintechs
- Who: autónomos and micro <10 retailers and service providers.
- Where: national. One review implies the Basque Country, since it mentions TicketBAI.
- Day-to-day reality: Bank maintenance fees keep creeping up, and fintech card acquirers sometimes hold funds or block transfers without explanation: "Me an retenido 60 euros sin ninguna explicación" ("They've withheld 60 euros with no explanation"). Another merchant was sold a terminal that failed TicketBAI: "me han vendido un aparato que no sirve para lo que su comercial me prometió" ("they sold me a device that doesn't do what their salesperson promised").
- Evidence:
  - Average current-account maintenance fee of 160 €/year in 2025, up 6% on 148.82 €. Santander, CaixaBank, Sabadell, Deutsche Bank and Cajamar charge up to 240 €. Cajamar doubled its fee from 120 € to 240 €. Consumer accounts, not business accounts. — [Diario de León / Asufin, 27 May 2025](https://www.diariodeleon.es/economia/250527/2049229/bancos-disparan-comision-mantener-cuenta-corriente-160-euros.html)
  - SumUp pricing (observed 23 Oct 2026): pay-as-you-go 1.49% for in-person card payments; "Pagos Plus" 19 €/month (189 €/year) at 0.75%; free business account with next-day payouts. — [SumUp ES pricing](https://www.sumup.com/es-es/precios/)
  - SumUp Trustpilot reviews from Spanish reviewers, Oct 2026: funds retained (1 Oct), transfers blocked (2 Oct), "no atienden por teléfono" ("they don't answer the phone", 19 Oct), TicketBAI failure (12 Oct). — [Trustpilot SumUp, 1–2★ filter](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/sumup.com?stars=1&stars=2)
- Scale: SumUp has 60,139 Trustpilot reviews (global). I found no counts of Spanish merchants.
- Cost/impact: 160–240 €/year in account fees; 1.49% per transaction on pay-as-you-go.
- Driver & status: bank fee increases (+6% in two years); fintech risk controls. Status: ongoing.
- How it is solved today: switching to free accounts (Asufin names Bankinter as offering a free non-online current account; SumUp's free business account); negotiating TPV (card-terminal) rates.
- Software-solvable?: partly. Fee auditing and comparison are possible; the core is a banking product.
- Confidence: medium (fee data is consumer-oriented).

### Inferences
- In the sources I could reach, the loudest cross-sector owner voices concern subsidy-driven digitalisation (Kit Digital) and online reputation, more than telecom, energy or banks. That may partly reflect source access (no Reddit, X or LinkedIn).
- Review reputation is both essential and a fraud vector for local businesses. Monitoring plus legitimate review generation looks software-solvable; removal does not, because platforms control it.
- B2B customers' lack of consumer-style remedies (telecom; probably energy too) makes "contract and renewal guard" tools plausible, but owner voice is thin. Treat this as a hypothesis.

### Gaps
- Reddit (r/spain, r/askspain, r/SpainEconomics, entrepreneur subreddits) could not be accessed (crawler blocked), so I could not verify which subreddits exist. X, LinkedIn, Burbuja.info and emprendedores.es comments were not reachable within the search budget.
- No 2024–2026 evidence found for: fake AEPD inspectors, fake business-directory or registry invoices, Kit Digital phishing, fake "Google" verification calls.
- No SME-specific data on insurance renewals, advertising costs (Google or Meta Ads) or customer-acquisition costs.
- No business-account bank fee data (the Asufin figures are for consumer accounts).
- No 2024–2026 CNMC enforcement data on energy mis-selling to SMEs.

## Q2. The software market: what Spanish SMEs use and pay for, prices, price rises, review complaints, churn reasons, open-source alternatives, local champions vs international players

### Takeaway
SME invoicing and accounting in Spain has consolidated under foreign groups:
- TeamSystem: Billin, Quipu, DELSOL.
- Visma: Holded.
- Cegid: Contasimple.
- Wolters Kluwer: a3.
- Namirial (e-signature): Signaturit.

The low end is saturated with free or sub-10 € plans, including the AEAT's free VERI*FACTU app and the open-source FacturaScripts. Owner complaints are therefore less about list price than about fairness:
- hard cancellation and auto-renewals;
- invoice caps and features gated behind higher tiers;
- losing access to data at exit;
- no phone support;
- Verifactu errors.

### Cited Findings
- Visma "bought Holded, a Spanish ERP and accounting company, in June 2021". — [Wikipedia: Visma, observed 23 Oct 2026](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visma)
- Quipu is branded "TeamSystem Quipu" and links to teamsystem.es. Its footer also links a "Quiénes somos" page on quipu.factorialhr.com, which is unexplained and should not be over-read. — [getquipu.com, observed 23 Oct 2026](https://getquipu.com/es/)
- Software DELSOL (Mengíbar, Jaén) "forma parte de TeamSystem". Products: TeamSystem Contasol, Factusol, Nominasol, Tpvsol. Claims more than 50,000 Spanish SMEs. — [sdelsol.com, observed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.sdelsol.com/)
- Billin is now officially "TeamSystem Facturas" after TeamSystem's acquisition. — [Infoautónomos, updated 25 May 2026](https://www.infoautonomos.com/blog/mejor-programa-facturacion-pymes/)
- Contasimple is listed as "Cegid Contasimple"; a3factura as Wolters Kluwer. Prices checked 3–5 Jul 2026. — [Softabase guide](https://softabase.com/es/guides/best-verifactu-invoicing-software)
- "Signaturit Group pasa a ser Namirial", and Universign has joined Signaturit Group. — [signaturit.com, observed 23 Oct 2026](https://www.signaturit.com/es/precios)
- A3 is "ahora parte de Wolters Kluwer" and "La opción obligada si tu asesor externo lo usa" ("the obligatory option if your external adviser uses it"). — [GuiaDeSoftware, updated 8 Sep 2026](https://www.guiadesoftware.com/blog/software-contabilidad-pymes-espana)
- Holded: monthly billing roughly doubles the annual-billing prices. The Plus plan is capped at 250 invoices a year, including converted quotes and corrective invoices; Básico 1,000; Estándar 3,000. — [Softabase, prices checked 3–5 Jul 2026](https://softabase.com/es/guides/best-verifactu-invoicing-software)
- The two Holded Premium figures fit this. Softabase gives 99.50 €/month on annual billing; El Independiente quotes Premium at 199 €/month (probably the monthly-billing price). ### VOX-08: SaaS price creep and essential features gated behind higher tiers
- Who: autónomos, micro <10 and small 10–49 using invoicing, accounting or HR SaaS.
- Where: national.
- Day-to-day reality: Owners report yearly price rises and having to move up a tier for basics: more invoices, sending invoices from their own email domain so they don't land in spam, phone support. "el precio solo sube y sube cada año" ("the price just keeps going up every year"). Invoices sent from Holded "las envía directamente a SPAM o no llegan al cliente" ("go straight to spam or never reach the client"), and the fix requires "un plan superior del doble" ("a plan twice the price").
- Evidence:
  - Holded reviews: 1 Jul 2026 (price rises), 30 Sep 2026 (spam and forced upgrade), 25 Mar 2026 (invoice caps). — [Trustpilot Holded](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/holded.com?stars=1&stars=2)

  - Factorial: "nos dijeron un precio y después nos añadieron paquetes para tener los servicios" ("they told us one price and then added packages to get the services", 3 Dec 2025), and a renewal tariff rise (24 Apr 2026). — [Trustpilot Factorial](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/factorialhr.es?stars=1&stars=2)

  - Declarando: "nunca me informaron de que además cobrarían 60 € + IVA por cada alta de trabajador" ("they never told me they'd also charge €60 + IVA for each employee registration", 3 Aug 2026). — [Trustpilot Declarando](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/declarando.es?stars=1&stars=2)
- Scale: Holded claims more than 80,000 companies (promotional, El Independiente, 26 Jun 2026). Similar complaints appear on at least three vendors' pages.
- Cost/impact: a plan "twice the price"; monthly billing at about 2× annual (Holded); 60 € + IVA per employee registration (Declarando).
- Driver & status: consolidation under private-equity-backed or strategic groups (ownership facts above; motive is my inference). Verifactu is a natural upsell moment (inference). Status: ongoing.
- How it is solved today: switching to cheaper or free tools (Billin 6.60–20; Contasimple 0–15.95; FacturaDirecta 0–40; FacturaScripts 0; the free AEAT app).
- Software-solvable?: yes. Flat, transparent pricing without invoice caps is a product decision.
- Confidence: medium. Reviews are unverified but consistent across vendors; I found no documented list-price history.

## Q3. Kit Digital aftermath: renewals vs cancellations, complaints about agentes digitalizadores, market openings

Kit Digital reached more than 860,000 grants before applications closed at the end of October 2025, but by February 2026 only about 25% of the budget had been paid. Owners describe:

## Q5. Buying habits: SEPA direct debit vs card, invoice expectations, Spanish-language and phone/WhatsApp support, gestoría influence

### Takeaway

- the gestor acts as gatekeeper for software choice, and vendors build gestor access and gestor-filing plans;
- owners strongly expect phone support, and vendors restrict it to higher tiers;
- prices are quoted ex-VAT, with annual discounts and short trials.

I found no evidence on SEPA direct debit versus card preference, and no vendor advertising WhatsApp support.

### Cited Findings
- Gestoría as gatekeeper: compatibility with the gestoría "pesa más que la lista de funciones"; a3 is "La opción obligada si tu asesor externo lo usa"; skipping coordination with the gestor leads to manual exports and imports. — [GuiaDeSoftware, 8 Sep 2026](https://www.guiadesoftware.com/blog/software-contabilidad-pymes-espana)
- Vendors design around the gestor:
  - Cuéntica lets you give a "gestor de confianza" (trusted gestor) access with their own credentials and permissions. — [Cuéntica](https://cuentica.com/precios/)
  - Declarando has a "Sin impuestos (tu gestor presenta)" ("tax filing excluded: your gestor files") price column. — [Declarando](https://declarando.es/precios)
  - Quipu has an "Asesorías" section and an affiliate programme. — [Quipu](https://getquipu.com/es/)
  - Anfix shares data in real time with the gestoría. — [El Independiente, 26 Jun 2026](https://www.elindependiente.com/eli/2026/06/26/programas-de-facturacion-con-verifactu-cuales-cumplen-y-cuales-elegir/)
- Owners treat the gestor as the trusted filter. A Forocoches user advises: ask your gestor and hang up when subsidy sellers call. — [Forocoches, 14 May 2025](https://forocoches.com/foro/showthread.php?t=10343559)
- Ex-VAT pricing is standard: "todos más IVA" ("all plus IVA") (FacturaDirecta, per [Infoautónomos](https://www.infoautonomos.com/blog/mejor-programa-facturacion-pymes/)); "más IVA" (Cuéntica); "+ IVA" (Declarando); "sin IVA" (Sesame); "Impuestos no incluidos" (DELSOL).
- Phone support is a tier feature, not a default (Holded: Premium only; Quipu: from Solution; Factorial: no urgent line). See VOX-11. Spanish local phone numbers appear on vendor pages (Quipu 935 245 580; Cuéntica 976 910 380; DELSOL 953 22 79 33; Declarando 964 800 301), observed 23 Oct 2026.
- Payment methods: none of the fetched pricing pages states whether it accepts SEPA direct debit or card. Quipu's trial needs no card ("Sin introducir tu tarjeta", "no card required"). One SumUp review disputes a SEPA debit ("no le di autorización SEPA al emisor", "I didn't give the issuer SEPA authorisation", 26 Sep 2026), which shows direct debits are used and disputed. — [Quipu](https://getquipu.com/es/plan-de-precios); [Trustpilot SumUp](https://es.trustpilot.com/review/sumup.com?stars=1&stars=2)

### VOX-21: The gestor decides the software; otherwise, double data entry
- Who: autónomos, micro <10, small 10–49.
- Where: national.
- Day-to-day reality: The owner's software choice is limited by what the gestor uses (for example a3). Otherwise the owner exports and the gestor re-imports or re-keys. On a3: "La opción obligada si tu asesor externo lo usa" ("the obligatory option if your external adviser uses it").
- Evidence:
  - GuiaDeSoftware (8 Sep 2026), as above. Single analytic source. — [GuiaDeSoftware](https://www.guiadesoftware.com/blog/software-contabilidad-pymes-espana)
  - Vendor gestor features: Cuéntica, Declarando, Quipu, Anfix (sources above).
  - A gestoría used Kit Digital to fund its client's fees. — [Forocoches, 31 Jan 2024](https://forocoches.com/foro/showthread.php?t=9844561)
- Scale: no figure found for the share of autónomos who use a gestor or for how often the gestor chooses the software.
- Cost/impact: duplicated data entry and export/import hours (not quantified).
- Driver & status: gestorías standardise on professional suites (a3 by Wolters Kluwer, among others). Status: ongoing.
- How it is solved today: CSV and Excel exports, gestor access to client software, or the gestor-mandated tool.
- Software-solvable?: yes. Connectors and export formats for the main gestoría suites, and gestor-side multi-client dashboards.
- Confidence: medium-low (qualitative; one analytic source plus vendor product design).

### Inferences
- Selling to gestorías as a channel, or at least making the product gestor-friendly with a3 or Sage export and free gestor seats, is probably decisive for any SME back-office SaaS in Spain.
- Spanish-language phone support is a visible differentiator: complaints cluster on its absence. WhatsApp support appears unoffered among the vendors checked, which is a testable hypothesis.
- Card versus SEPA preference is unknown. Offering both, plus annual payment with about 2 months free and proper ex-VAT invoices with the customer's NIF, matches the visible norms. The NIF requirement is standard Spanish invoicing practice, not verified from a source here.

### Gaps
- No data on SEPA direct debit vs card for SaaS payments by Spanish SMEs.
- No survey data on how much gestorías influence software purchases, or on the share of autónomos using a gestor.
- No evidence on owners' WhatsApp support preference.
- No sources on invoice expectations beyond ex-VAT pricing (for example NIF or intra-community VAT handling for foreign SaaS).

## Top pains
Ranked by severity times breadth, based on the evidence above:
1. **Kit Digital aftermath (VOX-15, VOX-16, VOX-17)**: more than 860,000 grants, consistent complaints of low-quality, overpriced deliverables, leftover monthly fees and exit charges, plus an open fraud investigation into signatures used without consent. It is national and timely: Oct 2026 is past most subsidised years.
2. **SaaS cancellation friction, auto-renewals and data held at exit (VOX-09, VOX-10)**: recurs across at least six popular Spanish SME vendors (Holded, Quipu, Factorial, Billin, Declarando, Legálitas), and B2B buyers have weak legal recourse.
3. **Verifactu-driven forced adoption, confusion and errors (VOX-12)**: covers nearly every invoicing business by 2027, carries 50,000 €/yr fines, and comes with conflicting dates and reported rejections. The market is crowded, so the pain is about reliability and trust, not price.
4. **Cyber-fraud and breaches (VOX-03, VOX-04)**: INCIBE incidents up 25.6% in 2025 and AEPD breach notifications up 163% in Jan–Aug 2026. BEC averages more than 15,000 € per incident (secondary source).
5. **Fake-review extortion and dependence on reviews (VOX-01, VOX-02)**: widespread among local businesses (strong signal from Catalonia); removal "rara vez funciona"; no Spanish scale data.
6. **Price creep, tier-gating and the support gap (VOX-08, VOX-11)**: invoice caps, monthly billing at about 2× annual, and phone support only on premium tiers hit the micro segment hardest.
7. **Gestoría cost and gatekeeping (VOX-19, VOX-21)**: 60–150 €/month for autónomos (estimate), and the gestor determines tool choice, which shapes the whole market.
8. **HR software minimums for micro-employers (VOX-14)**: a 15-employee minimum (Sesame) and modular pricing (Factorial) price out micro-firms. Demand depends on the labour-law status.

## Noise and dead ends
- **Fake AEPD inspectors and "coste cero" GDPR consultancies:** documented only in 2018–2019 ([Público, 11 Jun 2018](https://www.publico.es/sociedad/denuncian-fraude-consultoras-ofrecen-pymes.html): the AEPD investigated at least six firms using FUNDAE-funded schemes; [INCIBE, 20 Aug 2019](https://www.incibe.es/empresas/blog/cuidado-los-servicios-gratuitos-adecuacion-lopdgdd)). The AEPD press list I checked (Sep–Oct 2026) shows no recent warning. Weakly evidenced for 2024–2026.
- **Fake business directories and registry or trademark "invoices":** no Spanish 2024–2026 source found. The search returned unrelated results and the EUIPO misleading-invoices page returned 403. Unverified.
- **AEAT-impersonation "fake invoice" emails:** the only source is from 2020 ([Escudo Digital](https://www.escudodigital.com/interior/estafas-suplantan-a-la-agencia-tributaria-para-reclamar-facturas-falsas-a-empresas.html)). Folded into the general phishing pain (VOX-03).
- **Bank maintenance fees as a SaaS opportunity:** the data covers consumer accounts, free alternatives exist (a free Bankinter account per Asufin; SumUp's free business account), and the problem is a banking product rather than software. Already cheaply solvable by switching.
- **Energy mis-selling to SMEs:** evidence is old (2017–2019 CNMC data) or a single 2023 complaint without confirmed business status. Also overlaps with other researchers' cost scope. Low confidence.
- **Insurance renewal hikes:** the only hit was a consumer pet-insurance complaint (more than 33% rise, OCU, Apr 2026, [snippet](https://www.ocu.org/reclamar/lista-reclamaciones-publicas/subida-abusiva!!-m-C3-A1s-de-un-33-25/5b110bad09d63eec76)). Not SME evidence.
- **Unpaid Kit Digital agents (VOX-18):** real, but an administrative payment problem in a closed programme. Not software-solvable beyond minor paperwork automation.
- **The Kit Digital "free laptop" motive:** forum users say many applicants wanted the hardware (for example the 1,000 € laptop). Not a software pain.
- **Pure "cheaper invoicing app":** already solved at 0–10 €/month (the AEAT app, FacturaScripts, Billin, Contasimple, FacturaDirecta, Verifactu-only tiers). Price alone is a dead end; differentiation must come from fairness, support or integration.
- **Advertising and customer-acquisition costs (Google and Meta Ads):** no Spanish evidence captured. The only signal is supply-side local-SEO pitches on Forocoches with no prices. Unresolved, not disproven.
