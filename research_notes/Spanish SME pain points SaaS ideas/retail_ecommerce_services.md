## Local retail: closures, rent, footfall, platform competition, shoplifting, payments, opening hours, digitalisation

### Takeaway
Spain's neighbourhood retail is shrinking. UPTA counts about 13,600 net small-shop closures in 2025, and the Comunidad de Madrid lost 14% of its proximity shops between 2020 and 2024. The causes are rent shocks at lease renewal, owners retiring without successors, rising fixed costs and platform competition, and none of these is mainly a software problem. The best-quantified operational pain is theft: €2.8bn a year, or 1.1% of turnover, with 51% committed by repeat offenders. The multirreincidencia reform (LO 1/2026, in force 10 April 2026) now rewards shops that report every incident. Card fees of about 0.3–2% and the 30-day "precio anterior" rule affect every shop but are less acute.

### RET-01: Steady attrition of neighbourhood shops (cierre del comercio de proximidad)
- **Who:** Independent retail run by autónomos and micro-businesses (<10 staff): bakeries, butchers, newsagents, jewellers, appliance shops, haberdashers, stationers, bookshops.
- **Where:** National. Best documented in the Comunidad de Madrid and the city of Barcelona.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Owners face rising fixed costs (rent, energy, compliance) against flat neighbourhood demand. Many close at retirement or lease renewal rather than reinvest. The Madrid retail confederation (COCEM) complains that small shops carry big-store obligations: *"Se le pide lo mismo a una panadería de barrio que a una gran superficie"* ("a neighbourhood bakery is asked for the same as a hypermarket").
- **Evidence:**
  - UPTA counted 13,586 fewer small retail businesses in 2025, about 1,132 per month. December 2025 alone lost 2,347, against 1,748 in December 2024. There were 716,637 retail affiliates in December 2025, and UPTA expects more than 12,000 closures in 2026 without action. — [Talent24h/OKDIARIO, 23 Jan 2026](https://talent24h.okdiario.com/2025-pone-en-jaque-al-pequeno-comercio-de-espana-con-mas-de-13-500-negocios-en-dicho-ano/). The same article also cites "586 small shops closed" in December with no explanation, which conflicts with the 2,347 figure.
  - UPTA's president repeats "más de 13.500 pequeños negocios se han cerrado el año pasado" and blames "la especulación inmobiliaria" (property speculation). — [El Español, 17 Apr 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/sociedad/20260417/espana-pierde-comercios-cierran-negocios-subida-alquiler-genera-crisis-sector/1003744206489_0.html). This is the same originating source as above, not an independent one.
  - INE data show the Comunidad de Madrid's "comercios de barrio" falling from 50,853 (2020) to 43,769 (2024), down 14% or 7,084 shops. The steepest year was 2023, with about 6,000 closures. COCEM sector data: butchers −24% over 5 years, appliance shops −14% over 2 years, jewellers −20%, press/newsagents −62%. — [Madrid Informa, 19 Jul 2025, updated 9 Mar 2026](https://madridinforma.es/?p=38863). The headline frames the 7,084 drop as "one year", but the data cover 2020–2024.
  - In Barcelona, at least 10 emblematic shops closed in 2025, including Ferretería Lanza (1928), Papelería Lesseps (1909), Perfumería Regia (97 years), Bolsos Ballester (115 years) and Muebles Cáceres (80 years). — [Ara, 3 Jan 2026](https://es.ara.cat/sociedad/barcelona/barcelona-esfuma-comercios-emblematicos-han-cerrado-2025_130_5608130.html)
  - El Español's claim of "at least 400" small-business closures in Jan–Apr 2026 is inconsistent with UPTA's monthly rate. Treat it as unreliable.
- **Scale:** About 13.6k net closures a year nationally (UPTA), out of 716,637 retail affiliates (Dec 2025).
- **Cost/impact:** Business closures. The causes UPTA cites are rising fixed costs, tax pressure, "competencia desigual con grandes plataformas" (unequal competition with big platforms) and missing public policy.
- **Driver & status:** Market forces: rent, costs, online competition, demographics. No single regulation drives it. Status: ongoing in 2026, per UPTA's forecast.
- **How it is solved today:** No tool evidence found. Municipal and regional support schemes belong to the subsidies researcher.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. The main drivers (rent, succession, demand) are structural. Software can only cut admin cost or lift footfall at the margin.
- **Confidence:** High on the trend; medium on exact counts, which come from UPTA and INE as reported by the press.

### RET-02: Commercial rent shocks at lease renewal
- **Who:** Shops, bars and personal-service micro-businesses (including tattoo studios) on short commercial leases.
- **Where:** City-specific. Madrid and Barcelona (Raval, Barri Gòtic, Sant Antoni, Gràcia).
- **Day-to-day reality:** Businesses that are still profitable close or are sold on (traspaso) when the landlord doubles or quadruples the rent at renewal. The Raval traders' association: *"estamos perdiendo tejido empresarial, está quedando un monocultivo de gente de grandes franquicias"* ("we are losing our business fabric; what's left is a monoculture of big franchises").
- **Evidence:**
  - Average commercial rent in Madrid and Barcelona is €19.2–21.3/m², or €1,536–1,704 a month for 80 m². A Raval restaurant's rent rose from €800 to €1,400, with a renewal quote of more than €2,400. A tattoo studio open 8 years saw rent go from €1,600 to €2,100, and the owner sold the business even though it *"era rentable"* ("it was profitable"). — [El Español, 17 Apr 2026](https://www.elespanol.com/sociedad/20260417/espana-pierde-comercios-cierran-negocios-subida-alquiler-genera-crisis-sector/1003744206489_0.html). The article's national €15/m² figure is inconsistently labelled.
  - Bar Kasparo (Barcelona) closed after 30 years when the landlord wanted to quadruple the rent. Discos Revólver closed citing very high rent. La Casa de las Zapatillas had already been pushed out of its premises by property pressure in 2022. — [Ara, 3 Jan 2026](https://es.ara.cat/sociedad/barcelona/barcelona-esfuma-comercios-emblematicos-han-cerrado-2025_130_5608130.html)
  - In Madrid, rising rents, unaffordable business transfers ("traspasos inasumibles") and premises left empty for years are cited as closure causes. — [Madrid Informa, Jul 2025](https://madridinforma.es/?p=38863)
- **Scale:** Not quantified nationally (gap).
- **Cost/impact:** +31% (tattoo studio), +75% (restaurant), up to 4× (Kasparo), in individual cases.
- **Driver & status:** Real-estate and tourism pressure. Commercial leases are freely negotiated under the Ley de Arrendamientos Urbanos; this is general knowledge, not verified in this session. No pending regulation found.
- **How it is solved today:** Renegotiation, moving or selling the business. No software evidence.
- **Software-solvable?:** Mostly no. At most: rent benchmarks, renewal alerts and relocation search.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Case evidence comes from two outlets; the rent averages from one.

### RET-03: No successor: retiring owners cannot sell or hand over the shop
- **Who:** Long-established family shops (autónomos and micro).
- **Where:** Barcelona and Madrid documented; likely national.
- **Day-to-day reality:** An owner reaches retirement (one aged 82) with no heir or buyer, and the shop's goodwill vanishes when it closes.
- **Evidence:** In Barcelona: Ferretería Lanza (owner retired at 82, no successor), Papelería Lesseps (no buyer found; customers had shifted to Amazon), Bolsos Ballester (no generational succession after 115 years) and Muebles Cáceres (retirement). — [Ara, 3 Jan 2026](https://es.ara.cat/sociedad/barcelona/barcelona-esfuma-comercios-emblematicos-han-cerrado-2025_130_5608130.html). In Madrid, lack of generational succession is cited as a closure driver. — [Madrid Informa, Jul 2025](https://madridinforma.es/?p=38863)
- **Scale:** Not quantified (gap).
- **Cost/impact:** Lost sale value and closure.
- **Driver & status:** Demographics and market. No regulation.
- **How it is solved today:** No evidence found on transfer marketplaces or gestorías.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Matching buyers and sellers, and valuation, could help. The core problem is that many of these businesses are no longer attractive to buy.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low (qualitative, two sources).

### RET-04: Repeat shoplifting (multirreincidencia), staff aggression, and the evidence burden under the new penal reform
- **Who:** All physical retail; food retail is most affected and small shops suffer most.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** The same people steal repeatedly. Staff report more verbal and physical aggression, and security guards are hard to hire. Since 10 April 2026, a petty thief can face the prison penalty of art. 234.1 CP once they have three prior final convictions. In practice a conviction record exists only if incidents are reported and prosecuted (inference).
- **Evidence:**
  - The Barómetro del Hurto 2025 (Checkpoint Systems and AECOC) puts unknown loss at €2,817M, or 1.1% of turnover, up from 0.74% (about €1,800M). Breakdown: external theft 59%, internal theft 18%, administrative errors 15%, supplier fraud 9%. 51% of thefts are by repeat offenders (3 or more a year), 48% of whom are under 30. 74% of respondents say aggression towards staff has risen a lot, and 65% find security staff increasingly hard to hire. — [The Objective, 4 Nov 2025](https://theobjective.com/economia/2025-11-04/hurtos-perdida-2817-millones-comercios-espanoles/)
  - AECOC (via Europa Press) gives more than €2,800M a year and the same 51% figure, and adds that small shops suffer on average 30% more thefts because they have fewer security resources. It welcomed the reform's removal of the €400 cumulative-value threshold. — [Última Hora, 5 Dec 2025](https://www.ultimahora.es/noticias/comunidades/2025/12/05/2525909/aecoc-celebra-reforma-multirreincidencia-causa-perdidas-comerciales-2800-millones.html). Conflict: Ara cites €2,917M for the same barometer **(snippet)**. — [Ara](https://es.ara.cat/economia/comercio/aceite-perfumes-ropa-interior-son-productos-robados-tiendas_1_5551266.html). Its headline names olive oil, perfume and underwear as the most-stolen goods.
  - **Ley Orgánica 1/2026, de 8 de abril, en materia de multirreincidencia** was published in the BOE on 9 April 2026 and entered into force on 10 April 2026 ("al día siguiente de su publicación"). Its main changes:
    - Art. 234 applies the art. 234.1 penalty when the offender has at least three final convictions for offences under the same title, at least one of them a delito leve.
    - Art. 22.8 still excludes prior delitos leves from reincidencia, except where they form part of the multirreincidencia aggravation (hurto and estafa).
    - A new art. 235.10.º aggravates theft of mobile phones.
    - New LECrim art. 105.3 lets local authorities bring criminal actions for theft.
    - LECrim art. 544 bis allows precautionary bans on going to specified places.
    — [BOE consolidated text](https://www.boe.es/eli/es/lo/2026/04/08/1/con). A legal commentary says repeat thieves of goods under €400 can now get 6–18 months' prison instead of a fine **(snippet)**. — [LegalToday, 10 Apr 2026](https://www.legaltoday.com/opinion/articulos-de-opinion/la-reforma-penal-de-la-multirreincidencia-un-nuevo-modelo-de-respuesta-frente-a-la-delincuencia-reiterada-2026-04-10/). Conflict: the same snippet says the rule needs "at least three thefts for a joint amount over €400". That contradicts AECOC (the €400 cumulative threshold is removed) and the BOE text (three prior final convictions, no value threshold mentioned). Rely on the BOE text.
- **Scale:** All physical retail; €2.8bn a year.
- **Cost/impact:** 1.1% of turnover, and 30% more thefts in small shops.
- **Driver & status:** LO 1/2026, **in force since 10 April 2026**.
- **How it is solved today:** Electronic article surveillance (EAS) tags, CCTV and security guards; 65% report trouble hiring guards. Checkpoint Systems, a tag vendor, co-authors the barometer. No prices found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Incident logging, packaging CCTV clips, preparing police reports (denuncias) consistently, and district-level alerts (within GDPR limits) all help. Physical deterrence and policing do not.
- **Confidence:** High on loss figures and the law; medium on small-shop specifics.

### RET-05: Card-acceptance fees and a fragmented payments stack, with Bizum arriving at the till
- **Who:** All retail and personal services. Low-volume autónomos are most exposed.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Shops pay a percentage on every card payment plus terminal fees. Many nudge customers towards cash or Bizum. Bizum in-store payments started on 18 May 2026, unevenly, and their pricing has not been published.
- **Evidence:**
  - Merchants pay 0.5–2% per card transaction depending on business type and volume. Bizum's in-store pricing "aún no se ha hecho público". Bizum has more than 27M users. — [Diario de León, 18 May 2026](https://www.diariodeleon.es/sociedad/260518/2085361/bizum-llega-comercio-fisico-desafia-duopolio-tarjetas-tradicionales.html)
  - Card fees are 0.3–1.5% depending on contract. Bizum entered store and supermarket terminals from 18 May 2026 with a staggered rollout and more than 28M users. Its fees are described as "más liviana" (lighter) than cards, with no figure given. — [Moncloa.com, 29 Apr 2026](https://www.moncloa.com/2026/04/29/bizum-supermercados-tiendas-18-mayo-3374007/)
  - SumUp Spain charges 1.49% per in-person card payment on pay-as-you-go, or 0% when the customer pays with a SumUp card. Pagos Plus costs €19/month, TPV Plus (POS) €49/month and Facturas Plus €10/month. — [SumUp pricing, accessed Oct 2026](https://www.sumup.com/es-es/precios/)
  - Shopify POS charges 2.6% + 10¢ per in-person payment on Basic, and POS Pro costs $89/month per location. The page showed USD to our fetcher, so EUR prices were not seen. — [Shopify pricing, accessed Oct 2026](https://www.shopify.com/es-es/precios)
  - BBVA has added Bizum to its Android terminals; a second terminal costs €3.5 + IVA **(snippet, single source)**. — [Finantres](https://finantresnoticias.com/negocios-y-autonomos/bizum-para-empresas-bbva-lo-lleva-al-tpv-fisico/)
- **Scale:** Every card-accepting business; no count found.
- **Cost/impact:** Illustration: at 1.49%, a shop taking €10,000 a month by card pays about €149 a month.
- **Driver & status:** Market pricing by acquirers. Bizum in-store **launched 18 May 2026** (market development, not regulation).
- **How it is solved today:** Bank terminals, SumUp, Shopify POS, Bizum and cash.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Fee audits and steering customers to cheaper payment methods (Bizum, account-to-account) are possible. The fees themselves are set by acquirers.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Sources disagree on the range (0.3–1.5% vs 0.5–2%).

### RET-06: Ultra-low-cost cross-border platforms (Temu, Shein, AliExpress) undercutting local shops
- **Who:** Retailers of fashion, accessories, toys, homeware and gadgets (micro and small), and Spanish online SMEs in the same categories.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Local shops compete with parcels that entered duty-free until July 2026, often priced below local wholesale. Customers browse in-store and then buy online.
- **Evidence:**
  - About 12 million parcels a day reach the EU from Temu, Shein and similar platforms. There were 4,600 million shipments in 2024, 91% from China. The European Commission counts about 5,900 million low-value items that reached consumers duty-free in 2025. — [OCU, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.ocu.org/consumo-familia/compras-online/noticias/tasa-aduana-paquetes-baratos)
  - A fixed EU duty of €3 per product category now applies to parcels under €150 from outside the EU, from 1 July 2026 until about 1 July 2028. — [OCU, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.ocu.org/consumo-familia/compras-online/noticias/tasa-aduana-paquetes-baratos). RTVC calls it temporary until the 2028 customs reform **(snippet)**. — [RTVC, 2 Jul 2026](https://rtvc.es/entra-en-vigor-la-tasa-de-3-euros-para-compras-en-plataformas-como-shein-temu-y-aliexpress-2-julio-2026/). Euronews describes it as €3 per item, which conflicts on the calculation **(snippet)**. — [Euronews, 1 Jul 2026](https://es.euronews.com/my-europe/2026/07/01/se-acabo-comprar-sin-impuestos-en-shein-temu-y-aliexpress-la-ue-impone-un-nuevo-recargo). A separate handling fee "by 1 Nov 2026" appears in a single, unverified source **(snippet)**. — [Revista Mercado](https://revistamercado.do/technology/devices/el-fin-de-las-compras-baratas-en-shein-y-temu-la-medida-arancelaria-en-europa-que-frena-su-exito/)
  - UPTA names "competencia desigual con grandes plataformas" as a closure cause. — [Talent24h, 23 Jan 2026](https://talent24h.okdiario.com/2025-pone-en-jaque-al-pequeno-comercio-de-espana-con-mas-de-13-500-negocios-en-dicho-ano/). Papelería Lesseps' customers moved to Amazon. — [Ara, 3 Jan 2026](https://es.ara.cat/sociedad/barcelona/barcelona-esfuma-comercios-emblematicos-han-cerrado-2025_130_5608130.html)
- **Scale:** National; billions of items a year EU-wide.
- **Cost/impact:** Impact on Spanish shops' sales not quantified (gap).
- **Driver & status:** EU €3 customs duty **in force since 1 Jul 2026** (temporary). Handling fee unverified.
- **How it is solved today:** Not applicable.
- **Software-solvable?:** No. This is a market and trade-policy force.
- **Confidence:** High on volumes and the duty; low on the Spanish impact.

### RET-07: Thin or decaying web presence after Kit Digital
- **Who:** Micro-businesses (<10) and autónomos in retail and services.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Most micro firms still have no website. Subsidised sites were often built fast with little SEO. After the subsidised period, owners either pay for maintenance or let the site lapse; the lapsing part is partly inference.
- **Evidence:**
  - INE figures (published Apr 2025), as reported by Asest: 29.6% of companies with fewer than 10 employees had a website, against 33.2% in 2024. That leaves about 70%, or roughly 2.4M autónomos and micro firms, without one. The same article claims 37.0% a year after 33.2%, an internal contradiction. It also cites Kit Digital vouchers of up to €12,000 and a typical agency site at €600–1,500 plus monthly maintenance (unsourced). — [Asest (undated)](https://asest.es/story/pymes-y-autonomos-sin-web/)
  - On Kit Digital providers: *"la calidad de los entregables suele ser la sacrificada en pro de entregar cuanto antes"* ("the quality of deliverables is usually sacrificed in order to deliver as fast as possible"). The article also says SEO management can be "prohibitivo" (prohibitively expensive) for small firms. — [Emprendedores, 27 Oct 2023](https://emprendedores.es/notas-de-prensa/el-kit-digital-no-es-la-solucion-a-la-digitalizacion-del-marketing-seo/)
  - An individual's opinion: *"muchas de esas webs están completamente abandonadas, desactualizadas o simplemente… no generan resultados"* ("many of those sites are completely abandoned, outdated or simply bring no results") **(snippet; opinion)**. — [LinkedIn profile post](https://es.linkedin.com/in/davidmn)
- **Scale:** About 70% of micro firms have no website (Asest's reading of INE).
- **Cost/impact:** €600–1,500 to build plus maintenance (unsourced).
- **Driver & status:** Kit Digital, funded by NextGenerationEU. **The programme's 2026 status was not verified**: the acelerapyme.gob.es page returned 403.
- **How it is solved today:** Agencies (agentes digitalizadores), website builders and Google Business Profile.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Low-cost managed sites, Google Business Profile and review management. It is a crowded market.
- **Confidence:** Low-medium. The INE figure is second-hand and the abandonment evidence is anecdotal.

### RET-08: Sunday and holiday trading rules, and fights over tourist zones (ZGAT)
- **Who:** Small shops competing with large chains.
- **Where:** Regional and municipal: Comunitat Valenciana (Benidorm, Alicante), Canarias (Arafo, Santa Úrsula), La Rioja, Castilla y León.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Small retailers oppose declarations of zonas de gran afluencia turística (ZGAT) that let big stores open on Sundays. They also have to track annual lists of permitted Sundays, which differ by region and municipality.
- **Evidence:**
  - Benidorm 2026: shops without free trading hours may open 11 Sundays and holidays. Shops under 300 m² have general freedom of hours, unless they belong to non-SME groups **(snippet)**. — [Benidorm, Horarios comerciales 2026 (PDF, Feb 2026)](https://contenidos.benidorm.org/sites/default/files/descargas/2026-02/HORARIOS%20COMERCIALES%202026.pdf)
  - Alicante is a ZGAT with an extended period from 15 June to 5 January. The Observatorio del Comercio Valenciano proposes 11 Sundays **(snippet)**. — [Ayuntamiento de Alicante](https://www.alicante.es/es/node/46360)
  - In Canarias, Arafo's consultation on a ZGAT declaration drew opposition from unions, neighbourhood associations and small local retail. In Santa Úrsula the PP accused the local government of abandoning small retail **(snippet)**. — [Diario de Avisos, Jun 2026](https://diariodeavisos.elespanol.com/2026/06/arafo-apertura-dominical-poligonos/)
  - La Rioja allows 10 Sundays or holidays a year. — [La Rioja](https://www.larioja.org/comercio/es/apertura-festivos-domingos/festivos-habiles-2026)
- **Scale:** A national patchwork.
- **Cost/impact:** Not quantified.
- **Driver & status:** Regional trading-hours rules on top of the state framework; the state law was not verified in this session. 2026 calendars **in force**.
- **How it is solved today:** Regional calendars and lobbying by associations.
- **Software-solvable?:** No. This is political; software could offer at most a calendar lookup.
- **Confidence:** Medium on the rules; low as a quantified pain.

### RET-09: Compliance with the 30-day "precio anterior" rule on discounts (rebajas, Black Friday)
- **Who:** Every physical and online retailer that advertises price reductions.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Every advertised discount must be measured against the lowest price charged in the previous 30 days. Shops without price-history records risk fines (the risk is inference; no enforcement data found).
- **Evidence:** Ley 7/1996 art. 20.1 defines the "precio anterior" as *"el menor que hubiese sido aplicado sobre productos idénticos en los treinta días precedentes"* ("the lowest price applied to identical products in the preceding thirty days"). The paragraph was modified by RDL 24/2021, effective 28 May 2022. Fines: leve up to €6,000; grave €6,000–30,000; muy grave €30,000–900,000 (art. 68). — [BOE, consolidated Ley 7/1996](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-1072). Single official source; no data found on enforcement campaigns.
- **Scale:** All retailers.
- **Cost/impact:** Fines as above.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 7/1996 art. 20, **in force** (current wording since 28 May 2022).
- **How it is solved today:** Not researched (POS and e-commerce price-history features).
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Automatic logging of the 30-day lowest price and compliant labels or badges.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The rule is verified; how much it hurts is unverified.

### RET-10: WhatsApp selling and reminders become metered (Meta per-message pricing)
- **Who:** Shops and service businesses that send notifications, appointment reminders or promotions through the WhatsApp Business Platform (API) via a provider. Users of the free WhatsApp Business app are not affected.
- **Where:** National (Spain is a standalone market on Meta's rate card).
- **Day-to-day reality:** Reminders, order updates and promotions are billed per delivered message. From 1 Oct 2026, utility messages sent inside an open 24-hour service window are charged too.
- **Evidence:** Per-message pricing since 1 Jul 2025. Spain's marketing rate rose on 1 Jul 2026. Since 1 Oct 2026, utility messages inside an open service window, and service messages beyond the free 1,000 a month, are chargeable. — [Meta for Developers, WhatsApp pricing, accessed Oct 2026](https://developers.facebook.com/docs/whatsapp/pricing/). Booksy includes 100 marketing SMS a month and prices bigger campaigns on request. — [Pabau, 8 Oct 2026](https://pabau.com/es/blog/booksy-precio/)
- **Scale:** Unknown. There is no data on how many Spanish SMEs use the API rather than the free app (gap).
- **Cost/impact:** Spain's per-message rates sit in a rate-card file that was not captured.
- **Driver & status:** Meta pricing (market). Changes on 1 Jul 2025, 1 Jul 2026 and **1 Oct 2026 (in effect)**.
- **How it is solved today:** WhatsApp business solution providers, or manual use of the free app.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Message-cost optimisation and channel mix; the rates are set by Meta.
- **Confidence:** Medium-low (single official source; adoption unknown).

### Inferences
- LO 1/2026 makes prior convictions the trigger for prison. That should create demand for tools that log every incident and produce police-ready evidence packs, so shops (and, under the new LECrim art. 105.3, local councils) can build cases against repeat offenders. This is interpretation; no source says it.
- The closure drivers (rent, succession, platform competition) are not things software fixes. In local retail, software opportunities are narrower: cutting payment costs, automating compliance (the 30-day price rule) and loss prevention.
- Bizum in-store (from May 2026) may push card fees down. Fee-audit tools could lose their value proposition if Bizum undercuts cards.
- The fall in micro-firm website share (33.2% to 29.6%) despite Kit Digital fits the idea that subsidised sites lapsed, but the reported figures contradict each other. Check the INE primary data before relying on this.

### Gaps
- Confederación Española de Comercio (CEC) closure data, INE retail trade index (ICM) and the INE ICT-in-companies survey were not consulted directly; the search budget ran out.
- No evidence gathered on stock management, loyalty programmes, Instagram selling, footfall measurement, or closures and rents in Valencia, Sevilla, Zaragoza, Málaga, Bilbao, Palma, Las Palmas, Murcia, Alicante, Valladolid, Vigo or A Coruña.
- Kit Digital's status in 2026 (deadlines, last segments) is unverified (403 on acelerapyme.gob.es).
- Prices for loss-prevention technology (EAS tags, CCTV, guards) not found.
- Not verified: whether a duty to accept cash payments was adopted, and the Ley de Servicios de Atención a la Clientela. Both overlap with the cross-sector compliance researcher.

## E-commerce SMEs: shipping and returns, marketplaces, fraud, consumer law, packaging EPR, plastic tax, Canarias/Ceuta/Melilla, OSS, advertising

### Takeaway
For online SMEs, the best-evidenced pains are returns (€13.3bn of online returns in 2025; 23.7% of non-food orders; serial and late returners cause 47% of returns) and transit damage (15.2M items returned a year, over €1bn). On top sits a growing compliance stack, all of it in force and all of it software-shaped: packaging extended producer responsibility (EPR) under RD 1055/2022, plus the EU packaging regulation (PPWR) since 12 Aug 2026; the plastic packaging tax; the European Accessibility Act (EAA) since 28 Jun 2025; and the General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR) since 13 Dec 2024. Shipping to Canarias, Ceuta and Melilla is still blocked or surcharged. Amazon's fees were trimmed in Dec 2025–Jan 2026 but a 1.5% FBA surcharge was added in April 2026.

### RET-11: Returns: high volume, serial and late returners, and pressure to keep returns free
- **Who:** Online SMEs in fashion, footwear and electronics (micro to medium).
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** About a quarter of non-food orders come back. Consumers punish shops that charge for returns. A minority of serial and late returners consume a large share of margin.
- **Evidence:**
  - ZigZag and Retail Economics estimate €13,300M of online returns by end-2025. 23.7% of non-food orders are returned (clothing 31%, footwear 27%, electronics 23%). Serial returners are 11% of shoppers but account for 23% of returns (about €2,600M), with a return rate near 48%. Late returners are 14% of shoppers and 24% of returns. The average return takes 9.1 days, and 32% come back after 10 days. — [Law&Trends, 28 Oct 2025](https://www.lawandtrends.com/noticias/consumidores/las-devoluciones-online-en-espana-superan-los-13-300-millones-en-2025-1.html). ZigZag sells returns software, so it has an interest in the topic.
  - Per idealo, 35% of Spaniards might stop buying from shops that charge for returns **(snippet; date not seen)**. — [Forbes](https://forbes.es/ultima-hora/255575/tres-de-cada-diez-espanoles-podrian-dejar-de-comprar-en-tiendas-que-cobren-por-las-devoluciones-online/). Per Veepee and IESE, only 21% are willing to pay for returns, and never more than 10% of the price **(snippet; 2022)**. — [Modaes](https://www.modaes.com/entorno/las-devoluciones-siguen-frenando-el-online-solo-el-21-esta-dispuestos-a-pagarlas)
- **Scale:** All online fashion and electronics sellers.
- **Cost/impact:** €13.3bn of returned goods value. Handling cost per return not found.
- **Driver & status:** Consumer expectations of free returns, plus the statutory 14-day withdrawal right (general knowledge, not re-verified). **In force.**
- **How it is solved today:** Returns portals and multi-carrier tools: ShippyPro Professional at €207.20/month for 4,000 labels; Outvio at an estimated ~€375/month (€4,500/year), a figure from its competitor ShippyPro. — [ShippyPro comparison, Apr 2026](https://www.shippypro.com/es/shippypro-vs-outvio). Sendcloud has a free plan and a "Lite" plan at €28/month billed annually **(snippet; page blocked)**. — [Hackceleration, Jun 2026](https://hackceleration.com/es/labs/sendcloud-precio)
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Returns analytics, serial-returner detection, exchange-first flows and fit/sizing tools.
- **Confidence:** High on volume; medium on cost.

### RET-12: Parcels damaged in transit, and the packaging trade-off
- **Who:** Online retailers of all sizes.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** About 5.5% of parcels arrive damaged. Retailers absorb the reshipments and returns while also being pushed to use less packaging (RET-15).
- **Evidence:** A DS Smith study of about 100 executives at Spanish online retailers found:
  - 15.2M items returned each year because of shipping damage, and 5.5% of parcels arriving damaged.
  - 21% of shoppers received a damaged item in 2025, and 88% of retailers call it a problem.
  - Total cost above €1,000M a year (average damaged item worth €68). Retailers spend on average €30,876 a year handling damaged returns, and large distributors more than €500,000.
  - Causes: rough handling 57%, packaging deficiencies 27%, weather and temperature 16%. 58% of consumers would be less likely to reorder after a damaged delivery.
  — [Alimarket, 16 Jan 2026](https://www.alimarket.es/envase/noticia/419911/el-ecommerce-en-espana-cierra-el-ano-con-15-2-m-de-articulos-devueltos-por-danos-durante-el-envio). This is a single source, sponsored by a packaging maker.
- **Scale/Cost:** As above.
- **Driver & status:** Carrier handling (market). PPWR empty-space limits mostly apply from 2030 (see RET-15).
- **How it is solved today:** Carrier claims and packaging suppliers. No tool prices found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Automating carrier claims, damage analytics by carrier and route, and choosing the right box.
- **Confidence:** Medium (single, interested source).

### RET-13: Marketplace fee stacks (Amazon) and opaque margins
- **Who:** Online SMEs selling on Amazon.es.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Sellers stack a monthly fee, a referral fee, fulfilment and storage fees and surcharges, so the margin per product is hard to see. (Ad costs not found.)
- **Evidence:** Amazon.es fees:
  - Professional plan €39/month; Individual plan €0.99 per item.
  - Referral fees are typically 8–15%. Fashion: 5% up to €15, 10% from €15 to €20, 15% above €20. Home: 8% up to €20, then 15%. Beauty: 8% up to €10, then 15%. Electronics: 7%. Minimum €0.30 per item.
  - FBA storage costs €19.99–27.54 per m³ a month in Jan–Sep and €29.23–52.20 in Oct–Dec.
  - A 1.5% fuel and logistics surcharge applies to FBA fees from 17 Apr 2026.
  - When Amazon refunds the referral fee on a refund, it keeps the lower of €5 or 20% of that fee.
  - Fee cuts on 15 Dec 2025 (clothing referral 8%→5% up to €15) and 5 Jan 2026 (home 15%→8% up to €20; low-price FBA rates extended to €20).
  — [Amazon Sell Spain, accessed Oct 2026](https://sell.amazon.es/precios). Single official source. Miravia and other marketplaces not found.
- **Scale:** Not found (gap: number of Spanish SMEs selling on Amazon).
- **Cost/impact:** Commonly 15% referral plus fulfilment and storage, before advertising.
- **Driver & status:** Market. Fee changes Dec 2025–Apr 2026.
- **How it is solved today:** Not researched (Seller Central reports, third-party profit tools).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Per-product profitability and fee audits; Amazon sets the fees.
- **Confidence:** Medium (fees verified; seller complaints not captured).

### RET-14: Selling and shipping to the Canary Islands, Ceuta and Melilla
- **Who:** Online SMEs on the peninsula that sell to the islands, plus Canarian buyers and businesses.
- **Where:** Regional: Canarias, Ceuta, Melilla.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Many shops simply refuse: *"No realizamos envíos a Canarias, Ceuta y Melilla"*. Buyers face courier "gestión aduanera" (customs handling) fees even below €150. Sellers have to deal with the islands' own taxes (IGIC, AIEM, and IPSI in Ceuta and Melilla) and with customs declarations (DUA).
- **Evidence:**
  - Per Idealo, more than 60% of online shops do not ship to Canarias and only about 4% offer urgent delivery.
  - IGIC's general rate is 7%; AIEM's general rate is 5%. The DUA has been waived for goods under €150 since 2017, and the Canarian tax agency extended the IGIC exemption under €150 in June 2021.
  - Couriers still charge handling fees. Examples: €15 of fees on a €30 dress, and €30 of "other taxes" on shoes.
  — [Xataka, 16 Jan 2022](https://www.xataka.com/servicios/comprar-online-canarias-se-ha-convertido-dolor-cabeza-para-usuarios-asi-odisea-para-adquirir-productos-islas) (older source).
  - A petition started 17 Jul 2026 says couriers' "gestión aduanera" and "presentación al despacho" fees *"a veces duplican el valor del producto"* ("sometimes double the product's value"). It asks for a ban on such fees under €150 and on transport geoblocking. It had only 3 signatures, so it is weak evidence of salience but confirms the practice continues. — [Change.org, Jul 2026](https://www.change.org/p/envíos-justos-y-sin-bloqueos-para-canarias-ceuta-y-melilla)
- **Scale:** Over 60% of shops not shipping (2021–22 data). Number of affected buyers not cited.
- **Cost/impact:** €15–30 per parcel in documented cases; lost sales for sellers who don't ship.
- **Driver & status:** Canarias sits outside the EU VAT area (IGIC/AIEM under its special tax regime); Ceuta and Melilla use IPSI. **In force; no 2024–2026 change verified**, as Canarian tax agency updates could not be checked.
- **How it is solved today:** Correos, customs agents and couriers' DUA services. No prices found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes or partly. Landed cost at checkout (IGIC/AIEM), automated customs data, and choosing carriers for the islands.
- **Confidence:** Medium on the structure; low on current 2026 numbers.

### RET-15: Packaging EPR: registration, annual declarations, and the EU packaging regulation (PPWR) from 12 Aug 2026
- **Who:** Anyone who packs goods or first places packaged goods on the Spanish market, including online shops shipping their own products, importers and intra-EU buyers. Marketplaces and couriers are liable in some cases.
- **Where:** National, plus the EU layer.
- **Day-to-day reality:** A seller needs a producer registration number (it must appear on invoices), proof of membership of a collective compliance scheme (SCRAP), packaging data by material filed every 31 March, and since 12 Aug 2026 the PPWR declarations of conformity and marking. Distributors must refuse products from unregistered producers.
- **Evidence:**
  - From the consolidated text of RD 1055/2022 ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-22690); no later amendments shown, accessed Oct 2026):
    - Producers had to register in the envases section of the Registro de Productores de Producto within 3 months. A single, simplified registration applies to service packaging and to producers placing under 50,000 kg of commercial/industrial packaging. The registration number must appear on invoices and commercial documents (art. 15).
    - Annual market data are due before 31 March, in simplified form for producers under 15 t a year, certain e-commerce platforms and some first distributors (art. 16).
    - Producers established outside Spain must appoint a Spanish authorised representative; otherwise the first distributor is liable. In distance sales, couriers take on the financial and reporting duties for transport packaging, and e-commerce platforms do the same for third-party sellers' goods (art. 17).
    - Distributors, in store or at a distance, may only sell products from producers that have a registration number (arts. 28–30).
    - Producers not previously in a scheme had to set up schemes by 31 Dec 2024 (transitional provision DT3.1). New marking duties (art. 13) apply from 1 Jan 2025 (final provision DF5).
    - Shops of 300 m² or more must inform customers on packaging (from 1 Jan 2023). Food stores of 400 m² or more must devote at least 20% of sales area to unpackaged goods (art. 7.4).
  - The PPWR (EU 2025/40) applies from 12 Aug 2026 and repeals Directive 94/62/EC. It requires a Declaration of Conformity and marking that links the packaging to it and identifies the responsible operator. Empty-space limits, recycled content and recyclability rules mostly arrive from 2030. — [Interempresas (GENCI), 22 Jul 2026](https://www.interempresas.net/PrimeraPagina/652360-El-envase-profesional-ante-el-Reglamento-europeo.html)
  - Cartón Circular, a scheme for industrial packaging, has published its 2026 tariff **(snippet)**. — [Industriambiente, Oct 2025](https://www.industriambiente.com/media/uploads/noticias/documentos/ENTREVISTA_A_CARTON_CIRCULAR-web.pdf)
- **Scale:** All packers and first placers on the market; number not found.
- **Cost/impact:** Scheme fees and fine amounts not captured (gap).
- **Driver & status:** RD 1055/2022 **in force since 29 Dec 2022** (deadlines of 31 Dec 2024 and 1 Jan 2025 have passed). PPWR (EU) 2025/40 **applies since 12 Aug 2026**, with later milestones from 2030.
- **How it is solved today:** Collective schemes (GENCI and Cartón Circular for commercial and industrial packaging), consultants and gestorías. No prices found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Capturing packaging bills of materials and weights by material, generating declarations and PPWR declarations of conformity, and checking suppliers' registration numbers.
- **Confidence:** High on the rules; low on how much it hurts and what it costs.

### RET-16: Plastic packaging tax paperwork (Impuesto especial sobre los envases de plástico no reutilizables)
- **Who:** Manufacturers, importers and intra-EU buyers of non-reusable plastic packaging. This includes online SMEs importing packaged goods with more than 5 kg a month of non-recycled plastic.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Above the 5 kg a month threshold, a seller must register, keep a stock ledger, file self-assessments (modelo 592) and hold certificates of recycled content.
- **Evidence:** From Ley 7/2022 ([BOE consolidated text](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2022-5809)):
  - The rate is €0.45 per kg of non-recycled plastic (art. 78).
  - Imports or intra-EU acquisitions are exempt when their non-recycled plastic does not exceed 5 kg a month (art. 75.f).
  - Taxpayers must register in the Registro territorial, and intra-EU buyers must keep a libro registro de existencias (stock ledger) (art. 82).
  - Recycled content must be certified under UNE-EN 15343:2008 by an ENAC-accredited body (art. 77.3).
  - Fines: €1,000 for failing to register; €75 per incorrect invoice or certificate (art. 83).
  - The AEAT page (updated 20 Oct 2026) lists modelo 592 and modelo A22 (refunds) under Orden HFP/1314/2022, and Nota Informativa 19/2024 on the art. 75 pre-declaration. — [AEAT](https://sede.agenciatributaria.gob.es/Sede/impuestos-especiales-medioambientales/impuesto-especial-sobre-envases-plastico-reutilizables.html)
- **Scale:** Unknown.
- **Cost/impact:** The tax itself plus admin time; fines as above.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 7/2022, **in force**. The 2023 start date is general knowledge; the AEAT guidance is current as of Oct 2026.
- **How it is solved today:** Gestorías, ERP systems and certification bodies.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Computing kilograms from purchase data, keeping the stock ledger and filing modelo 592. This overlaps with the tax researcher.
- **Confidence:** High on the rules; low on the pain's intensity.

### RET-17: Web accessibility obligations for online shops (EAA / Ley 11/2023)
- **Who:** Online shops with 10 or more employees or €2M or more turnover. Microenterprises providing services are exempt.
- **Where:** National, transposing EU law.
- **Day-to-day reality:** The whole buying journey (site or app, product information, checkout and payments, search, customer service, invoices) must meet WCAG 2.1 AA and EN 301 549.
- **Evidence:** Applies from 28 Jun 2025. The only exemption is for microenterprises (fewer than 10 employees and under €2M turnover). Sanctions include fines, suspension of the service or taking the website down. — [Legalitas, 12 Jan 2026](https://www.legalitas.com/actualidad/european-accessibility-act). Sanctions follow each sector's regime, with Title III of RDL 1/2013 as a fallback **(snippet)**. — [Cuatrecasas](https://www.cuatrecasas.com/es/portugal/propiedad-intelectual/art/claves-proxima-entrada-vigor-ley-accesibilidad-espana). The microenterprise exemption covers services and generally not manufacturers of physical products **(snippet)**. — [captcha.eu](https://www.captcha.eu/what-is-ley-11-2023/)
- **Scale:** Small (10–49) and medium online sellers; count not found.
- **Cost/impact:** Audit and remediation costs; fine amounts not found.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 11/2023 / Directive 2019/882, **in force since 28 Jun 2025**.
- **How it is solved today:** Accessibility audits and overlay tools; no prices found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Automated scanning and monitoring help; full compliance needs design and development work.
- **Confidence:** Medium. The law is clear; no enforcement data found.

### RET-18: Product-safety information on online listings (GPSR)
- **Who:** Online and marketplace sellers of consumer products, including second-hand and refurbished goods.
- **Where:** EU-wide, and therefore national.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Each listing must carry the manufacturer or EU responsible-person details and safety information. Marketplaces enforce this.
- **Evidence:** The GPSR (EU) 2023/988 applies from 13 Dec 2024 to products offered online to EU consumers, including used and refurbished ones. Marketplaces need a single point of contact and must act quickly on dangerous products. — [CCPC (Ireland), 13 Dec 2024](https://www.ccpc.ie/news-and-media/news/article/2024/12/13/new-eu-product-safety-laws-now-in-place). Spanish evidence is thin: a Spanish toy makers' association (AEFJ) petition on unsafe marketplace products **(snippet)**.
- **Scale/Cost:** Not found.
- **Driver & status:** GPSR, **in force since 13 Dec 2024**.
- **How it is solved today:** Compliance-service providers (the search showed vendor blogs only).
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Managing product compliance data and pushing it to listings.
- **Confidence:** Low (no Spain-specific pain data).

### Cited Findings
- The €3 EU duty is usually paid by the platform or importer, which may pass it on. If it isn't included in the price, the courier may advance it and charge the buyer on delivery. — [OCU, 1 Jul 2026](https://www.ocu.org/consumo-familia/compras-online/noticias/tasa-aduana-paquetes-baratos)
- Consumers' 9.1-day average return time against the 14-day withdrawal window suggests retailers are carrying returned stock for long periods (ZigZag data above).

### Inferences
- E-commerce compliance (RD 1055/2022 registration and declarations, PPWR, plastic tax, EAA, GPSR, the 30-day price rule) is the most software-shaped cluster. It is recurring, data-heavy and mostly invisible to small sellers until a marketplace, distributor or inspector asks. RD 1055 arts. 28–30 turn distributors into enforcers, because they may not sell unregistered producers' goods. That creates a business-to-business check that software could automate.
- Shipping-label tools are commoditised (free Sendcloud tier, ShippyPro from €207/month). The open needs sit at the edges: islands and customs (RET-14), damage claims (RET-12) and returns abuse (RET-11).
- Amazon's 2025–26 cuts on low-price items ease part of the fee pain. The April 2026 FBA surcharge shows fees keep moving, which supports per-product margin monitoring.

- Carrier rate changes for Correos, SEUR, MRW and GLS, and Packlink pricing: not found.
- Miravia, eBay and other marketplace fees: not found.
- Payment fraud and chargebacks in Spain: only global, Latin American or vendor sources found (e.g., Chargeflow cites Juniper: friendly fraud ≈22% of chargebacks worldwide in 2026). No Spanish figures.
- Advertising and SEO costs (Google and Meta CPCs, customer acquisition cost) for Spanish SMEs: not found.
- EU VAT OSS (€10,000 threshold): left to the tax researcher.
- Scheme fees (Ecoembes for household packaging, GENCI and others for commercial), and fines under Ley 7/2022 for packaging non-compliance: not captured.

- EAA and GPSR enforcement actions in Spain: not found.

## Personal services: hairdressers, barbers, beauty salons, gyms and studios, tattoo, pet businesses, repair, laundry

### Takeaway
The clearest evidence concerns salons. Booking marketplaces charge 25–30% on a new client's first visit (Treatwell 25% with a 12-month minimum contract; Booksy Boost 30% capped at €50). Hairdressing VAT stays at 21% after repeated rejected attempts to cut it. Independent gyms face a consolidating low-cost market: the top 10 chains hold 54%, VivaGym+Synergym has more than 450 clubs, and Basic-Fit went from 90 to 139 Spanish centres in a year. Pet shops have operated under Ley 7/2023 since 2023, with no dog, cat or ferret sales in shops and fines of €500–200,000. No Spanish data could be found on no-shows, salon staffing, gym churn, laundries, repair shops or tattoo sanitary rules.

### RET-19: Booking marketplaces: commissions, lock-ins and no-shows
- **Who:** Hair salons, barbers, beauty and nail centres (autónomos and micro), and boutique fitness studios.
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Salons use Booksy and Treatwell for visibility and online booking, and pay a commission on marketplace-sourced first visits. Owners complain of being charged even when the client doesn't show, of support that doesn't answer, and of 12-month contracts.
- **Evidence:**
  - Treatwell charges a 25% commission on first bookings by new marketplace clients, 0% on repeat bookings, and 2% + IVA when the client prepays online. The minimum contract is 12 months. Its Advanced plan bundles VeriFactu/TicketBAI e-invoicing. — [Treatwell pricing, accessed Oct 2026](https://www.treatwell.es/partners/precios/)
  - Booksy costs €34.99 + IVA a month plus €8 per extra staff member, with no lock-in. Its "Boost" marketplace promotion takes 30% of a new client's first visit. Payment fees: 2% + €0.15 for mobile payments, 0.99% + €0.10 for Tap to Pay, and 1.5% for instant payouts. — [Booksy blog, 10 Aug 2026](https://biz.booksy.com/es-es/blog/cuanto-cuesta-un-software-de-reservas-para-salones)
  - Boost is capped at €50 + IVA per client, and a 5-person team pays about €66.99 + IVA a month. Complaints aggregated from Capterra: poor support, unexpected commissions, weak Boost results, difficulty exporting client data. — [Pabau (competitor), 8 Oct 2026](https://pabau.com/es/blog/booksy-precio/). A second source confirms €34.99 a month and a 7-day trial **(snippet)**. — [La Voz de Ibiza](https://lavozdeibiza.com/?p=163018)
  - A salon owner's review of Treatwell: *"comisiones abusivas que cobran, aunque el cliente no venga"* ("abusive commissions they charge even if the client doesn't come"), and partner support described as non-existent. Treatwell replied in March 2025 **(snippet)**. — [Capterra](https://capterra.com/p/181827/Treatwell/reviews/?page=2)
  - No Spanish no-show statistics were found. Vendor claims (e.g., AgendaPro) cite unnamed studies.
- **Scale:** About 72,300 hairdressing and beauty establishments in 2021 **(snippet; DBK study, Dec 2022)**. — [DBK](https://dbk.es/en/estudios/16575/summary)
- **Cost/impact:** Illustration: 25% of a €40 first visit is €10 (Treatwell); 30% is €12 (Booksy Boost).
- **Driver & status:** Market.
- **How it is solved today:** Booksy and Treatwell as above. Koibox from €40/month and Flowww (aesthetics) from €59/month **(Capterra snippets)**. Bewe targets sports centres and salons; no price found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Yes. Direct booking with deposits or card-on-file, own-channel marketing and client-data portability. The market is crowded.
- **Confidence:** Medium. Pricing is solid; the size of the no-show problem is unknown.

### RET-20: 21% VAT on hairdressing and beauty services
- **Who:** Hairdressers, barbers and beauty centres, mostly autónomos and micro (in 2021, 60% of salons had a single worker).
- **Where:** National.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Salons charge 21% VAT. They cannot pass it all on to price-sensitive clients, and they lose business to undeclared home-based work (an intrusismo claim common in the sector; not evidenced in the sources here).
- **Evidence:**
  - Hairdressing moved from the reduced rate (8%) to 21% in Sept 2012. There were about 50,000 salons before the pandemic and about 35,000 in June 2021. Stanpa warned of 17,000 closures and costed the cut at about €140M. In June 2021 a cut already approved by parliament was vetoed. — [El Independiente, 5 Sep 2021](https://www.elindependiente.com/?p=1801776)
  - Six amendments to the 2023 state budget restoring 10% were rejected. Valencian salons' billing was 25.28% below 2019 (national −20.2%) and costs rose 27.2%. The protesters called the repeated vetoes "expolio fiscal" (fiscal plunder). — [Valencia Plaza, 5 Dec 2022](https://valenciaplaza.com/valenciaplaza/peluquerias-valencianas-celebran-una-vigilia-de-luto-para-reclamar-una-reduccion-del-iva-al-10)
  - The government blocked amendments using art. 134.6 of the Constitution, putting the cost at €194M. Congress passed two non-binding resolutions, which were ignored **(snippet; date not seen)**. — [Vozpópuli](https://www.vozpopuli.com/actualidad/iva-erc-bng-pp-presupuestos.html)
  - Count conflict: DBK's 72,300 establishments (2021) against the sector's own ~35,000 salons (June 2021). The definitions are probably different (DBK includes beauty).
- **Scale:** Tens of thousands of establishments (see conflict above). DBK puts 2021 sales at €4,050M **(snippet)**.
- **Cost/impact:** An 11-point rate gap on price-sensitive services.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 37/1992 (IVA), general rate. **Proposals to cut it have been repeatedly rejected (2021, 2022). No 2025–2026 change was found.** The AEAT page (updated 13 Oct 2026) confirms reduced rates of 10% and 4% but its list of items could not be viewed, so the current rate for hairdressing is not verified at item level.
- **How it is solved today:** Not applicable.
- **Software-solvable?:** No (tax policy).
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### RET-21: Independent gyms squeezed by consolidating low-cost chains
- **Who:** Independent gyms, boutique studios and CrossFit boxes (micro and small).
- **Where:** National. Madrid, Catalonia and Andalusia hold about 60% of gyms.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Chains backed by private equity open and acquire clubs fast and set a price ceiling of €20–40 a month. Independents compete on niche and service while needing enough members (one estimate says 400–600 active members) to break even.
- **Evidence:**
  - DBK: €1,500M market in 2024 (+14%), about €1,650M in 2025 (+10%), with +7–8% forecast for 2026. 4,600 gyms in Sept 2025 (+3.5%). The top 5 held 37% and the top 10 54% in 2024, with the rest a fragmented field of small independents. — [Emprendedores, 14 Jan 2026](https://emprendedores.es/franquicias/analisis-sectoriales/gimnasios-oportunidades-en-franquicia-2026/)
  - VivaGym+Synergym: more than 450 clubs, over €270M revenue and about 1M members. VivaGym made seven acquisitions in 2025. Basic-Fit Spain grew from 90 to 139 centres in one year. The 15 largest chains lost more than €420M in 2020–2023.   - An average gym costs €30–50 a month; Basic-Fit and McFIT start from €20; sign-up fees run €30–100. — [eldiario.es, 16 Jan 2025](https://www.eldiario.es/consumoclaro/pagar-cuota-gimnasio-hacerte-equipo-casa-sale-rentable_1_11960124.html)
  - More than 6.5M members in 2025, and acquiring a member costs 5–25× more than keeping one (both unsourced). — [Asest, 20 May 2026](https://asest.es/story/el-negocio-de-los-gimnasios/)
  - Small and medium gyms need 400–600 active members to be profitable **(snippet)**. — [Fitgeneration](https://fitgeneration.es/merece-la-pena-montar-un-gimnasio/)
- **Scale:** 4,600 gyms, most of them independents.
- **Cost/impact:** Price ceiling set by the chains; churn cost not quantified (gap).
- **Driver & status:** Market consolidation; ongoing in 2026.
- **How it is solved today:** AimHarder for boxes (no public price) **(Capterra snippet)**. Trainingym pricing not found.
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Retention and CRM, class booking and dunning help; software cannot fix price competition.
- **Confidence:** Medium.

### RET-22: Pet shops and animal businesses under Ley 7/2023 (bienestar animal)
- **Who:** Pet shops, breeders and animal-related professionals (residences, trainers). Whether groomers (peluquerías caninas) are covered is not addressed in the text read.
- **Where:** National basic law, applied by the regions.
- **Day-to-day reality:** Shops can no longer sell dogs, cats or ferrets. They must keep sale contracts and records, register each sale within 3 working days, separate animals from the public with physical barriers, and stay off direct online sales, while several implementing regulations are still pending.
- **Evidence:** From the consolidated text of Ley 7/2023 ([BOE](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2023-7936); no amendments to the law shown, accessed Oct 2026):
  - In force 29 Sep 2023.
  - Dogs, cats and ferrets may be sold only directly by registered breeders, not in pet shops (art. 27 l; art. 55.1). Shops had a 12-month transition (DT4), so the ban applied from about 29 Sep 2024 (computed).
  - Each sale needs a written contract and written information for the buyer. Records must be kept at least 3 years, and the sale entered in the Registro de Animales de Compañía within 3 working days. Minimum age at sale is 2 months, or 4 months if sold from a different facility (art. 55).
  - Shops must have physical barriers between the public and the animals, and staff must supervise any direct contact (art. 56).
  - Direct online sale is prohibited, and ads must show the breeder's or núcleo zoológico's registration number (art. 57).
  - Fines: leve €500–10,000; grave €10,001–50,000; muy grave €50,001–200,000 (art. 76).
  - The government had 6 months to regulate the Central Registry. Registration of breeders, sellers and behaviour professionals becomes mandatory 12 months after that regulation (final provision DF5).
  - The owners' training course and dog owners' liability insurance depend on implementing regulation (art. 30).
- **Scale:** Number of pet shops not found.
- **Cost/impact:** Lost puppy and kitten sales, fines, and record-keeping.
- **Driver & status:** Ley 7/2023, **in force**. The shop sales ban applies since about Sept 2024. **Whether the pending regulations (registry, course, insurance amount) have been approved by Oct 2026 was not verified.**
- **How it is solved today:** No software found (gap).
- **Software-solvable?:** Partly. Contract, record and registry workflows, and keeping ads compliant. The ban itself is not.
- **Confidence:** High on the rules; low on business impact.

## What these businesses pay for software and services, and what they complain about

### Takeaway
Listed prices are modest: €20–60 a month for booking or POS software, €39 a month for an Amazon seller account, and €200–375 a month for mid-market shipping suites. The real cost sits in percentage fees: card fees of 0.3–2%, platform payment fees of about 2%, marketplace referral fees of 8–15%, and booking commissions of 25–30% on new clients. Documented complaints are about unexpected commissions, unreachable support, lock-in (Treatwell's 12-month minimum), difficulty exporting data (Booksy), and rushed, low-quality subsidised websites (Kit Digital).

## Top pains
1. **RET-04 Repeat shoplifting and staff aggression.** €2.8bn a year (1.1% of turnover); 51% by repeat offenders; small shops suffer 30% more thefts. LO 1/2026 (in force 10 Apr 2026) ties prison to prior convictions, which rewards systematic reporting. Partly software-solvable.
2. **RET-01/RET-02 Neighbourhood retail attrition driven by rent and costs.** About 13.6k net closures in 2025; Madrid −14% (2020–24); rents of €19–21/m² in Madrid and Barcelona, with documented renewal jumps from +31% to 4×. The most severe pain, but mostly not software-solvable.
3. **RET-11 Online returns.** €13.3bn in 2025; 23.7% of non-food orders; serial and late returners cause 47% of returns; consumers refuse to pay for returns. Software-solvable.
4. **RET-15/RET-16 (with RET-17/RET-18) The e-commerce compliance stack.** Packaging EPR registration and declarations, distributors' duty to refuse unregistered producers, PPWR since 12 Aug 2026, plastic tax above 5 kg/month, EAA, GPSR. All in force, recurring and data-heavy. Highly software-solvable; the rules are well evidenced but the size of the burden is inferred.
5. **RET-19 Booking-marketplace commissions and lock-in for salons.** Treatwell takes 25% of a first visit with a 12-month minimum; Booksy Boost 30% (cap €50); complaints about being charged for no-shows. About 72k establishments (DBK 2021). Software-solvable but crowded.
6. **RET-12 Transit damage.** 15.2M items returned a year, over €1bn, about €30.9k per retailer per year (single vendor-sponsored study). Partly software-solvable (claims, carrier analytics).
7. **RET-05 Card-acceptance costs.** 0.3–2% on every card sale for all shops. Bizum in-store (from 18 May 2026) may compress fees. Partly solvable.
8. **RET-06 Ultra-low-cost cross-border platforms, and RET-20 21% hairdressing VAT.** Both are widespread and severe: about 5.9bn duty-free items reached EU consumers in 2025, and the VAT gap affects tens of thousands of salons. Neither is software-solvable; included for completeness.

## Noise and dead ends
- **Chargebacks and friendly fraud:** no Spanish data found, only global, Latin American or vendor content. Weakly evidenced in this pass.
- **No-show losses in salons:** often cited, but no Spanish statistic exists in the sources found; vendor claims are uncited. Deposit and reminder features are also already standard in Booksy, Treatwell and others, so the problem is cheaply solved.
- **Multi-carrier shipping and label tools:** commoditised (free Sendcloud tier; ShippyPro €207/month; Outvio). Not a gap except in niches (islands and customs, damage claims).
- **"Abandoned Kit Digital websites":** anecdotal (a LinkedIn opinion and a 2023 article). The INE figures as reported conflict (29.6% vs 33.2% vs 37.0%). Needs primary INE data before use.
- **Cutting hairdressing VAT to 10%:** a long-running political demand, repeatedly vetoed or rejected (2021, 2022), with no 2025–26 movement found. Not software-solvable.
- **Sunday trading and ZGAT rules:** regional politics; at most a calendar lookup service.
- **Competition from Temu, Shein and AliExpress:** a market and trade-policy force; the EU €3 duty has applied since 1 Jul 2026. Not software-solvable for local shops.
- **Rent pressure:** severe but not software-solvable; at most rent benchmarking.
- **Amazon fee pain:** easing for low-priced items after the Dec 2025 and Jan 2026 cuts. Partly offset by the April 2026 FBA surcharge.
- **WhatsApp per-message costs:** only affect users of the API, not the free app. Likely minor for most micro-businesses.
- **The "≥400 closures in Jan–Apr 2026" figure (El Español):** inconsistent with UPTA's ~1,132 a month. Discard.
- **GPSR:** a real EU obligation, but no Spain-specific pain or enforcement evidence found.
- **Pet-shop dog, cat and ferret sales ban:** already fully in effect since about Sept 2024 (adapt or exit). The remaining compliance (records, registry) is small and depends on regulations whose status is unknown.
- **Repair shops and right to repair; laundries; tattoo sanitary rules; pet groomers:** dead ends in this pass. Nothing could be verified once the search budget ran out (EUR-Lex and ECHA fetches failed). They are unknowns, not confirmed noise.
